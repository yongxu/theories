# Knowledge is a common good

This work belongs to the commons. Its text, its Lean code and its data, like the knowledge in them, are no one's property, its writers' included. So it has no licence, by choice: a licence treats knowledge as property its holder lends out on terms.

What its writers wrote needs no one's permission for anything. Read it, copy it, translate it, change it, build on it and share it, for any purpose. Where a law would still give its writers a right over it, they waive that right as far as the law allows, will never assert it, and will pass it to no one who would. Nothing is asked in return. Saying where it came from helps others trace and check it; that is a help, not a condition. May it go wherever it helps life go on.

So "no licence" here is not "all rights reserved". It is the opposite: the writers reserve no right and will assert none, so no permission is needed.

Material from others, wherever it sits in the repository, is not the writers' to release. People's own words kept in `sources/` are theirs to share as they choose, and editions, translations and datasets made by others stay under the terms their provenance notes give.
