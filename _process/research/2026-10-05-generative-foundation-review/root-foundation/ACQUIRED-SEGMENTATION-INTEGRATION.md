## 3. Preserved failures and the corrected conjecture

1. **No experience:** the target utterance remains unparsed even though the underlying operation was formed.
2. **Same event frequencies, different relations:** a reordered exposure contains the same count of every elementary event but changes the learned boundaries and prevents this use. This comparison controls unigram counts, not all substring frequencies.
3. **A known gap:** `a, gap, b` supplies no observed `a→b` adjacency. Deleting the gap would invent evidence. The model does not detect unmarked omissions.
4. **Insufficient support:** 27 units suffice for grouping but leave too little for the selected response. At 19 units, further recording stops after the actual five-event prefix and the utterance remains unparsed.
5. **A locally regular error:** repeated `abab` can join across the intended `ab` boundaries. Recurrence alone does not determine the target units.
6. **Partial success:** a seven-event prefix already parses `cd ab cd`, while it fails on `cd cd` and lacks support to execute. The initial stronger conjecture that this prefix also fails on the first utterance was false. The failed check is preserved in the first retained run, and `partial_experience_can_parse_one_form_without_general_grouping` checks the corrected distinction. A successful instance is not general recovery.
7. **Selective continuation:** parse failure triggers the supplied broader exposure, which can repair grouping and enable response; a recognized `ab` does not trigger it. Successful local recognition can therefore leave a wider grouping problem undiagnosed.

These failures concern specified encoding and use relationships. They do not show that every other segmentation is intrinsically wrong, nor do successful outputs independently establish ethical quality.

## 5. Primary evidence and serious comparisons

**Segmentation and later meaning.** Graf Estes, Evans, Alibali and Saffran (2007), [primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3864753/), examined segmentation followed by object-label learning. The indexed primary text of Experiment 2's methods/results was read; the direct PMC view presented a browser challenge and the author PDF returned an error, so full uninterrupted retrieval is not claimed. Twenty-eight 17-month-olds contributed data, with 13 further exclusions reported. Word and boundary-spanning part-word test labels were matched in exposure frequency but differed in internal transitional structure. Looking-time results supported learning for word labels, with a condition-by-trial interaction; they did not show the same learning for part-word labels. The procedure and exclusions constrain generalization. **Our inference:** acquired grouping should be connected to later binding and use, not treated as equivalent to meaning acquisition. The present model supplies that later decoder, so it explains only the grouping-to-existing-use connection and does not reproduce the experiment or derive semantic binding.

**Socially organized access.** Kuhl, Tsao and Liu (2003), [author-hosted primary paper](https://ilabs.uw.edu/wp-content/uploads/kuhl_2003_pnas_1532872100.pdf), compared infant foreign-language exposure through live interaction and audiovisual/audio recordings. Methods, results and relevant discussion were read. The studies used twelve sessions over four weeks; 21 infants completed Experiment 1 and 28 Experiment 2. Live Mandarin exposure supported the tested phonetic discrimination; recorded conditions did not show comparable learning. Recorded exposure used the same speakers and materials but contained more syllables, and visual attention differed across conditions. Thus this is not an identical-input isolation of one social mechanism. The authors discuss attention, motivation and referential information as possible contributors. **Our inference:** a model that simply gates stored events must explain why that gate changes and distinguish these candidates. The paper does not establish social interaction as universally necessary for all learning. Its phonetic task is distinct from the preceding segmentation/label-learning task; the experiments are not one observed developmental chain.

Statistical segmentation, compositional interpretation and failure-contingent sampling have established precedents. This construction claims an explicit verified connection and preserved failure scopes, not a novel segmentation algorithm, an exclusive generative architecture or a new empirical result. A conventional statistical/compositional model can realize the same mechanism and receives that explanatory credit.

## 12. Developmental evidence changes the scope of the claim

The prior section's Yu–Smith naming comparison remains correlational. It motivates retaining actual access and social coordination, but does not identify this geometric model or prove that active movement always improves learning.

**Libertus and Needham (2010), [primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2991490/).** Indexed primary methods and results excerpts were read; direct access presented a browser challenge. Parent-guided training contrasted infants' own sticky-mitten contacts and manipulation with observation of parent-produced movements over two weeks. The reported live assessment showed increased reaching/grasping and changed visual exploration following active training. The procedures also differed in opportunities for self-produced contact and manipulation; a pure isolated motor-command effect is not established. **Our inference:** motor experience and subsequent access/exploration can be investigated as connected developmental variables. This evidence does not prove the model's movement law, name-binding mechanism or evolutionary origin, and no source data were reanalyzed.

**van den Berg et al. (2022), [preregistered primary study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cdev.13835).** The methods, reported results and relevant discussion were read. Ninety-six Swedish infants were randomized across sticky-mitten, observational and no-training conditions. The study found no greater reaching/grasping improvement from sticky-mitten training under its measures. It retained missing observations under its intent-to-treat protocol. The authors discuss procedural differences, including the absence of repeated home visits and assessments used in earlier work. **Our inference:** this constrains any general claim that brief active training reliably creates the needed motor capacity. It does not show that motion has no consequences for accessible input, or that every social-perception finding is false. Neither this study nor the earlier positive report jointly measures the full movement–access–binding history represented here.

The evidence therefore supports investigating particular developmental pathways while requiring measured access, retention and learning outcomes. It does not license a universal favorable developmental cascade. An evolutionary explanation additionally needs evidence about the development and transmission of the relevant capacities and their historical selection; these training studies do not supply that history.

## 25. Updating present trust does not identify historical reinterpretation

**Ronfard and Lane (2018), [primary author manuscript](https://cdn-dev.vanderbilt.edu/t2-my-dev/wp-content/uploads/sites/2066/2016/09/Ronfard-Lane-Children-Update-Epistemic-Trust-in-press.pdf), DOI 10.1111/cdev.12720.** Read methods, focal-analysis description and relevant results/discussion (manuscript pp. 10–14, 18–30); no raw-data reanalysis or independent regression reproduction. Participants were also assigned apology conditions. Children adjusted subsequent trust as accuracy evidence accumulated; the authors did not find the predicted recency/primacy pattern. One adjusted third-versus-second-round comparison differed; the overall pattern is not a universal absence of timing effects. The task measures use of current recommendations after feedback, not later reinterpretation of an unresolved earlier claim. Each earlier sticker location had already been revealed. A single inaccurate utterance also does not establish a persistent change in its production mechanism. Thus this supports revisable reliance, without identifying retrospective scope or our change envelope.

**Gershman, Monfils, Norman and Niv (2017), [primary paper](https://gershmanlab.com/pubs/GershmanMonfilsNormanNiv17.pdf), DOI 10.7554/eLife.23763.** Read the model and pertinent scope on pp. 4–11; no simulation rerun or whole-paper replication. ## 27. Forming an access distinction and bringing it into later learning

9 October 2026. A history of looking, obstruction and shared activity can change what later movements or reports elicit. Some effects persist as discrimination of access, others as retention of an encounter, and others enter a subsequent choice. These are distinguishable contributions within the unfolding, not three independent substances or necessarily three separate brain modules. Their coordination is an explanatory target.

**Results reused:** `historical-source-binding`, `retrospective-source-binding`, `encounter-selection-and-revision`, and the retained-use boundary in `encounter-control-and-retention`. **Delta:** primary developmental and comparative evidence constrains the psychological correspondence of their available access, memory and application interfaces. This source assessment is evidence for existing records, not another independent result or mathematical claim. **Supplied correspondence:** recognizing a barrier, tracking an encounter and applying a task instruction remain capacities of the experimental participants; their entire formation is not experimentally reconstructed. **Evidence:** five primary papers below, with methods/results and relevant discussion read; no raw-data reanalysis, independent replication or supplement-wide audit. **Integration:** experience can alter an access-sensitive response, but successful transfer to one response does not establish automatic use in every later inference.

**Meltzoff and Brooks (2008), [primary author paper](https://ilabs.uw.edu/wp-content/uploads/08Meltzoff_Brooks_Self_Experience.pdf), DOI 10.1037/a0012888, pp. 1259–1263.** Randomized experience with opaque, windowed or control cloths preceded identical gaze-following tests. Among 96 included twelve-month-olds, opaque experience reduced following of a blindfolded adult; among 72 included eighteen-month-olds, experience with a seemingly opaque but see-through cloth increased following. An additional 37 and 31 infants, respectively, were excluded for specified procedural/behavioral reasons. Coding was blind to treatment and head-turn direction. Crucially, the adult's test cloth was actually opaque even after see-through training: altered following demonstrates experiential transfer, not guaranteed correct access attribution. Different cohorts and procedures do not establish a within-child developmental trajectory. The findings support experience changing subsequent use of another's movement; mental-state inference, learned access relations and their implementation remain to distinguish.

**Nurmsoo and Robinson (2009a), [primary paper](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/32065/1/Nurmsoo%20%26%20Robinson%202009a.pdf), DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8624.2008.01243.x, pp. 24–26.** In a cooperative hidden-object task, 67 children encountered a puppet's errors under adequate or inadequate sensory access. Children were explicitly taught which access disclosed color or hardness. They subsequently followed the now-informed puppet more often when earlier errors had arisen under inadequate access. This measures prospective reliance: on test trials the puppet contradicted the child's guess regardless of its truth, and the answer was not revealed. Following the puppet therefore cannot be equated with measured judgment accuracy. The study supports sensitivity to the circumstances of earlier errors within this interaction, without testing retrospective correction of the old statements.

**Nurmsoo and Robinson (2009b), [primary paper](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/32064/1/Nurmsoo%20%26%20Robinson%202009b.pdf), DOI 10.1111/j.1467-7687.2008.00750.x, pp. 43–46.** Experiment 2 tested 148 children with two video speakers who had misnamed familiar objects, one while blindfolded. When both could see and offered conflicting novel names, the groups did not prefer the previously blindfolded speaker. Many children could nevertheless explain that speaker's errors through inability to see. Experiment 3 added initial evidence that both speakers could answer correctly: the 28 children still showed no preference, although 83% of the 24 spontaneous explanations cited the blindfold's effect. These null preferences do not establish equivalence or absence of an access representation. They constrain the claim that explaining impaired access automatically yields selective subsequent word learning.

**Our inference from the contrast.** A positive result in the cooperative task and absent preference in the naming task require an explanation sensitive to what the activity asks participants to do. The studies differ in content, live/video interaction, number of informants, instruction and choice structure; they do not identify which difference causes the contrast. “Task dependence” records this unresolved connection, not a sufficient causal explanation. It would be unwarranted either to deny children's access sensitivity altogether or to count an explicit explanation as proof that the relation guided their learning.

## 28. Comparative components and the limits of evolutionary inference

**Kano, Krupenye, Hirata, Tomonaga and Call (2019), [primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6800361/), DOI 10.1073/pnas.1910095116, Methods–Discussion.** Forty-seven apes were assigned opaque or see-through barrier experience, followed by the same test video of an actor behind a barrier during an object's relocation/removal. Differential looking favored the object's pre-barrier location more after opaque experience. That comparison used the 30 apes who looked at a box: 13 opaque and 17 see-through; the other 17 contributed no defined differential score. First looks did not distinguish conditions. The tested sample comprised 29 chimpanzees, 14 bonobos and four orangutans, insufficient to infer species equivalence. The effect supports experience-sensitive anticipation under a shared test display. Learned visibility relations and limited belief tracking remain alternatives to a full propositional theory of mind. No supplementary-data reanalysis is claimed.

**Basile and Hampton (2017), [primary author paper](https://hamptonlab.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/basile.and_.hampton.2017.pdf), DOI 10.1016/j.cognition.2017.06.009, Experiments 1–3 and Discussion.** Twelve experienced adult male rhesus monkeys distinguished images encountered through touching or classifying. Moving the classified item earlier increased confusion between studied items without a comparable increase in unstudied-item choices. Six completed a subsequent cue-dependent selection experiment, averaging 43,746 training trials; the other six moved to another scheduled experiment, not a demonstrated failure group. Reversing study order preserved selection by study method. Here “source” means manner of encounter, not speaker identity. The results support dissociable item and source information and trained flexible use; they do not establish spontaneous social source monitoring, conscious episodic recollection, or a human-like implementation. Source forgetting and confusion are not uniquely separated.

**Evolutionary interpretation.** The shared explanatory target is how retained effects of encounters participate differently in later activity. These studies make some constituent capacities experimentally tractable beyond humans; they neither establish that the whole human arrangement is present nor show how it evolved. Similar performance may involve conserved organization, convergent acquisition or task-specific training. Comparative developmental trajectories, matched demands, inheritance and ecological histories would be needed to discriminate those possibilities. The current evidence therefore constrains the components an evolutionary account must connect. It supplies neither a lineage reconstruction nor a selection explanation, and does not convert all participants' capacities into one observed evolutionary sequence.
