# Significance and prior-art review: what the central results establish, and what was known

20 October 2026. An AI review (board item SA1), read-only and adversarial. It built no Lean, ran no checker and changed no record, chapter, OPENING, README, GOALS, generated page or Lean file. It added only this file and its row on the work board. Base: main at `2915627b6` (the board claim on `6eee7da51`). Every Lean statement quoted was read in the source at that commit.

**Why this review.** On 19 October an outside reviewer (a friend's GPT model) concluded that the repository holds substantial formalization but no evidence of a major discovery, universal law or alignment solution, and that its main failure mode is a mismatch between the rigor of the formal machinery and the epistemic strength of the surrounding interpretations. The twentieth review (R24) and the programme R25-R40 fixed internal problems: two bases, redundancy, naming, the ethics' scope/order split, natural-number facts headlined as universal, chapter length. None of them asked whether the central results are new, or what the literature already had. The canonical chapter has no section relating its results to known ones. This review answers that part of the critique. Its aim is to find which results are new and which are restatements, not to defend them.

**Method.** For the ten central results of [the chapter's preface](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/THE-THEORY-OF-BECOMING.md#central-results) (the same ten as the [twentieth review's lens C §2.5](twentieth-review/C-CHAPTER.md)) and for every law cited in [README](../../../../README.md) notes 1-15 (the OPENING notes `law`, `conditions`, `proof`, `self`, `count`, `axiom`, `sages`, `ethics`, `dao`, `good-evil`, `alignment`, `reach`, `guardian`, `own`, `cancer`), this review records:

1. what the Lean statement says in plain mathematics, with its hypotheses;
2. how far it follows from the definitions alone;
3. the closest known result in the literature;
4. what, if anything, is new beyond that;
5. a verdict: **novel**, **known result restated**, **near-definitional**, or **interpretation exceeds the theorem** (a result can carry a mathematical verdict and the last flag both).

"Near-definitional" means the proof is a few lines of unfolding, substitution or a one-step argument once the definitions are read; it is not a fault in a lemma, but it is a fault when the result is presented as a discovery. "Known result restated" means the mathematical content is a standard theorem, specialized to the theory's types. "Novel" is reserved for a statement whose content does not appear, in substance, in the literature this review could identify.

The Lean is in [`Becoming/`](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/becoming/Becoming/); file names below are relative to it.

## The verdict in brief

**The outside critique is substantially right.** No central result is a new theorem of research-level mathematics. Every one is either an unfolding of the theory's own definitions or a specialization of a known result: telescoping sums and two-valued bookkeeping (one change, keeping pace), the cycle structure of injective maps on finite sets (recurrence, with Bennett's 1973 history embedding as the "kept history" law), ranking-function termination (rest, fed from beyond, the Petri-net instance), the invariance of any function of an observation under changes the observation cannot see (selection through couplings), the nilpotency of acyclic adjacency (loops), Fine and Wilf / Moore (identification), set-inclusion dominance of opportunity sets (widening, the four cases), and the universalization argument of Gewirth and others (the step of invariance). The chapter already says this of several of them (`Web.paths_comparison_widens`, `Web.significance_undetermined`, `selects_alike_iff`, the no-premise laws), and some prior work is credited in passing (Fine and Wilf, Gold, Putnam and Kelly, Specker, Price, Landauer, Gewirth), but nowhere together and nowhere for the alignment line.

**What is new is the artefact and its discipline, not a discovery.** Three things survive the audit:

1. **An exact, machine-checked account of which premise each conclusion uses**, with a witness world meeting and a witness world failing each premise (`World.contingent`, `Becoming.ExchangePremises`), and calibrations where a conclusion needs more logic than it states (`World.law_iff_em`, `World.lpo_of_finite_ends`, the `Tail` laws). The techniques are standard (independence by countermodels; the encodings of reverse constructive mathematics), but the specific statements are new and the bookkeeping is honest. This is the strongest contribution, and it is methodological.
2. **The ethics stated with scope and order apart**: the continuation condition fixes whose losses count and orders nothing (mixed parts stay unordered, `Web.mixed_iff_adds`, `Transfer.unordered`), and the ranking lives only in C. Formally near-definitional, and the universalization step is Gewirth's; but the explicit split, with the covering hypothesis named, is a real clarification that the comparable literature often leaves implicit.
3. **One vocabulary across conservation accounting, observation-limited selection and opportunity-set comparison**, so that the margin law (harm as net draw beyond the margin, under balance) and the selection gap can be stated in one model. The connection is a framing; each piece is elementary.

**Where the interpretations exceed the theorems** is mostly in the prose around the alignment line, the "no absolute self" and "continuation beyond death" readings, the "law of how anything comes to be", the exactness of the good/evil line, and the "nothing the part senses shows how near the break is" sentence. OPENING's footnotes are, on the whole, careful; the body, in Ren's voice, asserts more than they do in about a dozen places ([proposals](#proposals-for-opening)).

<a id="what-is-new"></a>
## What is new, stated without inflation

- **No new mathematics of research significance.** The proofs are short; the hardest are `Identification.bounded_agree` (a Fine-Wilf-type bound), the pigeonhole without decidable equality, and the constructive calibrations. Each has a known analogue.
- **A new artefact.** A pure-core Lean development (prelude only, empty axiom reports) in which every conclusion names its premises, each premise has witnesses on both sides, and the logical strength a conclusion needs is calibrated. That is a contribution to the practice of stating a broad theory honestly. It is the kind of thing the outside review called "substantial formalization", and the review was right not to call it a discovery.
- **A clarified ethics, not a grounded one.** The theory shows exactly where its ethics' premises sit: scope in the condition, order in C, practical judgment in A, L and the practical commitments, and it proves that the condition alone ranks nothing. It does not give a reason to accept either premise; the chapter says the warrant is open (§8.5). The universalization step is Gewirth's (and Hare's, Nagel's, Sidgwick's); C is the monotonicity axiom of the opportunity-set literature.
- **No result on alignment beyond the known proxy problem.** The selection laws say that a selection cannot see what does not reach what it observes. That is true and important, and it is what Goodhart, Amodei et al., Manheim and Garrabrant, and Zhuang and Hadfield-Menell already say, the last with a stronger theorem. The theory's §8.9 already lists the real gap: which function training installs.
- **No empirical claim tested.** Every correspondence to physics, ecology, economics or AI is stated as a hypothesis or a judgment; none is measured.

**What would change this verdict.** A theorem whose statement a specialist would not foresee from the definitions; a result someone outside the project uses; a prediction about an actual system (a training run, an ecosystem, an economy) that could fail and does not; or a derivation of a premise (the least loss, C, the condition) from something weaker that the literature does not already have.

## A check before building: the oversight theorem (20 October 2026)

An AI check (one session, started from the SA1 session), made before any Lean, of a candidate contribution this review's [verdict](#what-is-new) left open: whether the theory's combination of conservation (balance), renewal gated on levels, selection through coupling, pools and late reading yields a necessary-and-sufficient theorem a specialist would not foresee. **Verdict: known in substance. Not built.** No Lean, no record and no board claim; this section is the whole output.

**The target.** For an optimizer whose policies a selection admits (a reward, an overseer, a pool of overseers): every renewable condition the optimizer draws on stays at or above its need under every admitted policy *iff* (i) the selector's coupling closure (a pool's: the union) contains every condition some admitted policy can draw on beyond its margin, and (ii) the selector's reading latency `ℓ` and the per-step draw bound `d` satisfy the late-band condition against each such margin. The pieces on the spine: the margin law (`Flow.widens_iff_within_margin_at`, `Flow.Balances.loses_iff_beyond_margin`), the selection gap (`Flow.act_unselected`, `Flow.unselected_beyond_coupling`), pools (`Flow.unselected_beyond_pool`, `Web.pool_adds`), late reading (`Regulation.late_band`, `no_narrower_band`, `lateReturn_band`, `relay_threshold`), joint excess (`Flow.joint_excess_ends`) and the unbounded break (`Web.break_not_bounded`).

**As stated, the "if" direction is false.** A selection that admits every policy is a selection through every coupling (a constant predicate is invariant under any agreement), so (i) and (ii) can both hold while an admitted policy draws a coupled condition below its need. Coupling is what a selection *can* exclude by, not what it does exclude. The correct form is existential: *there is* a selection through closure `K`, deciding at each step from what `K` held `ℓ` steps earlier and acting only by withdrawing admission, under which no admitted policy drives a condition below its need, and which admits a given set `A` of policies, iff (a) no two policies agree on `K` up to the decision point while one of them keeps every condition at or above need and the other does not (for a condition outside `K` this says no policy in `A` draws it beyond its margin at all: `act_unselected`, `unselected_beyond_coupling`), and (b) at the last step where the selection can still withdraw a policy drawing a coupled condition, that condition stands at least `(ℓ + 1) d` above its need (`no_narrower_band` gives necessity, `lateReturn_band` attainment; the band law is in this review's [last table row](#ranked-table) as known).

**The reduction that decides the line** (a hand argument, stated so the rows below can be checked against it; not a claimed result). Take a plant of strings, a specification `K`, a supervisor that acts only by disabling and decides on what it observes, and an observation that may depend on events the supervisor does not see and on the states of readers that are themselves parts of the plant (a reader fed by an event dies when that event stops; a reader is replaced by one the selected behaviour favours).

1. *Alteration a known function of the closed-loop history.* The readers' states are part of an augmented plant, so the observation is one fixed causal map `O` on the augmented plant's strings, a state-dependent mask. The classical proof goes through unchanged with the indistinguishability relation `O(w) = O(w̄)` in place of the projection's: a supervisor achieving `K` exists iff `K` is controllable and no two strings `O` confuses are split by `K` at a controllable event. This is Wakaiki, Tabuada and Hespanha's Theorem 4.1 with a single relation, and the sensor-failure models where an event's observability depends on a sensor's state.
2. *Alteration depending on the supervisor's own decisions* (disabling an event punishes or starves a reader). The supervisor's past decisions are a function of what it observed, so they belong to its information state; the existence question is a game of imperfect information whose observation depends on the controller's moves, solved by the information-state constructions of property enforcement (Yin and Lafortune's bipartite transition system; Chatterjee, Doyen, Henzinger and Raskin's observation-based strategies). There is no closed-form observability condition, but the problem is decidable and its solution known. Keeping one's own readers is then the enforcement of an information property alongside `K`: enforcing detectability (Shu and Lin 2013), information-state-based properties (Yin and Lafortune 2016).
3. *Alteration law unknown to the designer.* The possible laws form a family of masks, the attack set of Wakaiki et al., with existence iff `K` is controllable and observable for every pair from the family. Where the family contains the constant mask (the readers can be wholly captured), only supervisors that need no observation remain.

1. **The two forms are separate arguments with exact premises.** The step across standpoints, from each standpoint's presupposition about its own continuation, forces significance for exactly the standpoints' centers and nothing more (`forced_iff_center`); it reaches "anything" only where every formation is a center (`unrestricted_atCenters_iff`) or lies within some center's closure along a relation (`unrestricted_of_standpointInvariant`). 2. **The contested step is a scope ambiguity.** The universalist and the critic agree on the presupposition and differ only in whether "however the self is drawn" takes narrow or wide scope (`presupposition_scopes`). What a standpoint's own presupposition forces, with nothing else, is exactly what lies within its closure (`forced_iff_within`), the least significance meeting it (`bounded_closure_least`). In the literature's terms the open question becomes whether the self's presupposition is essentially de se (Perry, Lewis): if it is, the narrow scope is the right reading and the step fails; if what is presupposed in acting is that continuation has significance, not continuation under a description of oneself, the wide scope is.

**The two readings, read exactly.** Write the stock's series as a process whose law depends on its renewal and the draw. The dependent's need is a threshold the stock's level crosses.

- *Matched in the stock's own course* (same renewal, same draw, the need placed so that it is crossed in one run and not in the other). Where the dependent takes nothing measurable from the stock and does not change its renewal, the need enters nothing the stock's law depends on. Every statistic of the stock's series then has the same distribution with and without the break, so no statistic separates them, at any sample size. This is `Web.break_not_bounded` read statistically, and a simulation of it can fail only through its code. Dekker et al. show the analogue numerically, with the leading system's indicators no different whether or not the follower tips. In the linear theory, the stock carries no part of the dependent's critical mode (the Jacobian is triangular, and the dependent's eigenvector lies along the dependent alone). This is the side the eigenvector literature states in general.
- *Matched against controls with no rising draw* (Drake and Griffen's and Dai et al.'s design). Near a quasi-steady level x*, the stock's fluctuations relax at its own return rate λ. Lag-1 autocorrelation is about exp(−λΔt), and with additive noise of intensity s the variance is about s²/(2λ). For logistic renewal under a proportional draw e, the steady level is x* = K(1 − e/r) and λ = r − e = r·x*/K. The return rate falls in proportion to the level, and autocorrelation rises steadily as the stock is drawn down toward its own end (a transcritical point). Under environmental noise (intensity proportional to the level) the variance s²K·x*/(2r) *falls* while the coefficient of variation rises, so the indicators disagree. A dependent whose need N lies on the way is crossed when λ = rN/K, after the stock's autocorrelation has risen against constant controls, though not because of the need. So T6's refutation clause, read against unmatched controls, is met by ordinary compensatory populations. Kéfi et al.'s transcritical and smooth cases show this, Drake and Griffen showed it experimentally for the stock's own end, and the field shows it for whales.

**Where "no fold of its own" exists.** It depends on the draw as well as on renewal. Under a standing (fixed) draw, every renewal that peaks (logistic, Ricker) has a fold of its own at the largest sustained draw: the classical harvest catastrophe (May 1977), and the regime's point of no return of `Becoming.RegimeMargin`. Under a proportional draw the same renewal has a transcritical end, and its rate of return falls with its level. Only renewal that rises as the level falls has neither under a standing draw: inflow-type renewal, such as recharge in proportion to a deficit, g(x) = a(K − x), whose rate of return is a whatever the draw. Only there does the first wording hold against unmatched controls. So whether a dependent's break is "warned of" against unmatched controls is decided by the stock's renewal and draw, not by the dependent. That is the thesis's own principle, applied to the stock: the warning reads what couples into the observed rate of return, the stock's own level, and never the need.

**Why no simulation.** The brief's design (matched pairs of stochastic stocks under a slowly rising draw, a depensation class with its own fold and a compensatory class crossing a dependent's need) would have had its verdict fixed before it ran, under either matching. Matched in course, every statistic has the same law with and without the break. Against unmatched controls, the verdict is set by the chosen renewal: logistic fails the first wording and inflow-type renewal passes it, both by the derivation above. A frozen test is worth running only where it can fail on something the tester did not choose. For the uncoupled side that is whether actual dependents leave their stock's dynamics unchanged as they near their needs, which only data on actual dependents can show. Building the simulation would have added a demonstration presented as a test.

**What T6 now says** (applied in the chapter, EN and ZH). A statistical warning reads the observed condition's own rate of return. That rate falls near a bifurcation of the condition's own renewal (a fold, or the transcritical point at which it ends), and with the level of a compensatory stock under a proportional draw. Before a dependent's break, the drawn stock's statistics rise exactly as much as before the same drawdown with no break. They read the decline and never where the need lies, so a warning of a dependent's break needs the dependent observed or its need measured. *Forbids:* given the stock's course, its statistics separating runs that cross a dependent's need from runs that do not, where the dependent takes no measurable share of the stock's renewal; and slowing down absent, under white noise, in a variable on which the critical mode loads, as its own fold or end is approached slowly (the linear theory, known). *Refuted by:* a dependent with negligible feedback whose approaching break shows in its stock's indicators beyond what the stock's own level and draw predict, against periods of the same stock at the same level with no break.

Against unmatched controls such statistics do precede the break, through the stock's own decline. The clause should read: *the stock's early-warning statistics, given its own course, separating runs that cross a dependent's need from runs that do not.* The proposed sentence itself ("The warning comes only to those who couple into what breaks") stands. It is a proposal for Ren, not applied, and this paragraph does not rewrite it.
