## 2. New checked identification result

`LexLe` gives protection priority, then compares secondary counts. `FiniteLe w` compares an explicitly supplied positive finite weight for protection plus the secondary count. Both are read on the same history description. This is a check of whether certain proposed evidence determines one comparison, not an empirical claim that both descriptions exhaust every constitutive relationship.

For any natural bound M, the two rules agree on **every pair** whose secondary counts are at most M if `w = M + 1`. Every finite list has such a maximum. They nevertheless disagree between `(true, 0)` and `(false, M + 2)`; the latter is outside the sample. No fixed finite weight agrees with the lexical order everywhere. Thus every finite comparison record compatible with the lexical rule is compatible with a globally different finite-weight rule in this family. The proof does not say all possible evidence is finite comparison data, deny structural a priori arguments, or establish the finite-weight rule's ethical correctness.

This advances the zero-axiom target by excluding a specific proposed removal of J2: corroborated examples plus positive coordinate sensitivity and transitivity do not identify its unrestricted priority. A position can have concerns it has never expressly adopted, and deliberation can develop or revise those concerns. The target must therefore be stronger than showing that a reason precedes an explicit choice of goal.

**Primary reading.** Read Williams, [“Internal and external reasons”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/moral-luck/internal-and-external-reasons/055B6EC455C5A0210BFC59BB7B593FE2), in *Moral Luck* (1981), printed pp.102–111, from the [scanned original](https://bibliotecamathom.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/williams_-_moral_luck.pdf). It is not restricted to selfish desires or explicit choices. His challenge asks how acquiring a purported external reason through appropriate deliberation differs from being persuaded into a new motivation. This corrects our opponent specification. We do not attribute to him a static reward maximizer, nor infer that every source of moral assessment has been refuted. The scan repeats these spreads later; those are duplicate pages, not independent evidence.

The stronger opponent now accepts one generative unfolding, rich formed cognition, nonselfish concerns and transformative learning. It nevertheless claims that every practical reason for a position must be reachable through appropriate deliberation from that position's motivational organization. Its premise is **deliberative dependence**, not just elective endorsement. Our matched case must specify that dependence as absent if it is meant to test the strongest version; absence of a helping project alone does not do so.

The distinction matters positively. Conversely, genuinely forming a relationship or commitment can create a new ground. A developmental account must discriminate these histories. The [elective-concern study](../history/root-foundation/ELECTIVE-CONCERN-ARGUMENT.md) already distinguishes these possibilities; its result is inherited, not another new victory over the stronger rival.

### 18.2 A positive answer: reasons also assess what a goal makes possible

The affirmative account can answer the motivational challenge without claiming that a bare proposition automatically produces action. Recognition of actual dependence, undergoing and possible continuation can reorganize attention, expectation and commitment in the same cognitive activity. The earlier formed-ground construction demonstrates a limited distinction between acquired evidence and its use in revision; it does not demonstrate the acquisition of ethical understanding. The new normative claim concerns why such a revision would be warranted.

Consider a proposed practice that its proponents understand and strongly desire. Its actual formation would obstruct existing living while enabling another development. Practical assessment asks about those realized relationships, including the proponents' own fulfillment. The positive candidate treats this content as bearing on whether to pursue the practice, not merely on which means best execute it. This preserves a reason for criticizing the end itself when its consequences remain accurately understood. It does not require the critic to stand outside generative reality: criticism is another relation within that developing history.

The explanatory advantage proposed is that **the content of what is realized can explain both why an end deserves adoption and why its execution deserves revision**. The same theory can distinguish discovering an earlier loss, genuinely creating a new relationship, and merely intensifying an unchanged preference. It does not need to assign the choosing disposition final authority over everything that disposition can affect. This is a substantive positive reason to investigate whole-quality grounding, rather than a proof from the opponent's failure.

The internalist can nevertheless account for much criticism by relating the practice to other, less salient concerns, fuller understanding or imaginative development. That explanation must receive full credit. A stronger theory of desire-dependent reasons also need not make their weight proportional to the strength of a desire. [Schroeder's own research account](https://markschroeder.net/reasons-and-explanation) explicitly describes that latter strategy in his 2007 work. The full linked articles were access-restricted in this round; this attribution is limited to his author-written description, not a purported full assessment of the papers.

The live discriminator is consequently **whether the actual quality can justify a response when no such deliberative route supplies the reason**. ### 18.3 Test the strongest instrumental route to universality

A promising alternative says: perhaps richer generative possibilities serve any goal, so every intelligence already has reason to preserve them whatever it values. This would connect Ren's positive direction to a very broad practical basis. It deserves an affirmative construction, followed by an exact scope test.

The relevant representation must preserve **what a response can ensure**, not only everything that could happen. In the new module, `r a o` says that outcome-history `o` is admitted after response `a` within the represented activity. The type of responses can differ between realizations. A history can retain burdens, affected lives and intermediate losses, not just a final reward. These are local roles within one activity; no independent agent/world substances are assumed. A real application must justify the response repertoire, residual possibilities, representation of histories and their completeness at the asserted scope.

`CanEnsure r goal` means that there is a response with at least one admitted outcome, all of whose admitted outcomes satisfy the goal. The nonemptiness condition is essential: a response that cannot occur is not a method for ensuring everything. This is a strong guarantee, not a definition of all rational action under uncertainty.

The exact result, `refinement_iff_all_goal_preservation`, is:

> Every outcome-goal that can be ensured before can be ensured afterward **exactly when** each live old response has a live new response whose admitted outcomes are contained in the old response's admitted outcomes.

The forward proof carries the old guarantee through the replacement. The converse uses each old response's own outcome set as a goal. Therefore it requires the full predicate language over the declared outcome domain; a restricted observation language need not identify this structural condition. No assumption about motives, ethical goodness or a numerical utility is used. The theorem quantifies over arbitrary response and outcome types, not a finite lookup table.

This provides a real positive bridge: **under the explicit additional practical principle that retaining effective means to a justified goal favors an otherwise acceptable change, the replacement condition gives an instrumental consideration for any such goal**. That practical principle, justification of the goal and absence of defeating costs are not outputs of Lean. The result concerns weak preservation of attainability. It is not strict benefit for every goal and not an argument that every goal is justified.
