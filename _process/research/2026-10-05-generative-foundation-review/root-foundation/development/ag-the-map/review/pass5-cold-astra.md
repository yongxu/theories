**Remaining findings, most consequential first**

**1. Persistence is being turned into a ranking without an argument that makes the turn.**

> “A life that goes on holds its own continuation: what does not hold it ceases.” — ONE-PAGE, ethics; similarly 07 §4 and USES.

If “holds” means maintains the conditions sufficient for continuing, the sentence is a persistence claim. If it means `IncreasingInOwn`, it requires a preference over every relevant pair of alternatives. The first does not entail the second. A system can be kept going by its environment without ranking alternatives at all; an agent can continue while being indifferent between two courses, one of which adds a future item marked as its own. Neither description contradicts its continuing. These are conceptual alternatives the proposed argument has not excluded, not claims of a new empirical test.

Moreover, maintaining one’s continuation does not entail preferring the continued existence of everything capable of affecting it. Influence can be hostile. The Lean correctly leaves the marking and monotonicity as hypotheses; the prose should not use the ordinary sense of survival to make those hypotheses sound inevitable.

**Fix:** “A life continues through conditions that sustain it. The ethics attends to those conditions and to what its continuation changes for others. The theorem below studies an order that favours every widening adding to what is marked as its own; we call this self-concern. Whether and how living systems form such an order remains open.”

This also calls for replacing 07’s heading “How scope and order emerge together from continuation” with **“Where self-concern and widening coincide.”** The existing section proves that coincidence under assumptions; it does not model the emergence named by the heading.

**3. “On listed courses” hides a restriction that matters especially to an ethics of continuing life.**

The list contains admitted **step–formation pairs**, not alternative paths. The theorem assumes the list covers everything admitted by both paths over all natural-number steps. **Fix:** “If every listed item is marked as its own, an order that favours widenings adding an own item favours every widening. **Where I think message 9 itself has a hole**

Ren writes: “there is no warrant, ethics emerges from continuation condition, not another way around, there is no any dualistic good or evil.” I agree with rejecting an external tribunal and with saying the work’s purpose positively. I also agree that naming the proof’s hypotheses should not turn the ethics into something waiting for permission to exist.

But **“emerges” needs to identify a claim**. If it means that caring, valuing and moral distinctions arise within living relations, it is a substantive explanatory direction; the current work has not yet shown how. If it means that this particular ethical order follows logically from the continuation condition, the current definitions and proofs do not establish it. The condition gives significance without positive valence and ranks nothing. Replacing C with self-concern in the special case relocates the order requirement; it does not derive ordering from significance alone. If it means “this is the ethics we form and offer from attending to continuation,” that can be said plainly now, without pretending the other two tasks are finished.

Likewise, **“no warrant” is too broad if it excludes reasons within becoming**. People can give reasons, contest them, discover that they ignore a dependence, and revise a course. None of that places reasons outside the map. A claimed universal reason would need its own argument; dismissing it solely because universality sounds external would repeat the very mistake of placing a distinction above the relations being examined.

Finally, **“no dualistic good or evil” should not become “no consequential ethical disagreement.”** A captor and someone bearing its losses can see the same facts and still rank them differently. Calling the disagreement insufficient reach does not explain it. The map becomes more useful for alignment when it can represent that disagreement, the conditions forming both rankings, and the effects of changing them, without declaring its own ranking the inevitable verdict of complete seeing.

I would say the intended ethics this way: **“We offer this ethics for life, humans and AI within it. It attends to what each continuation draws on, what its course makes possible or ends, and who bears the loss, including those whose loss never returns to the actor. Right and wrong form within these relations; no authority outside becoming is sought. The proofs show how specified scopes and orders compare courses. How those scopes and orders themselves form, persist and change is part of the map still to be drawn.”**
