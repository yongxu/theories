**2. Closing relations can support an alignment design, but does not identify its ethical success.**

> “The first of those conditions … and the pooled couplings … can be read as ways of making a loss return to the selector: they couple the loss into what a selection reads, and where the selector is also affected, at some remove, by what it drains, relations close and its own continuation reaches what it drains.” — 07, “Within the whole map.”

My argument against treating closure as the design objective is practical as well as formal. **Where I disagree with, or see a hole in, messages 9–12**

I read message 9 through messages 10–12 and the final note, rather than treating its first formulation as unchanged. Ren accepted that going on is not yet ordering, that own closure is a reading, that the ranking has a finite scope, and that losses beyond return are offered for attention. Those concessions make the present direction coherent.

**“There is no warrant” is sound as a rejection of an outside tribunal, not as a rejection of reasons within becoming.** Someone bearing a loss can still ask why the map omitted it, why a comparison counts an addition positively, or why a chosen boundary is appropriate. The fact that their question and our answer form within becoming makes them part of the inquiry. It does not answer them. The revised §1 recognizes this; the opening should not undo it.

**Withdrawing C as a second foundation does not remove the work of explaining order.** Self-concern already requires a positive response to certain widenings. When its marking becomes total, those are all widenings on the listing. That is a useful exact relationship, but it supplies neither how a life comes to rank that way nor why it should preserve an admitted possibility that makes its realized life worse. The menu and imposed-admission witnesses are therefore central ethical questions, not leftovers from the withdrawn foundation.

**The closed and one-way cases are not two exhaustive territories, one producing ethics and the other needing alignment.** Real systems can have partial, delayed and uneven return, contested boundaries, substitutable dependencies and losses that matter to a participant without threatening its continuation. The new temporal witnesses improve the map here. An alignment design needs to say which differences reach which decisions, with what response and what opportunity for correction; making more relations close cannot substitute for that account.

Finally, **attention still needs a route into decisions when losses conflict.** The work properly refuses to pretend widening settles the fishers’ livelihood against the bed, or makes a menu’s equilibrium benefits irrelevant. J1/J2 already supply some further comparisons. Developing, contesting and testing such commitments is work within becoming; it need not reinstate an external foundation. The strongest next connection would show how affected parties’ losses can alter a real joint selection—including its rule—while retaining disagreement, effective challenge and the ability to revise the map itself.
