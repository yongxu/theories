## Where messages 9 to 12, or the reading this pass added to 07, have a hole

1. **The reading "alignment as closing relations" conflates a return in reading with a return in dependence.** It opens: condition 1 and 05's pooled couplings "can be read as ways of making a loss return to the selector: they couple the loss into what a selection reads, and where the selector is also affected, at some remove, by what it drains, relations close and its own continuation reaches what it drains." What condition 1 and a pool engineer is a *reading* fact: the difference couples into what the selection reads (03's couplings, `Flow.act_unselected_pool`). What the ethics' hinge takes is a *dependence* fact: `p`'s closure under `AffectedBy` is the whole, and `AffectedBy g h` is `h` exchanging with a condition `g` *depends on*. A pool that reads a loss makes the selector see it; it does not make the selector depend on what is lost. The reading's own last sentence says so ("Coupling a loss in is not depending on it, and is not shown to make a selection respond to it"), but the "where" clause in its first sentence carries the whole weight and nothing in the two conditions supplies it; as written, a reader may believe pooled coupling closes relations in the sense the ethics needs. The lake already shows the two: the fish are mutual (the fishers depend on them and affect them), so a self-concerned order keeps the fish's renewal with no step, and the largest steady catch does stay within it (T8's own note); the bed gives nothing, so it is read under the scope instruction and never depended on, and only the step counts it. Fix, for 07's paragraph and AJ §1: lead with the distinction. "Alignment's first condition and 05's pools make a loss *read*; the ethics' hinge needs it *borne*: the selector depending, at some remove, on what it drains, as the fishers depend on the fish and not on the bed. Where a selector both reads and depends on what it drains, self-concern already ranks every widening above (`increasingInOwn_iff_increasing_of_unrestricted`, which uses the condition at the selector's standpoint only); where it reads and does not depend, seeing is needed and not enough, and counting is the step offered; where it neither reads nor depends, nothing in it can turn." That also gives "seeing is needed and not enough" its exact place: it is the boundary between the two halves, not a remark beside them.
2. **The reading says "relations close" for a one-center fact.** The theorem uses `unrestricted` only at `p` (its docstring), so what alignment would need to engineer is not mutual dependence among all formations but that everything the selection drains comes, at some remove, to affect something the selector depends on: a return path for each drain, the selector's own upstream closure being the whole. That is weaker and more designable than "relations close", and it is what the reading should name; "relations close" is the fronts' global condition, borrowed for a local claim. Fix: "where everything the selector drains comes to affect, at some remove, what it depends on (the theorem takes the condition at the selector's standpoint only)".
3. **Message 12's placement of alignment.** Message 12 accepted "the ethics emerges on the half where relations close, while alignment works on the half where they run one way"; the new reading puts alignment on the closing. Both are right at different relations: alignment closes relations of reading, the ethics' equivalence needs relations of dependence to close, and the step covers what cannot close in dependence. The fronts say "along that relation" each time they say "reach", which is right; the reading drops the relation when it says "close". Fix: name the relation at every "close" in the reading, as in 1.
4. **The ethics still has no bearer in the account, and the reading makes that sharper.** It says alignment engineers the condition; nothing engineers the order. A rule, a reward or a market is not shown to hold any order over courses, and "its own continuation" is a reading for it; a joint selection of people and AI has no identified center. So "a self-concerned order already ranks" has a hypothesis on both sides, as every front now says ("not shown ... a filter"). What is new is that a design can aim at the condition and has nothing to aim at for the order. 08 §12 item 12 names it; the reading should say in one clause that the order side is open, so the two halves are not read as one engineered and one offered.
5. **"Emerges" is still an equivalence under a hypothesis**, as in my seventh pass, and the fronts now show the hinge instead of the slogan alone, which is the right answer to that hole: a newcomer can read the definition, follow "there", and check it. I would not change the word.
