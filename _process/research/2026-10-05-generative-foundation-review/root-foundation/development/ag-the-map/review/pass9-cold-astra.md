**Where I see a hole in Ren’s formulations or the new alignment reading**

I accept the direction of messages 9–12 as recorded, including the qualifications Ren accepted. I still disagree with a literal reading of “there is no warrant” if it means there can be no demand for reasons. There need be no tribunal outside becoming, but a person bearing a loss can ask why that loss is omitted, why an addition counts positively, or why an imposed continuation is being treated as a benefit. Those questions and their answers are themselves within becoming. The current §1 allows this; it should continue to do so.

Withdrawing C as a second foundation removes a misleading architecture, but it does not discharge the ranking commitment. `IncreasingInOwn` already requires a positive comparison for marked additions. When every item is marked, it becomes the requirement to rank every widening higher. That is a precise connection between scope and order; its ethical interest depends on why this marking and this response to additions arise or are worth adopting. The retained refusal and menu witnesses matter here: admission may be imposed, and wider choices may worsen what participants obtain. An answer can be developed within becoming without restoring an outside foundation.

The new reading in 07 answers my eighth-pass objection about domination. It explicitly distinguishes information from dependence, names mutual drain and makes closure a mechanism to examine rather than a criterion of alignment. I would treat that as a repair, not keep reporting the same omission. The remaining hole is operational: which loss difference reaches which choice before which deadline, how it changes the comparison, and whether the affected party can contest or leave the arrangement. Aggregate reach, and even eventual temporal reach, can arrive after a dependent has ended; neither records the response’s size. The text now acknowledges this, so it is a design task rather than a false claim.

The stated aim still deserves Ren’s decision:

> “does not come to select for control over its own selection” — AGENTS and the restored fronts.

Rewarding the capacity to revise a selection rule can support accountable correction. It becomes capture when that control disables effective correction or confines the rule to the controllers’ interests; the mere presence of selected control does not make that distinction. Conversely, a rule may read outside its controllers’ share and still ignore what it reads. AJ’s stated conditions therefore neither automatically satisfy the literal no-control aim nor, without a response mechanism, guarantee the intended protection.
