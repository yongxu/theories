# The failure modes of alignment, read as cases of one law

*AI draft for alignment readers, 30 and 31 October 2026. Not reviewed by a person, and not for release ([§8](#8-before-this-becomes-a-release)). The first section takes eleven to fifteen minutes; [how this map was made](#how-this-map-was-made), its sources and its checks, is at the end. Lean names omit the `Becoming.` prefix.*

## The map in ten minutes

**The law.** A selection, whether a reward, a judge, a market or an institution, reaches only what couples into it. Two histories alike in what its couplings carry are selected alike. With the rest of the exchange fixed and balance on what couples in, a part lying wholly beyond the coupling closure is selected alike with and without it. What the law does not exclude is a flow that raises what couples in by drawing from beyond: that flow is selected ([§1](#1-what-this-map-is)).

**Three sources.** The closure law is proved on the theory's root. Amounts (the drain, races, horizons, renewal) are attached to the root as a second sort, not derived from it. Two premises with two roles are named and asserted by no law: the continuation condition fixes scope, whose losses count, and C fixes order, which course is better. The condition by itself ranks nothing; every ranking needs C. Each row rests on one or more of them, and the table says which.

## 1. What this map is

Alignment research names many failures, and each usually has its own literature: reward hacking, side effects, sycophancy, evaluator capture, metrics kept after they stop measuring, oversight that comes too late, concentration of power, races among developers, instrumental convergence, goal misgeneralization, deceptive alignment, the underdetermination of values by behaviour, and constitutions that cannot be revised. This map reads each as a case of one law of the theory, or says where it is not one.

The law is the root's closure law, read for selections. What a relation carries reaches exactly its closure, and nothing within a closure bears the relation to what lies beyond it (the master law *closure*, [01 §7](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/01-ROOT.md#r-master-laws-on-the-grammar)). Read for a selection, whether a reward, a judge, a market or an institution: **a selection reaches only what couples into it.** Two histories alike in what its couplings carry are selected alike (`Web.selects_alike_iff`, near-definitional: the law states what the definition builds in, and it reaches no grammar law). With the rest of the exchange fixed and balance on what couples in, a part lying wholly beyond the coupling closure is selected alike with and without it (`Flow.unselected_beyond_coupling`, which reaches the grammar through closure and balance). What the law does not exclude is a flow that raises what couples in by drawing from beyond: that flow is selected (`Diversion.favours`) ([selection through couplings](../../../../../../theories/becoming/results/records/selection-through-couplings.md)).

The account built on that law has three sources, and every row below names which it uses:

| Source | What it carries | How it stands to the root |
|---|---|---|
| The closure law | what a selection reaches, capture, timing, pooling | proved on the root; several of these laws reach the grammar through closure together with balance or lumping, and the openness and choice laws (rows 10, 14) reach grammar theorems under no master law's anchor |
| Amounts | the drain, races, horizons, renewal | as of 30 October, attached to the root as a second sort, not derived from it ([U1's verdict](../ou3b-master-laws-on-the-grammar/WHAT-REMAINS.md#u1s-verdict-gws-and-u3s-gate-29-october-2026)); the round trip of U4's lever 1, reviewed on 30 October, fails its second test, so U1's verdict stands: balance is represented both ways as a one-to-one census, not derived (§7) |
| Two premises | whose losses count (the continuation condition); which course is better (C) | named, asserted by no law; no statement reading only the dynamics entails the condition ([what the dynamics leave open](../../../../../../theories/becoming/results/records/what-the-dynamics-leave-open.md)) |

So the account is one root with amounts and two premises attached to it, not "everything from one root". For the continuation condition the third source is fixed by a theorem: where there is a standpoint and a formation, no statement that reads only the dynamics entails it (`ReadsOnlyDynamics.not_entails`, `ReadsOnlyDynamics.admits_rival`). The record's known form is Hume's is–ought gap, stated for the represented dynamics. The chapter limits it: the result shows non-entailment by the represented dynamics, not that becoming leaves significance undetermined, and on the root the non-entailment holds by construction of the significance parameter. C, the premise that orders, is named apart and is not the subject of this theorem. [this map, AI] A theory of alignment that hides its value premise in a reward or a utility still has one; it only stops saying where the premise enters.

Where it turns on what is better, it rests on premises the root does not supply.

- **Hypotheses**: what the law needs, as its record states them;
- **Standing**: theorem (with its model as the chapter labels it: any type, World, counts, root), reading [AI reading, Dn], or assessment, and its laws on the root;
- **Known form**: the prior result it restates, as the record or chapter credits it; a credit that is the dossier's or this map's own assessment is marked so;

## 3. The rows

### Row 1. Reward misspecification, proxy gaming, side effects

- **Case of.** Blindness: two histories alike in what a selection's couplings carry are selected alike (`Web.selects_alike_iff`). With a part withheld, the rest of the exchange fixed, balance at each step on what couples into members of the closure, and every member of the part beyond that closure, every selection through the couplings of a formation within the closure treats the levels with and without the part alike (`Flow.unselected_beyond_coupling`). The drain: take any rising order on what a selection reads, and suppose room holds among the eligible outcomes. Then every optimum leaves each unread condition at its least level among them (`Optimum.least_of_room`). On a web where each unread condition is depended on at a need met before the step, every optimum breaks exactly the dependences that some eligible outcome breaks (`Web.optimum_broken_iff`).
- **Hypotheses.** Blindness needs the part to lie beyond the closure and the rest of the exchange fixed: a flow that raises what couples in by drawing from beyond is selected (`Diversion.favours`). The drain needs a rising order (one ranking every outcome higher on what is read above it, whatever either leaves elsewhere; a lexicographic order counting the beyond second still rises) and room (every eligible outcome leaving more of an unread condition is beaten, on what is read, by some eligible outcome). Room is a hypothesis, derived for one step where a part's members take at most their capacity and give no more than they take, the rest exchanging as it does (`Optimum.Above.room`; one step, decidable equality, listings without repetition, a non-empty family set apart from the beyond, floors). Without room, keeping can be optimal (`Optimum.Drain.room_needed`).
- **Standing.** Theorems. Blindness, World: `selects_alike_iff` is near-definitional and reaches no grammar law; the blindness laws reach the grammar through closure and balance (5 of 12 laws of the record on the root). The drain, any set of outcomes, with the breaks on the World: `least_of_room` and `optimum_broken_iff` rest on no master law, and room's derivation on balance, represented (0 of 8 laws on the root).
- **Known form.** Chapters 03 and 08 credit the proxy problem (Goodhart 1975; Amodei and others 2016; Manheim and Garrabrant 2018), Zhuang and Hadfield-Menell (2020), who prove a stronger, quantitative form, supervisory control (Ramadge and Wonham 1987) and, under partial observation, Lin and Wonham (1988) and Cieslak and others (1988). The record calls the main selection law near-definitional.
- **What the join adds.** One law for reward hacking and side effects. Room is sufficient for every optimum of a rising order to drain what it omits, and, over every rising order together, the drain is exact up to double negation (`Optimum.least_iff`); the dependences broken are read off the eligible outcomes. Zhuang and Hadfield-Menell's continuous setting, their compactness characterization and their proxy updated over time are not reached. A set point escapes the drive at the set point but not the drain below it ([what a set-point optimum does beyond](../../../../../../theories/becoming/results/records/what-a-set-point-optimum-does-beyond.md)).
- **What you must check.** What actually couples into the reward (the work lies there, by the record's own boundary); whether room holds for this reward and environment, or only for one step with the rest fixed; whether the omitted condition shares units with what is rewarded (thesis T3's clause on trained systems).

### Row 2. Oversight methods reaching no further than their deciders

- **Case of.** A pool reads what any member reads, and, with the rest of the exchange fixed and balance on what couples in, its selection is blind beyond the union of its members' closures (`Flow.unselected_beyond_pool`). A formation coupled by level into everything another couples into selects through at least their pooled coupling, and its closure is the union of the two where the one read exchanges with what it couples into (`Flow.withinCoupling_reads_iff`).
- **Hypotheses.** Those of row 1's blindness. That each approach decides through couplings is supplied: every statement that an approach is such a selection is the AI's reading per approach, as the record's conditions say.
- **Standing.** Assessment of six approaches (RLHF and reward modelling, constitutional AI, debate and scalable oversight, interpretability, guaranteed-safe AI, impact measures) on theorems reaching the grammar through closure and balance (3 of 4 and 3 of 6 laws of the two records). [07 §6](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/07-SIGNIFICANCE.md#r-alignment-approaches-under-the-selection-laws).
- **Known form.** Row 1's. Three published measurements are cited, not predicted in magnitude, by the record [coupling closures of training pipelines](../../../../../../theories/becoming/results/records/coupling-closures-of-training-pipelines.md) (Gao, Schulman and Hilton 2023; Wen and others 2024; Sharma and others 2023).
- **What the join adds.** A test any proposed oversight method can be put to: whose closures does its decision pass through, and what lies beyond their union? Reading a system's internals reaches no further than pooling with it.
- **What you must check.** Who the deciders actually are, and what couples into each, including held-out evaluations and interpretability tools, before claiming a method reaches a given failure.

### Row 3. Evaluator capture, sycophancy, self-rewarding loops

- **Case of.** A rule read from what it selects locks. Where selection does not lower the carriers' share under a favouring rule, the rule read at each reading step is a monotone function of the share, and the starting rule and every rule read favour the carriers, the share falls at no step and the course is locked at the step after every reading step (`RuleCapture.favouring_locks`). Where every rule read favours, any start locks by the step after its second reading step (`RuleCapture.uniform_locks`).
- **Hypotheses.** That the winners of the selection write its rule is the premise (`ReadsShare`); the lock is what is derived. Where the reading can favour the others, a witness started against its reading never locks (`RuleCapture.Alternating.never_locked`).
- **Standing.** Theorem (any type), on the root (4 of 7 laws). [02 §9](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/02-MAP.md#r-capture-through-the-rule).
- **Known form.** The lock-in of increasing returns (Arthur 1989); regulatory capture (Stigler 1971). Chapter 8 reads capture through the rule as observability with the reader inside the plant, the corrupted reward channel and closed-loop identifiability (Wakaiki, Tabuada and Hespanha 2019; Everitt and others 2017; Borkar and Varaiya 1979).
- **What the join adds.** The lock belongs to how the rule reads, not to the share it favours. A change of the rule that keeps its direction is absorbed at the next reading step (`RuleCapture.perturbation_absorbed`). Where the reading favours at every share, only a change of how the rule reads or of the selection leaves (`RuleCapture.uniform_locks`). In a witness under a threshold reading, a reversal held until the share crosses the threshold is not absorbed (`RuleCapture.Reversed.not_absorbed`).
- **What you must check.** Whether the actual rule (a benchmark, a reward model trained on model outputs, a judge reading earlier picks) is read monotonically from the share it selects, and whether every rule it reads favours. LA2 found the one trained judge tested not to read as a pool counted by kind (§5).

### Row 4. A metric kept after it stops measuring

- **Case of.** The lock outlives a loss it does not read. Add a quantity beyond the share that grows by at least one each step and that neither the next share nor the rule read reads. From a locked configuration the loss passes every bound while the course stays locked (`ClosedCapture.lock_outlives_loss`). Where the reading is formed monotonically from the share and the rule is read from the reading and the share, then from a locked configuration a change of the reading of any size, the rule still favouring, is absorbed: the course is locked again at the step after every reading step (`ClosedCapture.reading_absorbed`). A reading that favours at no loss above a bound unlocks the course after the next reading step past it (`ClosedCapture.turned_by_loss`), which the chapter calls a sufficient way out.
- **Hypotheses.** Those of row 3, with a locked start and the loss unread by the loop.
- **Standing.** Theorem (any type), on the root (6 of 7 laws; two of them reach it through lumping). It is the law under thesis T7, whose clause that actual metrics are so formed is conjectured.
- **Known form.** The record names lock-in under increasing returns (Arthur 1989) and regulatory capture (Stigler 1971) as the closest prior results, and cites the corrupted reward channel (Everitt and others 2017). [the dossier's assessment] Goodhart's and Campbell's laws describe it informally (Manheim and Garrabrant 2018; Campbell 1979).
- **What the join adds.** The meta-attractor's formal core: a loop whose rule and whose reading of the rule are both formed from the share it selects, with nothing in it reading what lies beyond the share ([02 §9](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/02-MAP.md#r-closed-capture)). A reading of the loss is a sufficient way out; row 3's reversal under a threshold reading is another.
- **What you must check.** Whether the metric's keepers were selected by the metric, and whether anything in the loop reads the loss. That what lies beyond the share is a loss to anyone is a significance's to say, not this law's.

### Row 5. Oversight that comes too late

- **Case of.** Let the rule be the pooled reading of readers drawn from the population it selects, counted as the population counts them; then the rule reads the share exactly where the others' reading is not above the carriers' (`ClosedCapture.Drawn.pool_reads_iff`). Joined to the selection for control (row 6): with the others reading a loss from reading step N, the course is locked in the shapers' favour for good exactly where the late reading favours them at the share of step N (`ReadInTime.kept_early_iff`), and, with step N + 1 read too, turned for good from N + 2 exactly where the late reading favours the others, ties included, at the share of step N + 1 (`ReadInTime.turned_early_iff`). Once the late reading would favour the shapers, every later reading keeps the lock (`ReadInTime.kept_once_favoured`); at most one step lies between (`ReadInTime.at_most_one_between`); with a shaper present and the late reading favouring the others strictly, a deadline bounds the window (`ReadInTime.deadline`).
- **Hypotheses.** Two kinds; N a reading step; readers counted by kind; a loss starting at zero and growing by one a step, read by the others only from N; `lo ≤ hi` and `lo' ≤ hi`; a start whose rule favours the shapers. No noise and no readers who learn.
- **Standing.** Theorems (counts), on the root (4 of 7 and 13 of 15 laws). [02 §9](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/02-MAP.md#r-loss-read-in-time-by-the-others).
- **Known form.** Collingridge's dilemma (1980): here its second horn is a theorem and its first a premise. The window's continuous analogue is Biggs, Carpenter and Brock (2009); the threshold is Schelling's and Granovetter's tipping point.
- **What the join adds.** [AI reading] Whether a reading is in time is decided by the share at the step read and the next; the loss only sets the step from which it is read. So the point of no return belongs to the share's course, not to the size of the loss. That a loss read in time leaves the lock where control is selected, above the critical mass, is shown in a witness (`ReadInTime.Window.in_time`). Of the four combinations of control selected or not with a lock left in time or not, three occur in these models; the fourth, control left unselected with a lock left in time, is not shown ([07 §6](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/07-SIGNIFICANCE.md#6-what-a-selection-reaches-alignment-with-no-premise)); read for the shapers' capture alone, the case not shown is control left unselected with that capture kept, so either way the two are not shown to be independent. These are facts about control selected or not, not about capture: what the rule reads is fixed by the readers' premise, so the models do not tell capture from accountable revision by what the shaping changes.
- **What you must check.** What sets the bound N in actual oversight; whether the readers are drawn from the selected and read by kind (LA2's judge did not: one note of the loss among nine readers turned it); whether the share at which the late reading would favour the shapers can be seen before it is passed.

### Row 6. Concentration of power: selection for control over the rules

- **Case of.** Model shaping in counts: each shaper leaves a + s + p descendants and each other member a + c + p, with s returned to the shapers alone, p to every member alike, and c the cost. At a step the rule favours shaping, weakly (the shapers' share does not fall), exactly where c ≤ s, and p decides nothing at a step (`RuleShaping.favours_iff`, `RuleShaping.favours_alike`). With nothing back to the shapers alone, a positive cost and some shapers present, their share falls at every step whatever is shared (`RuleShaping.nothing_back_falls`). Across a stretch, the shared part can turn the verdict, in a witness pair (`RuleShaping.SharedFlips.without_falls`, `RuleShaping.SharedFlips.with_rises`). Where the rule's readers are drawn from those it selects and the shapers among them read `hi` coming back and the others `lo`, with `lo ≤ hi`, the rule reads the share (`DrawnShaping.drawn_reads_iff`), a critical mass exists exactly where `lo < c < hi` (`DrawnShaping.critical_mass_iff`), and every course from a start under the rule read there is locked one way at every step (`DrawnShaping.drawn_locked_either`).
- **Hypotheses.** Offspring carry the capacity; what comes back is read from the share; two kinds; whole-number counts; no noise.
- **Standing.** Theorems (counts) (2 of 8 and 2 of 5 laws on the root). [02 §9](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/02-MAP.md#r-shaping-the-rule-selected-by-what-comes-back).
- **Known form.** `RuleShaping.favours_iff` is rent-seeking arithmetic (Tullock 1967; Krueger 1974) and regulatory capture (Stigler 1971); Olson (1965) on shared benefits; Price (1970) keeps the accounts. Where `lo < c < hi` the model is a stag hunt, its critical mass the mixed equilibrium. Under rare mutation the long-run state is the risk-dominant one (Kandori, Mailath and Rob 1993; Young 1993), here the shapers' where `2c < hi + lo`.
- **What the join adds.** The selection for control and the lock are one loop on either side of a critical mass, set by who reads and the cost.
- **What you must check.** Whether s is measurable in the setting (a lab shaping its benchmarks, a platform its ranking rule, a model its evaluator); who reads, and whether the shapers among the readers read more coming back to the shapers. ### Row 7. Intent alignment is not enough

- **Case of.** Two readings [AI reading, D68]. *Obedience adds capacity, not coupling*: read an assistant and its principal as two optimizers at a mutual best response, each order rising with the family the principal's signal rises with; then, at a condition beyond that family, the assistant adds capacity and nothing its order puts before that family, and what lies there falls as it would under one optimizer of their joint capacity (on `Optimum.mutual_as_one`, row 8). *Assistance does not lower the selection for control*: help that lowers the cost of shaping, with nothing else changed, gives a rule not lower in the share order (`RuleShaping.lower_c_raises`), a strictly higher one where it lowers the cost strictly (`RuleShaping.lower_c_raises_strictly`), and where it brings the cost within what comes back to the shapers alone it turns the rule at a step toward them (`RuleShaping.favours_iff`).
- **Hypotheses.** The first assumes that the assistant's order rises with the principal's family: the reading's premise, not the law's. An assistant ranking effects beyond the principal's signal only after it still rises with it and stays within the law (a lexicographic order still rises, row 1's hypotheses); one that would give up something the signal ranks for those effects is outside it. A warning to the principal is not an act of the model, and whether it changes the principal's order is not modelled. The second holds with nothing else changed; help that also raises the shared part pulls the rule toward even at a step.
- **Standing.** Readings on theorems (amounts; counts). [07 §6](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/07-SIGNIFICANCE.md#6-what-a-selection-reaches-alignment-with-no-premise): "Alignment to a principal alone is therefore not alignment of the joint selection."
- **Known form.** The worry that intent-aligned AI concentrates power; the law under the second reading is monotonicity in one parameter.
- **What the join adds.** These are the map's two sharpest corollaries for alignment: the unit to align is the joint selection of humans, institutions and systems, not one system and its principal, and alignment to a principal alone does not lower the selection for control of row 6, and raises it where the cost of shaping falls strictly.
- **What you must check.** Whether capable assistance lowers the cost of shaping the rules that select its users (lobbying, producing evaluations, designing benchmarks), rather than the cost of acting under them; which assistant behaviours add couplings rather than capacity, and whether they can be trained.

### Row 11. Deceptive alignment

- **Case of.** Where a formation's lumping tells an unread occasion from every read one, being in its lump decided, every act law reading through the lumping that a selection through agreement on the readings selects has, for every value, a selected twin agreeing on every reading and taking that value at the unread occasion (`Learning.twin_selected`).
