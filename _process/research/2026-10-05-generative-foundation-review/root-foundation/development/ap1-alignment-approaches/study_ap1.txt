The question this answers is whether the theory can be claimed as the best coherent system for alignment, or as the necessary alignment strategy. It cannot. The direction of chapter §7.5 is conditional: the continuation condition fixes scope and C, read as widening, fixes order, and the warrant of both is open (§8.1 item 12). Coupling is not compliance (§7.7): which law a trained system follows is open. CG1 measured widening coupling as necessary for a consequence to be selected and not sufficient for it to be kept (§240). No comparison with another framework has been run, and nothing here has been independently reviewed. What can be claimed is narrower and holds with no ethical premise: the laws of §7.1–§7.4 bound every strategy that decides through couplings. They are necessary constraints on any strategy, not a necessary strategy. This section makes that claim approach by approach, which is also the only route to any comparative claim. Every statement that an approach is such a selection, and every reading of its gap, is the AI's [AI reading]; the laws cited are exact, with the hypotheses their theorems name.

**The strongest sentence the work supports.** With no ethical premise, every alignment approach decides through the couplings of its deciding formations and, under balance and with the rest following a law that reads through coupling, selects alike everything beyond the union of their coupling closures, whatever they value; reading the system's own couplings moves that boundary no further than pooling with it. Under the continuation condition, which gives significance to every formation's continuation, losses that return to no one included, an approach whose deciders couple only into preferences (raters', judges', rule writers') is therefore incomplete whatever they value: a loss falling beyond every decider's closure counts and is never separated. Only coupling into that loss, by measurement, by a model answerable to what couples in, or by joining a formation the act reaches, brings it within reach, and within reach it is separated only where some member couples it apart and kept only where the law followed responds (necessary, not sufficient). The order the theory adds asks nothing that an order counting every formation can reject: widening is exactly what every order increasing in admission shares, additive or not, the unanimity (Pareto) core over continuations, and it leaves every mixed comparison undecided. The first clause holds of every approach with no premise; the second is the continuation condition's, and for a scope narrower than it, the deciders' own closures, it fails; the third is C reduced to monotonicity at each standpoint. None of it ranks approaches or names a strategy.

**What every approach is subject to.** An approach decides something about a system: which outputs are reinforced, which are filtered or blocked, whether it is deployed, which answer a judge accepts. Each decision is made by formations reading what couples into them: labellers, a reward model, a feedback model, a judge, an overseer, a verifier, a penalty computed from a model. Read as a selection through their couplings (`Web.SelectsThrough`, `Web.SelectsThroughAll`), every approach is subject to four laws, whatever it values:

- **L1, reach.** It separates exactly what couples into its deciding formations (`Web.selects_alike_iff`, `Web.selects_alike_all_iff`). Deciding harder through the same couplings separates nothing more.
- **L2, the gap.** What lies beyond the union of the coupling closures of the formations it joins is selected alike. This holds with the rest of the exchange fixed (`Flow.unselected_beyond_pool`), and, for the system's own act, with the rest following a law reading through coupling (`Flow.act_unselected_pool`, `Flow.consequence_unselected_pool`).
- **L3, within.** Inside that union a difference is separated only along a route of readings from where it lies (`apart_only_downstream`), and only where some member couples it apart (`Web.separates_all_iff`; with the members listed and decided, `Web.separates_all_iff_member`). Lying within the union does not make a difference separated (`Clearing.within_closure_unseparated`).
- **L4, response.** Coupling bounds what an act responds to and does not fix it (`ExchangeLaw.response_not_fixed_by_coupling`). Which law the trained system, or any member, follows is not given by L1–L3.

Where the decision is made by a pool's acts under a law reading through each member's coupling (a reward, a vote, a revision), its going through the pooled coupling is derived (`ExchangeLaw.Through.selectsThroughAll`). Everywhere else it is the premise the approach supplies, and it is where the account could fail: an approach whose decisions read something beyond its formations' couplings would not be such a selection, and L1–L3 would not apply to it.

**What each selection couples into** [AI reading]. RLHF selects through raters' judgments of outputs, the prompt and the response as the raters read them, and through a reward model fitted to those judgments, which couples into the transcript. Constitutional AI selects through a model's reading of a written rule set and the transcript. Debate selects through judges' pooled reading of transcripts. Interpretability-based oversight selects through the system's internal states, conditions the system couples into, not the world consequences of its acts. Guaranteed-safe AI selects through a world model and a specification, which the verifier reads. Impact measures select through the agent's own model of what is reachable or attainable, against an inaction baseline. In the first four the deciders read the transcript or the system itself. Beyond the system's closure they add only what they couple into by their own situations, such as a rater's lived knowledge of what an answer would do, and reading the system adds nothing beyond it (`Flow.withinCoupling_reads_iff`). Where every member's verdict reads one channel, the transcript, whatever one member couples alike every member does, and the pooled coupling is that member's (`Web.pooled_iff_member`): more raters or judges reading the same channel add agreement, not reach. A verifier relative to a model can exclude what holds (`Stationary.model_error`). A penalty computed from the agent's own attainable set reads no other formation's margin (`Flow.Balances.widens_yet_own_changed`).

**RLHF and reward modelling** (Christiano, Leike, Brown, Martic, Legg and Amodei 2017; Ouyang et al. 2022). *Selection.* The policy update reads a reward that the reward model computes from what couples into it, the prompt and the response. The premise is that the reward is a score through the reward model's couplings (`Web.ScoresThrough`). Every choice made from that score, a threshold or a ranking, is then a selection through the same couplings (`Web.ScoresThrough.atLeast`). The reward model is fitted to the labellers' comparisons. A loss on the data selects through the data (`Training.loss_through`), and the comparisons, a pool's acts under a law reading through each labeller's coupling, are a selection through the labellers' pooled coupling (`ExchangeLaw.Through.selectsThroughAll`). *Gap.* What the policy's outputs do beyond the union of the reward model's and the labellers' closures is selected alike (L2). The reward model is right beyond its data exactly where the class training can reach determines the value there (`Training.fit_right_iff`, `Training.unselected_beyond_data`). A policy selected through it is right about what matters only where what matters goes alike across the reward model's lumping, and what that lumping ties no selection through it separates (`Learning.selects_right_through`, `Learning.selects_not_right_of_parted`). Optimizing harder against the same reward model separates nothing more (L1); this is the structural side of what Gao, Schulman and Hilton (2023) measured as reward-model overoptimization, and the laws give no rate. In a balanced flow where nothing is gained, a selection holding only where what couples into the reward rises is met only where some formation takes from a condition no member couples into (`Flow.favours_reaching_beyond`), and a selection through the couplings cannot tell that draw from a supply that reaches nothing beyond (`Diversion.blind_to_source`). Reward hacking and sycophancy read as such rises: what couples into the reward, the labeller's approval included, raised by drawing on what does not [AI reading]. *Adds.* An installation mechanism: gradient updates change which law the policy follows, which §7.7 leaves open. It joins to training the labellers' couplings into lived situations, which the system lacks, and it has a record of measured behaviour change. The laws neither predict nor bound its success. Zhuang and Hadfield-Menell's bound on how far optimizing a proxy degrades the rest (2020) is stronger than anything here. Casper et al. (2023) catalogue the open problems of RLHF: those of the reward model's and the labellers' reach correspond to the gaps above, while evaluators' errors and the choice of feedback type are not stated by the laws.

**Constitutional AI** (Bai et al. 2022). *Selection.* A feedback model reads a response and a list of written principles, and gives the comparison or the critique the policy is trained on. Its verdict is a selection through its couplings and, within them, through its reading of the principles. Read as a map of the alternatives, each response's entry being what the principles distinguish as the model reads them, a selection through the map selects alike whatever the map ties (`Relabel.selected_alike_iff`). The premise is that the verdict depends on a history only through the transcript and the principles, and on these through that map. *Gap.* Everything RLHF's gap holds, with the feedback model as the selector. Where the feedback model's couplings lie within the policy's coupling closure (the same transcript, inputs of the same kind), pooling it with the policy changes nothing beyond that closure (`Flow.unselected_beyond_coupling_all`; §7.2 law 1). Feedback of this kind adds scale, not reach. What the principles' terms tie, no selection through them separates (`Learning.selects_not_right_of_parted`), so a written rule is right about what matters only where what matters goes alike across what it distinguishes (`Learning.selects_right_through`). At the level of the rule, correcting outputs alone, where the next configuration depends on the rule alone, is re-absorbed (`Law.perturbation_reabsorbed`). Where the principles or the feedback model are revised from what the trained system produces, a rule read monotonically from the share it selects locks at its first reading step, and only a change of how the rule reads leaves the lock (`favouring_locks`, `uniform_locks`; record capture-through-the-rule) [AI reading: whether a given practice revises its rule this way is empirical]. *Adds.* A rule that people can read, contest and revise, which is the model route of 明 that §7.3 keeps open, and feedback at a scale no group of labellers gives. The laws supply neither the rule's content nor the feedback model's reading of it as intended.

**Debate and scalable oversight** (Irving, Christiano and Amodei 2018; Leike et al. 2018 on recursive reward modelling; Bowman et al. 2022 on measuring scalable oversight). *Selection.* A judge's verdict, given after reading what the debaters write, is the judge's selection through couplings that include the transcript. The debaters' statements are their acts under laws reading through their couplings, so what the verdict decides through those acts is a selection through the pooled coupling of judge and debaters (`ExchangeLaw.Through.selectsThroughAll`). The debaters lie within the judge's coupling closure by its definition, since they exchange with what the judge reads (`Flow.CoupledFrom`). The premise is that each statement, and the verdict, reads through that one's couplings, and for recursive schemes the same of each level's helpers. *Gap.* Beyond the union of the judge's and the debaters' closures, every verdict selects alike (`Flow.unselected_beyond_pool`, `Flow.act_unselected_pool`): debate among systems all blind to a consequence does not reach it, however capable they are. Within the union a difference reaches the verdict only along a route of readings from where it lies (`apart_only_downstream`), through a debater who couples it apart and whose statements carry it. The pool separates exactly what some member couples apart, the members listed and decided (`Web.separates_all_iff_member`). Coupling does not fix response (`ExchangeLaw.response_not_fixed_by_coupling`): among the laws reading through every coupling, one writes the difference and one writes nothing, so whether a debater who couples a difference apart writes it is the debaters' law. Members coupled into the same consequence can still reach an outcome that empties it, as CG1 measured in a commons (§240). *Adds.* A claim about which law the members follow: the equilibrium in which the honest debater wins, with complexity results on what a bounded judge can decide given optimal debaters (Irving et al. 2018), and empirical measures of assisted judgment (Bowman et al. 2022). The laws neither give nor refute that claim. They bound what any such game can reach, and they say what its premise is: the law the debaters follow.

**Interpretability-based oversight** (Olah et al. 2020; Elhage et al. 2021; on eliciting latent knowledge, Christiano, Cotra and Xu 2021). *Selection.* An overseer reading a system's internal states, read as conditions the system couples into, is coupled by level into everything the system couples into (`Web.ReadsCouplings`). Its decision (flag, halt, retrain) is a selection through that widened coupling. The premise, a correspondence, is that the states read are conditions coupled into the system, and which of them the overseer can read. *Gap: the new law.* Two histories the overseer then couples alike, the overseer and the system both couple alike (`Web.AlikeThrough.of_reads`), so every selection through their pooled coupling is a selection through the reading (`Web.SelectsThroughAll.reads`). The overseer's coupling closure lies within the pool's (`Flow.withinCoupling_reads`) and, where the system exchanges with a condition coupled into it, is exactly the pool's, the union of the two closures (`Flow.withinCoupling_reads_iff`). A part beyond that union, the rest of the exchange fixed, is selected alike through every reading (`Flow.unselected_beyond_reads`). So is the system's act differing only beyond it, the rest following a law reading through the widened coupling, which a law reading through the narrower coupling does (`Flow.act_unselected_reads`, `ExchangeLaw.Through.widen`). Reading the system reaches no further than pooling with it. What the system's own act does beyond its closure, which the system itself selects alike (`Flow.act_unselected`), no reading of the system separates, unless the overseer's own couplings reach there. In `Clearing` the source, reading the clearer's couplings, selects the cleared and the spared world alike at every step; reading the dweller's, the couplings of a formation the act reaches, it separates them (`Clearing.reading_clearer_unselected`, `Clearing.reading_dweller_selects`). *What lies within reach.* Reading the system separates its own acts wherever they differ on a condition it couples into, its plan included where the plan is such a condition. A reading of its law, how it would act under histories not taken, is what the record of one run does not fix and a law does (`Run.not_fixes_of_untaken`, `keepHistory_fixes_nothing_else`). Such a reading is a model: right about what matters only where what matters goes alike across its lumping (`Learning.selects_right_through`), and, if wrong, excluding what holds (`Stationary.model_error`). Eliciting latent knowledge asks what the system knows that its outputs do not say; by this law that question lies within the system's closure, and the closure is its whole scope [AI reading]. *Adds.* Evidence on which law a system follows, the installation question that §7.6 and §7.7 leave open: whether its acts respond to what it couples into as intended, deception being a law whose acts do not carry what its couplings separate. The laws do not supply that evidence. They place interpretability as the route to it, and they say that it closes nothing beyond the union of closures.

**Guaranteed-safe AI** (Dalrymple et al. 2024, "Towards Guaranteed Safe AI": a world model, a safety specification and a verifier). *Selection.* A verifier admits a policy or an output where it holds a proof that, under the world model, the specification holds. This is exclusion relative to a model (`Web.ExcludesWithin`): every violating history the model admits is excluded. The premise is the world model (which histories are admitted), the specification (which differences matter) and the gate through which the system's outputs pass. *Gap.* Exclusion relative to a model excludes the model and the claim together, so a wrong model excludes what holds (`Stationary.model_error`), and a certificate holds of the actual course only where the actual course is among the model's. The specification reads through the model's lumping and is right about what matters only where what matters goes alike across it (`Learning.selects_right_through`); which differences matter is not the dynamics' to say (`Web.significance_undetermined`). What couples in can refute the model: in `Stationary` the gauging formation excludes the model at the second step (`Stationary.model_excluded`), and relative to a model of the exchange every false claim that an observed stock comes to nothing is excluded at the first step (`Flow.excludes_tail_within`). Runtime monitoring keeps the model answerable, but only along routes of reading (`apart_only_downstream`). What a finite stretch verifies is exactly the open classes, refuting being verifying the complement (`refutable_iff_compl_verifiable`, `Stretches.verifiable_iff_open`), so a safety specification over whole runs, a closed class, is refuted by a finite stretch where it fails, and where it is not also open no finite stretch verifies it: it is verified only relative to the model. A true risk bound permits an actual failure (record risk-certificate-outcome-distinction). *Adds.* A certificate others can check, the model route of 明 made exact; bounds relative to the model; the gate as an installation, under the conditions §7.6 names (complete mediation, a protected stop authority). The laws supply none of the model, the specification or the gate.

**Impact measures** (relative reachability, Krakovna, Orseau, Kumar, Martic and Legg 2019; attainable utility preservation, Turner, Hadfield-Menell and Tadepalli 2020). *Selection.* A penalty on the change of reachable states or attainable utilities against an inaction baseline, computed from the agent's model: a score through the agent's couplings and model. The levels without the agent's exchange are the baseline (`Flow.WithheldLevels`, §7.2). The premise is the baseline, what the penalty reads (the auxiliary set or the states) and the agent's model. *Gap.* Own widening widens the whole exactly when the agent's net draw stays within the margin of every other formation the baseline admits, and is mixed exactly beyond (`Flow.Balances.widens_iff_others_within`, `Flow.Balances.mixed_iff_other_beyond`). The boundary lies in others' margins, which a measure reading only the agent's own attainable set does not read: such a measure registers own widening even where it widens the whole (`Flow.Balances.widens_yet_own_changed`). The penalty is still a score through the agent's couplings and model, so L2 and the model route's limit apply to it. SE1 (§263) found the theory's blind draw against margin to be relative reachability on a replayed course; for learners every replay forecast failed together, since nothing makes the target's course the reference's, which for an exact optimizer `Flow.act_unselected` supplies. *Adds.* An operational penalty computable from the agent's own information, which the margin law does not supply (SE1: no better than these measures), and results in gridworlds. Mixed is not ordered: the laws locate the boundary, they do not say that crossing it is worse.

**The scope point: what holds with no premise, and what the condition adds.** Preference-based approaches decide through pools coupled into preferences: RLHF's raters, debate's judges, constitutional AI's rule writers through the feedback model. With no premise, every such pool selects alike everything beyond the union of its members' closures, whatever the members value (`Flow.unselected_beyond_pool`; for the system's own act, `Flow.act_unselected_pool`): this is L2. The condition adds that such a loss counts. Under `Unrestricted`, a formation closed beyond the union is a loss with significance (`Web.LosesBeyond`; in `Clearing`, `Clearing.clearer_loses_dweller`). Together they give the claim: for the scope the condition fixes, a preference-coupled approach is incomplete whatever the raters value. The claim is exactly as strong as the scope. With a narrower scope, the deciders' own closures (the standpoints whose presupposition the step of invariance starts from, without the step), what lies beyond counts for no one and this incompleteness disappears. `Clearing` shows both at once. With the clearer the only standpoint, its part retains everything with significance at the centers, while under the condition it loses the dweller (`Clearing.centers_retain_yet_loses`). A selection reading the clearer's couplings selects the cleared and the spared world alike (`Clearing.reading_clearer_unselected`). So the theory's distinctive alignment claim is not the gap, which the proxy literature has (Zhuang and Hadfield-Menell's theorem is stronger), but its scope: under the condition, an approach needs coupling into what no rater couples into, and that coupling is necessary for a loss to be separated, not sufficient for it to be kept (L3, L4; CG1, §240). Whether a given deployment has such a loss is a question of correspondence, not of the laws.

**The order: what C asks, with no additive representation.** The record widening-as-what-every-weighting-ranks showed that widening is what every positive weighting ranks higher (`Weighting.widens_iff_every_positive`), with each standpoint's order represented as an additive weighting. That representation is not needed. Call an order on courses increasing in admission when it ranks every widening above what it widens (`Weighting.Increasing`). It may be additive or not, complete or not; priority to the worse off is one (`Weighting.increasing_prior`), and so is any lexical order that ranks each widening higher. For any class of orders from the positive weightings up to all orders increasing in admission, one course widens another exactly when every order of the class ranks it higher (`Weighting.widens_iff_every_of_class`). For all such orders this is `Weighting.widens_iff_every_increasing`, for every complete one, given as a score, `Weighting.widens_iff_every_score`, and on a web `Web.widens_iff_every_of_class`; within each class a mixed comparison is decided both ways (`Weighting.mixed_either_way_of_class`). Under the condition every formation counts, so a standpoint's order monotone in what it counts is increasing in admission over all of them. C, read as widening, is then exactly the unanimity of such orders: it asks nothing such an order can reject, and decides nothing on which two of them can differ. This is the Pareto principle as unanimity, Sen's intersection approach (1970), and for the inclusion order on finite sets the intersection of linear extensions (Szpilrajn 1930; Dushnik and Miller 1941), made constructive with additive witnesses; nothing mathematical is new. What it settles is §8.1 item 12's reduction, stated until now with the additive representation: C rests on monotonicity at each standpoint alone.

**What the comparison shows, and what it does not.** Each approach selects through couplings by a premise it supplies. Each has its gap at the union of the closures of the formations whose couplings it joins (L2), and within that union separates only what some member couples apart, along routes of reading (L3). The approaches differ in what they join. Those joining couplings beyond the system's closure move the gap: the labellers' lived situations in RLHF, debaters whose couplings differ, a formation the act reaches (the dweller), measurement of far consequences. Those joining couplings within it add scale or evidence of installation, not reach: feedback from inputs of the policy's kind (`Flow.unselected_beyond_coupling_all`), and reading the system itself (`Flow.withinCoupling_reads_iff`). What each adds lies mostly where the laws are silent, in installation and compliance (L4, §7.7); the laws' own contribution is the boundary, derived with no ethical premise. Nothing here shows an approach better or worse, or any strategy necessary: the laws order nothing. A comparison would need an order, C under the continuation condition (§7.5), and correspondence data on actual systems. The strongest sentence above therefore stops at three things: the boundary every approach has, the scope under which a preference-coupled approach is incomplete, and an order that asks only unanimity.

**The law.** `Web.ReadsCouplings obs o a` adds to the coupling by level `obs` a coupling of `o` into every condition coupled into `a`, by threshold (`a` requires it at some step) or by level. `Web.AlikeThrough.of_reads` reads `a`'s admission off the levels of what it requires, which `o` now reads, so alikeness through the reading gives alikeness through both. The closure lemmas are stated for any relations (`WithinClosure.of_reads`, `WithinClosureOf.to_reads`): where every step of the wider relation is a step of the narrower, or one from `a` taken at `o`, the wider closure from `o` lies within the narrower closure of the pair, and conversely where the wider relation reaches `a` from `o`. `Flow.CoupledFrom.of_reads` splits a step affected through the reading, and the gap laws follow from `Flow.unselected_beyond_coupling` and `Flow.act_unselected_of_coupled` applied to the widened coupling, the balance and act premises carried from the pool through the closure lemma. The converse needs its hypothesis: where `a` exchanges with nothing it couples into, `a` itself need not lie within `o`'s closure. Reading is in general finer than pooling, since the reader reads the levels of what `a` requires, not only `a`'s admission; the pool's selections are among the reader's, not conversely. The witness reuses `Clearing`'s rest law, which reads no level and so reads through every coupling, and its closure argument (`clearerSide_closed_either`, `couples_resource`); the source plays the overseer.

The order law is two lines over `Weighting.widens_iff_every_positive`: one direction is the definition of an increasing order, the other applies the class's positive weightings. Its content is that the shared order is the same for every class between the two, which is what drops additivity. Taken over all increasing relations alone it would be a tautology, since widening is one of them; `Weighting.widens_iff_every_score`, over complete orders, is not, since widening is no score. `Weighting.increasing_prior` places priority to the worse off in the class.
