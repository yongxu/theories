**First judgment (before reading the design note)**

**1. Accuracy and misreadings.**
- **Current wording.** It is accurate, but the gerund "widening what can form" reads as an activity aimed at more, and "better" has no comparison class. A first-time reader hears "more is better", so the widest course is best. This is the misreading the change is meant to fix.
- **(A).** The second sentence is exact. The first sentence is what gets read: "widening is the direction to take" is the current wording turned into an imperative. A reader will ask which sentence is C, because the first is about what to do and the second about what is better.
- **(B).** This is the most exact, because it is pairwise and relational. "Widens what the other lets form" is clumsy, though: it can read as one course enlarging the other's formations. It also brings in a third verb ("lets form") next to "admits" and "can form".
- **(C).** This is the worst. "Orders two options by their difference only" invites "by how big the difference is", which is the scale again. It also fails to single out C: an additive weighting also compares two options only by what each has that the other lacks. What is distinctive about C is that it decides only when one side of that difference is empty.

**2. "The direction to take."** A reader will mostly take it as a decision rule. Next most likely, given a theory of becoming, they will take it as a heading reality follows. It is vaguer than "better", because a direction suggests a gradient with no stated baseline. It also carries the normative weight without flagging it as a value claim. Yes, it blurs warrant with guidance. "To take" says what one does, while 07 §5 says choices need further named premises. It also clashes with 07 §6's "a direction follows only conditionally": a reader following that link sees C called *the* direction outright, then reads that a direction follows only conditionally.

**3. Two sentences on a ten-minute page.** Length is fine, since the page spends more words on less. The problem with (A) is that its two sentences say different kinds of thing, not that there are two.

**4. My wording.**

> *C*: a course that widens another is better than it. What can form on a course is what it *admits*: formations (what takes and gives) whose conditions meet their requirements. One course *widens* another where at every step it admits all the other admits and at some step more, which needs no premise. C is silent where each admits something the other lacks, and sets up no scale and no best. Every verdict that one course is better than another needs C or a premise in its place.

For the fisher case:

> it widens fifty, so by C it is better than fifty; widening does not weigh the lost catch [AI reading]. If it ends a livelihood fifty keeps, the two are mixed and C does not decide between them.

**5. The role clause and the fisher verbs.**
- **"An order of courses" vs "a ranking".** It is a slight improvement, but most readers still hear a full ordering. "Every verdict that one course is better than another" is plainer and stays true to what 07 §4 claims.
- **"C takes it over fifty".** This is worse than "ranks it higher". C doesn't take anything; agents do. It makes the choice-rule reading stronger. "By C it is better" is the best of the three.
- **"Unranked" vs "does not decide".** "Does not decide between them" is better than "leaves them unranked", which can read as "equal" or as an assertion that the two cannot be compared.

**After reading the design note's §2: my view stands, more firmly.**
- **"Derived".** §2 says C can be stated as the direction "and its comparison derived from it", and that "better" then "names nothing beyond the side of a difference the direction is on". That is backwards. The direction, widening, needs no premise. C's whole content is the claim that widening is better. 07 §4.4 says itself that C's normative content lies in "calling widening better". Putting "direction" first and deriving "better" from it hides that content in "to take", which is the weight-in-a-definition the project's own instructions forbid.
- **The fronts.** §2 says "the fronts can carry the first sentence alone". That would put only the imperative on the page readers see first. I'd treat this as a real problem.
- **The choice-rule risk.** §2 admits it, and answers that the statement "says which way the difference points, not which move to make". The reader doesn't have the note. "To take" literally names a move.
- **Consistency with other pages.** The collective page calls widening "a direction, as a criterion", and T8 calls 生生 the way. This has some force. But T8 is an argued thesis and C an unasserted premise, and sharing the word blurs the two. If "direction" must appear, put it in the glossary or 07 §4 as a gloss ("widening, read as a direction, is a criterion"), not in C's statement.
- **The author's claim.** Saying a ranking is a reader's construct doesn't change what C is. C is an evaluative relation on pairs. Renaming it a direction to follow turns a claim about what is better into a claim about what guides action, and the account keeps those apart.
