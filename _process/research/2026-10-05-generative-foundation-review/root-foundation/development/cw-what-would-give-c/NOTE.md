# CW: what would give C's order on a listing

*AI-written, 6 November 2026, for board item CW ([the atlas's plan, §5](../aa-attractor-atlas/PLAN.md#5-finishing-the-map-the-work-items)). About fifteen minutes to read. It states what `Becoming.RoutesToC` proves, what the plan asked of it and where the answer differs. It gives no reason for C. What its readers reported, and what was done about each finding, is in [READER](READER.md).*

## The question

AGENTS says that deriving C from a weaker premise is open work, and that a premise derived from something weaker is one of the three things that could move [08 §8's verdict](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/08-METHOD.md#known-results). The plan asked: is there a premise weaker than C that, with the continuation condition, gives C's order on a stated class (the plan's word; here a listing's frame, since class has another sense in the glossary)? It asked for each candidate stated in Lean and each pair related, and said beforehand that if none does, that is a proved limit. It also said CW would settle what "weaker" means.

## The answer

Read C as a condition an order meets or fails: every extension ranked above what it extends. Call a premise on orders strictly weaker than C at a datum where C implies it and some order meets it and fails C.

**Beside help that reads no order and holds at a datum, no premise giving C there is strictly weaker there; at other data or in scope one can be.** A premise that gives C with such help implies C there, so no order meets it and fails C (`ReadsNoOrder.implies_comparison`, `ReadsNoOrder.not_strictly_weaker`), and such a premise that C also implies is C there (`ReadsNoOrder.iff_of_weaker`). This is immediate. It answers the question read as "weaker where it gives C": no.

Self-concern shows this in the law's own terms. On a frame reading a course by what it admits on a listing, with the process free and every claim counted as living, so that scope is already total, an order meets C exactly when it is increasing in admission (`Weighting.comparison_listingFrame_iff`), and with every listed item marked, an order is self-concerned exactly when it meets C there, through the law (`Weighting.increasingInOwn_iff_comparison`): one premise in two forms, and no reason for either. It asks less wherever its own continuation misses a listed item (`Weighting.exists_increasingInOwn_not_increasing`). With the condition on the root, the marking reading what the line reaches, it gives an order increasing in admission on every listing (`Weighting.increasingInOwn_iff_increasing_of_noOutside`, landed), and with P2 and P4 on a listing of what lies downstream (`Weighting.increasing_on_downstream_of_regularity`, new). Read as "weaker elsewhere, and giving C where the help holds", the plan's question has a positive answer, with self-concern, a premise that reads an order, as its case. At each datum where the help holds, self-concern is exactly as strong as being increasing in admission, which is why this is no derivation.

So a claim of "something weaker" has to say what it means. The senses met here: weaker across data; weaker in scope, C over a listing's frame being less than C over all of what it compares; weaker at a datum beside help that reads an order; strictly weaker and not giving C with help that reads no order; or not weaker at all, the same strength read differently. Being easier to warrant is another sense, and not a Lean question. The chooser route is the same strength read differently, with an `↔`, and so is the one-item law among transitive orders. The parts of the one-item law have the third: being increasing gives one verdict on every one-item widening (`Weighting.Increasing.stepAlike`), which with transitivity does not give it back (`Weighting.ranksLighter_alike`, `Weighting.ranksLighter_reverses`). The downstream route has the first two. The standpoints route is a different kind of law, saying when every such profile's relation is increasing.

**Every route ends in an order increasing in admission on a finite listing,** which is what C asks only on that listing's frame. A course on a listing need not be what any level path admits, and a listed level path admits nothing from some step on. So between level paths a route gives what C asks only with admission decided and both paths' admissions listed, one way, and no route here gives C on all of a web's level paths ([07 §4.4](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/07-SIGNIFICANCE.md#r-self-concern-and-c-under-the-condition)).

The chain from the older laws stays as it was: a positive weighting's order is increasing (`increasing_ranks`); on three listed items no positive weighting's order is exactly widening (`widening_is_no_weighting`, new); increasing gives self-concern (`increasingInOwn_of_increasing`), and not back unless everything is marked (`exists_increasingInOwn_not_increasing`); transitive and ranking each one-item widening gives increasing (`increasing_iff_by_one`), and without transitivity it does not (`byOne_not_increasing`, new).

The word "direction" is kept out of this, because [CD](../cd-c-as-a-direction/DESIGN.md#1-the-finding) found that it already has four senses and that C cannot be restated through it without loss. CD says "better" adds one thing, that the side of the difference where widening lies is better; the one-item law says what that one thing amounts to, and the chooser law how else it can be read.

## What is worth keeping

**Which side ranks higher need not come from an item's worth to everyone; it can come from its possible worth to someone.** This is not new here; it is put here because the chapter did not say it. [07 §4.4](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/07-SIGNIFICANCE.md#order-premise) reads widening as what every positive weighting shares, and says so: positivity says that each counts. The chooser route assumes less of each chooser. It may put an item below refusal, so no chooser is assumed to find any item good, and, where the route takes admitting not to be imposing, none does worse from a wider course. That is Foster's effective freedom (1993; 2011) with every preference admitted, which he states is set inclusion, and its strict part follows Nehring and Puppe (1999): an element adds exactly when it is uniquely best from some possible preference. 08 already reads covering through Kreps, whose representation has `reach` as one state's term and aggregates what this intersects; that reading is the AI's. What is added is only the law checked on a listing, with refusal, and its use here.

The qualifier matters. That some chooser does better comes from the range. The law says exactly what the range must hold: for each listed item, a chooser putting it above every other listed item and above refusal (`gains_every_widening_iff`). A chooser who would leave everything added gains nothing (`reach_eq_of_refused`). The order the route ends in still ranks a course adding an item above the course without it, even if every actual chooser would leave that item. So the work positivity does is done by the range, which says what could be valued and not what is, and what is assumed instead is that a merely possible chooser counts (`outside`): a pair of values, none asserted to exist. Scope has moved, not gone. Nor does this derive C: calling better what no chooser does worse from and some does better from is the premise, which at any datum is exactly as strong as being increasing in admission (`increasing_iff_ranks_noneWorseSomeBetter`).

**It says where C's route stops, and leaves open whether C does.** Where each chooser's choice bears on what the other gets, widening the one menu both choose from can lower what each gets by the game's payoffs, at the pure Nash equilibrium of one play (`Restraint`). The chooser route is stopped by this. All three witnesses relate their courses by the same `WidensOn`, so on the listing's frame every order meeting C ranks the wider above (`Refusing.comparison_ranks_wide`, `AnotherReads.comparison_ranks_wide`, `Restraint.comparison_ranks_wide`), while the net weighting falls, what the chooser takes stands lower in the reading order, or what each gets falls. That is no inconsistency, since C's comparison reads what is admitted, the other a net weighting of what is imposed, another's order or what each gets by the payoffs, neither reading is shown to be the one that counts, and orders meeting C exist there; it is a conflict, and where the added act does not count as living there is no extension and C does not rank it. Nothing in a course's admitting a formation makes the formation chosen, so `Refusing` may be the nearest of the three to what C compares; it stands with `Restraint`.

Whether C itself is touched is open. A menu of acts is neither shown to be nor shown not to be a course of what can form: `listingFrame` reads anything listed as a course by construction. The case the account compares as courses in 07 §5 runs the other way (`Ranking.restraint_ranks_over_drawers`, under the whole-run reading): what it compares is the events a reading includes, or under the possibility reading what each course admits, and not what is open to act, which does not show that no wider course has this shape. The shape, so that the question has a test: one course widening another while, at the pure Nash equilibrium of one play among those choosing within it, what each gets falls. The witness is a theorem on that frame, and there it stands against the route and against reading C as "more options are better" (the thesis G. Dworkin examines, 1982), and is what C does there; that does not lapse. Its bearing on courses of what can form, and so on C, stands until the full account is shown to remove it, by showing that no wider course has this shape, as AGENTS asks of a counterexample found in a weaker model. If one has it, it stands against C there too, and the narrower course is a closing from which each gets more: it neither widens the other nor keeps it (`Web.not_widens_of_closes`), so C does not ask that it be ranked above, and T8 names a pure closing argued good for life as what refutes T8, so the witness would have that refuter's shape, still wanting the argument that the closing is good for life, what it closes counted. The nearest law on several choosers is `Optimum.MutualBest.full`, on a flow, where restraint is no mutual best response; it compares no courses by C and does not show what each gets falling. Whether the menu's shape is the shape of T3's drains is not shown here.

**The shared relation needs less marking than the condition, and pays in `NotBelow`.** Every listed item being someone's own is less than the condition held de se. What it costs is that no standpoint ranks a widening below, at any item, its own or not, and an order ranking below every widening that adds nothing of its own breaks exactly that (`notOwnBelow_stops`); read as positional concern [AI reading, new], with the added item possibly no one's. A widening adding only what is no one's own is not ranked by every such profile. With standpoints read as present ones a future beyond every present line would be such an item, and so read this route does not do the work of the step of invariance; with later formations among the standpoints it would be someone's own, and which formations hold standpoints is open ([07](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/07-SIGNIFICANCE.md#open)).

## What differs from the plan

- The plan's candidates were monotonicity at each standpoint, self-concern, positive valuers, graded significance, standpoint-Pareto and quasi-option value. Monotonicity at each standpoint is `Increasing` at that standpoint's order, on the listing's frame exactly C (`comparison_listingFrame_iff`), and standpoint-Pareto is `ParetoAmong`. Positive valuers and graded significance are one node here, a positive weighting, since a positive grade is a positive weight. Quasi-option value is not stated: it needs learning across a deferred irreversible step, and there is no time in `reach`. Nor does the route carry Kreps's reason for valuing flexibility: his agent values it because she has yet to learn her tastes, while one chooser here has plain indirect utility. The route shares his state-wise maximum, reading a range of choosers where he has states. So it is not Arrow and Fisher's quasi-option value, the value of information usable only if an irreversible step is deferred, which 03 §12's holding for a reading covers. GAP-MAP 6.3's join of the two stays to be written.
- The plan asked for §4.6's P1–P4. Only P2 and P4 are used. P3 is not needed for an order on a listing. P1 presupposes significance and ranks nothing, so self-concern is a further premise and is not obtained from P1.
- The plan expected a lattice with every pair related. Here only the pairs a route uses are related, because most other pairs compare premises of different types (an order, a profile of orders, a menu) and would be relations by fiat. So the module is named for routes and not a lattice.
- It adds a limit the plan did not name, `ReadsNoOrder.not_strictly_weaker`, and a bridge it did not name, `comparison_listingFrame_iff`, which closes the gap between `Increasing` and `Comparison` that the ethics reader's flag 12 raised, on that frame; on level paths it stays one way.
- "Weaker" no longer appears in CD's own text. In 07 §4.4 the sentence on monotonicity at the standpoint now says which sense holds of it, scope, and 08 §8 states the first three.

## What would refute or extend this

It is checked Lean, so a refutation would be a reading: that one of the premises does not say what its name says, or that a modelling choice carries a route. The choices are stated in the module's header: natural-number values; two-valued admission; one refusal value for all items; a finite listing; a chooser who takes one item; its values, its refusal and its being there at all fixed across courses; "later" a reading, with no time in `reach` and no chooser asserted to exist; `ParetoAmong` counting a silent standpoint as no objection; `MutualBest` as a pure Nash equilibrium, in one play with no binding agreement, not a claim about what actual choosers do; `listingFrame` reading a course by what it admits, with every claim counted and the process free. Extensions: refusal priced rather than free; a chooser who learns, joined to 03 §12; several choosers compared by C on courses, joining `Optimum.MutualBest`, which has them on a flow, to the menu witness, to say whether `Restraint` bears on C and on T3; and whether any premise is weaker across data and also easier to warrant, which is not a Lean question.

## Standing

No new mathematics, by the module's own statement. 08 §8's verdict does not move. What a reader would be told differently: what "weaker" can mean; that the side C ranks better can be had with no chooser assumed to value any item, the range of valuations doing that work, as the multi-preference literature on opportunity sets already has it (Jones and Sugden 1982; Pattanaik and Xu 1998; Nehring and Puppe 1999; Foster 2011); that every route is on a listing; and that reading C as "more options are better" meets a standing witness, which has the shape of T8's stated refuter if a wider course of what can form has it. Each is a statement about premises, not a reason for C.
