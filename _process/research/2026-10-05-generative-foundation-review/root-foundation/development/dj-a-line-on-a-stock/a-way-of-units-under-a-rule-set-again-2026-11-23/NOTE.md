# A way of units on a stock under a rule set again: what was built on 23 November, and how

*AI-written (Claude Fable, board row DJ), 23 November 2026. A Lean landing: one new leaf module, `Becoming.WayUnderARule`. One AI implementer wrote it in two passes from a brief frozen beforehand; two other AI sessions attacked the brief on paper; two AI readers who took no part read a first form of the Lean and of the tellings, and both were mended; a checker who took no part then read the mended module and tellings before the core run, and what it found is mended, those last mends read by the writer only. No person has read it. Unit, way, rule, share, level, need and test are working names.*

## Where it comes from

On 19 November this row worked out on paper a model with units that each need a level and leave offspring, a rule set again from their share, and that level as what they need, and left it unbuilt ([the record](../reads-2026-11-19/NOTE.md)); on 20 November it set the course found there among the others open from its start ([the record](../reads-2026-11-20/NOTE.md)). On 23 November the coordinating session asked Ren whether the next step should be one claim at what it called the central gap, and he pressed "Yes, propose it". It then proposed building what those records had left on paper (`PROPOSAL-one-course-both-outcomes.md.txt`, with rows FM's and DJ's attacks and two further exchanges appended). After he had been told how the attacks changed it he pressed "Yes, if it survives". Both are buttons' labels. The coordinating session read the second as a yes to the build in the changed form and under this row's cap, and said so to him ([his words, the questions they answered and that reading](../../../../../../../sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-11-23-be-the-new-coordinator.md)). The row claimed it on the board before any build.

## What the attack on paper changed

The proposal built first the statement the reader of 19 November had found underneath the exact condition, over any sequence of rules; after row FM's attack, by a kind with no rule: a line on a stock never ends exactly where its course comes to a holding configuration none of whose descendants is of a growing kind. That reader had read its left-to-right half classically ("finitely many, so a last one"). This row's attack (`ANSWER-row-DJ.md.txt`): by an argument on paper, that half cannot be proved under the package's rule of an empty axiom report. On a course whose one growing unit is the first place where an open decidable property of the numbers holds, with a level that stays where it is whether the way takes one or two, the net never ends provably; "so there is a step after which no growing unit comes" then decides whether the property holds anywhere. Row FM, by its message to this row, read that course premise by premise and found no hole. That an empty axiom report cannot give such a decision is taken from what is known of Lean's logic and was not checked here.

What is built has a right side that is a test at one configuration, and is stronger than the equivalence of 19 November in one way that the coordinating session saw: both outcomes come with their steps. Row FM's account, taken as the telling: the level and the units' needs decide that one of two things comes; the lock's premises decide that the first of them lasts.

The brief (`BRIEF.frozen.md.txt`, frozen at 13:30:57 WIB; `BRIEF.md.txt` is the same with its "After") gave the measure for the main statement in seven cases, three premise lists, and seven things that would count as nothing gained. Row FM read it whole and worked the measure and both courses by hand; the coordinating session read it against the definitions at their statements and ran both courses by hand, without opening the program, `RuleCapture` or any proof. Neither found a false step; their changes are its "After".

**The main statement** (`tested_or_ends`). Under a rule a unit leaves its kids; the next rule is set from the way present before the step and the rule in force, by any function. Given that which rules favour is decided; that under a rule that favours each unit's kids together take more than it; that under any other they together take what it takes; that kids need no more than their parent; that every unit the course reaches needs something; that nothing is given from nothing and the start is at most `hi`; that, at every step with some need met there and at every step before, the step under what the way takes keeps every level up to `hi` within `hi`; and that at such steps whose rule does not favour, giving is short of what the way takes `T` at levels left below `y T` and at least `T` wherever the level left `z` has `y T ≤ z` and `z + T ≤ hi`: the course comes to a step at which the configuration holds and neither the rule in force nor the rule set next favours, some need met at every step before it, or to a first step at which no need is met. `first_tested_or_ends` takes the first step with the test.

**Under those premises alone the two outcomes are not shut out of each other** (`BothOutcomes.both`). One unit that needs 1 and takes 1; three rules of which only the last favours, each followed by the next and the last by itself; from one unit at level 2. The test holds at the start: the configuration holds (the need is met, and 1 + 1 is at most 2) and the rule in force and the rule set next do not favour; two steps on the rule favours and the way doubles; at step 3 two units take 2 from a level of 2 and nothing is left, and at step 4 no need is met. A reader found this course by hand; it is now built. No reading of that way of setting the rule from a share of the way has all three of: a share not raised under a rule that does not favour, a monotone reading, favouring kept upward (`BothOutcomes.not_read_from_a_share`; which of the three fails is not stated).

**That the first outcome lasts** (`tested_lasts`) asks more and less. More: under a rule that does not favour every unit has kids; the rule set next is a reading of a share of the way; under a rule that does not favour the share of all the kids of a way is not above the way's; the reading is monotone; a rule above a favouring rule favours. Less: it does not ask that kids take more under a favouring rule, that units need something, that nothing is given from nothing, or that anything is decided; and of the premise on giving along the course its proof uses only the second half, at the step with the test. Under both sets of premises together the net never ends exactly where the course comes to a step with the test, and ends exactly where it comes to a step with no need met, in both with some need met at every step before it (`unending_iff_tested`, `ends_iff_unmet`; right to left of the second asks no premise).

**From a locked start** (`favours_while_met`): where the next rule is a monotone reading of a share of the way, a rule above a favouring rule favours, the share of all the kids of a way under a favouring rule is not below the way's, and the start is locked, with no premise on the stock, the rule favours at every step up to and including the one after the first step with a need unmet; so a step with the test comes at least two steps after a step with a need unmet (`unmet_before_tested`).

**The two courses.** One counted unit `F` that takes 14 and one kind `O` that takes 1 and needs 1; under the favouring rule `F` leaves `F` and `O`, and `O` leaves two `O`; under the other each leaves itself; the share is the number of `F`, and the rule read favours where it is at least 1; from one `F` at level 28 under the favouring rule, on giving `min z (32 - z)`, cap 32, margin `y T = T`.

## How the tests written beforehand came out

1. *The main statement needs a further premise, or an axiom.* Not tripped. It was proved under the first list as frozen in one scratch build, by three nested claims by structural recursion, with only three premises along the course. Then a reader found that the frozen list had one premise more than the statement needs (7 below), and it was taken out.
2. *Only the equivalence goes through.* Not tripped: the "or" with both steps is proved.
3. *The carrying of the rule is proved afresh.* Not tripped. By the lister's print of what each proof term uses (`rows-final.txt`, by `RowsRule.lean.txt`), `RuleCapture.turn_locked` is used in `lasts_from` and in `locked_while_met`.
4. *A course's premises are not proved, or an outcome is computed where a law was to give it.* Not tripped as written, with one thing a reader found said too widely: `goes_on` uses `tested_lasts`, and the two `favours_through_…` use `favours_while_met`; in `ends_untested` "no step has the test" is by the law, but "the net ends" is by the computed step 4 and the half of `ends_iff_unmet` that asks no premise. The docstring said "by the law" of both and is mended. The premises are one conjunction, the definition `TwoKinds.Premises`, proved once for any positive need of `F`; a reader compared it clause for clause with the three theorems.
5. *The twins differ from the old theorems, or use them.* Not tripped: both `rfl` checks typecheck, and by the lister neither twin's proof term names the old theorem.
6. *The module says nothing that three older laws in a row do not say.* Not tripped, by the reader of the Lean. New: for a course whose rule is set again by any function there is no third outcome; none of `need_unmet`, `way_ends`, `unending_iff_holds` or `turn_locked` shuts out a course that goes on for ever changing rule without ever holding under two rules in a row that do not favour, and the two-step case of the measure does. A join more than a new saying: `tested_lasts` is `unending_iff_holds` right to left with `turn_locked` at the orders reversed, and what it adds is the junction, that the test at one configuration gives the reversed lock one step later. A re-saying: `locked_while_met` and `favours_while_met` are `turn_locked` in the model's terms, with the one step more. Near nothing: `ends_iff_unmet`. Not said by the module: anything of `need_unmet`'s content.
7. *A premise follows from the others, or is not used.* Tripped in substance, not by its letter. `some` (under a rule that does not favour every unit has kids) was in the frozen first list and was used once in the main statement's proof, to get a unit of the next way in the two-step case; the reader of the Lean showed that the decided split on whether some need is met there gives the unit or the second outcome. Row FM had passed that case on paper with the premise in, and said so afterwards; the coordinating session had found no false step in the seven cases. The mend pass took it out of the main statement and its chain; the lasting needs it (a reader's course by hand, not built: a unit that takes nothing and leaves nothing under a rule that does not favour). Also by that reader: `tested_lasts` takes the premise on giving along the course whole and uses only its second half at the step with the test (the form that takes only what is used is `lasts_from`; this row's remark). And "some need met at every step before it" follows from the test, by an argument not in the module; the statements keep it, as `LineOnAStock.unending_iff_holds` does.
