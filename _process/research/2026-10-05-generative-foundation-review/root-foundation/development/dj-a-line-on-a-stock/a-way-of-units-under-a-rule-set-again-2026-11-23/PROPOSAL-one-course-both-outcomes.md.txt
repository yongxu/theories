- There is an exact condition, searched on 45 million small starts with no disagreement and read on paper by one reader who found no false step (its statement corrected by that reader). Call it S3: under its premises, a way of units under a rule set again from their share never ends exactly where its course comes, with some need met at every step before, to a configuration that holds and at which neither the rule in force nor the rule read off the present share favours.
- That reader found what S3 is made of. A statement with no rule in it, S3′: on a line on a stock where some units' offspring take more and the others' take the same and are not none, offspring need no more than their parent, every unit needs something, and the stock's premises hold along the course, the line never ends exactly where its course comes to a holding configuration none of whose descendants is of the growing kind. And one lemma the package has (`RuleCapture.turn_locked` with the orders reversed). `LineOnAStock.unending_iff_holds` would be S3′'s case where no unit grows, exactly; `way_ends` its case where every unit grows, in its second conjunct.
- The row's verdict that day: "as a statement about a lock" S3 is "the lock-free statement with a certificate"; write no Lean from it now; and one question that no proof answers and is his: whether a rule that stops favouring because the units it counts were the first to go without is what the idea means by turning.

So I do not propose a new model. I propose to build what was found on 19 November and left on paper.

## Why build it now, when the row chose not to

1. **It is the statement the direction page says is not there.** "What is not there": no statement in the package has units that each need a level and leave offspring, a rule set again from their share, and that level as what they need, all three. S3 has the three on one course with both outcomes.
2. **It is the alignment paragraph's last two sentences as one theorem, with no reading of the level handed in.** Left to itself the favouring goes on until a need goes unmet; the way then goes on without end exactly where it comes to a configuration that holds and from which no rule favours. And from a locked start, by the same reader on paper, any such configuration comes at least two steps after the first unmet need: with nothing handed in, the running down reaches the rule only through an unmet need of what the share counts. Row FM's read of 23 November found that wherever there is a rule and a share, what the selection responds to is handed in. Here it is not: the response is that a unit whose need is unmet leaves nothing.
3. **The test that turned it down measures something else.** S3 was judged by whether the lock does the joining and whether an old proof retires. By that test a statement that puts proved pieces on one course is nothing gained, and the tries of 19 to 23 November that the direction page tells came back so. The aim asks for a map with its parts placed. The piece that places these three is missing as a statement, and this is it. That is my reading of why the work kept returning dictionaries, and rows DJ and FM may hold it wrong.
4. **It can fail in Lean.** The paper proof's left-to-right half is read classically ("finitely many, so a last one"); in the style of the package it is one nested induction, proved afresh, with `favours` decidable. Whether it goes through with no further premise is not known.
5. **It gives him something to look at and not a wording to judge.** The course of 19 November and its twin, as Lean: a favoured unit that needs 27 of a level of 28, the level falling to 26, the unit leaving nothing, the rule no longer favouring, and twelve small units going on without end; with the counted unit's need one less, the rule never stops favouring and the line ends. Whether that is turning is his to recognise, after he has seen it.

## Which premises do which work (row DJ, after row FM's read; 23 November, 13:07 WIB by the clock)

Row FM named the one place in the measure where no component falls at a single step: every need met, the rule in force not favouring, the configuration holding, and the rule read off the share favouring. Row DJ closes it by taking that step with the next; the room above the take falls from k to k+2 whatever the share does. Nothing is asked of the lock's premises in that half. So the premise lists part:

- **The "or"** (a first step with the test, or a first step with no need met): `grows`, `even`, `some`, `down`, the needs, `nothing`, `high`, `keepsAlong`, `regimeAlong`, `favours` decidable, and any way of setting the next rule from the present way and rule.
- **That the first outcome means the way never ends:** add `lowers`, `monotone`, `upper` and a reading of the share at every step. Without them a course can pass the test and end later.
- **The clause for a locked start:** add `raises` and the locked start.
