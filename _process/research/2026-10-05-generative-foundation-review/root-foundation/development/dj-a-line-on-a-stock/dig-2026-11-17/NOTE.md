**How it came out, in short.**

*The picture was wrong at its centre.* I had written that October's Lean does not let an amount be added from nothing. It does: its balance (`Flow.Balances`) asks only that whatever gives be listed as present, and a formation may give at every step while taking and requiring nothing. What forbids giving from nothing, across a step whose exchange lies within finitely many conditions, is a further premise (`Flow.Dissipative`), which six of October's examples of going on for ever are proved to fail (`witnesses_not_dissipative`); the examples that meet it take from a fresh condition at every step or have no enclosed step (`Lone`, `Clearing`, `Spray`). Under it and balance, with finitely many conditions in all, no taking recurs without end (`finite_taking_not_recurring`); and a finite family taken from at arbitrarily late steps, its exchange enclosed in finitely many conditions from some step on, is not from any step on left unfed from beyond it (`fed_from_beyond`). All seven first-round readers found that balance allows it; five named the dissipation premise; three cited the finite law, two each the fed-from-beyond law and the six failing examples.

*Every first-round guess held only in part; of the second round, E4 (a join that would shorten) failed by its one reader, and E6 ("stated nowhere") failed by one reader of four, the other three finding it held in part.* The frozen file has each verdict with its evidence. *Three things I said were not in the Lean are, in part, in it.*
1. Staying within what is made again: as a sufficient condition for going on, both needs met at the start, in `RelationalRenewal` (`within_renewal_renewing`; beside the package, in `deeper/`); as a property of the joint take on a condition in `Continuation` (`WithinJoint`; each taker within what is given does not give the joint, `Pair.own_not_joint`); and exactly on the line under seven premises, one of them that each unit's offspring take what it takes (`LineOnAStock.unending_iff_holds`).
2. Conditions each giving to its own level by a law of what is left of all, as laws of a step: in whole numbers, for finitely many conditions under constant draws, where each giving rises with what is left of each and within a band, a condition ends exactly where it starts below a threshold, found by running the step from where the others start, all starts within the band (`RegimeMargin`'s `Indexed.ends_iff_below`, `fates`); two that each give nothing where nothing is left of the other, their draws positive, end together or not at all (`ends_together`). Nothing passes between the conditions, giving costs nothing and there are no units; by a search for their names no other module uses these laws.
3. The difference between a part's drawing on what others can spare and on what they need, two-way, on a balanced flow set against the levels with the part's exchange withheld: a formation is supported at a step exactly where, for every condition it requires, its need with what the part has taken up to then is at most the withheld level with what the part has given (`Admission.supported_iff_within_margin`). It is stated of a part's net exchange on a record, not of an arising and not as a law of a step; what I had said was stated nowhere was the difference for an arising, and three of the four readers who reached that guess found it not stated in their files. Beside it: a part that returns to each condition at every step at least what it takes closes nothing (`closes_nothing`); and, in a balanced flow, where a family and the conditions beyond it together gain nothing across a step and each of those conditions meets the need named on it, a rise of the family that with what the whole loses exceeds what those conditions stand above those needs breaks one of those dependences (`DrawnBeyond.breaks_beyond`, one-way); where every condition a group's members couple into lies in the family and a selection holds only where the family so rises, the broken dependence is of a formation outside the group, on a condition no member couples into (`favours_breaking_beyond`).

Also there: a drawer that needs the level at no more than its renewer does and overdraws it at every step from some step on ends itself, and one that does not need it goes on while what needs it loses it for good (`RelationalRenewal.dependent_overdraw_ends_itself` against `independent_overdraw_persists`, general laws of one step with a fixed cast); and leaving something at a cost to the one that leaves, with what comes back to it stated (`Optimum.kept_returns`, `drain_perfect_iff`, for a fixed number of drawers; these two stand on the sweep reader, who read the statements and did not unfold the definitions in their hypotheses).
