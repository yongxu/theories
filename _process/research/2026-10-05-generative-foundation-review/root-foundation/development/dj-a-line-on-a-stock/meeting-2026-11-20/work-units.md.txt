## The model

`LineOnAStock.lean` as it stands. Units with `need`, `use`, and `kids : U → List U` fixed. After a step at level `x` each unit leaves its kids where `x` meets its need and nothing where it does not (`after`, line 138); every unit present takes; the level becomes `(x − H) + g(x − H)` with `H` what the way takes (`left`, line 146; `RegimeMargin.step`, line 249). The net goes on while some unit present has its need met (`Goes`, line 150). The programs keep a way as counts by kind; what is taken, who is in the next way and `Goes` depend on the counts only.

The point of no return of a standing use `H` is `H + y`, `y` the least `z` with `H ≤ g(z)` (`RegimeMargin.ends_iff_below`). "The regime at `H` up to `hi`" is `Short g H y`, `Meets g H y hi`, `Keeps g H hi`.

Givings searched: `g(z) = min(z, r, (c − z) // s)` for `0 < z < c`, nothing elsewhere, with `c` from 8 to 40, falling slope `1/s` for `s` from 1 to 4, a flat top `r` or none. Each gives nothing from nothing and keeps every level up to `c` within `c`. With `s = 1` the regime holds up to `hi = c` at every `H` that has a root; with `s > 1` `Meets` holds only up to `c − (s − 1)·H`, and levels above that fall towards it.

## Three writings of "growing needs more of the level than going on"

All three are in the model as it stands. They differ in what the fall takes away when it stops the growing, and that is what decides everything below.

- **W1, the frozen file's writing (kids with a higher need; what growth added is kept).** A core `A` (need `n`, use `a ≥ 1`) leaves itself and `jb ≥ 1` buds `B` (need `m > n`, use `b`). A bud leaves `ia ≥ 1` cores and `ib` buds. With `p` cores and `q` buds: below `n` nothing is left; from `n` to below `m`, `p' = p`, `q' = p·jb`; at or above `m`, `p' = p + q·ia`, `q' = p·jb + q·ib`.
- **W2 (the kind that multiplies needs more and leaves a kind that only stays; kids need no more than their parent).** A grower (need `m`, use `b`) leaves growers and stayers; a stayer (need `n ≤ m`, use `a`) leaves itself. Below `m` the growers are lost for good.
- **W3 (kids with a higher need; what growth added is lost).** A core leaves itself and `jb` buds; a bud leaves only buds. The cores never change in number. Below `m` every bud is lost and each core buds again. `LineOnAStock.KidsNeedMore` (line 1471: `A → B A B`, `B → B B`) is of this shape.

## U0. A fall inside the regime, with a use that does not fall afterwards, ends the line

**Statement.** `FromNothing g`; `Short g D y` and `Meets g D y hi`; at some step `t` the way takes `D`, the level `x_t ≤ hi`, and the level falls, `x_{t+1} < x_t`; at every later step at which some need is still met the way takes at least `D`; every unit of the course needs something. Then the net ends.

**Argued.** (1) If `x_t ≥ D + y` then `z = x_t − D ≥ y` and `z + D = x_t ≤ hi`, so `Meets` gives `g(z) ≥ D` and the step leaves `z + g(z) ≥ x_t`: no fall. So `x_t < D + y`. This is `RegimeMargin.holds` (line 730) at one step, contraposed; only `Meets` is used. (2) Below `D + y`, a step under a draw of at least `D` leaves nothing or less than the level (`LineOnAStock.step_lowers`, line 720, which is `TurningInTime.held_above` contraposed). So the levels fall strictly until nothing, which meets no need.

**Standing.** Each half is an old law; U0 is the two in a row. Not found as one statement in what was read; the nearest is `LineOnAStock.fall` (line 773), which asks a take above every giving in place of one fall. Its use is what follows.

## U1. In W1 the fall stops growth, and inside the regime always too late

**(a) The use never falls while `n` is met.** From a start with a core and `q₀ ≤ jb·p₀`. *Argued:* each core met leaves a core, so `p` does not fall; `q_{j+1} ≥ p_j·jb ≥ p_{j−1}·jb`, and at a step below `m` `q_{j+1} = p_j·jb ≥ q_j`. *Searched:* 0 of 1,198,368 courses.

**(b) Too late, always.** W1; `FromNothing`; `Keeps g 0 hi`; start at most `hi` with a core and `q₀ ≤ jb·p₀`; the regime (`Short`, `Meets` up to `hi`) at what the way takes at each step where the level falls. Then: if the level falls at any step, the net ends; and from every start at or above `m` the net ends. *Argued:* the first sentence is U0 with (a). For the second: while the level is at or above `m`, every step from the first on adds a core, and a way that goes on takes less than the level (`taken_below`, line 1281), which is at most `hi`; so some step has a level below `m`, the first such step is a fall, and the first sentence applies. *Searched:* all 196,128 starts at or above `m` on givings of slope 1 end; of the 1,542 courses on which growth was stopped by the fall and the line never ends, every one had its last growing step at a level above `hi` for what the way took there (or a use with no root); none was inside the regime.

So in the frozen file's own writing, G4's "never ends" side is empty inside the regime the Lean's exact laws assume: the bud's need goes unmet, the bud is lost, but each core buds again at once, so nothing that takes is lost and the fall has already passed the point of no return. A stop in time was found only from levels above the giving's upper rest, where the level is falling towards a rest and not running down.

**(c) The first stop does not decide; a later one can.** Let `T` be the first step with level below `m`, and `H` what the way after that step takes (`p_T·(a + jb·b)`).
- *Necessary at every stop, argued:* if `n ≤ x_T < m` and `x_{T+1} < H + y`, the net ends (the use stays at least `H` by (a); `step_lowers`). *Searched:* 0 failures at 1,542,452 stops.
- *Not sufficient, searched:* on 104,662 courses the condition holds at the first stop and the line ends. In every one the level under `H` rises to `m`, growth comes, and the line ends later. In none of them had any growing step come before that first stop.
- So G4's sentence, as worded for W1, **fails test 5**.

**(d) What is exact instead (E).** Say a configuration `(w, x)` *stands* where some unit of `w` has its need met at `x`, the step leaves the way as it is (`after need kids w x = w`), and along the level's course under what `w` takes every level meets the same needs of `w` as `x` does.
- **E-if, for any units, giving and start (argued):** a course that comes to a configuration that stands, some need met at every step before, never ends. By induction the course from there is `(w, level under taken w)`: `after_alike` (line 328) with the third clause, then the second clause; the unit met at `x` stays met.
- **E-only-if, for W1 (argued):** `FromNothing`, `Keeps g 0 hi`, start at most `hi` with a core, `n > 0`. If the net never ends, every level meets `n`; the cores never fall in number and are fewer than `hi`; each step at or above `m` from the first on adds one; so from some step on every level is below `m`, the way is `(A B^jb)^p` at every step after, and that configuration stands.
- **In the regime's terms (argued where the configuration is within `hi`):** with `Short` and `Meets` at `H`, `(w, x)` stands exactly where some need is met, the way is left as it is, `H + y ≤ x`, and the level it rises to under `H` is below `m`. Under `Meets` and `Keeps` the level from `x ≥ H + y` never falls and is at most `hi`, so it comes to rest within `hi − x` steps, and the last clause is a finite check.
- *Searched:* "never ends exactly where the course comes to a configuration that stands" agreed with the fate on all 1,198,368 courses of W1, in both forms. On 12,366 never-ending courses an earlier candidate lay above `hi` and a later one within it.
- The upper clause is what the Lean's `Holds` does not have and, with kids needing no more than their parent, does not need: here going on asks that the level not rise to a need not yet met. A higher start ends where a lower one never does in 22,618 of the (world, start way) pairs searched.

**Worked, by hand.** `n = 1`, `m = 3`, `a = 1`, `b = 0`, `A → A B`, `B → A`, `g(z) = min(z, 8 − z)`. From one core and one bud at 2: the bud is unmet, the way stays, one is taken and one given: it stands (`H = 1`, `y = 1`, level 2, rest 2, below 3). ## U2. In W2 the first stop decides, in G4's form

**Statement.** Units as in `LineOnAStock`. A predicate `S` (the growers) every unit of which needs `m`; every unit of every generation of `w` that is not of `S` leaves itself only and needs `n`; `0 < n ≤ m`; `FromNothing g`; the start `x ≤ hi`; `Keeps g H₀ hi`, `H₀` what the stayers of `w` take. Write `X_j` for the level of the course as it was (all kids at every step from `w`, the level under what the whole way takes), `R_T` for the stayers of the `T`-th generation and `H_T` for what they take. At each `T`: `Short g H_T y_T`, `Meets g H_T y_T hi`, `n ≤ H_T + y_T`. And the course as it was comes below `m` at some step. Then the net from `(w, x)` never ends **exactly where** there is a step `T` with `m ≤ X_j` for every `j < T`, `n ≤ X_T < m`, `R_T` not empty, and `H_T + y_T ≤ X_{T+1}`.

In words: growth stops at the first step the level is below `m`; the line never ends exactly where, one step later, the level is at or above the point of no return of what the stayers left by then take.

**Argued.** Before `T` every need is met (`n ≤ m ≤ X_j`), so the course is the course as it was (`course_asItWas`, line 951). At `T` the growers leave nothing and each stayer leaves itself, so the way at `T + 1` is `R_T` and the level is `X_{T+1}`; no grower comes back (`gone_from`, line 674). From there the way is fixed and the level runs under the one draw `H_T`: `RegimeMargin.ends_iff_below` (line 741) with `holds` (line 730), the levels within `hi` because every way of the course takes at least `H₀` (the scratch's `kept_within_more`, line 65). If `X_T < n` or `R_T` is empty, no need is met at `T` or at `T + 1`.

**Searched.** 1,548,288 courses; 39,025 undecided (growers that take nothing and multiply) left out. On the 1,228,283 that meet the premises the two sides agree, 19,096 never ending. Where `Meets` or `within` fails the right side holds on 1,890 courses that end: both are needed. Where the course as it was never comes below `m` (163,894) the line never ends and there is no `T`: that premise is needed too; it follows from `rising_takes_all` where what the way takes rises at every step.

**It can come too late: yes.** On 160,504 courses growth was stopped by the fall with stayers left and the line still ended. On 10,678 of them the level one step later would have been at or above the point of no return had the growers present at the stop, born at the last growing step or before, not taken there. On 219 it was at or above the point of no return of the stayers there were before the last growing step.

**In time.** 1,336 courses never end after at least one growing step; 84 of them have growers that leave stayers.

**Worked, by hand.** `n = 1`, `m = 8`, stayer use 1, grower use 3, grower → grower and stayer, `g(z) = min(z, 3, 8 − z)`. From one grower and no stayer at 8: ways `(0,1), (1,1), (2,1), (2,0)` (stayers, growers) at levels `8, 8, 7, 4`, then `(2,0)` at 4 for ever. `T = 2`, `H_T = 2`, `y_T = 2`, `X_3 = 4`. What goes on was all left by the grower. From two growers at 8: `(0,2)` at 8, `(2,2)` at 4, `(2,0)` at 0. `T = 1`, the two stayers would need 4 at the next step; the two growers born at the last growing step take 6 of the 4 there is.

## W3: no single condition; ways that grow, lose the growth and grow again for ever

*What there is instead (argued in outline, searched).* The cores never change, so below `m` the way always takes one amount `H₀`. With the regime at `H₀` and `n ≤ H₀ + y₀`: the line never ends exactly where, if the start is below `m`, it meets `n` and the next level is at or above `H₀ + y₀`, and at every bust (a step below `m` after a step at or above it) the level meets `n` and the next level is at or above `H₀ + y₀`.
