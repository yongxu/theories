## What was asked

The package says that a course can no longer be kept in two places, and no module, result record or chapter line was found that has both.

- In `ReadOffInTime`, a course of levels is **recoverable** at step `N` where some feasible course agrees with it up to `N` and keeps what is asked. The feasible courses and what is asked are handed in. The first step at which a course is not recoverable is its **point of no return**. The lock's point of no return, and a loss read in time, are stated on it.
- In `JointKeeping`, a guard is **keepable** from a state where some run of a process from that state stays within the guard. Keeping several conditions together is stated on it and, in scratch, a step law and October's renewal.

Six guesses (J1 to J6) and what would count as nothing having changed were frozen at 16:40 WIB, before any agent ran and before any Lean was written ([the frozen file](FROZEN-TWO-NO-RETURNS.md.txt); [as frozen](FROZEN-TWO-NO-RETURNS.as-frozen-1640.md.txt), [its hash](FROZEN.sha256.txt)). Under its line "After", before any Lean was written: four points of the coordinating session, taken in as tests; the survey session's search, which found no place with both; and what the first reader found ([its report](read-older-and-already-stated.md.txt)). That reader found no join in the Lean it read. ## What the module states

[The module](ScratchNoReturn.lean.txt); [its build log](build-ScratchNoReturn.log.txt). The statements below were read in the module by the writer; each is given with its premises.

1. **Where the feasible courses are the runs of a process and what is asked is a guard at every step, recoverable is: the guard held so far, and keepable from here.** For every process, guard, run and step `N`, with no other premise: taking as feasible the courses of states of the process's runs, as asked the guard at every step, and as agreement equality of states up to `N`, the run's course is recoverable at `N` exactly where the guard holds at the run's states up to `N` and is keepable from its state at `N` (`recoverableBy_run_iff`). This is stated on a notion the module defines, `RecoverableBy`, which is the package's `Recoverable` with the agreement at each step a relation handed in; the package's `Recoverable` and `NoReturnAt` are it at agreement of level vectors condition by condition, by `rfl` (`recoverable_same`, `noReturnAt_same`).
   On the package's own `Recoverable`, for a process whose states are the level vectors, the same holds under two premises: the guard, and each step in the vector it starts from, do not tell apart two level vectors that are equal at every condition (`recoverable_levels_run_iff`). The module's header says that function extensionality would give both premises and that it rests on an axiom the package does not admit. With a reading of a level vector off each state, in place of states that are level vectors, it holds under one premise, that the guard does not tell such vectors apart, and then says: some run from a start agrees with the course up to `N` in what is read, the guard held on it up to `N`, and is keepable from its state at `N` (`recoverable_runLevels_iff`).
2. **Keepable is recoverable at step 0.** For every process, guard and course (not assumed a run), with no premise, on `RecoverableBy` with equality: the course is recoverable at step 0 exactly where the guard is keepable from its first state (`keepable_iff_recoverable_zero`).
3. **The point of no return is the first state from which the guard is not keepable.** For every process, guard, run and step `N`, and any family of statements, one for each step, that has the form of 1 (the premise `j1`): the family fails at `N` and holds before it exactly where the guard is not keepable from the run's state at `N` and is keepable from each state before (`first_not_keepable_iff`). On the package's own `NoReturnAt`, under 1's two premises: `noReturnAt_levels_iff`. Such a first step exists exactly where the guard is not keepable from some state of the run, under the further premise that keepability is decided at each state of the run (`exists_first_iff`; it calls the package's `first_of_decided`).
4. **Only with the past.** For a run whose first state that the guard is not keepable from is at `N`: the guard held up to `n` and is keepable from the state at `n` exactly where `n < N` (`recoverable_iff_of_first`, no other premise; on the package's `Recoverable`, under 1's two premises, `levels_recoverable_iff_of_first`, which does not call the package's `NoReturnAt.recoverable_iff`). With "the guard held up to `n`" left out it fails, in a built case (`keepable_alone_not`): on the successor law of the numbers, with the guard "not 1" and the run from 0, the guard is not keepable from 0 and is keepable from 2. Along a run, keepable can be had again after it was lost, where the guard was broken in between; recoverable cannot (the package's `Recoverable.earlier`).
5. **A step law, for any family of the form of 1.** Such a family holds at `N + 1` exactly where it holds at `N` and the guard is keepable from the run's state at `N + 1` (`rec_succ_iff`; it calls the scratch `keepable_step_iff`). It is not stated on the package's `Recoverable`, and the second reader says that given `j1` it follows by rewriting.

The lock's `MeetsNeed` is a guard at every step, by `rfl` (`meetsNeed_same`). What `FourReaders` asks (`DwellerKept`) was not checked in Lean; the second reader says it is a guard on the level and the step together, not on the level alone.
7. **Every indexed family is the courses read off the runs of a process, one made for it.** The process `Prophecy K` has as state an index and a count; a step keeps the index and adds one to the count. For every indexed family, a course is in the family exactly where it is read off a run of that process from count 0 (`indexed_iff_runLevels`, no premise); the same for every family that does not tell apart courses equal at every step and condition, with the member itself as the index (`family_iff_runLevels`). On that process a state has one run: the guard is keepable from an index and a count exactly where it holds at that index and every later count (`prophecy_keepable_iff`). So on it the choice is all in where a run starts and none in its steps, and keepable says only that the one course from here keeps the guard.
8. **On states that are the courses' own values, the courses of runs are closed under dropping a first step** (`runCourse_tail`, no premise). Its docstring adds that a family not closed so is the family of no process on those states. That the lock's `Turned`, or the four readers' family, is not closed so is not proved in the module.
9. **The lock's two laws again, with their statements unchanged.** With no premise, the lock's course is recoverable at `N` exactly where some run of `Prophecy` over the step of the turn, from count 0, has the course's level up to `N`, met the need up to `N`, and the need is keepable from its state at `N` (`lock_recoverable_iff_runs`). Through that and 7, the module proves again the lock's `recoverable_iff_above` and `noReturnAt_iff_first_below` under their eight premises, and checks by `rfl` that each new statement is the old one (`recoverable_iff_above_same`, `noReturnAt_iff_first_below_same`). The new proofs do not call the two old theorems or the two halves of the first; they call the two public halves of the law before them (`turned_meets_of_above`, `above_of_turned_meets`) and copies of three private lemmas. Under the same eight premises, `H + y ≤ X N` exactly where `X` met the need up to `N` and the need is keepable, on `Prophecy`, from the state "turned at `N`, at step `N`" (`lock_edge_iff`).
   The second reader's finding, which I take: these proofs put keepable in and take it out again, and rest on the law before them; by 7, `lock_edge_iff` is that law at the turn at `N`. No sentence should say that an old law became a case.
10. **Not proved: the lock on a carrier of the level and whether the way has turned** (the frozen file's J3). The worker expects it to fail, since the run that never turns gives the course itself, which is not among the turned courses. That is argued, not proved.

## What it draws

This is my reading, held loosely; no report states it whole.

- **The two notions are one on the part where what can still be done is made step by step from where the course is.** There the point of no return is the first state from which the guard is not keepable, the guard having held so far.
- **The package's two uses of recoverable are made runs of a process in the module only by a state that carries one choice from the start** (which reader; the step of the turn), after which nothing is chosen along the way. Whether a process that chooses along the way has the lock's courses as its runs is not proved either way.
- **The past is part of it.** Keepable from a state says nothing of how the course got there, and it can come back where the guard was broken in between. Recoverable is of a course with its past, and does not come back.
- **It places nothing between the two ways.** It says where two words of the map are one word, and where they are not shown to be.
