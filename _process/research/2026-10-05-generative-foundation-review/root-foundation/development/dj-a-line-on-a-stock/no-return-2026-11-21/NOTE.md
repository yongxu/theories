## What the module states

[The module](ScratchNoReturn.lean.txt); [its build log](build-ScratchNoReturn.log.txt). The statements below were read in the module by the writer; each is given with its premises.

1. **Where the feasible courses are the runs of a process and what is asked is a guard at every step, recoverable is: the guard held so far, and keepable from here.** For every process, guard, run and step `N`, with no other premise: taking as feasible the courses of states of the process's runs, as asked the guard at every step, and as agreement equality of states up to `N`, the run's course is recoverable at `N` exactly where the guard holds at the run's states up to `N` and is keepable from its state at `N` (`recoverableBy_run_iff`). This is stated on a notion the module defines, `RecoverableBy`, which is the package's `Recoverable` with the agreement at each step a relation handed in; the package's `Recoverable` and `NoReturnAt` are it at agreement of level vectors condition by condition, by `rfl` (`recoverable_same`, `noReturnAt_same`).
   On the package's own `Recoverable`, for a process whose states are the level vectors, the same holds under two premises: the guard, and each step in the vector it starts from, do not tell apart two level vectors that are equal at every condition (`recoverable_levels_run_iff`). The module's header says that function extensionality would give both premises and that it rests on an axiom the package does not admit. With a reading of a level vector off each state, in place of states that are level vectors, and runs taken from a set of starts: for any course (not assumed a run's), where what is asked is the guard at every step (`keeps_iff`) and under one premise, that the guard does not tell such vectors apart, the course is recoverable at `N` exactly where some run from a start agrees with it up to `N` in what is read, the guard held on that run up to `N`, and is keepable from its state at `N` (`recoverable_runLevels_iff`).
2. **Keepable is recoverable at step 0.** For every process, guard and course (not assumed a run), with no premise, on `RecoverableBy` with equality: the course is recoverable at step 0 exactly where the guard is keepable from its first state (`keepable_iff_recoverable_zero`).
3. **The point of no return is the first state from which the guard is not keepable.** For every process, guard, run and step `N`, and any family of statements, one for each step, that has the form of 1 (the premise `j1`): the family fails at `N` and holds before it exactly where the guard is not keepable from the run's state at `N` and is keepable from each state before (`first_not_keepable_iff`). On the package's own `NoReturnAt`, under 1's two premises: `noReturnAt_levels_iff`. Such a first step exists exactly where the guard is not keepable from some state of the run, under the further premise that keepability is decided at each state of the run (`exists_first_iff`; it calls the package's `first_of_decided`).
4. **Only with the past.** For a run whose first state that the guard is not keepable from is at `N`: the guard held up to `n` and is keepable from the state at `n` exactly where `n < N` (`recoverable_iff_of_first`, no other premise; on the package's `Recoverable`, under 1's two premises, `levels_recoverable_iff_of_first`, which does not call the package's `NoReturnAt.recoverable_iff`). With "the guard held up to `n`" left out it fails, in a built case (`keepable_alone_not`): on the successor law of the numbers, with the guard "not 1" and the run from 0, the guard is not keepable from 0 and is keepable from 2. So in a built case keepable is lost at 0 and had again at 2, the guard broken at 1. That it comes back only where the guard was broken is not stated in the module. Recoverable at a step is recoverable at every earlier one (the package's `Recoverable.earlier`), so it does not come back.
5. **A step law, for any family of the form of 1.** Such a family holds at `N + 1` exactly where it holds at `N` and the guard is keepable from the run's state at `N + 1` (`rec_succ_iff`; it calls the scratch `keepable_step_iff`). It is not stated on the package's `Recoverable`, and the second reader says that given `j1` it follows by rewriting.

## What it draws

This is my reading, held loosely; no report states it whole.

- **For the course of a run, with the runs as feasible and a guard at every step as asked, recoverable is the guard held so far and keepable from here** (no premise on the module's notion with equality; two on the package's `Recoverable`). There the point of no return is the first state from which the guard is not keepable, the guard having held so far.
- **The module makes the package's two uses of recoverable runs of a process in one way: by a state that carries one choice from the start** (when the clearer stops; the step of the turn), after which nothing is chosen along the way. Whether another process, one that chooses along the way, has either family as its runs is not proved.
- **The past is part of it.** Keepable from a state says nothing of how the course got there, and in a built case it comes back after the guard was broken. Recoverable is of a course up to a step, and by the package's `Recoverable.earlier` does not come back.
- **It places nothing between the two ways.** It says where two words of the map are one word, and where they are not shown to be.
