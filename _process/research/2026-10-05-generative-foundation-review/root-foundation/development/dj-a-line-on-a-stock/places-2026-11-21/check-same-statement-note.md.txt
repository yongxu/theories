7. MUST, on (b). "Where Lean accepts it, the statements of A and B are the same up to unfolding of definitions. It shows nothing more" (NOTE.md line 32). What acceptance shows is this. `@A = @B` elaborates only if the type of A and the type of B are unified as definitionally equal at the elaborator's default transparency. Given that, `rfl` adds nothing, because by proof irrelevance any two proofs of one proposition are definitionally equal. Definitional equality is more than unfolding definitions:
   - It includes β-, η- and let-reduction, recursor and literal reduction, structure η, and proof irrelevance inside the statements (two statements that differ only in a proof term count as the same).
   - Binder names and binder kind (implicit or explicit) are ignored, so the "same statement" may differ in which arguments are implicit. Argument order must be the same.
   - `@[irreducible]` definitions are not unfolded by the elaborator, so acceptance shows sameness at default transparency.
   - Universes: only the two checks of ScratchRenewalBridge write `.{u}` on both sides, so they show sameness at a universe variable u. Elsewhere universe levels are left to unification, and the regex strips `.{…}` without recording whether it was written.

   Exact wording I would stand behind: "Where Lean accepts it, the types of `A` and `B` (their statements) are definitionally equal: they reduce to the same term by unfolding definitions and Lean's other reductions, with binder names and implicitness ignored and proofs inside them identified. The `rfl` itself adds nothing, since two proofs of one proposition are equal. It shows nothing about how either was proved, and not that one is a case of the other." Whether `binrel%` elaboration could have inserted a coercion was not checked. That is remote for proofs of propositions, but the check as written does not exclude it.
