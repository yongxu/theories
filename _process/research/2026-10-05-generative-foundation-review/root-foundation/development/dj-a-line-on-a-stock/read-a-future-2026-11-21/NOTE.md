- **The law nearest to his sentence, which the writer then opened himself** (`WideningOnTheMap.lean`, first committed 30 October 2026; its title is "Keeping widening on the map: generation that loses nothing"). Read a course's moments with what is held at each as a chain of predicates, such as what the levels admit along a course. The change there is in what is held at each moment, not in kinds or carriers, and the law is of a chain along one course, not of a way of selecting.
- **After a loss, what is still active needs less, or no longer needs the condition** (`consumed_continues_transformed`), a general law; and changing what one rests on within a finite family nothing renews does not escape the end (`substitution_defers`).

## What it says of the re-saying

This is my reading, held loosely; no report states it. A first form of this section said that "more can go on after it than before" cannot be borne; the checker showed that was told too widely, and it is withdrawn.

- **What the Lean has is three readings of "the possible", with three verdicts.** Of one fixed process, read in a prestated space and by name, the whole possible never grows along a course (`Run.possible_of_next`; `Branching.reachable_invariant`). What a configuration admits can still rise (`widens_next_iff_rising`, `Rise`). And the possible under what is held grows where what is held changes (chapter 7; two statements read).
- **There is an exact law in his sentence's own shape.** He said: forms change over time, the continuation may continue, and it creates more and more future. The law of 30 October says, of what is held along a course: it keeps widening exactly where what is held changes after every moment and nothing held is lost, under two premises of deciding. Changing for ever without widening is the built case that loses at each step. So in the Lean's terms "more and more future" is not continuing through change alone; it is continuing through change that loses nothing. The direction page already lists this law under "Which two" as one of the pairs kept apart.
- **What that law is not.** It is of a chain of what is held along one course, handed in. It is not of a way of selecting, and no reader found a statement relating a way of selecting to the widening of what can go on after it. No law read concludes an opening in time from premises that are not already a going-on, a rise, or nothing lost.
- **The comparison is another thing.** In a balanced flow, with a part more can form than with it withheld, exactly where its net draw stays within the others' margins and brings one more within. No law read joins that comparison to the law above, or either to the grammar's generation in descent.
- **"More and more" has limits the readers name.** Keeping widening by name, with fixed requirements, is excluded for a finite, balanced, gainless whole (`whole_not_keepsWidening`). Where it holds, it needs the rest or an open boundary at arbitrarily late steps. Read within kinds, a built case (`KindRelay`) keeps widening while balanced, conserving and listed. Without dissipation, `Cycle` has more takers without end.
- **It is a description.** Nothing here ranks a way by how much it opens, and by his word of 10 November nothing should.

So what I would now put to him is in that law's terms, and I say it to him as a proposal: the second way is a going on in which what can go on keeps changing and nothing of it is lost, so that more and more can go on. That it can be said of a way of selecting, and set against the first way in one statement, is what is still not there.

## A correction, after the coordinating session read the law at its definitions (AI-written, added 21 November 2026)

The section "What it says of the re-saying" says there is an exact law "in his sentence's own shape", and that in the Lean's terms "more and more future" is continuing through change that loses nothing. That says more than the law. By the definitions the coordinating session opened (`KeepsWideningFrom Q N := (∀ S U, N ≤ S → S ≤ U → includes Q S U) ∧ ∀ S, N ≤ S → ∃ U, S ≤ U ∧ ∃ x, Q U x ∧ ¬ Q S x`), that nothing held is lost across any stretch is the first half of what "keeps widening" means in the Lean. It is not something the law finds about when more future is made. What the theorem adds, given nothing lost and its two premises (one of deciding, one of finding): something new keeps arriving exactly where what is held goes on changing, a change lying ahead of every moment. Its own docstring says "Nothing lost is what widening adds to the map's generation."

Ren said that forms change, that it is not held the same, that the continuation may continue, and more and more future. He did not say that nothing that could go on ever stops being able to. A chain that gains two points and loses one at every step has more and more and does not keep widening in the Lean; no statement of "more" in that looser sense is in this note. So the law is near his sentence in one respect, change that goes on, and stricter in another; that it has the shape of his sentence was my reading, and I withdraw it. The proposal at the end of that section ("what can go on keeps changing and nothing of it is lost") is withdrawn with it: to someone who has just said it is not holding the same, "nothing of it is lost" can read as the thing he set aside.
