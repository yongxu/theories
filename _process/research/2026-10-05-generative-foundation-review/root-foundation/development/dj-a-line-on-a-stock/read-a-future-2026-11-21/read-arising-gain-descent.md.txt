## 1. Arising.lean

**Q1. What stands for "what can go on after".** Not a set of what can form. `Web.Arises t f` (93): `¬ W.active t f ∧ W.active (t + 1) f`; `Web.Unceasing` (97): `∀ N, ∃ t f, N ≤ t ∧ W.Arises t f`. Stated of one web (one course). The account (16–19) says "what does not cease is arising, not any particular form" and "`Arises` asks nothing more of the formation: not that it is new, that it renews, or that it lives". On the grammar: arising is an active beginning 生 (`Web.arises_iff_sheng`, 725), and "arising whose line goes on is renewal (复生)" (`Web.renews_iff`, 748). The header (10–13) says arising carries "one reading of the second 生 of 生生", while on the delegation of 15 October "the second 生 ranges over what can form, active or not". So the module itself says it does not state "what can form".

**Q2. Laws concluding an opening or widening.** No general law concludes `Unceasing`. Built examples: `Oscillation.unceasing` (565), `Recycle.closed_unceasing` (484), `Tide.unrelated_depletion` (261). Exact two-sided statements, which restate rather than open:
- `Web.unceasing_iff_late_beginnings` (734): `W.Unceasing ↔ ∀ N, ∃ y : Nat × F, N < y.1 ∧ W.active y.1 y.2 ∧ «生» Steps.along W.activityClass y`. No premise.
- `Web.renews_iff` (748): `«复生» Steps.along W.activityClass (t, f) ∧ W.active (t + 1) f ↔ W.Arises t f ∧ W.active (t + 1 + 1) f`. No premise.

**Q3. Laws concluding a closing.** All general, all of the form "not at arbitrarily late steps":
- `Web.depletion_not_recurring` (170): for `cs : List C`, `T`, `event : Nat → Prop`, with `still : ∀ t, T ≤ t → W.Still cs t` (no member of `cs` rises from `T` on) and `depletes : W.Depletes cs event` (at each event step at which no member rises, some member falls), concludes `¬ ∀ N, ∃ t, N ≤ t ∧ event t`.
- `Web.Unceasing.rises` (182): from `depletes : W.Depletes cs (fun t => ∃ f, W.Arises t f)` and `unceasing : W.Unceasing`, for every `T`, `¬ ∀ t, T ≤ t → W.Still cs t`.
- `Web.Unceasing.activeRenewer_recurs` (190): adds `made : W.RenewedBy renews` (every rise is made by some active formation renewing it); concludes `¬ ∀ t, T ≤ t → ∀ f, ¬ W.ActiveRenewer renews cs t f`.
- `Web.substitution_defers` (201): `still` from `T` and `draws_down : W.Depletes cs (fun t => W.active t f)` give `¬ ∀ N, ∃ t, N ≤ t ∧ W.active t f`.
- `Web.dissipation_arising_not_recurring` (208): `q : Nat → Nat`, `unrestored : ∀ t, q (t + 1) ≤ q t`, `dissipates : ∀ t f, W.Arises t f → q (t + 1) < q t` give `¬ W.Unceasing`. The account (53–54): "the premise is physical; it is not a consequence of the theorem".
- Built example `Growth.ceases` (323): arising on a consumed condition ceases.
No law of a closing that stays closed in the sense of "no course from here keeps it" was found; these are of one course.

**Q4. Forms changing while going on continues.** `Relay.defers` (387), a built example: the consumer's dependence `Dissolves 0 () .first` and `Forms 0 () .second` (it changes what it rests on) and it is inactive from step 2 on. The general law behind it, `substitution_defers` (201), says the docstring's "Substitution defers the end and does not escape it": changing which members of a finite unrenewed family a formation draws on does not keep it active without end. So in this module a change of form while going on is stated, and the law concludes a narrowing, not a widening.

**Q5. From where, at what cost.** Under its premises, unceasing arising needs a member of the depleted family to rise at arbitrarily late steps (`Unceasing.rises`); with `RenewedBy`, renewing activity recurs (`activeRenewer_recurs`). Renewal is read as a net rise only (account 42–43; `Expansion.undepleted` in Flow). `Recycle.closed_unceasing` (484), a built example: a closed family of two pools with total held at one (`total_one`, 462) sustains unceasing arising with no dissipation. This is unceasing arising from within a closed finite family; it is return (the consumer arises again and again), not new formation.

**Q6. A way of going on or selecting related to widening.** Not found. The account (77–78): "More takers without end (`Becoming.Flow`), diversity and complexity are not stated here."
