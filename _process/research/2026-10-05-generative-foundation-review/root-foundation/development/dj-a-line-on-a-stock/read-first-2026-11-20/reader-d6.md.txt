## 1. The model, and R5 sentence by sentence

The model (`d6_model.py` 5 to 32, 56 to 138). Two counts, `nG` and `nT`. One step:

1. A named source gives `s` to each first condition, whatever its level.
2. Each unit asks its take of its first condition (`hT`, and `hG = hT + ch` for a giver). A unit not served takes nothing, gives nothing and leaves nothing. A shared first condition that is short is served G first, T first, or in proportion rounded down.
3. Each served giver gives `b ≤ hG` to its second condition, before or after this step's offspring.
4. Each served unit asks for `kG` or `kT` offspring. In mode `rival` each offspring takes `r` from the unit's second condition; in mode `thr` offspring are left exactly where that condition stands at 1 or more, and nothing is taken.
5. The next counts are the offspring.

Balance is asserted at every level at every step. A verdict is an end step, or "for ever" proved by a repeated state.

## 2. Is the selection half `favours_iff` or `grouped_rises_iff`?

The statements (`RuleShaping.lean`, with `RuleCapture.lean` 469 to 502):

- `CarrierCounts` is two numbers, the second positive. `select x g` multiplies each kind's count by its number in `g`. `favours g` is `g.other ≤ g.carrier`.
- `shaped a s p c` (:150) is the rule `⟨a + s + p, a + c + p⟩`, with `a > 0`.
- `favours_iff` (:160): `favours (shaped a s p c) ↔ c ≤ s`.
- `nothing_back_falls` (:287): with `s = 0` and `c > 0` the shapers' share falls strictly at every step.
- `present_along` (:271): carriers present stay present.

In this world every unit of a kind leaves the same positive number at every step, `s`, `p` and `c` are constants of the rule, nothing is ever short, and no line ends.

**What the program's numbers show.** I counted every step at which both lines are present, up to the end or the first proved repeat: 337,176 steps.

- **Nothing short for either line: 90,294 steps.** The step is exactly `select` by `⟨kG, kT⟩`. The givers' share stays in 66,587 (`kG = kT`) and falls in 23,707 (`kG < kT`); it never rises. This is `favours_iff` with `c = kT − kG` and `s = 0`. What is given, to which condition, and the extra take `ch` make no difference to the direction.
- **Something short: 246,882 steps.** The share moves by which units are served. It goes all three ways in every cell of second condition, `ch` and `kT − kG`. At 196,864 of these steps the step is not a `select` by any rule of that type: units of one line fare differently, or the non-givers leave none.
- **Only 3,256 runs** have nothing short at every such step, and in all of them both lines go on. Every run in which a line ends has a level short at some step.

**The reading asked for** (cost `ch`, own second condition as `s`, shared as `p`) fails already where nothing is short. With a shared second condition, `ch = 1` and `kG = kT`, `favours_iff` so read says the share falls; in 25,608 such steps it stays. `ch` is a take from the first condition, not fewer offspring; it costs offspring only where that condition is short, and then it costs a unit all of them.

Taking `c = ch + (kT − kG)` and `s = b` where the second condition is own, 0 where shared, the direction is as `favours_iff` gives it in 47,799 of the 90,294 steps with nothing short and 98,148 of the 246,882 others. With a shared second condition and a cost, where `nothing_back_falls` says the share falls at every step, it rises in 30,518 steps, stays in 78,287 and falls in 67,988.

**Smallest cases where the two verdicts differ.**

*The order of access.* Own first conditions, one shared second condition, `s = 1`, `A0 = 0`, `hT = hG = 1`, `b = 1`, `kG = 1`, `kT = 2`, `r = 1`, `B0 = 0`, gift before, start (1, 1). One unit of the second condition is given; one giver's offspring and two of the other's ask for it.

- `RuleShaping`: nothing comes back to the givers alone and they leave one fewer, so their share falls at every step and both stay for ever.
- Givers served first: the other line ends at step 1 and the giver goes on for ever.
- The other served first: the giver ends at step 1, the other at step 2.
- In proportion: both round to nothing and both end at step 1, the unit left standing.

Three verdicts from one rule. The order of access makes what is shared come back to one line alone.

*A line short of its first condition.* Own first and own second conditions, mode `thr`, `s = 1`, `A0 = 0`, `hT = 1`, `ch = 1`, `b = 1`, `kG = kT = 1`, `B0 = 2`, start (1, 1). The reading asked for gives `c = 1 ≤ s = 1`: the givers' share does not fall. In the program the source gives 1, the giver needs 2, is not served, and is gone at step 1; the other goes on for ever. The cost is paid as a need, and a unit short of its need leaves nothing whatever comes back.

**`grouped_rises_iff`** (:663) is not it either; the program's writer said so and I agree after reading `Grouped` (:636). Its shared part is `q` to every member of a group for each shaper in it: it grows with the number who give and is not divided. In the program what is given is one amount divided among those who ask, by order. The statement is one step's inequality over offspring numbers, with no level and no ending.

**M2, words to put instead:** "Where no unit of either line is short of anything (90,294 of the 337,176 steps at which both lines are present), a step of the program is `RuleShaping`'s selection by the two offspring numbers: the givers' share stays where the numbers are equal and falls where givers leave fewer, and what is given and where it lands make no difference (`favours_iff` with nothing coming back). At every other step the share moves by which units are served, that is by the levels and the order of access; no statement read gives its direction. Every run in which a line ends has such a step. The record's 'its selection half is `favours_iff`' was said of the guess D6."

## 4. Does the program's model meet `Dissipative`?

No. It meets the weaker thing the scratch proof lacked, and not the package's premise.

- `Flow.DissipatesOn` (`Dissipation.lean` :242) asks of every formation taking part that what it gives the family, with one more if it takes, is at most what it takes. `Dissipative` (:248) asks this of every closed family at every step.
- **The units.** A giver gives `b ≤ hG` out of what it took, and the grid drops `b > hG`. That is "no more than it takes", which `ScratchCovering`'s `give` was not (a number added to the level, tied to nothing taken). It is not "less when it takes" in the 82,944 runs with `b = hG`, unless the offspring's takes are counted as the unit's. The step itself does not check `b ≤ hG`; only `grid()` does.
- **The source.** It is named, and the balance asserted at every step includes it. It gives `s ≥ 1` at every step and takes nothing. Read as a formation taking part, it fails `DissipatesOn` on the family of all conditions at every step. So the flow balances with the giver named and is not dissipative: the layer of `Growing.growing` (`Flow.lean` :784).
- **What follows.** Under balance and dissipation with finitely many conditions, no taking recurs (`Dissipative.finite_taking_not_recurring`, :461). The program has 65,540 runs with a line going on for ever. Its goings-on are of the kind R1 describes: something a flow from beyond passes through, the beyond written as a formation that gives without taking.

LESSER for the draft: R5 and R7 say "a source that is said". Add that it is said and gives from nothing, so R1's bound does not bear on the program's runs.

## 5. Is the shape "two ways with a boundary between" in the table?

Both sets are there. A boundary that is a threshold in one thing is not shown by the stored outputs.

**In the stored outputs.** Call a rule everything but the start and the two starting levels: 6,720 distinct rules, each run from four starts and six pairs of levels. For 1,447 of them some start has the giver going on and another has the non-giver outlasting it and then ending. All are in mode rival.

- *By the start's counts*, levels fixed: 2,694 of 40,320 rules-with-levels have both. The commonest pattern is the giver going on from (3, 1) and the other taking over from (1, 3). `d6_validate.txt` shows the same for a shared second condition, rival, with no priority: from (3, 1) the giver outlasts in 1,356 runs and the other in 567; from (1, 3), 29 and 1,773.
- *By the second condition's starting level*, all else fixed: 1,782 rules have both. Raising it moves the giver from going on to being ended in 1,264, the other way in 517, and neither in 1.
- *By the first condition's starting level*: raising it from 0 to 6 ends the giver in 1,125 and saves it in 1,338.

So in the starting levels the boundary runs both ways across rules. Four starts cannot show a threshold's form.

**In the probe** (outside the frozen grid; four rules with shared conditions, proportional access, rival, and a cost; `nG`, `nT` from 0 to 12). In all four the starts fall into three regions: the giver goes on and the other ends; both end at the same step; the other outlasts the giver and then ends. The giver's region is kept when `nG` rises and when `nT` falls. For `s = 2`, `A0 = 0`, `hT = hG = 1`, `b = 1`, `kG = 1`, `kT = 2`, `r = 1`, `B0 = 2`, gift before, the boundary is exact:

- `nG > nT`: the giver goes on, the other ends.
- `nG = nT`: both end at the same step.
- `nG < nT`: the other outlasts the giver, then ends.

It is a threshold in which line is the more numerous at the start. Its mechanism in that rule is rounding. The first condition has 2 for `nG + nT` askers; where they are more than two, shares in proportion, rounded down, give the larger line one unit and the smaller none, at the first step. The smaller line is cut off before anything is given or used up. That is not the direction's boundary, where what sustains the selection runs down and makes a difference or does not.
