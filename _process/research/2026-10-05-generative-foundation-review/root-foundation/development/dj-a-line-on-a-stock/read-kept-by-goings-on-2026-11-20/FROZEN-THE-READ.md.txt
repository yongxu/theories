## The questions, the same for every place

For each place, from its statements and docstrings (a proof only where the statement leaves a question open), with the declaration's name and line for every answer:

1. **What is rested on, and is its being there the doing of a going-on in the model?** Name what needs what. Say whether what is needed is a level whose giving is a function handed in, or something another going-on gives, keeps or is.
2. **From where does what is given come, and what does giving cost the giver?** From its own standing; from something beyond the family (a source that requires nothing, a supply that is on or off); from nothing. Is the cost in a statement?
3. **Is there a loop?** Does the one that keeps rest on what those it keeps give or do, or only on something beyond them?
4. **Every general law that concludes going on without end** (not an ending, not a necessary condition, not a built course), with all its premises. Is any "exactly where"? What in its premises is handed in that would have to be someone's doing?
5. **What ends it, and who bears that?** Whose loss ends the others; does a statement say the loss falls on one that did not cause it?
6. **Does anything multiply, or get selected, among those that pass on and those that do not?**

## The guesses (each may be false)

- **R1.** In the package, no general law concludes going on without end for goings-on that are among what each other rests on, except where the giving is a function of levels handed in (`RegimeMargin`'s coupled and indexed levels). Elsewhere going on is a built example.
- **R2.** Where the source is said and a cost is stated, the general laws are one-sided: a family that keeps being taken from is fed from beyond it; a closed family dissipated on stops. No law gives a sufficient condition.
- **R3.** `LineageRegimes.Handover`'s relay and run-down are the nearest thing to the two ways in this sense as two built families of one model, and nothing states the edge between them.
- **R4.** Outside the package, October's files state more for supplied mechanisms (`ReproductionNetwork`, `CorrectionDependence`, `ResponseCapacity`, `CarrierDiagnosis`, `RenewingFormation`, `conditional/renewal`): at least one has a general two-ended law of a loop in which what is rested on is kept by what rests on it.
- **R5 (against going this way).** In every place, what keeps is a supplied function, a stipulated table of who is active, or a premise; nothing that keeps is derived from what it rests on. If that holds everywhere, a statement of the second way here would have to hand in the keeping, and the step is really the next one in the order (how what a loop registers comes to be and is kept).

## What would count as the read having found that the step is not needed, or not possible
