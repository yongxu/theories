## What the package has, by kind

1. **Where a source is said and a cost is stated, the general laws are endings and necessary conditions, and going on is built.** The reader of the first group, of `Dissipation`, `Continuation`, `Gain`, `Roles` and `Entropy`: a family that keeps being taken from is not, from any step on, left unfed from beyond it; a closed family dissipated on stops being taken from; an arising that recurs opens the family. "Being fed is needed" is the docstring's own word. Going on stands in built courses. The cost stated is the family's stock, never a giver's level, except in one built course. The reader of the second group found the same of `Interdependence`, `Flow` with `KeepingWidening`, `LineageRegimes`, `LineageDrawdown`, `Clearing` and the part of `Weightings` it read, with one exception that is not of going on without end: `Flow.possible_iff_covers` is necessary and sufficient for the existence of a flow over a finite stretch, on a web whose course of levels and capacities are handed in.
2. **General laws that conclude a going-on exist, and each hands in the going-on itself or the giving at every step.** `LineageRegions.generation_of_reproducing` and four laws of `KeepingWidening` take as premise a census in which every member has an offspring that descends, or a successor for every supported occurrence (the second reader). `Exclusion`'s `level_kept` takes "taken at most given" at every step; `Feeding.stays_met_iff_at` is exact, of a level, with the giving the flow's supplied amount at each step; `Web.changes_persist` concludes change at every later step from a handed-in function of the configuration (the first reader, who met the first two outside its group). So the first guess is false as worded and holds in a narrower form, in the second reader's words: "no law found derives going on from amounts, levels or a stock".
3. **Where both ends are exact and general, either the giving is handed in with no giver, or a keeper is rebuilt from what it keeps and what feeds the kept level has no giver.** The third reader:
   - The coupled levels (`Coupled.Indexed.ends_iff_below`, `fates`): nothing passes between levels, the givings read the levels and come from nothing, and the threshold is read off the run.
   - `RuleRebuilding`: members draw on a level; a door kept by holders limits the take; each cohort of holders is taught by holders. From a steady start, with entrants on offer at every step and no revision, a holder stands at every step exactly where the rule is within a band stated in its numbers, and the other side is general too. Handed in: the renewal, a constant with no giver; the count who would come; the teaching as a formula. Nothing multiplies or is selected between holders and others, and the opening account says "This is the steady state's law and not the rule's".
   - `ReaderRebuilding`: a reader rebuilt from the level it lifts stays while the supply is on, and one rebuilt from the supply does not come back: both general, not joined into one statement; the supply has no amount.
   - `Stock.repeat_covered_iff` (not on the list, met by this reader): exact, for every draw and replenish function and every course; what each act replenishes is handed in. `ContinuingRevision.repeats_iff` is its instance on one built cycle.
4. **Where a member gives from its own standing at a stated cost** (`PairFlow`, `PairSelection`): the general laws are a bound, a neutrality and an ending; going on is built or by construction. A sufficient general law where what is given is taken from a giver's own standing was not found by the third reader in what it read.
5. **Loops.** In the first two groups a loop of resting on stands as a premise of an ending (the gate: what gives to a condition depends on it) and in built courses; "Mutualism", the pair that rests on each other, is a loop of dependence with no flow in it at all. The first reader: `Web.Dependent` runs one way, from active to supported, so no law in its five files concludes that anything is active or gives. Two necessities are derived there: that a keeper exists where something rises, and that a keeper stops when what it rests on fails. "That a keeper keeps because what it rests on is there is derived nowhere in the group."
6. **A loss that falls on one that did not cause it** is stated only in built examples, by the second and third readers (the dweller of `Clearing` and of `Commons`, the last-born of `MultiplyingTakers`, `Overshoot`).

The third reader's summary of where the fifth guess stands in the package, which I take: "wherever the keeper's staying is derived, what feeds the kept level is handed in with no giver."

## Outside the package

By the fourth reader, who read whole the seven named files of the meta-attractor study, `RenewingFormation` and six of the eight renewal files, and most of the other two. No file of the group imports the package, and a search of the package found none of their names. In every file the mechanism is supplied, and each header says so.

- **`conditional/renewal`** (first committed 6 October 2026; its last commit, of 7 October, says every audit is empty). A configuration is a reserve and tokens of kinds, each with ticks left. At a tick, tokens age, and new ones may be formed: each needs a supporter still there after aging, of a kind with an edge to its kind, and the forming is paid from the reserve with what live tokens open. The law (which kind supports which, each kind's span, demand and rate) is supplied. Its theorem, which the writer opened at the statement: with finitely many kinds, a law admits a history that stays live for ever exactly where it has a population of kinds that is not empty, closed under support by kinds that outlast a tick, whose tokens open over their spans at least what forming them demands (`renewal_closure_theorem`). Where no such population exists every history stops by a deadline (`no_self_renewing_population_deadline`). Of a configuration: a history can keep the possibility of going on for ever that it starts with, or lose it, and cannot create it (`renewability_not_generated`); and that possibility is no function of the reserve and the kinds present (shown of one built law).
  What it hands in, in the reader's words and the files' own: going on is that *some* history stays live, "Nothing here says which history occurs, or that continuation is favorable"; which kinds are formed at each tick is a free action; the flow a live token opens is unlimited and outside the model; supporting costs the supporter nothing; the law is supplied ("Deriving it from the generative core is roadmap Phase 4").
- **`PopulationCorrection`**: both ends of one model with the edge exact, as the table of 20 November told it; constants, schedule and the number of slots handed in.
- **`ResponseCapacity`**: exact for the policy that never invests only; on the going-on side nothing is rebuilt.
- **`ReproductionNetwork`**, the nearest to several goings-on each keeping what the others rest on: one-sided, with a certificate and a supply at every step; its endings are built.
- **`RenewingFormation`** has no loop; it alone states a loss falling on a part that stayed within renewal.

Against the fifth guess's second clause, by this reader: in the renewal files, `ReproductionNetwork`, `ResponseCapacity`, `CarrierDiagnosis` and `PopulationCorrection`, who is there to keep is not a stipulated table but is read from the state the others keep. The amount given and the act that keeps are handed in everywhere.

A search by the writer for `renewal_closure_theorem` by name outside scratch found it in the files' own README, in history and hand-over pages of October, in a review of 16 October, in generated lists of declarations and in the table of 20 November; not in a chapter, the one page, the direction page or the plan. That is a search by one name.

## What it turns, set against the direction

This is my reading, held loosely; no report states it.

The direction asks for two ways of going on placed with respect to each other. What the read gives toward the second, in the sense read for:

- **In the package the pieces are again apart, and now it can be said how.** Where the cost and the source are stated, only endings and necessary conditions are general. Where a keeper's staying is derived, what feeds what it keeps comes from nothing. Where going on is concluded, the going-on or the giving is a premise at every step. No statement found has a keeper that keeps at a cost to itself, from a source that is said, with its going on concluded.
- **Outside the package, October's renewal files have an exact law of the other kind**: of what a supplied law admits, some history, with keeping costless and the flow unlimited. It has both ends, a deadline, and a possibility that can be lost and not made. It says nothing of which history occurs, so it has no selecting in it. It is joined to nothing in the package.

Ren said of October's study of the meta-attractor that it is a part of the map and is not to turn the direction. These files are another of October's studies, and I hold them the same way: a part, to be placed. What I notice, and have not checked: its "possibility that can be kept or lost and not created" is of the kind November's Lean calls recoverable and no return (`Recoverable`, `NoReturnAt`, `Keepable`), which compare a course with those still open from it; and what it lacks is what November has, a selection among courses and a rule that can lock. If that is so, the two ways would be placed by one relation: a law's population that can renew itself, and a way of selecting among the histories it admits that leaves the configurations from which some history still renews. That is a guess. Nothing has been set against it, and it is not to be worked before the statements on both sides are read against each other.

The next step I take, then: read the eight renewal files' statements against November's `ReadOffInTime` and `Viability`, and against the direction page's own words, to say whether that relation is there, is a renaming, or is not there. On paper, with what would count against it written first. No Lean.
