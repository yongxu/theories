## The frame all five places share (it answers part of questions 1 and 4 for each)

- `Flow F C` (`World.lean` 61) is three supplied fields: `present : Nat → List F`, `take : Nat → F → C → Nat`, `give : Nat → F → C → Nat`. What each formation takes and gives at each step is a function of the step and the formation, handed in. It is not a function of the levels.
- `Web F C` (`World.lean` 57, an abbreviation of `Dependence Nat F C`, 48) is five supplied fields of the step: `level`, `active`, `requires`, `need`, `draws`.
- `Flow.Balances` (`Flow.lean` 278): `∀ t c, W.level (t + 1) c + L.taken t c = W.level t c + L.given t c`. Under it a level changes only by what the formations taking part take and give. So a level's rise is some formation's giving (`BalancesOn.rise_given`, `Flow.lean` 476, used at Dissipation 372): in this frame a level is not "a function handed in" in the sense of the coupled levels; it is what named formations give and take, where those amounts are a supplied table.
- `Web.Dependent` (`Dependence.lean` 138): `∀ t f, W.active t f → W.Supported t f`. One direction only. No statement in what was read makes a supported formation active. `Flow.Acting` (`Flow.lean` 292): only active formations give. So what is derived is the negative half: an unsupported formation is not active and so gives nothing (`Flow.given_zero_below`, Continuation 457). The positive half, that a supported formation is active or gives, is nowhere a conclusion.

This is why, in all five places, a general law can conclude that a going-on stops, or what a going-on needs, and a going-on that continues is either a premise or a built course.
