The next thing in the order is an item the direction page has carried since 16 November: "How a loop comes to register what it did not register, other than by an update handed in." The idea on that page says: what a going-on rests on and what makes a difference to it need not be the same, and the gap between the two is where things run down unseen, or turn in time. The session's understanding, which Ren called good and which no proof states: the two ways differ in what makes a difference to each. In the one, what it rests on does not reach how it selects until something is lost. In the other it does, early enough, and keeping that so is the way's own doing.

Three reads of 20 and 21 November each found the pieces of the map apart, and each found that October's modules, which no session had opened for the direction, already stated part of what was sought. So before anything is proposed, this read asks what the Lean already states of a registering coming to be, changing, and being lost.

## The questions, the same for every place

For each place, from statements and docstrings (a proof only where a statement leaves a question open), with the declaration's name and line for every answer and every premise of every law written out:

1. **What registers what?** Which differences reach the going-on's next step, and where that is in the model: a function handed in, a state, a code, a route, a selection among carriers.
2. **Does what is registered change along a course, and by what?** An update handed in as a premise or a function; a move of the model; a selection among carriers each with a fixed reading; feedback; something else. Say exactly what is handed in.
3. **Is there a law in which a difference not registered at one step comes to be registered at a later one, and which says from what?** In full, with premises. In particular: is the cause the thing that was not registered (a loss, a fall, an ending), or a schedule, a teacher, a sample handed in?
4. **What does the new registering cost, who bears it, and what keeps it?** Is it kept only while something else is kept up?
5. **The other way: a registering lost.** Distinctions merged, readers removed, something unread; and whether the loss can be undone.
6. **Does any statement tie a change in what is registered to what the going-on rests on?** That is the gap the idea names, closing or opening.

## The guesses (each may be false)

- **G1.** In the package's modules on readings and the lock, every change of what is registered is an update handed in, a window on a level handed in, or a selection among carriers each with its reading fixed. Nothing there comes to be registered because of what it did.
- **G2.** The modules on learning, codes and inquiry state exactly when a reading can be acquired from feedback or samples, and in each the feedback, the teacher or the sample is handed in. None ties what is learned to what the learner rests on.
- **G3.** Losing a distinction is general and exact (what is merged stays merged; a reader removed is not brought back), and acquiring one is conditional on something supplied. If so it is the same asymmetry as the read of a future found between closing and opening.
- **G4.** Outside the package, October's files on correction, observation and goals state at least one exact condition under which a system acquires a correction or an observation it lacked, by a move of the model, with the mechanism supplied.
- **G5 (against the item as worded).** Whatever changes a reading is itself a slower reading, handed in one level up; the direction page already says "We do not expect a top". If every law found has that shape, "other than by an update handed in" cannot be met as worded, and what can be drawn is which loop sets which, and what each costs.
