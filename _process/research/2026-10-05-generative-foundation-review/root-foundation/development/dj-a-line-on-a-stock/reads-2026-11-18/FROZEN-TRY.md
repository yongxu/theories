# A try by program at the data under the map: what is tried, my guesses, and what counts against them

*AI-written (Fable, board row DJ). Frozen 18 November 2026, 11:07 WIB (from the clock), after one run of the existing program unchanged and before any new program was written or run. Scratch; nothing is built and no store is changed. What comes back is appended below with its time; nothing above an appended line is changed.*

## What it bears on

The design put to Ren on 18 November (`../../THE-MAP-AS-DATA.md`, "The data under the map") says the data should be in three parts and that the first, what the Lean says, is read by a program and not typed. Three things in that part, the model named from a statement, the idea's words kept as readings, and places computed and not typed, were untested. Its item 3 proposed this try; Ren said "Yes, run it" (18 November, about 10:50 WIB). The direction wants two ways of going on placed with respect to each other; this try asks whether a program can draw, from the proofs, where that placing stands on 22 statements.

## What was done before freezing

The existing program (the study's dump on main since 15 October, in its scratch copy of 14 November, unchanged) was run today: 31 seconds, nothing compiled, 59,442 constants in 409 modules, 22,992 theorems, sources clean at HEAD (`dump-0.log`, `constants-0.pickle`). That is not a guess. I have read its source. I have not looked at its output for any of the 22 statements.

## The fixture

- The 22 statements of round 17 (`../round17/FIXTURE.md`), and its nine questions (`../round17/QUESTIONS.md`), unchanged. The answers fixed from the Lean are `../round17/truth.md`.
- Six statements held out, chosen by a rule and not by looking: lines 5, 17, 29, 41, 53 and 65 of the list of 71 theorems that name the web's two classes (`HELD-OUT.txt`). No program is written with them in view; the auditors check them like the 22.
- A risk I cannot remove: I know the nine questions and their answers. The programs have no statement-specific code (an auditor checks that), and the two small tables I enter by hand (T3, T5) are said to be readings and are read by the judge.

## The pieces, each with a guess and what would count against it

- **T1. Every premise in full.** A program writes, for each statement, every binder as a variable with its type or as a premise with its statement in the Lean's own notation, named or not, and the conclusion in its parts. *Guess:* on the 28 statements the output has every premise and no premise the Lean does not have, and none of the 23 errors of entry the judge listed in round 17's typed files. *Against:* an auditor with the Lean finds a premise missing, added or changed; each is a fault of the program, listed. Or a kind of statement it cannot write (statement 4 is a definition).
- **T2. The form of each part.** The program marks each part of a conclusion and each premise with one form: holds exactly where (an equivalence); for every number; from some number on; within so many; never both; an equation; an order between two terms; there is; a named condition; none of these. *Guess:* at most a quarter of the conclusion parts come out "none of these", and no mark is wrong. *Against:* a mark that says a form the statement does not have; or more than a quarter unmarked.
- **T3. What each condition is about.** The program writes, for each named condition, the type and the term of each argument. Whether an argument is a law's parameter, a start, a state reached, a whole course, a step, a unit or a record is not in the types (a step and a level are both numbers), so a small table says it, entered by hand as a reading. *Guess:* thirty entered lines or fewer cover the 22; with them every premise and conclusion part gets what it is about; a judge with the Lean agrees with at least four in five. *Against:* more than thirty lines (then the layer is being typed again); agreement below four in five; or an auditor showing it can be had with no table, which would refute "not in the types".
- **T4. A model named from the statement.** The program names a statement's model by the structures and the step or course functions of the package that the statement names, and puts statements with the same ones together. *Guess:* its grouping of the 22 agrees with each of round 17's two hand encoders at least as well as they agree with each other (over pairs of statements, same model or not). *Against:* it agrees less with both; or a judge finds it puts together two statements that the Lean keeps apart.
- **T5. Words read as definitions.** A table of at most twelve of the idea's words or phrases, each read as definitions or as a premise of a named theorem, each with what the definition lacks of the word. The program then looks up, over the whole of today's output, every statement that names two definitions read as the same word or as the two opposite words, and how (assumed, concluded, in an equivalence). *Guess:* (a) it finds the ties known by hand (the two static equivalences between the web's classes and the fixed pair's; the two exclusions; renewing with a balanced record) and no statement tying any reading on the line to any on the web or the fixed pair; (b) it finds at least one tie that no page of ours tells; (c) at least a quarter of the readings point at a premise the Lean does not name, and those cannot be looked up by name. *Against (a):* a known tie missed, or a tie reported that the Lean does not have. (b) failing is a null, not a fault. *Against (c):* fewer than a quarter.
- **T6. Places drawn.** From T1 to T3 and the readings, the program writes per model: the classes of a whole course; where an equivalence sets a class against a condition on a start, a state or a law (a dividing condition); what is kept from a state on (a stay); what leads to what, within how many steps or from some step on (a passage); which two classes never hold together; which built courses are in or out of which class; and the ties of T5 between models. Each with its theorem and every premise. *Guess:* (i) every place on round 17's hand-written placement (`../round17/placement.md`) that one of the 22 carries is drawn; (ii) no drawn place says more than the Lean; (iii) the page for the 22 is at most 150 lines, and a reader who sees only it answers questions 2, 6 and 9 as the Lean has it; (iv) a reader set to refute it as a map still finds it is not one, and I expect the reason to be that it has almost no passages: it shows classes and dividing conditions and little of what leads from one to another. *Against:* (i) a place missed; (ii) any; (iii) the length, or an answer not as the Lean has it; (iv) is recorded as it comes.
- **T7. The nine questions from the program's output alone.** Two readers who see only the output (one without the places page, one with it) answer the nine; a judge with the Lean gives each a verdict as in round 17. *Guess:* no answer wrong, nobody chains on question 9, and at least five of nine as the Lean has it for each reader (the hand-typed file of round 17 got five). Shortfalls are of two kinds only: a theorem the answer needs lies outside the 22, or the reader. *Against:* a wrong answer the judge lays to the output; fewer than five; a shortfall the judge lays to something the output lacks and a typed layer had.

## The test

Nine AI readers after the programs have run, read-only, no Lean built: two auditors of the output against the Lean (T1 to T3, half the statements each, the held-out six split between them, and the programs' source for statement-specific code); one on the grouping (T4); one on the ties (T5); one on the places against the Lean (T6 i, ii); one set to refute the places as a map (T6 iv); two answering the nine questions (T7, T6 iii); one judge. Then one reader who did not take part reads the whole try and what I say of it before it is told to Ren.

## What this try cannot show

Whether any of it holds over the whole package. Whether a person can read what is drawn. Whether the readings I enter are right. Whether the eight faults listed in the existing program on 14 November matter here (they are not fixed; an auditor may meet them). It is not a store, and no store changes by it.

## Appended 18 November 2026, 11:47 WIB (from the clock): what was built, and what came back

**What was built** (all scratch, in this folder; hashes in `PROGRAMS.sha256`, taken before any reader started and unchanged since). `TRY-DUMP.lean` with `make_try_dump.py`: writes each named statement out of the Lean environment as a tree and in the Lean's own notation, with the definitions it names to a depth of three; about 20 seconds, nothing compiled, 398 constants written. `analyze.py`: the form of each part, what it is about, a model, ties and places. Two tables entered by me: `ROLES.tsv` (24 lines) and `READINGS.json` (9 words, 26 entries). Output in `out/`. While writing `analyze.py` I ran it four times and changed its rules three times after looking at what was unplaced (`out-before-*.STATEMENTS.md` are the earlier outputs): variables carry what the statement uses them as; variables a term is built from are part of what the term is; a field is told from another definition under a structure's name. Those changes were made with the 22 in view and before any reader; none is written for one statement, by both auditors' reading of the source.

**The readers.** `wf_e285a1b1-c5c`: nine AI readers (Fable), about 1.9 million tokens, 23 minutes, none failed; journal `journal-wf_e285a1b1-c5c.jsonl`, reports `reader-*.md`, `answers-1.md`, `answers-2.md`. No person has read any of it. I read the nine summaries whole and the reports only where a summary pointed.

**The guesses.**
- **T1, every premise in full: held but for two faults of substance.** Two auditors set 132 premises and parts of the 28 statements against the Lean. No premise is missing, added, reordered or reworded, and conclusions are cut where the Lean has "and". None of the 23 errors of entry in round 17's typed files recurs as an error; two recur as things the output does not say. The faults: (a) the Lean's printing hides an argument it treats as understood, so one held-out statement loses which web it speaks of, and the same would happen to every class of a single step defined after one line of `Dependence.lean`; (b) what the Lean writes after the colon (`∀ j, j ≤ n → …` in statement 18) is not kept apart in the environment, so the program lists a part of the conclusion as a variable and a premise. Lesser: how a variable is bound is not shown; parts are cut only at the outermost "and"; a definition's body gets no form.
- **T2, the form of each part: held.** No form wrong of 132; no conclusion part without one. Several are true and lose something (a bound behind a second "there is"; three equations called "several together").
- **T3, what a condition is about: held by its numbers, and not as I meant it.** 24 entered lines, under thirty. Not every premise is placed: 10 of 134 by the program's count. The auditors found 108 right, 16 wrong and 8 saying nothing of 132, four in five. But on three held-out statements one item of eight is right: the table was written for the 22 and does not reach a run or a world. All of the wrong ones on the second auditor's half came from the program's own rules, not the table (a role repeated at every premise that names a variable; a step count inside a definition read as a step). And "not in the types" is refuted in part: by that auditor twelve of the 24 lines could be read with no table, from which argument a function recurses on or from the shape of a field, and twelve cannot.
- **T4, a model named from the statement: failed.** The program agrees with the two hand groupings on 203 and 205 of 210 pairs; they agree with each other on 208; it makes 16 groups where they make 11 and 12. The reader with the Lean: on the seven pairs where they differ a hand grouping is as the Lean has it on five, the program on none, and two cannot be settled by any grouping, because statement 21 is stated of two models. The program never puts together what the Lean keeps apart. The reader's larger finding: a division into groups is the wrong form. A statement can be of several models, and models are joined by named functions (`Web.static`, `Law.unfolding`). What could be decided by program: for a course made by a step, the step itself after unfolding; for a course given whole, the type of the thing; between models, the functions and the theorems that equate steps or classes.
- **T5, words read as definitions: (a) failed, (b) held, (c) failed.** (a) Four of the five ties known by hand are found; renewing with a balanced record is missed, because my rule pairs only definitions under one word or under the two ways. No statement ties a reading on the line to one on the web or the fixed pair, and the reader's own search agrees. Three rows are mis-told by the older program's coarse marks ("assumed, or denied" is a denial in 28 of its 31 uses). (b) 34 of the 48 theorems listed are told on neither tracked page; most are single built courses; six are general. (c) Six of 26 readings point at something with no named definition, under the quarter I guessed, and that share is only what I chose to enter. Of my readings: the "lacks" for "ends" is wrong for two of four definitions; "can no longer be turned" misdescribes `Locked`; and every heading the program wrote says "(the two ways: …)" flatly, the wording the direction page was corrected for this morning.
- **T6, places drawn: (i) and (ii) failed, (iii) failed, (iv) as guessed and more.** Of 32 lines for the 22: 23 right, 7 of the wrong kind, 2 saying more than the Lean (one prints three of five premises under "never together"; one calls two classes one and shows neither premise). Five of the wrong kinds come from one mistake of mine in `analyze.py` (a test for the words "whole course" that also matches "no whole course named"). Of 28 places on round 17's hand-written placement, 22 are found (15 as the Lean has them), two in part, four missing; the four are all between statements, and two of them need what a proof uses. The page is 154 lines, 116 without the held-out six. From it alone, questions 2 and 9 are answered as the Lean has it and question 6 only partly. The reader set to refute it: it is not a map. That it has few passages is so and is not the main lack. What it names: no line says what is enough for a class, because the premises are not on the line; no words, so a person who does not read Lean cannot use it; 23 headings where a reader sees about five regions, with nothing relating them; and the two ways are read only as classes of a whole course, between which no course can pass, while the Lean has classes of a single step (`Web.RenewingAt`, `BreakingAt`, `UnderminingAt`) that are not in my readings. No line, for any model, shows a course passing from one way to the other. Its verdict: nearer a map than the hand placement in being tied to theorems, exact in what it quotes, and made again by rule; further in everything someone finding their way needs.
- **T7, the nine questions from the output alone: failed on the count.** No answer wrong, none silent, nobody chained on question 9. Three of nine as the Lean has it for each reader, the same three (2, 8, 9), against five for the hand-typed file of round 17. The judge laid six questions' shortfalls to what the output lacks and the typed layer had: above all that no definition is printed for the conditions a statement names, so `ConsumingIn`, `Holds`, `Meets` are bare names; then that the output does not open what a step is defined by, has no statement that two dividing conditions are one, nothing of what a proof uses, and nothing that is not Lean (the three sentences, the finding on paper).

**What I take from it** (mine; the last reader has not yet read this).
- Reading premises and conclusions by program is sound on these 28 and is where hand entry went wrong before. That part of the design stands, with three things to add that a program can do: print each named condition's definition, keep the arguments the printing hides, and take from the source what is written after the colon.
- A program's output alone answered fewer questions than a hand-typed layer. Some of the difference a program can close (definitions; the step a model steps by; what a proof uses). Some it cannot: plain words, and anything that is not Lean.
- "A model named from the statement" as a grouping is wrong in form. "Places computed, not typed" did not hold as tried: by outer shape about one line in four is of the wrong kind, and what a place needs most (its premises, and what relates one model to another) is not in the shape of one statement.
- The lead I did not expect: the Lean has renewing and breaking as classes of a single step, and my readings, like the direction page until this morning, took the two ways at the grain of a whole course only. Where a course passes from one to the other can only be said at the grain of a step. Whether October's statements at that grain say it is not looked at.
