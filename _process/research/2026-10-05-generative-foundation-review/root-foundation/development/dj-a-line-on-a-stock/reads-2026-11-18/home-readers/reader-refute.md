## 1. What counts against each guess

### H6: is there a chapter theorem that places the two ways?

There are four places where a chapter states, as a theorem with an "exactly where", where a course that is being used up can still be turned and where it cannot. None is on a model with both a line that multiplies or a rule set again, and a level that is needed. So H6 holds to the letter, and only because of its last clause.

**(a) 02 §11, `TurningInTime.turns_in_time_iff` and `turns_in_time_iff_before` (02:508).** Model: a configuration is a way (any type) and a whole-number level; the level steps by §5's regime step under what the way uses; there is a next only while the level meets a need. Premises as the chapter gives them: marks decided; the way after an unmarked step is `alone` of it (`AloneOutside`, stated one paragraph up); the way after a marked step of a kind every step keeps and that uses `H₂`; §5's premises for `H₂` up to `hi`; a positive need at most `H₂ + y₂`; the course as it was starting at most `hi` and below `H₁ + y₁`, giving short of `H₁` below `y₁`, every way on `alone`'s orbit using at least `H₁`. Conclusion: "the line never ends exactly where there is such a step `T` and the level of the course as it was at `T + 1` is at or above `H₂ + y₂`", and (`_before`) "exactly where that step comes before the point of no return of the course as it was". I opened both statements: the chapter says what they state. The thing used up (the level) and the turn are in one model. What is not in it: the marks, the need and the kept kind are given (02:523, "nothing here says how what brings a level's difference to a way comes to stand"); nothing multiplies ("Units only end; nothing builds one"); the way that goes on does not remake anything, the stock's giving law does.

**(b) 02 §11, the lock in that model (02:510).** `TurningInTime.window_is_pair`: §10's course, regrouped, is a course of the pair with the losses in `[N, K)` marked, at a need of nothing (`net_zero`), so the "level" there is the loss, which rises and is needed by nothing. `TurningInTime.locked_ends_with_net`: with the lock's step as the way's step and a positive need, a locked start "ends the first way, at a configuration that conditions nothing, still locked"; I opened it: the level's fall is a premise on the level's course (`renews`, `over`), not derived from the share. The chapter says so itself: "Being locked does not rule out a turn at a marked level", and 02:525, "neither theorem is derived from the other". So the thing locked and the thing used up stand in one carrier, with both as premises. No chapter theorem states the "exactly where" of (a) for a lock.

**(c) 02 §9, `ReadInTime.kept_early_iff`, `turned_early_iff`, `at_most_one_between`, `deadline` (02:433).** Model: counts of two kinds that leave offspring (`a + s + p` and `a + c + p`), the rule set again as the pool of readers drawn from the selected; a loss that grows by one a step and is needed by nothing. This is a rule set again and lines that multiply, and a placing of "can still be turned" against "can no longer"; nothing in it is rested on or used up. (Opened: the statement of `deadline`, the docstrings of `kept_early_iff` and `turned_early_iff`; `at_most_one_between` is the chapter's telling only.)

**(d) 05 §7, the optimum's cases (05:209, 05:221 to 229; read in part).** `Web.course_lost_iff`: on the World, a part re-optimizing at every step loses for good a dependence of another formation on a condition beyond its family "exactly where `r < k`, and never where `k ≤ r`". `Optimum.reread_kept_iff`, `reread_deadline_iff`, `gated_stays_below`: a re-reading from step `T` keeps the need "exactly where it is met at `T`", that is where the level starts at least `T (k − r)` above the need, and under the gate "a later re-reading restores nothing". `Optimum.Coupled.drains_below` and `kept_above` (05:209): among two or more drawers in turn, every perfect profile leaves nothing where `c < m`, and under the gate keeps exactly the need at every step to the horizon where `m < c`. These have a selection in them (an optimum, best responses), a base that others rest on, an exact line between using up and keeping, and a deadline for the turn. They have no lock, no multiplying line, a finite horizon for the drawers, and the re-reading is handed in.

Also a witness: 07 §3.6's `FourReaders` (07:269), a habitat with a renewer that needs it, a dweller and a clearer; "the cleared course is recoverable exactly while its level stands at that point or above" (`recoverable_iff_regime`, statement opened).

## 4. My judgment

"We are far from it" is about right for the whole of what is wanted: no model has the taking and the closing as outcomes of one course, with a line that multiplies and a way that itself remakes what it rests on. The paragraph under it is too modest in one respect and lopsided: it gives its length to the web's two classes and says no statement was found, while the account has exact statements of where a course being used up can still be turned (02 §11, 05 §7), of where a lock can still be left (02 §9 and §10), and one in the Lean with a lock on a needed level. The sentence it most needs to change is "The readers found no statement that places the two ways with respect to each other where a line multiplies or a rule is set again." It should say what there is and what each lacks: the turn placed exactly for a level under a draw, the leaving placed exactly for a rule set again, the two together only for a lock that already overdraws and with the reading handed in.
