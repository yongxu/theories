## S3 as I coded it

The step is `lock_on_units.py`'s `Model.step` for any number of kinds and rules. A way is a tuple of counts, one per kind; every predicate used (`Goes`, `Holds`, `taken`, and my shares) is blind to the order of a list. Units whose need the level meets leave their kids under the rule in force; the rule is set again from the share of the way present, before the step, at every step; every unit present takes; the level steps by `z + giving z` with `z = max 0 (x - taken)`.

| Lean notion | How it is coded |
|---|---|
| `Goes need (w, x)` | some unit present has need at most the level |
| never ends | by `stepWhile_unending_iff`, `Goes` at every step of the orbit. With every step reading, the step is a function of (way, rule, level), so a repeated (way, rule, level) with `Goes` at every step up to it is a never-ending course, and a step without `Goes` is an end |
| `Regime giving use y hi w`, `T = taken use w` | `Short giving T (y T)`: giving below `T` at every `z < y T`. `Meets giving T (y T) hi`: `T ≤ giving z` at every `z ≥ y T` with `z + T ≤ hi`. `Keeps giving T hi`: `step giving T x ≤ hi` for `x ≤ hi`. `y` is a given function, so I asked which `y T` can exist. Either some `z ≤ hi - T` has `giving z ≥ T`, and from the least such `z` (call it `y*`) to `hi - T` giving stays at or above `T`: then `y T = y*` is forced. Or no `z ≤ hi - T` has `giving z ≥ T`: then any admissible `y T` is above `hi - T`. Otherwise no `y T` exists and `Regime` fails at that take. The program checks this reduction against a brute force over `y` for every giving used |
| `Holds need use y (w, x)` | `w` not empty, every need of `w` met at `x`, and `T + y T ≤ x`: coded as `T + y* ≤ x` in the first case and false in the second (levels stay within `hi`, which the walk keeps by `Keeps`) |
| `along` | `Regime` at the way of step `k`, for every `k` with `Goes` at every step `j ≤ k` |
| `Locked M favours h (share w, g)` | `favours g` and `M.le g (h (share w) g)` |
| the reversed lock | not `favours g`, and `M.le (h (share w) g) g` |
| the right side | there is `k`, `Goes` at every step before `k`, with `Holds` and the reversed lock at step `k` |
| `M` | a rank on the rules: `M.le g g'` iff `rank g ≤ rank g'`. Two rules of one rank are each not above the other and are still two rules with their own kids, which is how a reading can depend on the rule in force and still meet `ReadsShare` |

`ReadsShare` forces this last point. Taking `x = y` in `∀ x y g g', P.le x y → M.le (h x g) (h y g')` gives `M.le (h x g) (h x g')` both ways. So where `M` is antisymmetric a reading that meets `ReadsShare` cannot depend on the rule in force at all; it can only where two rules are each not above the other.

Premises checked on each instance, none assumed from how the instance was made: `needs` (every kind needs something), `down` under every rule, `grows` under each favouring rule, `even` and `some` under each rule that does not favour, `nothing`, `keeps` (`Keeps giving 0 hi`), `upper`, `monotone` (`ReadsShare`, on every pair of shares of grid ways and every pair of rules), `lowers` (on every grid way). Per start: `high` (levels run from 0 to `hi`) and `along`. `every` is built in. The grid is every way that takes at most `hi`; no other way is on a course at a step followed by a step with `Goes`. So `monotone`, `lowers` and `raises` are checked on a finite grid, not for all ways: an instance could pass here and fail on a larger way that no course reaches.

`raises` is not a premise of the equivalence. S3's last line asks it for `k ≥ n + 2`, and the program checks it on the grid too.

### Without `lowers`

Instances that meet every premise but `lowers` were run in every family: 20,885,343 starts. 866,318 of them end although the right side holds (A 265,160, B 208,188, C 124,166, D 205,246, E 6,060, F 2,667, G 54,831). None never ends with the right side failing. So in these worlds `lowers` does work only from right to left: it is what keeps a turned rule turned.

The smallest course, shrunk by hand and run by the program. Two kinds F and O, each needing one and taking one. Under the rule that does not favour, F leaves F and O leaves an F. Under the favouring rule each leaves two of itself. The share is the number of F, read as favouring from one up. The tent with `hi = 2`. One O at level two, under the rule that does not favour:

```
step 0  way (0 F, 1 O)  rule 0-  level 2  share 0  read 0  takes 1  [Holds, reversed lock]
step 1  way (1 F, 0 O)  rule 0-  level 2  share 1  read 1  takes 1  [Holds]
step 2  way (1 F, 0 O)  rule 1+  level 2  share 1  read 1  takes 1  [Holds, Locked]
step 3  way (2 F, 0 O)  rule 1+  level 2  share 2  read 1  takes 2  [Locked]
step 4  way (4 F, 0 O)  rule 1+  level 0  share 4  read 1  takes 4  [no need met]   -> ends
```

The right side holds at step 0. The O leaves an F, the share rises under a rule that does not favour, the reading turns the rule to favouring, and the way grows to its end. Every premise but `lowers` is met (the program says so). The premise that removes it is `lowers`: with O leaving an O instead, the same start never ends and the right side holds.

On paper: from left to right `lowers` is not needed where the rules are finitely many. After the last favouring rule, the reversed lock can fail at a step only if the rule read is above the rule in force, and the rank cannot rise forever; `Holds`, once reached under a constant take, is kept. The proposal's sketch uses `lowers` in step 3 of that half. For any type of rules, as the Lean would have it, `lowers` or a well-founded order would be needed there. This is an argument, not a proof, and the search agrees with it (no never-ending course without the right side in the 20.9 million starts above).

### The sentence "In words"

S3's right side asks `Holds` and the reversed lock at *some* step. The proposal's gloss says more: "goes on without end only if the rule is turned and stays turned, and the level left when it is turned is at or above the point of no return of what is then taken."

In 7,923 never-ending courses (B 225, C 417, D 270, G 1,109, H 5,902) the rule favours at some step, and at the first step from which no rule favours again the level is below the point of no return of what is then taken, or that take has no such point within `hi`. The level goes on falling under the turned rule until a need is unmet, units are lost, and what is left holds. One of them (H needs 6 and takes 3, a top-up of 3 capped at 12, share all units, favouring from four up):

```
step 0  way (1 H)        rule 1+  level 6  share 1  read 0  takes 3  [Holds]
step 1  way (1 H, 1 L1)  rule 0-  level 6  share 2  read 0  takes 4  [reversed lock]
step 2  way (1 H, 1 L1)  rule 0-  level 5  share 2  read 0  takes 4  [a need unmet, reversed lock]
step 3  way (1 L1)       rule 0-  level 4  share 1  read 0  takes 1  [Holds, reversed lock]
...                                                                                   -> never ends
```

The rule is turned at step 1 and stays turned. What is then taken is 4, the top-up gives 3, and no level holds under a take of 4. S3 itself is right here: `k = 3`.

## Findings

- **MUST 1. The gloss under S3 says more than S3, and is false as worded.** "The level left when it is turned is at or above the point of no return of what is then taken" fails in 7,923 never-ending courses of this search (course above). What S3 states, and what the search bears out, is that at some step from which the rule stays turned the configuration holds; between the turn and that step the fall can go on and remove units. A telling of S3 should say that.
- **MUST 2. The proposal's count for S3 is too large.** "The answer in short" (item 3) and its findings say S3 was tested on about 9.2 million starts. Its third family (3,791,256 starts) fails S3's premise `grows` as stated: F and H leave themselves under the favouring rule (my program checks this), and it was run against a variant whose right side "also allows a holding way with no L left", which is nowhere written as a statement. Section 7 says so; the summary lines do not. S3 as stated was run by its proposer on 5,394,579 starts, with never-ending `Locked` starts only in the first family.
- **LESSER. No disagreement with S3 as stated** in 45,272,833 starts of 101,064 instances meeting every premise, in worlds (a) to (e). Not a proof.
- **LESSER. `lowers` is needed, and only from right to left.** Without it 866,318 starts end although the right side holds; the smallest course has one unit and `hi = 2`. No course without it never ends against the right side. The proposal's sketch cites `lowers` for the left-to-right half, where on paper finitely many rules would do.
- **LESSER. Every other premise does work but `keeps`.** `needs`, `down`, `grows`, `even`, `some`, `nothing`, `upper`, `monotone` and `along` each have a disagreement when missing alone. `keeps` showed none in 65,380 starts; `along` asks `Keeps` at each take met.
- **LESSER. The gloss's second sentence needs `raises`.** "For a locked start the turn can come only through a need the fallen level no longer meets" fails in 27,909 never-ending courses from `Locked` starts where `raises` fails. S3's formal last line does ask `raises`, and with it `k ≥ n + 2` held in all 96,041 courses where it applies.
- **LESSER. S3's own case is rare here.** A never-ending course from a `Locked` start with `raises` and every need met at the start occurred 162 times, only in the family made to find it, and never under the tent. In the other six families all 809 never-ending `Locked` starts with `raises` had a need unmet at the start. This agrees with the proposal's "fact against the proposal's reach" and sharpens it: of its 31,089 never-ending `Locked` starts, the proposal does not say how many met every need at the start.
- **LESSER. A reading that meets `ReadsShare` cannot depend on the rule in force where `M` is antisymmetric.** It can only pick among rules each not above the other. The proposal's model takes `h : A → R → R` from `RuleCapture`; the second argument does nothing in any instance over a partial order.
- **LESSER. "With the rule held favouring the course ends" is `way_ends`**, so that count is the count of never-ending courses from favouring starts and tells nothing more.
