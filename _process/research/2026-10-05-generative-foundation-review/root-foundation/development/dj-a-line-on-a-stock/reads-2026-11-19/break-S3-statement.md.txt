## 2. Is the right side well formed?

Yes, once written out. Three places where the proposal's words are loose.

**"`Locked` with both orders reversed."** `Locked` takes `M`, `favours`, `h` and a pair; it does not mention `P`. The last two conjuncts are exactly

`RuleCapture.Locked (RuleCapture.reverse M) (fun g => ¬ favours g) (fun w g => h (share w) g) (w_k, g_k)`.

So one order is reversed in `Locked`, and the predicate is replaced by its negation. The order on shares is reversed only in the premises of `turn_locked`.

**Kept by a step at which every need is met: yes.** Take `S := List U` with the order `P.le (share w) (share w')`, and `f w g := allKids (kids g) w`. `turn_locked` at `reverse` of that order, `reverse M` and `¬ favours` asks three things:

- `Raises`: `∀ w g, ¬ favours g → P.le (share (f w g)) (share w)`. This is `lowers`.
- `ReadsShare` for the two reversed orders. This is `monotone` by `ReadsShare.reverse`, pulled back along `share`.
- in the place of `upper`: `M.le g' g → ¬ favours g → ¬ favours g'`. This is the contrapositive of `upper`. No new premise is needed.

Where every need is met, the way after the step is `f w g` (`after_of_met` at `kids g`), so the model's step is `Pace.turn f _ true`, and `turn_locked` keeps the reversed lock. At a step with an unmet need nothing keeps it, and neither half of S3 needs that.

**"The form of `proportional_locks_down`."** Close, not the same. That theorem uses `CarrierCounts.disfavours` (`carrier ≤ other`), a predicate of its own that overlaps `favours` at equal counts, with its own lemma `lower`. S3 uses `¬ favours`, for which `lower` is `upper` read backward.

**A simpler right side says the same.** Under S3's premises, "`Holds` at `k`, `¬ favours g_k`, and `¬ favours (h (share w_k) g_k)`" for some `k` is equivalent to the right side as stated. The stated form gives it by `upper`. The other way, `lowers` and `monotone` give the reversed lock at `k + 1`, where the configuration still holds. So the order on rules need not appear in the conclusion: the way holds, and neither the rule in force nor the rule read off the present share favours. (On paper; no disagreement in the first search of section 7.)

**Two more notes on the premises.** `ReadsShare` at `x = y` gives `M.le (h x g) (h x g')` for all `g`, `g'`. So the rule read is a function of the share alone, up to equivalence in `M`: the reading keeps no memory of the rule in force. And `needs` must be said of some set of units. For `unending_iff_holds` to be a case it cannot be "every unit of the type"; it must be `Needing`'s form: every unit of every way reachable from `w` by `allKids (kids g)` under any rules. At one rule that is `Needing need kids w`, and `way_ends`'s `∀ u, 0 < need u` implies it.

## 4. The left-to-right half: where is the gap?

I found no false step. Read classically, the four steps hold. The answers to the doubts raised, then what the sketch leaves out.

**Step 1 (finitely many steps with an unmet need): holds.** A unit whose need is unmet leaves nothing (`after`), so it is not in the next way. By `mem_after_iff` every unit of the next way is a kid of a unit the level met, so by `down` it needs at most the level, which is below the unmet need. The largest need of the way never rises (`down` under every rule) and falls strictly at each such step. Units of lower need born later do not raise it. So there are at most as many such steps as the largest need at the start.

**Step 2 (finitely many favouring steps after that): holds.** After the last unmet step every way is all the kids of the one before. What it takes rises by at least the number of units under a favouring rule (`allKids_rises` at `kids g`; the way is not empty on a course that goes on) and is unchanged under one that does not favour (`taken_allKids`). It is below the level (`taken_below`), and the level is at most `hi` at every step (`kept_within` from `keeps`, or `within_along`). The level can rise, but not above `hi`, so the bound is real. The rule cannot go back and forth for ever: each return to a favouring rule costs at least one of at most `hi`.

**Step 3 (the reversed lock one step after the last favouring rule): holds.** Let every rule from `T` on not favour, every need met from `T` on. Then `g_{T+1} = h (share w_T) g_T`, and `share w_{T+1}` is not above `share w_T` (`lowers`), so by `monotone` the rule read off `share w_{T+1}` is not above `g_{T+1}`. The case the task raises, a share that stops moving while its reading favours, does happen: the rule then alternates (the proposal's course with `θ = 4`). It is excluded on a course that never ends, and by step 2, not by any premise on the reading. On such a course there is a last favouring rule, and the lock is found after it.

**Step 4: what `reaches_holds` asks.** `down`; `Needing` of the start's way; `nothing`; `along` (only `Short` is used, but the full `Regime` is the premise); and `all`: some need met at every step. Nothing about what the kids take, no `keeps`, no `high`. It returns some step that holds, with no bound on it.

**What the sketch leaves out.**

1. `reaches_holds` must be applied to the course from the step where the reversed lock is found, not from the start. From the start it may return a holding step under a favouring rule, which is not S3's right side. The proposal's "from there" can be read this way; it should be said.
2. "Finitely many, so a last one" is classical. In the style of the files read, the proof has to be one induction: on a bound on the needs (as `reaches_from`), then on what the cap leaves above the take (as `ends_high`), then on the level (as `reaches_from`), carrying the reversed lock. `reaches_holds` cannot then be used as it stands: when the lock is first found one does not know that no need will be unmet later, and an unmet need can break the lock. So step 4 is also proved afresh. This is more than "steps 1 to 3 and the assembly".
3. That induction must decide whether the rule in force favours. `favours : R → Prop` needs `DecidablePred`.

None of these makes the statement false.

## 5. The right-to-left half, through the tagging

**Is the tagged world a `LineOnAStock` world?** Yes. `U' := U × Nat`, `kids' (u, j) := (kids g_j u)` tagged `j + 1`, `need'` and `use'` by the first part. The rules `g_j` come from the model's own course, which is defined without the tagged world, so there is no circle in the definition. `kids'` depends on the unit and its tag, not on the tag only. `down` carries over from `down` under every rule. The needs carry over if `needs` covers the descendants under the actual rules (section 2).

**Does "holds for ever" follow when every rule after `k` does not favour?** Yes. Tag from `k`. Then `even` and `some` hold of every tagged unit, and `unending_iff_holds` applies as it stands to the course from `(w_k, x_k)`, with `along` shifted and `x_k ≤ hi`.

**But that every rule after `k` does not favour is not given; it is to be proved.** It follows from the reversed lock only at steps where every need is met, and that every need is met is what the tagged world's `course_generation`, `generation_below` and `RegimeMargin.holds` are to give. Two consequences for the route:

- `even : ∀ u'` cannot be supplied before the induction is done. Either the two inductions run as one, or the tagged world is given default kids (`[(u, j + 1)]` where `g_j` favours), which again needs `favours` decidable.
- The statement of `unending_iff_holds` is not enough. It says some need is met at every step; the lock needs every need met. So the route goes through its pieces (`course_generation`, `generation_below`, `RegimeMargin.holds`), which the proposal names, and not through the theorem.

On paper the half holds: by induction on `i`, at step `k + i` the reversed lock holds, the take is the take at `k`, the level is the orbit of the step under that one draw, which never falls below `x_k` (`RegimeMargin.holds`), every need is at most `x_k`, and the way is not empty.

**"For a `Locked` start, any such `k` is at least `n + 2`": holds on paper.** Steps before the first unmet need at `n` are steps of `turn` with `f := allKids (kids g)`; `turn_locked` (with `raises`) keeps `Locked` up to step `n`, so `g_n` favours; and `Locked` at `n` with `upper` makes `g_{n+1}` favour.

## 6. Do the stock, the lock and the needs each do work?

Plainly: **the stock and the needs do; the lock does not do the joining.**

The equivalence holds with the reading exchanged for any sequence of rules `g_0, g_1, …`, in both halves, if the right side reads: *there is a step `k`, some need met at every step before it, at which the configuration holds and from which no rule favours.* Left to right is steps 1, 2 and 4 above; step 3 is not needed. Right to left is `unending_iff_holds` on units tagged from `k`, as it stands. The proposal's section 6 grants this for the left-to-right half only. Call this statement S3′.

S3′ in turn is a statement about `LineOnAStock` alone, with no rule: units of a decidable kind whose kids take more, all other units' kids taking the same and not none, `down`, the needs, the stock. The net never ends exactly where the course comes to a holding configuration none of whose descendants is of the growing kind. `way_ends` is the case where every unit grows; `unending_iff_holds` the case where none does. (Argued on paper as above; searched small, section 7. I do not propose it; it shows what S3 joins.)

So S3 is S3′ and one lemma. The lemma is `turn_locked` with the orders reversed: under `lowers`, `monotone`, `upper` and a reading at every step, "holds, and from here no rule favours" is the same as "holds, and here the reversed lock holds". Each lock premise is needed for that lemma: with any one of `lowers`, `monotone`, `upper` dropped, small worlds disagree with S3 (section 7), and never with S3′'s left-to-right half.

What follows:

- The two old theorems are cases of the part of S3 that has no lock in it. At one rule the lock's premises are trivial and are not exercised.
- The new mathematics in S3 is the mixed induction of steps 1 and 2 joined to `reaches_from`'s. It is a join of `way_ends` and `unending_iff_holds`, in the proposal's first sense only: nothing retires if it is proved on top of them.
- The lock's part of S3 is a test at one configuration for a clause about the whole future. It is small and it is already in the Lean as `turn_locked`; `proportional_locks_down` is an instance of the same reversal.
- That the fallen level reaches the rule through who is left is not in S3's equivalence. It is in the last sentence only (`k ≥ n + 2`), which is `turn_locked` on steps where every need is met.

By the proposal's own second test in its section 6, then, S3 as a statement about a lock is S3′ with a certificate.
