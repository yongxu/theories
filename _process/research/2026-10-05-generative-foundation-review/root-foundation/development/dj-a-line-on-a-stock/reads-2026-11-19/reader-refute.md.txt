## (2) K2: does the lock do work?

**A pair in one model, differing only in the start's share, exists** (`refute.py k2`, `k2p`; bound 8, needs 1, 3, 5, `Raises` asked of every way):

- Count share, threshold 2, needs (1, 1), uses (1, 1). Under the favouring rule F leaves one F and one O, O leaves two O; under the other each leaves itself.
  - Locked start, two F, level 6: (2, 0, level 6), (2, 2, 8), (2, 6, 8), (2, 14, 0). It ends.
  - Not locked, one F and one O, level 6, the favouring rule in force: (1, 1, 6), (1, 3, rule not favouring, 8), and the same again. It never ends.
  - Same number of units, same take, same level; one F is an O.
- Proportion share, threshold one half, F and O alike in need, use and number of offspring (each leaves two of its kind under the favouring rule).
  - One F at level 3: (1, 0, 3), (2, 0, 4), (4, 0, 4), (8, 0, 0). It ends.
  - One O at level 3: (0, 1, 3), (0, 2, rule not favouring, 4), and the same again. It never ends.
  - The two starts differ in nothing but which kind the share counts.

Counts: with a count share, 52,560 locked starts that end, 16,403 with such a start beside them, 27 where the start beside takes at least as much; with a proportion, 15,504, 3,749 and 671.

**The stronger question: is the ending owed to the lock's premises?** Yes (`refute.py drop`). With every other premise of L-b in place:

- Without `Raises`: F and O each leave two O under the favouring rule. From one F, locked, level 4: (1, 0, 4), (0, 2, 6), (0, 4, rule not favouring, 8), and the same again. No need is ever unmet. 30 such in a small range.
- Without a monotone reading (favouring only where the share is exactly 1): F leaves two F. From one F: (1, 0, 4), (2, 0, 6), (4, 0, rule not favouring, 8), and the same again. 175 such.

**What this does and does not show.** The lock does work in L-b in exactly one place: it keeps the rule among those under which the units multiply. That the units multiply under a favouring rule and not under the other is how every model here is built (`kids 0` is "each leaves itself"). The lock does not make what is favoured draw more.

## (3) K3: is `Raises` for a share of units derived or assumed?

For the step at which every need is met (all the kids), it follows from something plainly about offspring. By brute force over all two-kind offspring with entries 0 to 3 and ways with entries 0 to 7 (`refute.py k3`), the two conditions agree in all 256 cases each:

- **A count** (the number of F): `Raises` holds at every way exactly where each F leaves at least one F.
- **A proportion**: exactly where each F leaves only F, at least one, and no fewer than an O leaves O. This is `RuleCapture.CarrierCounts.share_not_lower_counts`' comparison, with offspring of the favoured kind favoured.

Neither is as strong as what the lock concludes, so K3 as worded does not bite. Two limits do:

- **It is only for the step with every need met.** At a level below a favoured unit's need that unit leaves nothing and the count falls: (1 F, 1 O, level 4) with F needing 5 goes to (0, 1). So `Raises` is false of the model's own step, and L-a can say "locked" only up to the first unmet need. This is the same shape as `locked_ends_rising`'s `AloneOutside`, not an improvement on it.
- **For a proportion, `Raises` says nothing about the draw.** `ShareRises` (read whole): from `([C, O], 12)`, C leaves itself and O nothing; C's part of what is taken goes from 2 of 4 to 2 of 2, the units from two to one, what the way takes from 4 to 2, the level stays 12 and the net never ends. There is no rule or reading in it. It meets the proportion's condition above. So a share of units can rise while the draw falls; `Raises` and "offspring take more" are two separate premises, and in the proportion run L-b rests wholly on the second.

**But in these the leaving of the lock does nothing.** In all 608, in 1,040 of the 1,100, and in every example `results-3.json` and `results-4.json` keep, O leaves itself under both rules. I ran each again with a reading that favours at every share, so that the rule never changes: the course is the same and never ends. What happened is that the F ended and the O went on; the rule was set again afterwards and nobody was there for it to matter to.

**Where the rule's change is what lets the rest go on.** For that O must leave different offspring under the two rules. In the first range I found 60, all from overdrawing starts. A second, narrower search over larger bounds (`refute.py turn`: 24, 32, 40) found 40 from starts that hold. One:

- Bound 32, needs (27, 1), uses (14, 1). Under the favouring rule F leaves one F and one O, O leaves two O; under the other each leaves itself. From one F at level 28, which holds (28 less 14 is 14, and 14 is given back).

- The same start read so that the rule stays favouring: … (0, 12, 24), (0, 24, 24), (0, 48, 0). It ends.

All 40 end when the rule is kept favouring. So there is a course on which the favoured units' own growth draws the level down from a start that held, their need goes unmet, the share falls, the rule set again stops favouring, and that is why what remains holds.

**The never-ending locked course** (F met, leaving each step an O whose need is never met) is not an artefact of the Python against the Lean. `LineOnAStock.KidsNeedMore` is the same course: A needs 4 and takes 2 and leaves B, A, B; B needs 9; from `([B, A, B], 8)` the step leaves the configuration as it is (`fixed`, by `rfl`). I put those numbers through `lock_on_units.py`'s step with A as F: fixed, locked, every premise but "offspring need no more". It is a feature of the convention both share: every unit present takes, and an unmet unit leaves nothing. The Lean keeps it as the countermodel showing `way_ends` needs `down`; `KidHigherNeed` is its one-step cousin for `need_unmet`.

**One thing that follows, argued and not a Lean statement.** With one favouring rule, every unit's offspring taking more and needing no more: a course on which the rule favours at every step is `LineOnAStock`'s course under that rule's offspring, and `way_ends` ends it. So a line that never ends has left the favouring rule. No search found otherwise (`results-2`, `results-3`, mine).

## (6) L-d

- **The added premise is the Lean's.** `two_units_iff` carries `needs : 0 < n` and `within : n ≤ H₂ + y₂`; `unending_iff_above` carries `needs : 0 < need` and `within : need ≤ H₂ + y₂`.
- **Another form needs no added premise.** In each of the 3,648 differences O's need is met at the first level below F's need and unmet one step later (argued from the tent: with the right side true and O's need met one step later, the level under what the O take never falls; and so on every instance I ran). Stated as "the configuration one step later holds" (O present, its need met there, the level at or above its point of no return), which is `unending_iff_holds`' shape, the two sides agreed on all 2,548,800 instances I ran (bounds 12 and 16, F's use 1 to 6, O's 1 to 3), with nothing asked of O's need against its point of no return.
- **Is it `two_units_iff` after the favoured kind has gone?** Not `two_units_iff` itself: that law has a first unit taking one fixed amount and a start already below the point of no return of what both take. Here F multiplies, may leave O (so what the rest takes is not known beforehand), and the start may hold. What L-d is: L-b up to the first level below F's need; F gone one step later (nothing leaves an F); then `unending_iff_above`, or `unending_iff_holds` on the O alone. Three known pieces in a row.
- **Worth stating?** Only as a corollary. `LineOnAStock`'s account says it has no exact condition for a way whose offspring take more; L-d would be one, in a narrow shape (the growers share the greatest need, nothing else leaves them, the rest leave themselves). If stated, in the "holds" form.

## (7) Verdict

**Worth writing in Lean, if anything:** not L-b for its own sake, which is `need_unmet` composed with `locked_stays`. What the model has that the Lean lacks is the other side: the fall of the level making a difference to the rule, through the favoured units' unmet need and the share. `TurningInTime` gives its marked levels and its kept kind; chapter 2 section 11 says so under "What is given" ("the marks of a way that conditions on a reading are not" derived). Here the mark would be a unit's need. The smallest honest landing I can see is the model, L-a as an instance of `turn_locked`, L-b as a short corollary, the statement of (4) (a line that never ends has left the favouring rule), and the bound-32 course kept beside the same start with the rule held favouring. No exact condition for when it turns in time is in hand, and I would not promise one.

**What the writer should be careful not to say:**

- that the use rising with the share has come out as a conclusion (finding 3);
- that the pairs in `results-count.json` meet the lock's premise as stated (finding 1);
- that a lock "left" in `results-3.json`'s examples is the rule turning to any effect (finding 6);
- that `locked_ends_rising` becomes a case (finding 4);
- that `Raises` holds of the units' own step: it holds of the step with every need met;
- that L-a and L-b are shown beyond what was run. My wider search is still a search.

**Is leaving a lock because the favoured units ran out a turn in section 11's sense?** Section 11 has two. For a way of units it already calls the ending of a unit that takes, while another goes on, "a turn with no mark given" (`WayOfUnits.ends_while_net_goes`, as the chapter names it; I found the name in the Lean and did not read its statement). In that sense yes, but then the lock adds nothing: it is that turn, and in the examples the result files keep, the rule's change afterwards is idle. In the other sense (the way after a marked step is of a kind that is kept, and the line goes on exactly where enough is left) it is a turn only where what remains leaves different offspring under the two rules. The bound-32 course is one such, so the model can hold it. In the examples kept so far it is only an ending of some units.
