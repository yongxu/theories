## The three questions put

**In chapter 2 §9's count models, is there a stock or level?** No. Each model is two counts, carriers and others, each multiplied at a step by what the rule gives its kind, and a rule read from those counts. The only other coordinate is a "loss", a natural number that rises by at least one a step (`ClosedCapture.Accrues`) and that the loop does not read, or that some readers read. Nothing needs it and the step is total. `ChoiceCountCapture`'s first part has a web's levels, in a selection theorem the chapter calls near-definitional, with no counts in it. Chapter 2 §11 says the same of §9 and §10 in its opening: "their step is total, and nothing in them needs what they use."

**In `TurningInTime`'s lock on a level, is anything a unit with a need of its own?** No. The way is `A × R × Nat`: a share of any type, a rule and a step count. The net has one `need : Nat`. In `count_rises` the share is a natural number. `Three.rise_count_ends`'s docstring speaks of "one use per drawer", but the drawers are that number: `drawerUse v = v.1`.

**In `RuleRebuilding`, what is rebuilt from what, and does anything multiply?** The rule is rebuilt from its members. Taught by members, a cohort enters holding only where holders of at least the teaching age stand, and each such holder turns away a fixed number of those who would come untaught; a revision of the quota takes only where a holder stands. Nothing multiplies. The holders who enter are the quota whatever the number of teachers, so long as there is one; cohorts leave after the tenure; the others who enter fall as the teachers rise.

## Findings

1. **MUST (small). "The count multiplies" says more than the Lean.** The frozen file, where it tells `locked_ends_rising` with `count_rises`: "There the rule is set again from the share, the count multiplies, and the going on needs the level." The premise is a strict rise of a natural number under every favouring rule. Nothing in the statement is offspring or multiplication, and in the one worked course (`Three.rise_count_ends`) the share goes three, four, five, to ten. The sentence before it in the frozen file has it right ("a count that every favouring rule raises strictly").

2. **LESSER, and it bears on K5 as much as K6. In the model the programs run, L-a and L-b up to the first unmet need are two theorems the Lean already has.** All four programs have two rules, of which one favours, and a threshold reading. From a locked start with every need met, the rule in force is the favouring one, the reading of the share gives it again, and `Raises` keeps the share from falling, so the rule stays the same at every step until a need is unmet (`RuleCapture.turn_locked`). Over that stretch the course is `LineOnAStock`'s course with `kids` the offspring under that one rule, and under the program's premise `grows_all` the first unmet need within the bound plus one is `need_unmet`, with `falls_to_unmet` where kids need no more. So "L-a and L-b failed on no instance" could not have come out otherwise on these instances, unless the program differed from the Lean's forms. This is argued from the two statements, not checked in Lean. L-b says more than `need_unmet` only where the rule in force can change while the lock holds, which takes at least two favouring rules with different offspring; no program searched that. Under `grows_F_only` it is not `need_unmet` (which asks growth of every unit) but the same lemma, `rising_takes_all`.

3. **LESSER. After the favoured kind ends, the two-kind course is again a line on a stock under one rule.** In the programs' model every unit of the favoured kind has one need. Where the first unmet need is theirs, they all end at one step and the share is nothing; where the other kind leaves none of the favoured kind, one step later the rule is the other one and stays so. What is left is `LineOnAStock` (or `WayOfUnits` where the rest leave themselves) from that configuration. This is argued from the program's step, not run. So what the paper adds over the Lean lies at the junction: that the first stretch is driven to it by the lock, and what the level is when the rest take over. I did not read the results for L-c or L-d.

4. **LESSER. `PoolFromCourse.Borne` should be named beside L-c.** The frozen file's K6 names only §9. §10 has a worked course on which the favoured kind bears what the course costs, its share falls while the rule in force still favours it, and the lock is left with nothing reading the loss (`Borne.shapers_turned`, `Borne.shapers_left`). It differs as the table says, and it is not the paper's model. But "a lock left by its own overdraw" is new only in that the cost is a level the units need and take from, not in that a lock can be left this way.

5. **LESSER. "What would become a case" is narrower than it reads for `locked_ends_rising`.** That law is for a share of any type and any use that rises under the lock's step, and `count_rises` for any number that rises strictly. With one kind of unit the number of units is multiplied at each step, so the Lean's own worked course, which adds one a step, is not a one-kind case. L-b with one kind would give some instances of what the older law concludes, not the law.

6. **LESSER. The frozen file says the finding "corrects the page I landed yesterday"; as worded, the page's sentence is not false.** "Units that multiply, a rule set again, and a level they need" is not met by `locked_ends_rising`, which has no units. What the page lacks is the mention: neither `locked_ends_rising` nor `count_rises` is named anywhere on it (searched by name), and the sentence just before, "the lock keeps an overdraw that stands and does not bring one about", is true of the two laws it names and not of this one, which asks no overdraw at the start.

7. **LESSER. The writer's count of 13 theorems was not checked.** At the level of files, `TurningInTime` is the only file found that names `Locked` or `ReadsShare` and has a needed level, and no file that names `LineOnAStock` or `WayOfUnits` names the lock outside a docstring. Chapter 2 §9 has the first two and nothing rested on; §11 has a level that is needed, and a lock on it only with the level's fall handed in; chapter 5's course has a level that another needs and neither of the other two.":

The nearest is in the Lean and in no chapter (the plan's item 5): a locked way whose share is one number that every favouring rule raises, and whose use is that number times a fixed amount, on a level its going on needs and that cannot pass a bound, ends, still locked, within that bound plus one steps, with no overdraw asked at the start (`TurningInTime.locked_ends_rising` with `count_rises`). And in the paragraph before it, after the two laws named from the plan's items 1 and 2, "the lock keeps an overdraw that stands and does not bring one about" should be said of those two only.
