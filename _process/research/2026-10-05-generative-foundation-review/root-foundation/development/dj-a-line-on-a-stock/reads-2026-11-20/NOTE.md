## The question rested on a false sentence, and three more were wrong

- *"Every law of November is about one course as it runs."* False. `Becoming.Recoverable` and `NoReturnAt` (in `ReadOffInTime.lean`, first committed 10 November) say when some feasible course agreeing with this one so far still keeps what is asked; the feasible courses are a premise there. `TurningInTime` (13 November: `recoverable_iff_above`, `noReturnAt_iff_first_below`, `turns_in_time_iff_before` in its section 7, `Three.noReturn` in section 8) takes them to be the turns to one standing use, held, under its premises on the course, the stock and the need, and chapter 2 §11 tells those four; `FourReaders.noReturn`, among four stops, is November's too. Two of my readers found this independently. I had `Three.noReturn` open before I froze the sentence, and I had said it to Ren that morning.
- *"Along a course that ends, each region is left at some step."* Not of the innermost: a configuration kept whatever the moves stays so along every course (`kernel_certificate`, `Kernel.run`), so a course that ends was never in it.
- *"K is within R, and R within Kp."* Needs a premise: every configuration reached has a move. It holds on my processes. The Lean has, with that premise, only that what is kept whatever the moves is kept for every number of steps by some moves (`GuardHorizon.kernel_viable`); no theorem from `Kernel` to `Keepable` was found.
- *"October's root has names for that"*, and *"two of October's notions"*: `Recoverable` is November's, and one reader lists five more notions in the Lean that say "some course keeps it".

One reader's judgment, which I take: with the first sentence false the try could not have come out as a change. The comparison I froze left out the point of no return, which November had; with it put in, the first test trips on the Lean's own cases, and on processes I made up nothing could count. So what follows is no result against the frame. It is the premise corrected, and what November's Lean does not have made narrower.
