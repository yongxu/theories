# The regions against the Lean's definitions

## Verdict

K and Kp are the Lean's `Kernel` and `Keepable` on the writer's process, with nothing added. R is not a notion the Lean has; it can be written with the Lean's `UniformPolicy` over a closed set, and it is a union of sets with no largest member kept by one choice. On `Three` the table adds nothing to what the Lean states: its two sets are the regime's two points (6 and 10), and "step 1" and "step 3" are one edge counted two ways, a relation `turns_in_time_iff_before` already carries. The join of `Keepable` and `Recoverable` needs a short argument and a premise the guess leaves out, and no existing theorem was found that would become a case of it: it is a remark. Two sentences of the frozen file are false against the Lean (findings 1 and 2).

## Findings

**1. MUST. "Every law of November is about one course as it runs" is false, and `Recoverable` is not October's.** `ReadOffInTime.lean` was first committed on 10 November 2026 (its head note dates it 9 and 10 November). `TurningInTime` section 7, committed 13 November, states of the course as it was and the turns open from it: `recoverable_iff_above` (recoverable at `N` exactly where `H + y ≤ X N`), `noReturnAt_iff_first_below`, `noReturn_exists`, `turns_in_time_iff_before` (the line never ends exactly where `T + 1 < N`), and `Three.noReturn`. Chapter 2 §11 tells these. So November already has "can it still be turned" for one class of other courses (one turn to a standing use, held). What November lacks is narrower: moves as a `Process`, more than one turn, and a choice quantified over. The frozen file's "The question" and G2 ("two of October's notions") say otherwise; G1 itself cites `noReturn`.

**2. MUST. "Along a course that ends, each region is left at some step" is false of K.** `kernel_certificate` makes the kernel closed under every step and `Kernel.run` keeps every run from it within the guard. A course that ends is therefore in K at no step: K is never left. Along an ending course the regions give at most two steps (the first outside R, the first outside Kp), not three: G6's "leaves K, then R, then Kp, then ends" has no first term, and the second blind reader's "each clock is the leaving of one region" cannot stand as worded. For the two that remain, the order of first exits is forced by the inclusions, as G6 says.

**3. LESSER. R can be entered again.** R is not closed under the course. With one reading for all, two rules, `s` going by rule 0 to `s'` and by rule 1 out, `s'` staying by rule 1 and going out by rule 0: `s'` is kept by one rule held, `s` by none, yet `s` is in Kp and its course passes into R at step 1. "The step at which R is left" means the first exit only.

**4. K and Kp: the Lean's, nothing added.** `Generative.Process` is any relation `step : State → Action → State → Prop` (`GenerativeCore.lean` line 23). The writer's process is `Viability.disturbed next (fun _ => True)` for a law `next`, the moves standing where that file says disturbances. `Kernel P g s` is `∀ t, Reach P s t → g t`; `never_leaves_iff_kernel` reads it as no sequence of moves leaving the guard, and `kernel_largest` makes it the largest set within the guard closed under every step, which is what the program computes. `Keepable P g s` is `∃ r : Run P, r.state 0 = s ∧ ∀ n, g (r.state n)`. The program computes it as the largest set with some successor inside; that this equals `Keepable` is `Onward.KeepsMoving.run` (finitely many configurations), a theorem and not an unfolding, and on an unbounded carrier it takes dependent choice (`Onward.runs_iff_dependentChoice`, by its docstring; statement not read).

The same in November's own terms, with no `Process`: `pair after left s` is `next s (after s.1 s.2)` by definition, so each `after` is one choice of the move from the whole configuration. By `Law.stepWhile_unending_iff` (used in the proofs read), K is "`«不穷»` for every `after`", Kp "for some `after`", R "for some `after` that goes through the reading". The second and third need a short argument I did not check in Lean. For one determined `after` the three coincide, which is why the try of 16 November could not part them.

**5. R in the Lean's terms.** For `read : S → O`:

`∃ K : S → Prop, K s ∧ (∀ x, K x → guard x) ∧ UniformPolicy read (fun x a => K x → Enabled P x a ∧ ∀ t, P.step x a t → K t)`

That is a `GuardHorizon.Closed` set holding `s` whose move is one function of the reading. On a determined process it is the writer's R (take K the course under the choice). Existing theorems then apply unchanged: `closed_viable` (kept at every horizon), `uniform_finer` (a finer reading gives a wider R), `uniform_policy_iff_fiber_feasible` and `uniform_policy_iff_common` (for a given K, and given choice over the readings, a choice exists exactly where each reading has one move good at every configuration of K giving it; the second also asks that every reading be given by some configuration). The Lean would have to add the definition, and for R within Kp a run from a closed set. I found no existing theorem that would become a case of it; by rule 1 it is then a name.

How the Lean's three notions differ from R:
- `UniformPolicy read (Admissible P guard n)`, the premise of `uniform_viable` and the second blind reader's wording, quantifies over every configuration of `S`. On any of the writer's processes with one configuration from which no move is admissible it is false for every reading. It is a property of a process, not a region of starts. The writer's rewording for one start is right to leave it.
- `Viable` has no reading in it. By `dViable_iff`, on a determined process it is "some `n` moves keep the guard": the horizon form of Kp.
- `Keeping.KeptFor` (chapter 3 §9, plans) is the Lean's keeping from readings over a horizon, with recall, from a class of starts. From one known start on a determined process the class stays one configuration or empty, so `KeptFor n` is Kp for `n` steps whatever is read.

**6. LESSER, but it changes what test 2 measures.** By the last point, the gap between R and Kp is the gap between a rule that answers the same reading the same way at every step and a plan. A sequence of rules fixed beforehand reads nothing and is in Kp. "Kept by some course and by no choice from what it reads" should be told as "by no fixed rule from the reading"; under the Lean's `KeptFor` the gap is empty from every single start, at every horizon.

**7. "The largest set kept by one choice from a reading" is not well defined.** For one choice the largest kept set exists (`kernel_largest` on the closed loop). Across choices there is none in general, and R is their union. In the program's own instance (bound 8, needs 1 and 1, uses 1 and 1, under the favouring rule F leaves itself and O nothing), `(1, 2, 0, 5)` is kept only by the favouring rule held and `(0, 1, 0, 8)` only by the other. This is the shape of `statewise_not_uniform`. The Lean says when one choice serves a given set (`uniform_policy_iff_fiber_feasible`, `uniform_policy_iff_common`) and nothing about a largest set. Sets kept under every move have a largest (`kernel_largest`); sets kept under some move are closed under union, but no "largest `Closed`" was found in `GuardHorizon.lean`, read whole.
