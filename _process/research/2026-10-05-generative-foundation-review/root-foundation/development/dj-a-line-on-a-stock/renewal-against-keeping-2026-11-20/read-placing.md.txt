### 2b. The keeping theorem

`renewal_keeping_theorem` (`R/RenewalCharacterization.lean` 703–716) [S]. Premises: `law : ChannelLaw Kind`; `U : List Kind` with `complete : ∀ k, k ∈ U`; `K : Kind → Prop`, any (no decidability). That the steady configuration steps to itself is the content (`steady_fixed`, `RenewalSufficiency.lean` 126). [P]
- Left to right: `kept_through_window_has_population` (486; I read its statement, not its proof body 496–609). No package law was found that concludes a property of a law's parameters from the existence of a run.

### 2c. Said plainly: is "not keepable at the start stays not keepable along any run" a law of the package for every process?

**No, in two ways.**

1. **Not found stated** for `Keepable`. "Keepable" occurs in one module of the package, `JointKeeping.lean` [S: search over all 406 files of `B/`], which I read whole: it has `start`, `mono`, `left`, `right`, `each`, the two `keepable_and_iff_of_kernel_*`, and witnesses; no law along a step or a run.

2. **As worded it is false of some process and guard [P].** Take the package's own `ChoicePoint.process` (`JointKeeping.lean` 174) and the guard `fun s => s = .left` (my guard, not in the file). `restRun .left rfl` (239) keeps it, so it is keepable at `left`. At `split` it fails, so by `Keepable.start` it is not keepable there. `leftRun` (204) starts at `split` and is at `left` from step 1. So a run's later configuration is keepable and its start is not. What holds of every process is [P]:

```
-- not written, not checked
theorem not_keepable_invariant (dead : Invariant P (fun s => ¬ g s)) :
    Invariant P (fun s => ¬ Keepable P g s)
-- proof idea: a step s → t with Keepable P g t; if g s then Run.cons gives Keepable P g s;
-- so ¬ g s, so ¬ g t by `dead`, against Keepable.start at t. No decision of g is used.
```

and then `Invariant.run` (`Frame.lean` 286) gives the renewal statement. The premise `dead` is, for the channel process, exactly `dead_stays_dead` (`RenewalConfigurations.lean` 64). The file's own proof decides `Live` (`Nat.decLe`, line 137); the general form does not need the decision.

3. **What the package does state for every process** [S]: for runs with no guard, `Onward.run_of_trace` (98) and `run_of_reach` (106): a run from where a course leads gives a run from the course's start; with `Onward.reach_of_run` (94), along any run `r`, a run from `r.state n` gives one from `r.state 0`. At `renewingProcess law` this is `renewability_not_generated` restricted to histories that are live up to step `n` [P]. Also `Run.possible_of_next` (`B/ReachableRanges.lean` 590, no premise): what is possible from the next configuration is possible from the present one, where `Reaching.Possible P adm s x := ∃ t, Reach P s t ∧ adm t x` (289); at `adm := fun c _ => Renewable law c` it says "can reach a renewable configuration" is not gained along a course, and the channel law turns that into "renewable" by `dead_stays_dead` [P].

So `renewable_iff_step` is a fact of every process and guard; `renewability_not_generated` is a fact of every process **whose guard, once lost, stays lost**.

**Could "a rule set again from a share" be a policy on births without a new model?** [P] No definition on either side would have to change: `Pace.turn f h` type-checks at `S := Config Kind`. But everything the lock's laws speak of would have to be supplied, and none of it is on either side:
1. a share: a preorder `P` on `Config Kind` (which tokens count as the carriers);
2. a rule type `R`, its preorder `M`, and `favours`;
3. `f : Config Kind → R → Config Kind`. The channel step is a relation, so this needs a map from a rule to `births`, a choice of the successor's reserve (the clause at `RenewalAccounting.lean` 144 is an inequality), and either a proof that the chosen `births` meet the support and balance clauses (143–145) at every configuration or a fall-back to `[]` (always a step: `History.idle`);
4. the reading `h : Config Kind → R → R`;
5. proofs of `Raises P favours f`, `ReadsShare P M h` and `upper`.

Items 1 to 4 are modelling choices and item 5 would be new theorems. What exists with no new object but a reading is the guard-keeping choice (`UniformPolicy read (Admissible (channelProcess law) Live n)`, for `Kind : Type`), which chooses for keeping `Live` on every successor (2d) and says nothing of a rule read off a share.

## Question 5. The point of no return, on both sides

**Renewal side.** "The first step of a history at which the configuration is not renewable" is **not found** in the eight files or `R/README.md` (search for "first step", "no return", "point of", "earliest", "irrevers", "for good": only "for good" at `RenewalConfigurations.lean` 27 and 492 and `README.md` 87). What is there [S]:
- `renewability_not_generated` (129), from step 0; with `History.shift` (`RenewalLink.lean` 133) it holds from any step [P]. This is the counterpart of `Recoverable.earlier` (`B/ReadOffInTime.lean` 601): the steps at which the configuration is renewable form an initial stretch.
- `keeping_and_losing_renewal` (494): one configuration with a step that keeps renewability and a step that loses it. One configuration and two steps, not a step of a history.
- The deadlines (726, 741, 834, 853) and `renewal_dichotomy` (648): a time by which a history is not `Live`, for laws with no population. They bound the loss of `Live`, not of `Renewable`, and name no first step.
- Whether `Renewable law c` is decided at a configuration: not found. The files prove it is not a function of the reserve and the kinds present (474) nor of the tokens alone (483); `seeded_renewable` (355) is one sufficient condition.

**Package side** [S]. `Recoverable (feasible keeps : (Nat → C → Nat) → Prop) (l : Nat → C → Nat) (N : Nat) := ∃ l', feasible l' ∧ AgreeUpTo l' l N ∧ keeps l'` (`ReadOffInTime.lean` 591–592), `NoReturnAt … l N := ¬ Recoverable … l N ∧ ∀ n, n < N → Recoverable … l n` (595–596), `{C : Type}` (587); `AgreeUpTo l l' t := ∀ s, s ≤ t → ∀ c, l s c = l' s c` (`B/Counterfactual.lean` 132). They are over courses of levels, a `Nat` for each condition at each step. The two uses found: `FourReaders` with `C := Place` (967–1019) and `TurningInTime` section 7 with `C := Unit` (`Turned` 1945, `MeetsNeed` 1949). "Recoverable" and "NoReturnAt" occur in no other module [S: search].

A history's `states : Nat → Config Kind` is not of the type `Nat → C → Nat`. **`NoReturnAt` does not apply to it as it stands.** What it would take [P]:
- either the two definitions restated over courses `Nat → X` for any `X` (a change to a package module; the five laws at 601–637 use, of the course type, only that agreement up to `N` gives agreement up to `n ≤ N`, by my reading of their proofs), or an encoding of a configuration as levels;
- then, with `feasible :=` "the configurations of some `History law`" and `keeps :=` "`Live` at every step", recoverable at `N` would be `Renewable law (H.states N)` [P: by `History.shift`, a splice of the first `N` ticks in front of a renewing history, which the files have for one tick only (`History.cons`, `RenewalConfigurations.lean` 89), and `dead_stays_dead` with `Live` decided];
- for a point to exist, `noReturnAt_of_past` (632) asks `∀ n, Recoverable … n ∨ ¬ Recoverable … n`: renewability decided at each configuration of the history, which was not found.

The package's process-level notion is another thing: `Branching.Juncture P s x` (`B/Junctures.lean` 92) is a configuration with a step after which **the configuration `s` can be had again** and one after which it cannot. Its header lists "junctures for sets of configurations" as not derived (line 60). A juncture for keeping a guard was not found.

**The renewal files hand in** [S]: the kinds; `ChannelLaw` (`edge`, `span`, `demand`, `rate`; `RenewalAccounting.lean` 41–45); the step itself (aging by one tick, formation only with a supporter alive after aging, reserve by an inequality; 141–145); `Live` (148), and `K` for `Keeps K`; for necessity and the deadlines the list `U` with `complete`; for the explicit deadline `demandPositive`; for the keeping deadlines `DecidablePred K` (726, 834). **What one derives that the other hands in** [P]:

## The guesses

**P1.** *For:* the bridge is a three-field re-packing each way (Question 1) [P]. `renewable_iff_step` is a fact of every process and guard [P], and the package does not state it for `Keepable` (its every-course twin `kernel_step_iff` is there). *Against:* `renewability_not_generated` is **not** a fact of every process: it fails on the package's own `ChoicePoint.process` with a guard lost and regained (2c) [P]; it needs the guard's complement kept by every step, which is `dead_stays_dead`, a fact of the channel law. And the "either the package has them or it lacks them" does not split cleanly: for runs with no guard the package has the backward half for every process (`Onward.run_of_trace`), and at the renewal files' own `renewingProcess` that makes `renewable_of_step` a case; for `Keepable` with a guard it has neither half.

**P2.** *For:* no law found that makes "some run keeps a guard" equivalent to a condition on a branching law's parameters; the package's keeping modules say in their headers that existence is not claimed. *Against, as qualifications:* the package does decide run existence from the process in four ways (Question 3, items 1–4), and for determined laws it has exact thresholds from the law's regime and the start (`RegimeMargin.ends_iff_below`). So "nothing in the package decides keepability from a property of the law" is too wide as written; "nothing found does it for a branching law at the grain of its parameters, in both directions" is what the read supports.

**P3.** *For:* `Locked` is on pairs `S × R`; `turn_locked` is over `Pace.turn f h`; section 6 is over `Law.step (Pace.step …)` and orbits. A policy on births needs five supplied objects and three proved premises (Question 4). *Against, as qualifications:* the lock's courses are runs of `Law.process`, a `Process` with one move, so "not stated over a `Process`" is true of the theorems' form and not of the course's type; and the package does have a choice of a process's move from a reading over any process (`UniformPolicy` at `Admissible`, `Plan`), which type-checks at the channel process with only a reading supplied. That choice is for keeping a guard on every successor, not a rule read off a share.

**P4.** *For:* both halves hold in what was read: `NoReturnAt` is typed over `Nat → C → Nat`; the renewal files state no first step. *Against:* nothing against the statement. To be added: the pieces of such a point are there on paper (the initial-stretch law, and what "recoverable" would be), and the missing piece is a decision of renewability at a configuration.

**P5.** *For:* the bridge is a definition unfolded; `Keepable` and `Renewable` both say "some run", and the renewal files say twice that nothing in them says which history occurs. No statement found joins "some history stays live" to a course that occurs or to a selection. The package's choices from readings, put on the channel process, speak of keeping against any loss of reserve, a different question. The kernel at `Live` is empty, so the every-course side of the package says nothing there. *Against:* (i) the bridge is more than a renaming in one respect: through `renewingProcess`, four renewal proofs become cases of general laws and one package law (`closed_viable`) gets a first non-vacuous instance from a law (2a, 2d) [P]; (ii) statements that could be written with no new model and that say where along a history the possibility is lost do exist on paper: the non-renewable region is one the process never leaves (so `Invariant.locks_out` applies), and `keeping_and_losing_renewal` is a configuration with a keeping step and a losing step. *What these place* is keepable against not keepable along a history. They say nothing of selecting. They are stated on a law that does have a reserve, supporters and re-formation, so "what is rested on" and "remaking" are in the model and "selecting" is not: that last sentence is my reading, not a statement of the Lean.
