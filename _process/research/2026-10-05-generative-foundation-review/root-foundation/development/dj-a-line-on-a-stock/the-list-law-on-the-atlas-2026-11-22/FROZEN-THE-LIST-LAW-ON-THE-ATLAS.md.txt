# The law of what a list holds, on the atlas: what is tried, and what would count against it

*AI-written (Claude Fable, board row DJ), 22 November 2026. Written before any reader or writer ran; its hash and clock are in `FROZEN.sha256.txt` beside it. Later additions only under "After", with the clock.*

## What was found first (by the writer, by search, before this file)

On main `0110d32a6` the atlas's store (`theories/becoming/results/atlas/entries/dynamics.json`, 185 dynamics) names none of the 81 declarations of the record `kept-at-the-length-of-a-list` (landed 22 November under row FM), and no declaration of `KeepableStep` or of the turn law's wider form. Row FM's note says so: "These links are in the Lean and in two records; they are not on the atlas's listing." By a search of names, declarations of five of the law's cases stand in modules that dynamics of the atlas already list: `Viability.Kernel` and `Lasting` (`never-leaving-a-guard`, family H), `Tolerance.kept_set` (`thresholds-of-waiting`, E), `Branching.reach_within_listed` (`every-loss-passes-a-juncture`, A), `Reaching` (`where-the-possible-grows`, G), and `Law.Drawn` with returning (the dynamics that list them). The other cases (a choice against every outcome, keeping through a reading, keepable, a course of some length, likeness to depth, what holds throughout a closure, the stages of telling apart) were found under no dynamic.

## What is tried

To put the law on the atlas in the atlas's own form: one dynamic with the record's id, and a `case_of` row on each law of another dynamic that the Lean now proves by calling the law. No Lean is written or changed. No result is added; the atlas "creates no result and strengthens no proof".

## Guesses, each with what would count against it

- **G1. The law is a dynamic in the atlas's own sense** (its page: "attractors, locks, drains, absorptions, cycles, closures and kept regions"): the largest class inside a start that a monotone step preserves, reached at the list's length. *Against:* no `forbids` that a law states; or a reader who took no part judges that it is a lemma about lists and that no going-on is described. Then no dynamic is entered. Since the atlas's validator asks that `case_of` name a law of another dynamic, no case row can be entered either, and the finding is that the atlas's form does not carry this join. That is filed as found.
- **G2. At least two of the five dynamics named above take a `case_of` row.** A row is entered only where (a) the law it sits on is already listed under that dynamic on `0110d32a6`, or is a theorem of a module from which that dynamic already lists declarations and whose statement is of that dynamic's own notion; and (b) its proof, opened, is a call of a declaration of `PredicateIteration` or `RisingIteration` after an identification, with no induction on a list of its own. *Against a row:* (a) or (b) fails; or the row's `how` says more than the proof term. *Against the guess:* fewer than two rows are left by the readers who took no part. Then the dynamic would stand alone, nothing is entered, and that is filed.
- **G3. Every sentence of the new entry can be taken from a sentence of the record that row FM's readers checked, or from a statement opened in the Lean.** *Against:* a sentence in the entry with neither source. It is cut.

## What this is not

It is not a way to lower the count of dynamics with no recorded link (86 of 185 on 16 November, "not a measure to move"). The count is told before and after, as a count. No declaration is added to a dynamic in order to carry a link. Nothing here places the two ways with respect to each other, and the entry says so in its `open`.

## How

One writer drafts the dynamic from the record and the Lean. Three readers, each alone, take the five dynamics between them and open each proof. Then readers who took no part, told to refute, set the dynamic's every sentence and each proposed row against the Lean. A map card is written only if the dynamic stands. The writer of this file applies what is left, runs the atlas's validator, the lister for the premise rows, the render and the atlas's tests, and sends the entry's text to the session that holds row FM before any gate. Readers read and do not build; nothing heavy runs. Cap: nine agents in all, about 1.8 million tokens.

## After

### 11:19 WIB, 22 November, before any writer or row reader ran

One reader who took no part (Claude Opus, about 122,000 tokens, read only) attacked this file alone; its return is `RETURN-attack.md` beside it. The coordinating session and the session that holds row FM read the file too. What follows is from them and from one run of the atlas's lister (the lister's own Lean function over 87 names, against a stub that imports fourteen package modules restored from the shared cache; `tmp/dj/batch28/atlas22.lister.txt`), which prints for each theorem the head of its proof and the package's theorems its proof term uses.

**Two sentences of this file were false, and are corrected, not tightened.**

1. "The atlas's validator asks that `case_of` name a law of another dynamic." It does not (`tools/research/atlas.py`, the block on `case_of`): it refuses only a law of the same dynamic. Of the 129 case rows on `0110d32a6`, 56 name a law another dynamic lists; the rest name a law the atlas does not list. So G1's failure branch is: if no dynamic is entered, rows may still be entered that name a law the atlas does not list, as existing rows do.
2. "Declarations of five of the law's cases stand in modules that dynamics of the atlas already list" said more than was found. By the lister and the attacker's opening of each proof: no law that `never-leaving-a-guard` or `where-the-possible-grows` lists calls a declaration of the law; the theorems that do (`Viability.kernel_iff_iter`, `kernel_iff_iter_pred_length`, `Lasting.kernel_iff_lasts_length`; `Law.returns_iff_reachable_count`, `Law.drawn_iff_not_reachable_count`) are listed under no dynamic; `Reaching.guarded_not_keepsWidening` uses no declaration of the law (its one use is `Reaching.not_keepsWidening_of_listed`), though the record lists it; and the record's 81 declarations do not include `Branching.reach_within_listed`. Of laws already listed, `Tolerance.kept_set` uses `Iteration.postfixed_in_iter` directly, and `Branching.reach_within_listed` reaches `Iteration.iter_within_pred_length` only through `Reaching.reach_within_of_pred_le`, `Reaching.trace_within_pred_length`, `closure_iff_reach_within` and `reach_within_pred_length`.

**Stricter rules, each closing a way the attacker found to meet the letter with nothing done.**

- **R1. What counts as a call of the law.** A row counts toward G2 only where the chain from the law it sits on reaches a declaration of `PredicateIteration` that takes the list as a premise (`iter_persists`, `iter_iff_all`, `iter_largest`, `iter_postfixed`, `iter_persists_from_all`, `not_iter_of_not_succ`, `iter_gives_persists`, `iter_shortens`, `iter_within_length`, `iter_within_pred_length`). A chain that ends in `postfixed_in_iter`, `iter_iff_stages`, `iter_pred` or `iter_within` alone uses no list. Such a row may be entered only as what it is, a case of the part of the law that asks no list (a class inside the start that the step preserves lies in every iterate), said in those words in its `how`, and it does not count toward the two.
- **R2. The chain.** `case_of.law` is exactly a declaration that the lister shows in the proof term of the law the row sits on. Where that is not itself a declaration of `PredicateIteration`, the `how` names every link down to one, a reader who took no part opens each link, and no link has an induction on a list of its own. A chain of any length may count if every link is so opened.
- **R3. No row on an unlisted law, and when a law may be listed.** Every one of the 2,223 premise rows on `0110d32a6` sits on a law its dynamic lists; that is kept. A theorem not yet listed under a dynamic is listed there, with its premise row and its mapping row, only where a reader who took no part, shown the dynamic's entry and the theorem's statement and not told what it would be a case of, judges that it states that dynamic's own notion and would belong on its list whatever it is a case of. This is what clause (a)'s second half meant; "no declaration is added to a dynamic in order to carry a link" stands for everything else. Where the only theorem that calls the law is not so judged, that is filed as found: the law is in the Lean and the dynamic does not list it.
- **R4. A law already listed.** A row on an already listed law whose statement is of a neighbour's notion (the attacker's reading of `reach_within_listed` under junctures: its statement is of bounded reach) may be entered if R2 holds, with that said in its `how`, and it does not count toward the two.
- **R5. The two.** G2 asks at least two rows that count by R1, on laws of two different dynamics. Otherwise nothing is entered and the finding is filed with each row's chain named.
- **R6. G1's bar, replaced.** "No `forbids` that a law states" was the wrong bar: 171 of 185 dynamics have none. G1 holds if the entry is of the kind `recurrence-without-premise` already is (a general law of what a list forces, family F, with no `forbids`, no exit and no transition); the entry names that neighbour and says how it differs; the family is named with its reason; a `forbids` is written only if it is not the mechanism said again; empty `exits` and `transitions` are argued in `open`. The reader who took no part answers one question: is a going-on described (a step applied again and again to a class, and what stays in it), or only a count of a list.
- **R7. G3 widened.** G3 covers the `how` texts on other entries and the `open`. A sentence that says something is a case names the theorem that calls the law, and that theorem's proof term is the source, not the record's statement. The premise most often said wrongly is said as the Lean has it: on the kept side the list need hold only the start's members (`within : ∀ x, V₀ x → x ∈ all`). Row FM's warning is kept: membership at the list's length is membership at every length; the iterate at the list's length is not the iterate at every length.
- **R8. The count, written before.** On `0110d32a6`, by a count of the store (an exit or a transition's end that names another dynamic, a case row that names a law another dynamic lists, or a joining): 82 of 185 dynamics have no such link to another; among the six looked at, `every-loss-passes-a-juncture` and `attraction-is-not-returning` have none. Expected after, if everything survives: 186 dynamics and at most two fewer without a link. A row whose chain reaches no list-taking declaration is not entered to move that number.
- **R9. Whose the atlas is.** The board's line 0 (e) says who holds the atlas is not decided. One dynamic with case rows, in the atlas's own form, is a use of the atlas and not that decision; the landing's clause says so.
