# The law of what a list holds, entered on the atlas (22 November 2026)

*AI-written (Claude Fable, board row DJ). No Lean was written or changed and no result is added: the atlas "creates no result and strengthens no proof". What a reader who took no part changed in this note is said at its end. No person has read it.*

## What was found first

By a search of names on main `0110d32a6`: the atlas's store of dynamics (`theories/becoming/results/atlas/entries/dynamics.json`, 185 dynamics) named none of the 81 declarations of the record `kept-at-the-length-of-a-list`, which row FM landed that morning. Row FM's note says the same of its links: in the Lean and in two records, not on the atlas's listing.

## What was tried, written first

To put the law on the atlas in the atlas's own form: one dynamic with the record's id, and a case row on each law of another dynamic that the Lean proves by calling the law. Three guesses and what would count against each were written and hashed before anything ran (`FROZEN.as-first-frozen.md.txt`, 11:14 WIB). One reader who took no part then attacked that file alone (`RETURN-attack.md.txt`). It found that the tests could be met with nothing done, and that two of the file's sentences were false: the validator does not ask that a case row name a law of another dynamic; and no law that `never-leaving-a-guard` or `where-the-possible-grows` then listed calls a declaration of the law. Both were corrected and nine stricter rules, R1 to R9, were added under "After" at 11:19, before any writer ran (`FROZEN-THE-LIST-LAW-ON-THE-ATLAS.md.txt`; both hashes in `FROZEN.sha256.txt`). The rules that did most work:

- **R1.** A row counts only where the chain of calls from the law it sits on reaches a declaration of `PredicateIteration` that takes the list as a premise. A chain that ends in `postfixed_in_iter` alone may be entered only as a case of the part of the law that asks no list, said so, and does not count.
- **R2.** A row names exactly a declaration that Lean's own printing shows in the proof term of the law it sits on; its `how` names every link down to the law; a reader who took no part opens each link.
- **R3.** No row sits on a law its dynamic does not list. A theorem is newly listed under a dynamic only where a reader shown the dynamic and the statement, and not told what the theorem would be a case of, judges that it states that dynamic's own notion.
- **R5.** At least two rows that count, on laws of two different dynamics; otherwise nothing is entered.

What Lean prints of each proof term (its head and the package's theorems it uses) came from the atlas's own lister function, run once over 87 names (`aa-attractor-atlas/six/added/2026-11-22-the-list-law.lister.txt`).

- `Lasting.kernel_iff_lasts_length`: once `kernel_iff_iter` is listed under the same dynamic, the validator leaves it no law to name.
- `Law.returns_iff_reachable_count` under `recurrence-without-premise`, and `Parting.continues_persists` under `when-the-runs-of-a-process-settle`: the blind reader placed them there, and the readers who refuted the rows found that those dynamics list nothing from the theorems' modules, which the first file's clause asked. - `Reaching.guarded_not_keepsWidening`: its proof uses only `Reaching.not_keepsWidening_of_listed` and reaches no declaration of the law, though the record's law 14 lists it.
- By the blind reader, on the statement alone, no dynamic's own notion: `Branching.reachable_iff_all`, `Branching.iter_iff_reachable`, `Reaching.trace_within_pred_length`, `closure_iff_reach_within`, `GuardHorizon.viable_iff_closed`, `Viability.coded_kept_iff_closed`.

## What the readers judged of the law itself

Asked whether a going-on is described or only a count of a list, the reader who refuted the entry answered (`RETURN-dynamic-refuter.json`): a going-on is described, of classes and not of states; the list's count does real work only in one lemma, which decides where the descent stops and not what is kept; "the going-on is an operator's iteration, not a course in time. It is read as keeping only through the cases". The writer of the entry had said it would not object to a reader who ruled it a lemma and entered only case rows. The entry's `open` says that the number of an iterate counts applications of the step and that no statement of the law itself reads it as a time along a course.
