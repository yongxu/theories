## Why

On the evening of 22 November Ren asked the session that holds row FM whether the map really has a centre, and wrote: "we want the map to be represented as "the map" - is our approaching right?" ([his words](../../../../../../sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-11-22-does-our-map-have-a-centre.md); he asked and did not state). The coordinating session then put to this row, as its reading, that the atlas's links could be read off Lean when a page is drawn, where today they are typed by hand. This note counts what the represented map has today and what such a reading would add, so that the question can be put to him with figures and their limits. It decides nothing.

## What was counted, and by what

- The atlas's store on main `10742ca6f` (`theories/becoming/results/atlas/entries/dynamics.json`, 186 dynamics).
- A list Lean printed that day of every theorem declared in a module of the package, with the package's theorems that occur in its proof term (`AllProofHeads.lean.txt` is the file that prints it; `README-of-the-list.md.txt` says what each field is, that its first form had two defects, corrected that afternoon, and its limits; the list itself is not filed: 4.7 MB, sha256 beginning 1f7f88bed4bb6307, regenerated by that file in about half a minute).
- One script, `count_the_map.py.txt`, whose whole output is `counts.txt`. Every figure in the next two sections is a line of that output; figures given as the coordinating session's are its own.

Three kinds of link between two dynamics are kept apart:

- **A road:** an exit, or an end of a transition, that names another dynamic. It says how a course gets from the one to the other.
- **The same law seen twice:** a case row that names a law another dynamic lists, or a joining. It says two entries overlap.
- **Proved from:** a theorem listed under one dynamic has in its proof term, directly or through theorems no dynamic lists, a theorem listed under another. It is a dependence. It does not say "a case of" and it is not a road; and no one types the link, though it rests on the listing of theorems under dynamics, which is kept by hand.

## Where the measure flatters, by the coordinating session's attack

The coordinating session reproduced the main figures by a script of its own and set the measure against itself (`coordinator-on-the-measure.md.txt`, its words). In short, with this note's figures:

1. Chains through unlisted theorems make the larger numbers; by a direct use the largest piece is 74 and not 114.
2. In 22 of the 230 links every witness ends in a theorem also listed under the source dynamic: the link is there because one theorem is listed under two dynamics, which is a choice kept by hand.
3. Seventeen dynamics list no theorem of the package (six list nothing; eleven only definitions or theorems outside it). None can have such a link; all 17 are among the 54 and the 45. The coordinating session named the six.
4. It cannot see a road. 28 of the 105 hand-kept pairs are not proved-from pairs, and 26 of those 28 are roads; of the 58 road pairs, 32 are also proved-from pairs, but as a dependence and not as a passage. A passage from one dynamic to another is a statement and not a dependence, so the measure adds most where the map is least a map of change.
5. About a tenth of the package's theorems are under any dynamic; the chains of point 1 run through the rest.
6. A link runs from what uses to what is used: by the coordinating session's reading, the dynamics most depended on (`least-loss-rest`, 15) are the most basic, not a middle.

By the same attack the joined piece is not a star: without the one, three or five most depended-on dynamics it is still 110, 104 and 86 of the rest, or 94 for another choice among three tied at fifth (the coordinating session's figures, which the checker of this note reproduced; the order of search does not change them), and the theorems most links end in are, in its words, "laws and not plumbing". Its 2,676 declarations, 104 listed twice, 65 transitions and 129 exits are not the store's (2,785; 102 theorems; 105; 329).

## What it does not show

- That any proved-from link is a case, a join or a passage; that the dynamics so linked say one thing; that the map is nearer its aim.
- Anything about the nine tenths of the theorems that no dynamic lists, or about notions the atlas has no dynamic for.
- That the list is complete: it is one tool's output, whose first form had two defects found and corrected the day it was made, and a floor.
- What "represented as the map" asks for. That is Ren's to say. Whether the atlas should carry a layer drawn from Lean beside the hand-kept one is put to him and is not decided; it belongs to the board's line 0 (e).
