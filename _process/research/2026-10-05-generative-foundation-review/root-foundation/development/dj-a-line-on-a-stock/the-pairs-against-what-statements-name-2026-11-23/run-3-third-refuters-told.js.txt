Naming is not placing; the readers said how each statement places the two sides, or that it does not.

The verdicts they used. PASSAGE from X to Y: the premises place a course on X and the statement places THAT SAME course on Y, with what changes quoted: kind "t" an index (X at a step, Y at a later one or from one on), or kind "p" a parameter (the one statement gives Y on one stated range and X's own outcome on the other). BUILT CASE: a built example and no general statement (under both / under X and not Y / under neither). ACROSS: X said of one going-on and Y of another. House rules. This Mac is busy: run no Lean, no build, no test, no gate; change and write no file; your final message is your whole return. Say what you did not read. A sentence that says a thing is absent says where you looked. Nothing is pushed or sent anywhere.`

phase('Third')

phase('Refute')

Open each statement in the Lean at its lines (and its neighbours where needed: section variables, the definitions of the notions, a few lines of the proof where the verdict turns on what is called). ${extra}
For each entry ask, and answer from the statement:
1. Is the quoted part in the statement, exactly? Are the two notions said of the SAME thing at the SAME index, as the verdict needs (a WALL or IMPLIES between notions said of different things, or at different grains, is not what it claims; a PASSAGE needs the same course at two indices or on two ranges)?
2. Is anything handed in that the readers left out, and does a premise carry the conclusion (the far side assumed in a premise in other words)?
3. Is the statement a definition unfolded, a renaming, a decidability statement, or one call of another theorem with nothing added (look at the first lines of the proof)? Say which theorem.
4. For a notion read as WIDENS or NARROWS, or as LOCK or its reverse: did the readers read the right one from the arguments?
5. Does the verdict say more than the statement (for example "excludes" where only one direction is shown; "passage" where an iff has a condition on the start on one side and an outcome on the other)?
