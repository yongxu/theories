## In short

Most pieces of the picture you describe are in the Lean, each several times under different names, and almost none said in terms of another. Two are not there. In the models of this month (items 1 to 6 below), nothing that goes on gives back to what it rests on. And the meta-attractor in the wider sense has no theorem.

## What is proved

Call how much there is *the level*, and how much is taken each step *the take*. "Given" marks what a theorem assumes.

1. **A take that stands has one boundary.** Given that what comes back falls short of the take below some level, and covers it from there up to a ceiling: the level runs out exactly from below that level, and holds from there up. Of two takes the larger has the higher boundary. A take larger than anything that ever comes back has no side on which the level holds.
2. **A take that grows against a ceiling ends.** Given the growth: a line whose successors together take more comes to a need that is not met. So does a rule set again from its own share, where the share counts those who draw: it ends, the rule still set that way (the lock, item 4), given also that nothing reads the level. Both end within the ceiling plus one steps. No theorem states the crossing of the boundary; in one worked course the level stands at twelve while the take doubles.
3. **A take that falls can turn it, in time.** From a start already below the old boundary, the course is kept exactly where the level after the turn is at or above the new boundary, given that the lesser take then stays. This is proved for a member that stops. For a rule it is proved under conditions on which levels are read, or where the reading also lowers the share. A corrected rule that is set again from its share can be read in time and still end: one worked course.
4. **The lock is closed.** Given that a favouring rule does not lower the share, and that a higher share is not read as a lower rule: a rule set from its own share in its favour stays so. Between a lock for and a lock against, a critical share decides. These theorems have no stock in them.
5. **Where who is there depends on the level, a boundary in the level need not decide.** One worked case: from seven it ends, and from six it goes on for ever.
6. **Going on is not renewing.** An October theorem: a taker that needs nothing of the level, and takes more than comes back, goes on for ever while whoever needs the level loses it for good.
7. **Giving, in three corners, all outside items 1 to 6.** A course that takes from a stock and returns to it can be repeated without end exactly where its first pass is covered and what it returns covers what it takes (October). Stocks that give to each other: within a band, each one's threshold does not rise as its partners rise, and one built pair holds together where neither holds alone. And an October law, sufficient only: a level comes back where what goes on gives more than it takes for long enough.

## What is not there

- **The generative half, joined to the rest.** Something that goes on, needs its level, gives back to it, and can also grow, stop or be locked, with an exact condition. The three corners of item 7 have giving and none of the rest. 生生 as you mean it is not reached: the Lean's 生生 is renewal without end, with no stock in it.
- **The meta-attractor in the wider sense.** In the atlas the name is given to the lock (item 4). The wider sense I read in your texts, something whose effects rebuild what selects it across changes of who carries it, has no theorem; say if that is not yours. Cancer is in the Lean as a third thing with no rule in it: a line that rises inside a whole and ends with it. Nothing joins the three.
- **One space, with every start's fate.** Items 1 to 7 live in different models. Each model splits its own starts (items 1 and 4). No space with both a lock and a level has every start's fate, no split is said in the root's language of basins, and there is no drawing of the ways from one region to another.
- **Transitions as a quantity passing a value.** Two are proved and not said so: the take against the most that ever comes back (one side only), and the step of the first reading against the point of no return (exact).
- **How a lock comes about on a stock.** Nothing. In two worked courses a corrected rule returns to a lock, and in both the law that sets the rule from the share was there from the start.

## Two understandings of mine that the next step leans on

Say if either is off.

- **Cancer** is four things together. How it selects is closed on itself. Its take is not covered, or grows. Nothing of the level reaches how it selects in time. And it needs what it uses up. The lock's ending law (item 2) takes all four as given and concludes the ending. Nothing has them come about, and the whole it rises within is not in that law.
- **Generative** means that what goes on gives back to what it rests on, so that its going on keeps up the conditions of going on, its own and others'. Holding back (item 3) is not that.

## Added the same afternoon: the try was run on paper, and it failed

Three readers tested the guess and a fourth was set to refute them. By the three conditions above, nothing has changed, and nothing is worth building from it.

- **No condition on the start decides the fate in item 5's case.** From six it goes on; from five and from seven it ends. Six is a balance between two ways of ending: lower, the level runs down; higher, more takers come to be met and outrun it. The only condition that gives both fates is the course itself, run.
- **No old proof becomes a case, and the Lean would not get shorter.**
- **Members that give add nothing by themselves.** For members that are always there, "the stock gives by what is left" and "members give" are the same function under two names. Giving is new only where the givers can stop.
- **What did hold** (argued on paper and searched, not built). With a stock whose step rises in the level, each way's levels are cut into stretches with one fate each, and each cut is a threshold. What fails is that there is one cut, and that higher is safer.

What I take from it: one more general model is not how these pieces join. Where who is there depends on the level, more can be worse, and which fate a start has is not a matter of one boundary. What stays exact, and does not depend on the start, is which regions exist and what a crossing decides.

So the second try was not a space. It was the missing links between dynamics that are already there, each as a theorem that names both sides, or as a countermodel that shows them apart. Ten were tried on paper, with what counts as a link written down first.

- **Two are paths the Lean already proves** and the atlas does not show: how a lock forms (shaping the rule, where what comes back to the shapers covers its cost, leads to the lock), and a course that turns, which from then on is the regime's own level under the lesser take.
- **Two more are true and are not paths**: the lock joined to the level it needs, and the lock that outlives its loss as a case of the lock that stays.
- **One small law is worth building**: below the need of what renews it, taking from a condition does not go on for ever. My sentence for it was false as I wrote it; it holds of taking, not of arising.
- **Cancer is joined to nothing.** The rise confined within a whole ends because the whole ends. A growing line on a stock ends alone, and in one worked case the stock recovers and the rest goes on. They are apart, both ways.
- **A lead from that**, searched by me in 2,658 small worlds and not proved: a growing line takes everything with it where nothing else can live below the line's need. Where something can, the line blooms, crashes, and the rest recovers. That would be the first statement here with "it ends the whole it is in" as a conclusion.

With all of it entered and built, the atlas would go from 108 pieces to 103. That is how far this is from fully connected.
