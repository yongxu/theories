## In short

Most pieces of the picture you describe are in the Lean, each several times under different names, and almost none said in terms of another. Two are not there. Nothing that goes on gives back to what it rests on, with an exact condition. And the meta-attractor in your wider sense has no theorem.

## What is proved

Call how much there is *the level*, and how much is taken each step *the take*. "Given" marks what a theorem assumes.

1. **A take that stands has one boundary.** Given that what comes back falls short of the take below some level, and covers it from there up to a ceiling: the level runs out exactly from below that level, and holds from there up. Of two takes the larger has the higher boundary. A take larger than anything that ever comes back has no side on which the level holds.
2. **A take that grows against a ceiling ends.** Given the growth: a line whose successors together take more comes to a need that is not met. A rule set again from a share that counts those who draw ends, still locked, given also that nothing reads the level. Both within the ceiling plus one steps. No theorem states the crossing of the boundary; in one worked course the level stands at twelve while the take doubles.
3. **A take that falls can turn it, in time.** From a start already below the old boundary, the course is kept exactly where the level after the turn is at or above the new boundary, given that the lesser take then stays. This is proved for a member that stops. For a rule it is proved under conditions on which levels are read, or where the reading also lowers the share. A corrected rule that is set again from its share can be read in time and still end: one worked course.
4. **The lock is closed.** A rule set from its own share in its favour stays so. Between a lock for and a lock against, a critical share decides. These theorems have no stock in them.
5. **Where who is there depends on the level, no boundary in the level decides.** One worked case: from seven it ends, and from six it goes on for ever.
6. **Going on is not renewing.** An October theorem: a taker that needs nothing of the level, and takes more than comes back, goes on for ever while whoever needs the level loses it for good.
7. **Giving, in two corners.** Stocks that give to each other: each one's threshold does not rise as its partners rise, and one built pair holds together where neither holds alone. And an October law, sufficient only: a level comes back where what goes on gives more than it takes for long enough.

## What is not there

- **The generative half.** Something that goes on, needs its level, gives back to it, and has an exact condition. 生生 as you mean it is not reached: the Lean's 生生 is renewal without end, with no stock in it.
- **The meta-attractor as you mean it.** In the Lean the name is given to the lock (item 4). Your wider sense, something whose effects rebuild what selects it across changes of who carries it, has no theorem. Cancer is in the Lean as a third thing with no rule in it: a line that rises inside a whole and ends with it. Nothing joins the three.
- **One space, with every start's fate.** Items 1 to 7 live in different models. There are regions but no basins, and no drawing of the ways from one region to another.
- **Transitions as a quantity passing a value.** Two are proved and not said so: the take against the most that ever comes back, and the step of the first reading against the point of no return.
- **How a lock forms on a stock.** One worked case.

## Two understandings of mine that the next step leans on

Say if either is off.

- **Cancer** is four things together. How it selects is closed on itself. Its take is not covered, or grows. Nothing of the level reaches how it selects in time. And it needs what it uses up. The lock's ending law (item 2) takes all four as given and concludes the ending. Nothing has them come about, and the whole it rises within is not in that law.
- **Generative** means that what goes on gives back to what it rests on, so that its going on keeps up the conditions of going on, its own and others'. Holding back (item 3) is not that.
