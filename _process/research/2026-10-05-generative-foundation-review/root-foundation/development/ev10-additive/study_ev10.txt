- **Cauchy–Schwarz, counted.** For each two indices `i`, `j`: `2 · (x_i y_j)(x_j y_i) ≤ (x_i y_j)² + (x_j y_i)²` (`twice_le`: `u² + v² = 2uv + (v − u)²`), rearranged as `2 · (x_i y_i)(x_j y_j) ≤ x_i² y_j² + x_j² y_i²` (`cross_mul`, `square_mul`). Summed over both indices, the left is `2 (Σ x y)²` and the right `2 Σ x² Σ y²` (`pairs_cross`, `pairs_cross'`); halve.
- **On pairs.** `dist x y = excess x y + excess y x`. Expanding `dist(w_p, w_q) · dist(a_p, a_q)` gives four terms whose totals over ordered pairs are the concordance, the discordance, the discordance read from the second member (`discord_swap`) and the concordance so read (`concord_swap`). On the weights themselves the discordance is nothing. With `x = dist w`, `y = dist a` over `pairsOf ps`, Cauchy–Schwarz reads `(2S)² ≤ (2 C_ww)(2 C_aa)`, `S` the concordance with the discordance; divide by four (`twice_mul`).
- **Covariance.** Concordance less discordance is `N²` times the covariance and the concordance of a weight with itself `N²` times its variance (`variation`); the bound is `Cov² ≤ Var(w) Var(a)`. With the regression coefficient `β = Cov/Var(a)`, Fisher's additive genetic variance in offspring number through the amount is `β² Var(a) = Cov²/Var(a)`, so the bound says it is at most `Var(w)`.
- **Over generations.** With exact transmission, `mean_step` reads `size_t · sum_{t+1} + D = size_{t+1} · sum_t + C`, so the rise is at most `C` and the fall at most `D`, each at most `C + D`.
- **Witness.** `Unrelated`: members 0, 1, 2 with offspring numbers 1, 2, 3; the amount is one at member 1 only. Pairs (0,1): both rise, concordance 1; (0,2): the amount does not change; (1,2): offspring number rises, the amount falls, discordance 1. Squared differences: offspring numbers 1 + 4 + 1 = 6, the amount 1 + 0 + 1 = 2.

**Self-review.** Cauchy–Schwarz is classical; what is stated is its counting form over ordered pairs, which joins it to the spine's concordance and discordance with no subtraction and no rational number, and its reading as Fisher's additive part through one amount (Fisher 1930's additive genetic variance; Price 1972's reading of the fundamental theorem as the regression of offspring number on what is carried). The statement is an inequality, not Fisher's identity: the identity needs the ratio `β`. The equality case is shown only at the top (the amount offspring number itself); that equality holds exactly where offspring number is affine in the amount pair by pair (Lagrange's identity) is not stated. Several carried amounts at once, the actual breeding value, are not stated; the bound for each amount separately holds.
