The kit is [`development/ev6-coupled-ending/`](development/ev6-coupled-ending/): the edited sources (`tree/`) and the scripts that make them from main (`edit_continuation.py`, `edit_group_ending.py`), their join to the package (`integrate.py`), the apart register's entries (`apart.py`), this landing's text (`apply_text.py`, `study_ev6.txt`) and record (`make_record.py`), and the checker and connectivity logs.

- **The count over a family.** `BalancesOn.over` gives the family's stock after a step with its uptake as its stock before with its replenishment; so excess of uptake over replenishment lowers the stock by at least one (`family_excess_lowers_at`), by the same arithmetic as for one condition, and induction gives `n` (`family_excess_bounded_at`). `W.stock [c] t` and `L.replenishment [c] t` are `W.level t c` and `L.given t c` by definition (adding nothing), so `excess_lowers_at` and `excess_bounded_at` are the family law applied to `[c]` with no conversion.
- **The coupled gate.** With the stock below `m`, every giver to a member depends on some member `e` at a need of at least `m`, is supported (`GiversSupported`), so `m ≤ need ≤ level e ≤ stock < m` (`le_total_of_mem`), a contradiction; nothing is given, the replenishment is zero, and the stock does not rise (`family_stays_below_at`). The same chain makes a formation depending on any member at such a need inactive (`family_ends_below_at`). A gate on one condition is the coupled gate on its family of one (`gate_single`), so `stays_below_at`, `ends_below_at` and `joint_excess_ends_at` are now instances, and every module using them is unchanged.
- **The witness.** `Coupled`: the taker takes one from the patch at steps 0–2; the renewer, depending on the neighbour, takes one from it and gives one to the patch at steps 0–1. Levels: patch 1, 1, 1, 0; neighbour 2, 1, 0. Read alone, the patch is taken from exactly what it is given at steps 0 and 1 (`alone_within`), and the one giver to it does not depend on it (`alone_ungated`), so `joint_excess_ends` cannot be applied to it from step 0. Read as one, the pair's giver depends on the neighbour (`gated`), the uptake exceeds the replenishment at steps 0, 1 and 2 (2 > 1, 2 > 1, 1 > 0, `excess`), the stock 3 is below 3 + 1, and `family_excess_ends` ends both from step 3 (`all_end`). The endings are stipulated in the activity; the witness shows the law's hypotheses hold with them.
- **Groups.** `family_childless` composes `family_excess_ends` with `inactive_childless`. `coupled_end_together` makes every group's offspring total nothing (`group_childless`), so the groups are uniform in weight and both among-group sums vanish (`concord_uniform`, `discord_uniform` on group totals), for any amount. `ended_of_family` gives `Ended` where the groups with less of the amount depend on such a family (`ended_of_inactive`), so the among-group verdicts of `rises_iff_ended` and `Flow.rises_iff_inactive` apply.
- **Connectivity and naming.** The family laws are on the base (`Flow`, `Web`); the one-condition laws now go through them. "Patch", "neighbour", "taker" and "renewer" name the witness's parts structurally, as CG1 did; "coupled" names the gate's reading (a giver to one member depending on another), not a social relation.

**Self-review.** The generalization is exact: each one-condition law is the family law at `[c]`, by definition, and the proofs moved rather than doubled. The family law is weak where members differ much in level: its start counts the whole stock, so it ends a family only after as many steps of excess as the stock holds; the witness is chosen so the count is exact, not to show the law is tight in general. What the commons test measured (learners emptying a pool they were each coupled into) is not derived: which families run into excess is supposed. The group laws are compositions, kept because they state the reading the evolution chapter asks for: coupling, not sharing one condition, is what makes groups end together.
