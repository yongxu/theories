## 2. `under`, `outcomes`, `someValues`

**Every part of `outcomes` is used in `steps_either`.** From `outcomes t safe a usable` (l. 41) come `l`, `decided`, `cover`. `decided` is used at l. 44, to give at each listed `u` either `next t a u → X u` or `next t a u ∧ Y u`. `cover u step` is taken apart at l. 49 as `⟨v, mem, reached, rel⟩` and all three are used: `down u v rel (all v mem reached)`. The middle conjunct `next t a u'` is needed: the search gives, for a listed non-outcome, only the vacuous `next t a v → X v`, so without `reached` nothing is known of `X v`.

**Could it be weaker?** Not in strength. In form it could be simpler: the first conjunct and `next t a u'` together are the same as "a list of outcomes representing every outcome" (`(∀ u', u' ∈ l → next t a u') ∧ ∀ u, next t a u → ∃ u', u' ∈ l ∧ under u u'`), each form giving the other by filtering the list with the decisions. The reading supplies exactly that already: `seen_outcomes` answers `decided` by `.inl` at every listed code (l. 271), so the decision does no work there. With no reading it does (`stepOrNot` over `states`), and the present form saves that case a filter. `either` is used only at listed outcomes of `t`, and `okOrBad` only at `t`; no gain in narrowing them.

**`someValues` is used exactly as stated.** `seen_outcomes` l. 272: `cover o` gives `⟨v, listed, sub⟩`; `listed` finds the exact code of `v` on the list (l. 273); `sub` is used once, in the one direction it has: `(exactName s).mpr (sub s ((names s).mp h))` (l. 275). No other use.

**It excludes nothing the first module's premise admitted.** `everyValue` (`∃ values : List O, ∀ o, o ∈ values`) gives `someValues` with `o' := o`. With `O` empty both are met by `[]`. What happens there: no outcome code exists (`seenNext` needs a value), the list of codes is empty, the search over outcomes returns "all" vacuously (l. 48), so a guarded code with a usable move has a staying step into every X and its class is kept at every horizon. That agrees with `KeptFor` (its `∀ o` is empty). It is not an oddity of the process: `sense : S → A → S → O` with `O` empty and a move in hand forces `S` empty, so every class is empty.

**What neither says.** At any guarded code with a usable move, `someValues` yields `(O → False) ∨ Nonempty O`: the list is empty or it is not. So the premise carries a third decision, of whether a value exists, which the conclusion does not need (with `S := O := PLift p`, one code for the whole of `S`, guard true, the class is kept for ever and `someValues` cannot be supplied without deciding `p`). `everyValue` carried the same. It is a decision about `O`, not about the outcome, and V3 as frozen does not count it; the telling of the premises should name three decisions, not two.

## 5. Empty and odd cases

- **No moves.** `stayStep` holds nowhere and `outStep` everywhere, so every position is out at every horizon from 1, the ok ones too, though nothing happens at all. All theorems stay true (`steps_either` l. 52 on the empty list gives the out step). This agrees with the landed notions (`Viable … (n + 1)` asks an enabled move). "Out" and "lost" there mean "cannot go on staying", not "leaves"; no docstring says so (item 12 above).
- **No positions.** Everything vacuous; the bounded statements need a `t`.
- **`ok` and `bad` both at a position** (allowed in items 1 to 3, `steps_either`, `stage_either`, `stays_or_out`). Nothing false: the either-or is then inclusive, such a position is out at 0 and may sit in a stay-closed set. What goes is the gloss on `liveOrDead`: at a position ok and bad, `Out 1` holds through `bad` and the conclusion at 1 says nothing of live or dead. That is what "exclusively ok" in the docstring is for.
- **`live` and `dead` both at a move.** The same: inclusive, nothing false.
- **What item 6's premises force.** `okOrBad` with `apart` gives `bad t ↔ ¬ ok t` and `ok t ∨ ¬ ok t`; `liveOrDead` with `exclusive` gives `dead t a ↔ ¬ live t a` at ok positions. So in `closed_or_out`, `out_at_ok_length`, `out_iff_not_stays` and both cases' statements, the separate `bad` and `dead` are `¬ ok` and `¬ live` up to equivalence, and ok is decided everywhere. The four free predicates do work only in the theorems with fewer premises (never-both; the either-or without exclusions; the losing bound). Through a reading this reads: under the five premises keeping is decided at every horizon (`K n c ∨ ¬ K n c`) and `Outside` is `¬ codeGuard`.
- **A live move with no outcome** stays vacuously: `∀ u, next t a u → X u` is empty, so the position stays at every horizon and alone forms a stay-closed set, though no step is taken. By the letter of `stayStep`'s docstring that is right; "stays for n steps" then names no course of n steps. `GuardHorizon` rules it out: `Enabled P s a` is `∃ t, P.step s a t` (`GenerativeCore.lean` l. 27). The general module does not ask `live t a → ∃ u, next t a u`, and through a reading it can happen: a usable move whose seen classes have no code has no outcome on codes. That is why the reading's stage is `K` and not `Stays` on codes, and why `kept_above` needs `updates`; `Lost` on codes stays sound without `updates` (lost implies not kept) and may under-report.
- **A list with repeats or extra members.** Every theorem holds; the horizon is the list's length, so "the count of guarded codes" is exact only for a list without repeats that holds nothing else. Both Drain lists are such.
