**Is the whole of it the known determinacy of a finite safety game on knowledge sets?** Yes. The codes are the knowledge sets, the player picks the move, the value seen is the opponent's reply, `InClosedFamily` is a post-fixed point of the controllable predecessor, `Lost` is the opponent's attractor to "some configuration outside, or no usable move", and the bound is the count of safe positions. What saying it here adds, precisely:

1. **The premises, constructively, with no axiom.** What must be on a list: the moves; the guarded codes; for each guarded code and usable move, values whose seen classes hold every seen class. What need not: configurations (through a reading none is listed), unguarded codes, the values themselves (the level reading has all of `Nat`). What must be decided: each code within the guard or a configuration outside (this IS the either-or at horizon 0, `stage_either` l. 64, and the docstrings say so); usability at guarded codes. No decidable equality, no instance, no list without repeats.
2. **The split by premises:** never-both from nothing; the kept bound from `within` and `updates`; the rising bound from listings alone; one-or-the-other from five.
3. **The losing bound with nothing decided** is NOT part of the both-outcomes statement. Neither bounded statement calls item 3 (the report's lists of calls; I checked the proofs). For configurations it landed at 04:16 as `lost_shortens`; this build adds the all-horizons form (`out_within_length`; no `lost_within_length` found in GH ll. 216 to 384) and its reach to codes, unnamed there.
4. **"The reading as a case of the no-reading statement": the Lean does not have this** (section 3).

## 3. Is the reading "a case of the statement with no reading"? No.

Checked: `Config.closed_or_lost` (K ll. 60 to 65) is one call of `closed_or_out`. `Reading.closed_or_lost_within` (K ll. 295 to 300) calls `kept_or_lost`, `Viability.coded_kept_iff_closed` and `closed_never_lost`; it names no theorem of `Config`, of GH, or of items 2 and 6. Its bound comes from `Keeping`'s landed theorem, which is the falling law on `readingStep`, a different step from `stayStep` on codes, with a closed notion of another type (a family of classes, not a set of positions). The trial of B.5 shows what routing it through item 2 costs: 10 lines against 8, and the family of classes is still built by hand.

Nor is the reading an instance of GH's own form: GH's "live" is `Enabled P s a` (a successor exists) and its "bad" is `¬ guard s`; the reading's are `Usable P allowed (decode c) a` and a witness `Outside`. Both are instances of the positions form, which is why that form takes `live`, `dead`, `bad` as separate parameters.

**The true sentence.** With no reading and through a reading, "never both" and "one or the other" at each horizon are each one call of the same two lemmas about positions with a choice of moves against every outcome; the statement that a position is in a closed set or lost at the list's length is proved separately in each case, from that case's own kept-side bound.

**Sentences that say more than the Lean:**

1. "The reading is a case of the statement with no reading: its positions are codes." (The bounded statement through a reading calls nothing of the no-reading case and nothing of item 6.)
2. "One theorem gives both outcomes and the bound for positions of any type; `GuardHorizon` and `Keeping` are its two cases." (Item 6 has one case.)
3. "Through a reading, keeping is the staying step on codes." (Below it with no premise, above it only under `updates`; the landed kept step is `readingStep`; no theorem here states `Stays` on codes against `KeptFor`.)
4. "The bound on the losing side through a reading is the law's rising bound." (It is the kept side's count, carried over by the either-or at that one horizon; the rising bound is at the count of ALL codes and is not called.)
5. "An older result becomes a case." (Section 5a.)

## 4. What it does not have

**The general module (C and E, read whole).** Its vocabulary is: a type of positions, a type of moves, four predicates `ok bad live dead`, a relation `next`, a relation `under`, lists. In it: no quantity and nothing that runs down (no number but the horizon and a list's length; no order on positions); no share; no selection (no score, rank, judge or anything chosen again from what was chosen); nothing that sustains anything; no lock (it does not even state that `Out` rises with the horizon); no time of reading (`next` has no before and after of a value; that lives in `Seen`, in the case); nothing on how two ways of lasting stand to each other. There is no course in it either: no history, no plan, no trajectory; "stays for n" is membership in an iterate. The level is in the Drain case, not in any law.

**The gap sentence, clause by clause, against `Reading.closed_or_lost_within`:**

| clause | met? |
|---|---|
| "in one statement" | Yes, one conjunction. So did the first module's. |
| "both outcomes" | Yes, and exclusive. |
| "a choice" | Yes: some move on the kept side, every move on the losing side. |
| "a reading" | Yes, through codes that name the classes seen exactly (`updates`). |
| "of a course … the side where it ends" | **Weakest.** `Lost` is of a class of configurations not told apart, not of a course, and it is not an ending. On Drain each start alone is viable at every horizon (`start_viable` in the other session's module) while the two together are `Lost` at 3: what is lost is one choice good for all that cannot be told apart. `Lost` also holds where no move is usable and nothing leaves the guard. Even with no reading, `L n s` says every choice can be answered into leaving or stopping within n, not that the course ends. |
| "a bound on the ending side" | **By transfer.** In the proof the losing disjunct passes through `id` (K l. 299); the length does its work on the kept disjunct (`coded_kept_iff_closed`). That the number bounds the ending is the corollary `lost_at_guarded_length`, proved by refuting the closed side, and already had in negative form from landed theorems. Legitimate in a decided game, where the two bounds are one; but it is the kept side's bound read across a decision, and nobody should tell it as the rising law at work. |

So the statement meets the sentence as the first module's did, with lighter premises (guarded codes only; values up to inclusion) and with both readings of Drain under it. It is not thereby the centre: the sentence it meets describes a shape, and the shape is filled by a decided finite game.
