### (b) The either-or's own, or the definition's?

**`inOrOut` is the conclusion at horizon 0.** Line 106: `| 0, c => inOrOut c`. This type-checks because `KeptFor … 0 (decode c)` unfolds to `codeGuard decode guard c` and `L 0 c` to `Outside decode guard c`. The report's T3 line, "It assumes no decision about either outcome", is false at horizon 0; it holds from horizon 1 on.

**Some decision is forced whatever the wording.** Any losing side `L'` with `L' → ¬ Kept` and `Kept ∨ L'` gives `Kept ∨ ¬ Kept` at every horizon and code. At horizon 0 that is `codeGuard c ∨ ¬ codeGuard c`. At horizon 1, for a process with one move whose seen classes are all within the guard, the module's either-or gives `Usable ∨ ¬ Usable` of that move (kept needs it usable; lost, with the code guarded and no successor outside, leaves only `¬ Usable`). So a decision of each of the two kinds is the either-or's own. A losing side written as `¬ KeptFor` would need `KeptFor` itself decided, and its bound would be `not_iter_of_not_succ` (PredicateIteration line 187): the kept side read backwards.

**How much is asked is the definitions'.**

- `Outside` is a witness. With the negation `¬ codeGuard` in its place: `inOrOut` becomes **weaker** (`Outside c → ¬ codeGuard c`, not conversely without a search through the class). `lostReading_mono`, `lostReading_start`, `lostReading_commutes` and so `lostReading_shortens` go through unchanged: the first disjunct does not depend on `X`, and line 54 passes it straight on (`finish (.inl outside)`). `lost_not_kept` holds and is shorter (lines 76-77 would apply the negation to `kept` or `kept.1`). What is given up is that "lost at 0" shows a configuration. With the witness, `inOrOut` is more than excluded middle for `codeGuard`: it also says that a class not wholly within the guard has a configuration outside that can be shown. Even its double negation is not free for a class with unboundedly many configurations.
- The losing step is **not uniform**: `Outside` is a witness, `¬ Usable` is a bare negation (line 30).
