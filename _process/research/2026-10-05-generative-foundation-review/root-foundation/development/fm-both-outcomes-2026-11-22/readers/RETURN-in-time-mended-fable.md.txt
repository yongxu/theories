## 1. The general statement

**The proofs.** `KeptFor P sense guard allowed 1 B` unfolds (Keeping.lean ll. 97-99, `Seen` l. 80) to

`(∀ s, B s → guard s) ∧ ∃ a, Usable P allowed B a ∧ ∀ o t, (∃ s, B s ∧ P.step s a t ∧ sense s a t = o) → guard t`.

- Forward (ll. 21-23): `afterMove (sense s a t) t ⟨s, held, step, rfl⟩`. The value is taken to be the one the step gives. Right.
- Backward (ll. 24-25): `fun _ t ⟨s, held, step, _⟩ => guarded s t held step`. The equation on the value is not used. Right.
- `keptFor_one_iff_change_reading` (l. 32): the first statement at `sense`, composed with its reverse at `sense'`. Right.

**The edges.** One term covers all of them; there is no case split to get wrong.
- Universes: `universe u v w z`, `S : Type u`, `A : Type v`, `O : Type w`, `O' : Type z` (ll. 10-13, 30). These are Keeping.lean's own (ll. 68-72, l. 293). Each signature takes only `P sense guard allowed B` (and `sense'`).
- An empty type of values: a reading into it exists only where `S` or `A` is empty. With `A` empty both sides are false (no move). With `S` empty and a move in hand both sides are true.
- An empty class: both sides hold exactly where there is a move, which agrees with the premise of `keptFor_empty` (Keeping.lean l. 306).
- A nonempty class with no step from it: `Usable` asks `Enabled P s a`, which is `∃ t, P.step s a t` (GenerativeCore.lean ll. 27-29), on both sides, so both are false. The last conjunct alone would hold vacuously; the statement keeps `Usable` beside it.

**The docstrings.** The first (ll. 15-17) says the right side exactly: "some move is allowed and enabled throughout it" is `Usable P allowed B a`, and "leads only to configurations satisfying the guard from every configuration in the class" is `∀ s t, B s → P.step s a t → guard t`, of the same move. No more, no less. It names only the reading as arbitrary; process, guard, condition on moves and class are arbitrary too. The second (ll. 27-29) is its statement exactly.

Its right side, `∃ p : Plan A (Fin k) 1, p.Keeps P listed guard allowed B`, unfolds by `Plan.Keeps` (ll. 130-132) to `(∀ s, B s → guard s) ∧ Usable P allowed B p.first ∧ ∀ o t, Seen P listed B p.first o t → guard t`. **Worth having.** It is an unfolding. The only step that is not `Iff.rfl` is that `∀ o, (∃ s, … ∧ sense s a t = o) → Q` is `∀ s, … → Q`. Anyone who writes out `KeptFor` at one step reads it off. What it is worth is a boundary. The package says a reading can matter (`Matching.keeping_matters`, at two steps) and nowhere says that it cannot at one. The two together say that two is the least horizon at which a reading of steps can matter. That pairing is not in this module. It is a lemma to keep, told as an unfolding of the definition, not a result.

**It fails at horizon 2.** `Viability.Matching.keeping_matters` (Keeping.lean ll. 508-511) is the refutation, in the package: the same process, guard and moves keep `starts` for 2 steps under `keepsBit` and not under `keepsNothing`. I did not look for a smaller case and do not claim this one is the smallest.

**The telling leaves out the fact its conclusion needs.** It says level 2 is kept for two steps with no reading and at every horizon with the level reading. It does not say level 2 is *not* kept for three with no reading (`Drain.together_not_kept_none`, restated at l. 73). Without that, nothing in the telling shows a reading making any difference.

**"First makes a difference at the second move" is not right for what is proved.** Three things are being run together:
- No difference at one step: a theorem, for every process (`keptFor_one_iff_change_reading`).
- A difference on this process: only between imported theorems, for `Drain.starts`, at three steps. At two steps the class is kept under every reading (`twos_kept_two`), so at the second step nothing differs. REPORT.md l. 46 says the same: "the first horizon at which the two named readings differ is three."
- That the second move is the first one a value read can change: true of the form of `KeptFor` (one `∃ a` for the class, then `∀ o`, then the next `∃ a`). It is a remark about the definition. No theorem states it.

A difference at two steps exists on Drain on paper (`mixed`, in 1) and in the package on another process (`Matching.keeping_matters`). Neither is in this module or its imports.

**The corrected telling.**

> For any process, guard and condition on moves, whether a class is kept for one step is the same under every reading of steps (`keptFor_one_iff_change_reading`): it is that the guard holds on the class and one move usable from the class has every step it takes from the class end within the guard (`keptFor_one_iff`). This is `KeptFor` unfolded at one step. On the built process Drain, with every move allowed and the guard "level not zero", and for readings of steps into `Type`: both lanes together at level 1 are within the guard and are not kept for one step, since each move puts the lane it does not match at zero (`ones_kept_zero`, `ones_not_kept_one`); the part of that class in one lane is kept at every horizon under every reading of steps (`ones_in_lane_kept`); both lanes together at level 2 are kept for two steps under every reading of steps (`twos_kept_two`), are not kept for three under the reading that keeps nothing (`Drain.together_not_kept_none`), and are kept at every horizon under the reading that gives the level after each move (`Drain.together_kept_level`). So in what is proved on this process the two readings agree at one and two steps and differ at three. As a reading of the definition, and no theorem: the first move is chosen for the whole class before any value is kept, so a value can change only the moves from the second on, and both lanes at level 1 are not kept as far as a second move.
