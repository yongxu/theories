**`mixed_kept_one` (ll. 132-138)** is right.

**`mixed_kept_two_implies_values_ne` (ll. 143-155)** is right.

The step `rfl` is `(false,0) = next (false,1) true = (false, 1-1)` (KeptForViable l. 269). The guard `Drain.above (false,0)` is `0 ≠ 0`, applied to `rfl`. So the class is not kept because the move `true` puts lane false at level 0 at the very next configuration. **`mixed_not_kept_two_none_not_level` (ll. 160-164)** is right. Both `rfl`s hold by definition:

**`mixed_kept_lane` (ll. 168-174)** is right.

## 3. A theorem in which the reading makes a difference

**Yes: `mixed_kept_two_lane_not_none_not_level` (ll. 185-189).** The class is `mixed`, the horizon is 2, and the guard is `Drain.above` with `everyMove`. On the kept side is `keepsLane`. On the not-kept side are `noneRead` and `Drain.keepsLevel`. Beside it, `mixed_kept_lane` and `mixed_kept_end` hold at every horizon, for `keepsLane` and `endRead`.

**The least horizon.** Horizon 1 is reading-independent by `keptFor_one_iff_change_reading` (l. 32). Horizon 0 is reading-independent by the definition (Keeping l. 97, which does not mention `sense`), and no theorem states this. So 2 is the least horizon at which any two readings of steps can differ, and here they do. The pairing of these facts is not one theorem.

**What a reading must register.** `mixed_kept_two_implies_values_ne` states, for every reading into `Type`, a condition on the reading itself: it must tell apart two named steps. It is a necessary condition, not a characterization.

**The converse, worked on paper.** Write `v1 := sense (true,2) false (true,1)` and `v2 := sense (false,1) false (false,1)`, and suppose `v1 ≠ v2`.
- Take the move `false`, the only candidate. The class seen at a value `o` is `{(true,1) | v1 = o} ∪ {(false,1) | v2 = o}`. Its guard holds, since everything in it is at level 1.
- At `o = v1` it is `{(true,1)}`, and at `o = v2` it is `{(false,1)}`. At a third value it is empty. `KeptFor` asks of the empty class only a usable move, which always exists here (`usable_all`; Keeping's `keptFor_empty`, l. 306, gives every horizon). The empty class is therefore kept, and so is a part of `ones` in one lane (`ones_in_lane_kept`).
- **Something decided is needed.** To use `Drain.lane_kept` (or the next move inside `keptFor_one_iff`), one must name the lane `b` with `Lane b C`. Lane `true` needs `¬ (v2 = o)`; lane `false` needs `¬ (v1 = o)`. From `v1 ≠ v2` one gets `¬ (v1 = o ∧ v2 = o)`, but the proof needs `¬ (v1 = o) ∨ ¬ (v2 = o)`. That is the De Morgan step that in general requires weak excluded middle.
- **So the converse:**
  - holds **classically** for every `O : Type`, at horizon 2 and at every horizon `n ≥ 2` (and at 0 and 1 by `mixed_kept_one`);
  - holds **constructively** for `O` with decidable equality: `Unit`, `Bool`, `Nat`, `Bool × Nat`, every reading in this module;
  - is **not provable without a classical principle** at the generality of every `O : Type`. Taking `O := Prop`, the instance would yield `¬P ∨ ¬¬P` for an arbitrary `P`, given propext.
- **The module does not claim it.** No statement and no docstring gives the converse. With `[DecidableEq O]` it could be stated as an iff at every `n ≥ 2`: that would be the exact form of "what must be registered". It is not built.

**"Registering the level does not" is wrong as worded.** It is right only of `Drain.keepsLevel`, the level *reached*. A reading of the level the step starts from, `fun s _ _ => s.2`, gives 2 and 1 to the two steps. By the converse (constructive here, since `Nat` has decidable equality) it keeps `mixed` at every horizon. This is unbuilt, but it is a two-line case of the converse.

**It is also unfair to the level reached across classes.** On `Drain.starts` (both lanes at level 2), `Drain.keepsLevel` keeps the class at every horizon (`Drain.together_kept_level`, restated as `twos_kept_level`, l. 66), while `noneRead` fails at 3. There the first move `true` sends the two lanes to different levels, so the level reached tells the lane. On `mixed` the move `false` sends both to level 1, so it tells nothing.

**Gaps in the Lean.**
- "Registering the lane keeps it" for every reading from which the lane can be read off follows by `KeptFor.finer` from `mixed_kept_lane`. Only `endRead` is built.
- That `keepsLane` keeps `Drain.starts` is not proved anywhere. It cannot come from `together_kept_end` by `finer`, which goes from coarser to finer.

**What a careful sentence must add.** What a reading must register depends on the class. On `mixed` the one move that keeps the class for one step ends both lanes at the same level, so the level reached tells nothing there. On both lanes at level 2 the same reading tells the lane and keeps the class at every horizon.

**Corrected telling.**

> On the built process Drain, with every move allowed and the guard "level not zero", take the class of lane true at level 2 together with lane false at level 1 (`mixed`). It is kept for one step under every reading of steps into `Type` (`mixed_kept_one`), as any class is alike under every reading of steps at one step (`keptFor_one_iff_change_reading`). Any reading of steps into `Type` that keeps it for two steps gives different values to the step of the move `false` from (true, 2) to (true, 1) and the step of the move `false` from (false, 1) to (false, 1) (`mixed_kept_two_implies_values_ne`); in the proof, the move `true` puts lane false at level 0 at once. The reading that keeps nothing and the reading that keeps the level reached give those two steps one value and do not keep the class for two steps; the reading that keeps only the lane reached, and the reading that keeps the configuration reached, keep it at every horizon (`mixed_kept_two_lane_not_none_not_level`, `mixed_kept_lane`, `mixed_kept_end`). So on this class, at two steps, the least horizon at which a reading of steps can make a difference, the reading of the lane reached keeps the class and the readings of the level reached and of nothing do not. This is not a ranking of readings: on both lanes at level 2 the reading of the level reached keeps the class at every horizon (`Drain.together_kept_level`), because there the first move sends the two lanes to different levels, while here the one move that survives sends both to level 1. What a reading must tell apart is fixed by the class and its moves, not by the reading alone.

## 5. What it is not

- **No law.** Every new theorem is about one class on one built process with one guard. Nothing is quantified over processes except the older `keptFor_one_iff*`.
- **No quantity, share or "early enough".** The level runs down by `next_other`, but no theorem relates keeping or failing to how much level is left, or to a share. "Early enough" appears nowhere.
- **Against `Matching.keeping_matters`** (Keeping ll. 506-511). Its case already shows a difference at two steps: `keepsBit` keeps `starts`, and `keepsNothing` does not. Those are the only readings in it, and the only negative `KeptFor` in the package at two steps (grep: Keeping ll. 498 and 510; besides it only `keptFor_each_not_joint` at one step, JointKeeping l. 370). `mixed` adds three things:
  1. A statement over every reading into `Type` of what a reading must tell apart, `mixed_kept_two_implies_values_ne`. The package has no theorem that quantifies over readings to conclude something about the reading.
  2. A failing reading that is not `keepsNothing`. `Drain.keepsLevel` is not constant on the process's steps, and on another class it keeps at every horizon. Note that every reading is "finer than nothing" (`forget` to `Unit`), so the point is not fineness. The point is that it registers differences and not the one this class needs.
  3. The same reading keeping one class and failing another on the same process at the least horizon. That needs `together_kept_level` beside `mixed_not_kept_two_none_not_level`, which is two theorems; no single theorem conjoins them.
- **The weight.** "One more built case on a process with no share" is the right weight for the direction page. The three additions above deserve one line in the record (the board row, or the study note) and no line on the direction page. If anything goes toward the package, it is (1), best in its exact form: an iff under `[DecidableEq O]`, which is not built.
