## 1. Each statement against the Lean it rests on

Definitions used:
- `KeptFor` (Keeping.lean ll. 95-99): `| 0, B => ∀ s, B s → guard s`; `| n+1, B => guard on B ∧ ∃ a, Usable P allowed B a ∧ ∀ o, KeptFor … n (Seen P sense B a o)`.
- `Seen` (l. 78-80): `∃ s, B s ∧ P.step s a t ∧ sense s a t = o`.
- `Usable` (l. 89-90): `∀ s, B s → allowed s a ∧ Enabled P s a`.
- `Drain.process` step is `t = next s a` (KeptForViable l. 272). `next` (ll. 265-269) keeps the level on the matching move and applies `k - 1` on the other. `above s := s.2 ≠ 0`. `everyMove := True`. `noneRead := ()`. `keepsLevel := t.2`.

**`ones` (l. 11).** This is a definition. It has the same form as `Drain.starts` with level 1.

**`ones_kept_zero` (ll. 15-21).** At horizon 0 the goal is `∀ s, ones s → s.2 ≠ 0`. Each disjunct gives `level : s.2 = 1` by `congrArg Prod.snd`. Then `level.symm.trans zero : 1 = 0` is refuted by `succ_not_zero 0`, since `1` is definitionally `Nat.succ 0`. The proof is correct. `sense` does not occur in `KeptFor … 0`, so "under every reading" is idle here: the binder quantifies over a parameter the statement does not use.

**`ones_not_kept_one` (ll. 25-33).** The proof is correct.
- `kept.2 : ∃ a, Usable … ones a ∧ ∀ o, ∀ t, Seen … ones a o t → t.2 ≠ 0`.
- Case `a = true`. `o := sense (false,1) true (false,0)` and `t := (false,0)`. The Seen witness is `⟨(false,1), Or.inr rfl, rfl, rfl⟩`:
  - `ones (false,1)` holds by `Or.inr rfl`.
  - `P.step (false,1) true (false,0)` unfolds to `(false,0) = next (false,1) true = (false, 1-1)`. The `rfl` closes it because `1 - 1` reduces to `0`.
  - The third `rfl` gives `sense … = o` by the choice of `o`.
- The result is `(false,0).2 ≠ 0`, that is `0 = 0 → False`. The final `rfl : 0 = 0` closes it against `Drain.above`.
- Case `a = false` is symmetric: `next (true,1) false = (true,0)`.
- The `Usable` component is discarded (`_`, ll. 28 and 31).

The proof never uses anything about `sense`. The value `o` is taken to be whatever the step gives, and that is available for every reading. This is not a hidden fault, but it is the main point under question 4: at horizon 1, `KeptFor` does not depend on `sense` at all. `∀ o, ∀ t, Seen sense B a o t → guard t` is equivalent to `∀ s t, B s → step s a t → guard t`. The module does not state this equivalence.

**`twos_kept_level` (ll. 37-39).** This is exactly `Drain.together_kept_level` (KeptForViable l. 406), the same type renamed. It is correct.

**`twos_kept_two_not_three_none` (ll. 43-46).** The pair `⟨together_kept_two_none, together_not_kept_none⟩` has the stated types (DrainOnTheLaw l. 191, KeptForViable l. 347). It is correct.

**`one_lane_kept` (ll. 50-55).** `Drain.lane_kept sense n b : Lane b C → KeptFor … n C`, with `C` inferred as `fun s => s = (b,1)`. `Lane b C` is `∀ x, C x → x.1 = b ∧ x.2 ≠ 0`.
- `congrArg Prod.fst same : x.1 = (b,1).1`, which is definitionally `b`.
- `(congrArg Prod.snd same).symm.trans zero : (b,1).2 = 0`, which is definitionally `1 = 0`, refuted by `succ_not_zero 0`.

The proof is correct.

**`three_cases` (ll. 62-68).** It conjoins the three theorems above. The first conjunct's `∀ {O : Type}` binder matches the implicit `{O}` of `ones_not_kept_one`. It is correct.

**`twos_kept_two` (ll. 72-75).** `KeptFor.finer` (Keeping l. 293) has the form `{F : Type z} {fine : S → A → S → F} (forget : F → O) (consistent : ∀ s a t, forget (fine s a t) = sense s a t) : KeptFor … sense … n B → KeptFor … fine … n B`.
- The named arguments set `sense := noneRead` (so the coarse `O := Unit`) and `fine := sense` (so `F :=` the theorem's `O : Type`, z = 0).
- `forget := fun _ => ()`. `consistent` holds by `rfl`, since both sides reduce to `()`.
- The direction is right: from kept under the coarser reading (`noneRead`) to kept under the finer one (any `sense`).
- Universes: the theorem holds for every `O : Type`, as stated. `finer` would allow every `Type z`, but the module states only `Type`.

It is correct. Content: anything kept under `noneRead` is kept under every reading, because `noneRead` is the coarsest. So the third conjunct carries no information about readings. It is the `noneRead` fact carried over.

**`twos_kept_one` (ll. 79-81).** `KeptFor.shorter` applied to the above. It is correct.

**`one_step_of_slack` (ll. 86-90).** It conjoins the three theorems. It is correct.

**Vacuity and trivial guards.**
- No premise is vacuous. The classes are nonempty, and the guard `above` is not trivial.
- For an empty `O` no `sense : Bool × Nat → Bool → Bool × Nat → O` exists, since S and A are inhabited. The "every reading" statements are vacuous only there, which is harmless.
- **`Usable` contributes nothing in this module.** With `everyMove` and the total, deterministic `Drain.process`, every move is usable from every class (`usable_all`, DrainOnTheLaw l. 185). In `ones_not_kept_one` it is thrown away.
- The only part of the `n+1` clause that does work is its quantifier order: `∃ a` once for the class, then `∀ o`.

  - Proposed names: `ones_fail_at_one_twos_kept_two`, or `level_one_and_level_two_every_reading`.

## 3. The intended telling, clause by clause

| Clause | Theorem |
|---|---|
| "both lanes at level 1 are kept for one step under no reading of steps" | `ones_not_kept_one` (readings `S → A → S → O`, `O : Type`) |
| "each lane alone at level 1 is kept at every horizon under every reading" | `one_lane_kept` |
| "both lanes at level 2 are kept for two steps under every reading" | `twos_kept_two` |
| "at every horizon under the reading that keeps the level reached" | `twos_kept_level` |
| "for two steps and not three under the reading that keeps nothing" | `twos_kept_two_not_three_none` |
| "The reason is of KeptFor's form…" | **No theorem.** A remark about the definition `Viability.KeptFor` (Keeping.lean ll. 98-99: `∃ a, Usable P allowed B a ∧ ∀ o, …`) and `Seen` (l. 80). |

The "reason" sentence has two halves, and only one of them does the work.

- **"The first move is chosen for the whole class."** This is right, and it is the operative part: one `∃ a` for the whole class, before any `o`.
- **"A reading there is of a step, so a value is kept only after a move."** This is right about the definition, but at horizon 1 it is idle. As shown under 1, `KeptFor … 1 B` is the same for every `sense`. So "under no reading of steps" adds nothing to "under `noneRead`". The level-1 failure is the plain fact that each move lowers the unmatched lane to 0 (`next_other`, KeptForViable l. 290), and the class holds both lanes.

What makes the clause about time true is a contrast that the module has only in pieces. A reading of steps can see the start: `sense s a t` takes `s`, so `fun s _ _ => s.1` gives the lane exactly. It still cannot save `ones`, because its value comes after the first move. If the lane were known before the first move, each lane alone would be kept forever (`one_lane_kept`), and `ones` is the union of the two.

**A reading of the START is not of the form `S → A → S → O`.** `KeptFor` has no such reading, and the module never formalises "known before the first move" as a reading. A statement like `∀ o, KeptFor n (fun s => ones s ∧ s.1 = o)` would do it. `one_lane_kept` gives that only after the class is rewritten. So the telling's contrast between "of a step" and "before" rests on a remark and on `one_lane_kept` read as a reading of the start. No theorem says it.

Other limits a careful person would read past:
- "no reading" covers `O : Type` only. It would extend to every universe, but that is not stated.
- "every move allowed" and the nonzero-level guard are premises of every clause. The telling's first sentence does not name them.

## 4. What would make the case trivial or misleading

- **Not kept for 1 step at level 1 is reading-free.** It is "every move loses a lane". With the sense-independence of `KeptFor` at horizon 1 (true, but not stated in the module), "under no reading" adds no content beyond `noneRead`. Telling it as "no reading saves the class" suggests readings were tried and failed, when at this horizon they cannot enter at all.
- **The level-2 contrast is partly about the level, not the reading.**
  - Under `noneRead` the class is kept 2 steps, so both of the "two steps under every reading" are owed to the level alone. `twos_kept_two` is the `noneRead` fact carried over by `finer`.
  - The reading matters only beyond 2: it gives every horizon under `keepsLevel` against 2-not-3 under `noneRead`.
  - So the reading's effect shows only between `twos_kept_level` and `twos_kept_two_not_three_none`, both imported. It does not show between level 1 and level 2.
  - The module's new content (`ones_kept_zero`, `ones_not_kept_one`, `twos_kept_two`, `one_step_of_slack`) contains no comparison in which the reading makes a difference.
- **`Usable` is trivial** throughout (everyMove, total process).

The smallest true sentence the module supports: on `Drain`, with every move allowed and the guard "level ≠ 0", both lanes at level 1 together cannot be kept for one step, whatever reading of steps (into `Type`). The first move is chosen once for both, and each move puts one lane at 0. Yet each lane alone at level 1 is kept forever. At level 2 the same pair is kept two steps with no reading at all, and forever when the reading gives the level after each move. So a reading of steps, even one that tells the lane exactly, comes after the first move, and on this process it can help only where the class survives the first move.

It does not support:
- that readings come too late in general;
- any relation between levels and steps ("one step of slack");
- that a reading taken before the first move would save `ones` (not formalised as a reading);
- anything about `allowed` restricted, or a guard other than `above`;
- any law named "early enough" or "in time".
