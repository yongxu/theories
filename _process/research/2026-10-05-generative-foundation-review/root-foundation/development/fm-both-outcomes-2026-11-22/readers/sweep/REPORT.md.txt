# Two predicate steps at one horizon

*AI-written source read for Ren, 22 November 2026.*

**GuardHorizon proves exclusion twice**, once for `loseStep` and once for `lostStep`. A theorem saying that every state is viable or lost at each horizon was **not found in what was read**. Positive either-or theorems do exist elsewhere. The clearest concern a history fitting or being separated, a stock staying above a need or crossing it, and a finite stretch agreeing throughout or exhibiting a first difference.

**The proposed induction is sound. The scratch coverage proof fits it. The scratch exclusion proof does not fit pointwise disjointness on codes.** A covering code and an exact losing code can name the same class. There is a two-code counterexample below.

## 1. The general law and its bound

`PredicateIteration.lean:77` defines `iter` by `| 0 => V₀` and `| n + 1 => Φ (iter Φ V₀ n)`. Write `V n` and `W n` for the two iterates. For any relation `H` on their predicate spaces, `H V₀ W₀` and `∀ X Y, H X Y → H (Φ X) (Ψ Y)` imply `∀ n, H (V n) (W n)`. Zero is the start and the successor is the step applied to the induction hypothesis. The carriers can even differ. Coverage takes `H X Y := ∀ c, X c ∨ Y c`. Exclusion takes `H X Y := ∀ c, ¬ (X c ∧ Y c)` and **requires its own base**. Neither induction needs monotonicity, lists, decisions or commutation with implication.

The general mathematical statement **already is invariance on a product**. Take `next (X,Y) = (Φ X, Ψ Y)` and `K (X,Y) = H X Y` in `Lasting.lean:474`:

```lean
theorem closed_orbit {K : S → Prop} (closed : ∀ x, K x → K (next x)) {s : S} (held : K s) :
    ∀ n, K (Law.orbit next s n)
```

The orbit's projections obey the two iterate equations. `Tolerance.iter_eq_orbit` at 238 identifies predicate iteration with `Law.orbit`. `Invariant.run` in `Frame.lean:286` and `WindowedWaiting.invariant` at 199 give the run versions. `Tolerance.KeepsInclusionInto.course` at 257 transports pointwise implication between two different steps. `Iteration.imp_iff_iter_gives` in `PredicateIteration.lean:219` relates two iterates through implication to `G` under monotonicity and `CommutesWithImplication Ψ G`. A named theorem concluding a disjunction of two independent `Iteration.iter` terms was **not found in what was read**.

**Only the falling bound is needed for the paired finite result.** Let `N = all.length`. Assume `MonotoneStep Φ`, `∀ c, Φ V₀ c → V₀ c` and `∀ c, V₀ c → c ∈ all`. Split at `N`. The left branch gives `∀ n, V n c` by `iter_iff_all` at 153. The right branch already is `W N c`. Exclusion at `N` rules out both. Moreover, any `W m c` rules out the all-stages branch by exclusion at `m`, hence gives `W N c`. This also bounds the rising side, without commutation or even rising monotonicity. Independently, `iter_within_length` at 255 needs an ascending start, monotonicity, commutation to `G := W N c` and a list covering every `x` satisfying `W₀ x → G`. `Iteration.singleton_rise_needs_em` in `RisingIteration.lean:28` tests unconditional shortening on a singleton.

## 7. Counter-cases and ranking

**There is no counterexample with the stated start and a genuinely preserved `pair`.** The iterate equations preclude one. Three small cases locate the limits.

1. On `Unit`, identity steps and true starts preserve both coverage and disjointness *of inputs*. Both outcomes nevertheless hold at every stage: the disjointness base fails.
2. On `Unit`, let `Φ X := False` everywhere, `Ψ Y := Y`, `V₀ := False` and `W₀ := True`. Exactly one side holds on the entire actual orbit. Universal `pair` fails on the covered input `X := True, Y := False`. Conversely, if both steps ignore their inputs and return `P,Q`, applying `pair` to `True,False` gives exactly `∀ c, P c ∨ Q c`. In this case the induction only repeats the split assumed in the premise.
3. The two synonymous codes in section 2 refute pointwise coded exclusion. The two admitted disturbances in section 3 refute coverage of two universal branches. On one state with an arbitrary guard `p`, an unconditional viable-or-lost theorem would already give `p ∨ ¬ p` at zero.
