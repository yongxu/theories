## 1. The design in ten lines

1. There are N occupied nests, f fast parents, and ℓ grains in the crop.
2. Every parent needs one grain. Each fast parent eats one more when the crop reaches H.
3. Each fast parent books its own nursery and recruits one other nursery while any remain.
4. Every fed parent leaves a basal offspring. A booked nursery can also raise a fast offspring with priority for a nest.
5. A parent that tends a seed gives up that extra offspring. Diverting a booked nursery also costs c grains.
6. The rule reads the fast share and registers up to a fully funded diversions. The blind way registers none.
7. Each tended seed grows q grains from finite soil. A season without planted seed produces an empty crop.
8. Compare starts by fast parents and grain left after feeding. Fewer fast parents and more grain left favour continuation.
9. If q + ⌈H/q⌉ ≥ N, increasing the diversion allowance at any or every season preserves every previously successful prefix.
10. A finite table decides survival for each fixed allowance and detects the first exit from the region any course can keep.

## 2. Definitions

Take integers N, q, H ≥ 1 and c ≥ 0. A state is s = (f, ℓ), with 0 ≤ f ≤ N and 0 ≤ ℓ ≤ qN. The fast share is f/N. The **signed grain balance** is W(s) = ℓ − N − f·1[ℓ ≥ H]. When W(s) < 0, the cohort cannot feed and there is no successor. Otherwise write w = W(s) and choose an integer allowance a between 0 and N. Its successor Fₐ(s) is calculated by

```
m = min(N, 2f)                       booked nurseries
b = N − m                            ordinary planters
j = min(a, m, ⌊max(0, w−b)/(c+1)⌋)   funded diversions
z = min(w, b) + j                    seeds planted
Fₐ(s) = (m−j, qz)
```

Ordinary planters get seed first. Each diversion then pays one seed and c grains for switching. Each parent leaves one basal, nonfast offspring. Each undiverted booked parent leaves an additional fast offspring, displacing one basal offspring from the N nests. A planter spends its second nursery opportunity tending a seed. Thus m−j counts fast offspring in the **new generation**. A fixed way sets the same allowance each season. Its realised diversions depend on the current bookings and the funded bundles it registers. Allowance zero still reads the share to set bookings.

The cycling mineral totals qN. Before ripening, the z planted grains hold one unit each. The other qN−z units are in the soil, including the season’s recycled grain. Each plant mobilises q−1 soil units. There is enough because z ≤ N. Soil after harvest is qN−qz. Fixed yield and seasonal recycling are model assumptions.

Write **s ≼ t** when fₛ ≥ fₜ and W(s) ≤ W(t). This is a preorder, and an order on the pairs (f, W) after equal pairs are identified. Let L(s) contain the infinite allowance sequences whose every finite prefix can be taken from s. A fixed allowance a keeps s when its constant sequence belongs to L(s). A state is past return when L(s) is empty.

## 3. The main statement

**Comparison and exact fate.** Assume q + k ≥ N, where k = ⌈H/q⌉. If s ≼ t and uᵢ ≤ vᵢ at every season, each successful prefix of u from s has a successful prefix of v from t of the same length, with ordered endpoints. Hence u ∈ L(s) implies v ∈ L(t).

For fixed a, initialise a table B₀(f) = 0. In the update, calculate m, j and z from §2 at the candidate input w, including balances that no physical start realises. Set U = N(c+1) and take the minimum of an empty set to be ∞.

```
Bₙ₊₁(f) = min { w ∈ {0,…,U} | W(m−j, qz) ≥ Bₙ(m−j) }
Bᵃ       = the stationary table for allowance a
```

Then **a keeps s exactly when W(s) ≥ Bᵃ(f). Some course keeps s exactly when W(s) ≥ Bᴺ(f).** The blind way is least in the allowance order and ends from every positive fast count. From a physically viable start, any fixed way that ends has a first successor with W < Bᴺ(f). Every possible continuation from there ends, even if another brood can still be born. An initially unrecoverable start is already outside at season zero.

**Premises.** The integer ranges, finite nests and soil, feeding bill, recruitment, offspring priority, nursery cost, funding order, yield, available moves, and q+k ≥ N are structural. The initial preorder and pointwise allowance order are structural comparison hypotheses. Initial physical viability restricts the first-exit clause and is independently decided by its table. No premise assumes a fall, lock, rescue, surviving course, or monotone crop step. The rule for reading funded bundles is supplied.

**Proof of comparison.** The total planter count is

```
p = b+j = min(N, b+a, b+⌊max(0,w−b)/(c+1)⌋).
```

It increases weakly in a, w and b. Increasing b by one can lower the floor by at most one. Thus the favourable side has weakly more planters, weakly fewer new fast parents f′ = N−p, and weakly more planted seeds z = min(w,p).

The difficult step is the **next feeding**. On the same side of H, crop and fast counts give the required order of balances immediately. If the larger crop alone triggers extra feeding, it has at least k plants and at least one more plant than the smaller crop. Its grain advantage is at least q. Its extra bill is at most its fast count, which is at most N−z ≤ N−k. Each planted seed occupied a nursery that could have raised a fast sibling. Thus the grain advantage pays the bill, including at equality. Induction gives the prefix comparison.

**Proof of exact fate.** Row zero gives the balance needed to take one step. If row n gives the requirement for n+1 steps, the update asks whether the new brood can take those steps. Successor comparison makes the acceptable balances an upper interval for each f, with minimum weakly increasing in f. The same algebra applies to any formal w ≥ 0. All opportunities saturate by U. The rows increase with the horizon in the finite chain 0,…,U,∞, so they stabilise. Each fixed way is deterministic. Comparing any successful sequence with the constant maximal allowance gives the any-course equivalence. A failing fixed way reaches a terminal balance in finite time, so it must have a first exit if it began inside. Under allowance zero each successful harvest sets f′ = min(N,2f). A positive fast count either encounters a shortfall or fills the bookings, leaving zero plants and an empty next crop.

## 4. Which of R1 to R7 it has

| Requirement | What the construction gives |
|---|---|
| R1 | Parents leave competing offspring. Both next season’s fast share and its crop depend on what this generation forms. Nest capacity is fixed. |
| R2 | The finite crop comes back through living planted seeds. Seed, crop and soil balance. Yield and seasonal recycling are supplied. |
| R3 | Tending forgoes the parent’s own extra offspring. The common reserve bears the separate c-grain switching charge. |
| R4 | Bookings are rebuilt from offspring share. Blind recruitment locks from any positive fast count that keeps feeding long enough. |
| R5 | Bᴺ decides whether any course keeps a state, including states where another feeding is still possible. |
| R6 | The rule registers share and funded diversions up to its allowance. It does not revise its reading after a failed act. |
| R7 | The law compares whole sets of future allowance sequences. §5 gives a strict opening along a surviving course. Indefinitely many new forms or successive strict openings are outside the result. |

The reason for this cut is that the offspring forgone by a keeper bounds what the next generation can consume. That connection is where the comparison can fail.

## 7. Where it is weakest

The nursery must enforce f′+z ≤ N. Letting a nursery send out a fast offspring and tend a plant in the same season removes the reason comparison works. The T3 alteration allowing both already reverses a survival verdict. Overlapping recruitment, asynchronous harvest, and grain carried between seasons also fall outside the statement. The spacing bound is sharp to one grain as a uniform guarantee in the exhibited family. Necessity for each individual ecology has not been shown.

An in-bound pair with ordered fast counts, signed balances and allowances, but a successful lower prefix the upper course cannot match, would refute the comparison. A physical-state oracle disagreeing with either stationary-table equivalence would refute the classification. A real application would first have to establish the brood-versus-tending opportunity cost and the induced feeding bill. The evidence here is mathematical and computational.
