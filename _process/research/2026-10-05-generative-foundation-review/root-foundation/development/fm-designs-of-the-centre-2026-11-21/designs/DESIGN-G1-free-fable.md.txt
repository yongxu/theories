## 1. The design in ten lines

**Kind of design.** A race, on one course, between two things that the same multiplying runs down: the level, and the menu (who is still there to be formed). The lock is not a rule in this design. It is the menu having run out: what the selection stopped forming is no longer there to be formed. None of the six other angles is this: (1) nobody sets a rule and there is no permission to set one, the favouring is fixed and what changes is who is there; (2) no kind supports the forming of another kind, kinds meet only through one level; (3) the statement orders two first steps of one course, not courses with each other; (4) keepable is used only to say what the menu's running out costs, the future is not the carrier; (5) the law was written first, from the alignment paragraph of `AGENTS.md`, and then searched, not read off a search; (6) no closure law at a relation is used, the proofs are an induction on doubling and arithmetic of the level.

1. A configuration is a way `w` (a list of units, each of a kind, at most `N` of them) and a level `x ≤ C`.
2. Each kind has a need, a use, a giving and kids. The line on a stock's step is kept: units whose need the level meets leave their kids as candidates, and every unit takes its use.
3. Nothing is given but by units: the level after a step is what is left plus what the met units give, at most what is left, capped at `C`.
4. A step is a choice of which candidates are formed, at most `N` (the slots). A way of selecting is a rule for that choice from what it registers of the candidates and the level.
5. `F` is a set of kinds closed under kids that give nothing. A selection that registers only what a candidate leaves (its yield) forms `F` first, where giving costs yield.
6. Main statement, any way of selecting: the course never ends exactly where a unit outside `F` is formed at every step. At the first step `E` where none is, the configuration is kept by no way of selecting, whatever the level (it can be `C`).
7. Which of the two, where `F` is formed first, multiplies and has one need: by step `t̂ = ⌈log₂(N/n₀)⌉` at the latest, either the level has fallen below `F`'s need (step `D`) or `F`'s candidates have filled the slots (`E`). `E ≤ D`: ends. `D < E`: `F` is no longer formed and the course is the rest's own.
8. In a two-kind family the race has a closed form: the level drawn is `c·(n_t − n₀)/(k − 1)`, a fixed price for each favoured unit made, so the slots are filled exactly where the level above `F`'s need pays for the share. More level ends what less level keeps, as the law's content.
9. Under free selection more level closes nothing (keepable is upward closed in the level, way by way). It is forming whatever the level admits that makes a higher start end.
10. Tried on 3.9 million courses of small systems and 339,088 starts of the family: no failure of the statements as stated; failures when a premise is dropped.

## 2. Definitions

Names in code font with a namespace are the Lean's. The rest are new.

```
structure System (U : Type) where        -- U finite, decidable equality
  need use give : U → Nat
  kids  : U → List U
  slots : Nat                            -- N
  cap   : Nat                            -- C

-- configuration s = (w, x) : List U × Nat          (as LineOnAStock)
met   (w, x) := w.filter (fun u => need u ≤ x)
cand  (w, x) := LineOnAStock.after need kids w x          -- kids of the met units
left  (w, x) := x - LineOnAStock.taken use w              -- truncated; every unit of w takes
given (w, x) := total (met (w, x)) give
level' (w, x) := min cap (left + min left given)
Goes  := LineOnAStock.Goes need                           -- some unit's need is met

-- the process with moves (a Process (List U × Nat) (List U) of the package)
step s m t := Goes s ∧ m.Sublist (cand s) ∧ m.length ≤ slots ∧ t = (m, level' s)
Keepable s := ∀ n, GuardHorizon.Viable P Goes n s

structure Selecting where                                  -- a way of selecting
  pick : List U → Nat → List U                             -- from the candidates and the level
  sub  : ∀ c x, (pick c x).Sublist c
  fits : ∀ c x, (pick c x).length ≤ slots
next σ s := (σ.pick (cand s) s.2, level' s)
course σ s t := next σ iterated t times from s             -- write (w_t, x_t)
NeverEnds σ s := ∀ t, Goes (course σ s t)
```

- **Registering.** `σ` registers through `ρ : List U → Nat → O` when `ρ c x = ρ c' x'` gives `pick c x` and `pick c' x'` the same positions. Yield only: `ρ c x = c.map (fun u => (kids u).length)`. Others used in instances: what a candidate gives; yield and whether the level is below `L`; yield and whether forming by yield would leave no giver.
- **`F : U → Prop`** (decidable), with premises
  - H1 (closed): `u ∈ F → ∀ v ∈ kids u, v ∈ F`.
  - H2 (multiplies): `u ∈ F → 2 ≤ (kids u).length`.
  - H3 (gives nothing, lives on the level): `u ∈ F → give u = 0 ∧ 1 ≤ use u ∧ 1 ≤ need u`.
  - H5 (one need): `u, v ∈ F → need u = need v`.
- **`FFirst σ c x`**: with `cF := c.filter F`, if `N ≤ cF.length` then `pick c x` has `N` units, all in `F`; otherwise `cF.Sublist (pick c x)`.
- **First steps of a course.** `D` := least `t` with no `F` unit of `w_t` met at `x_t`. `E` := least `t` with no unit outside `F` in `w_{t+1}`. `R` := least `t` with `¬ FFirst σ (cand (w_t, x_t)) x_t`. Each may not exist. `n₀` := the number of `F` units in `w₀`; `t̂` := least `t` with `N ≤ 2^(t+1)·n₀`.
- **`θ w`** := the least `x ≤ C` with `Keepable (w, x)`, if any (well defined as a threshold by Lemma 1).
- **Corner.** With a handed-in giving `γ` added (`level' = left + γ left + …`), `give = 0`, no cap, no slots and `pick c x = c`, `next` is `LineOnAStock.step need kids γ use`. The statements below are at `γ = 0`.

The program keeps ways as multisets; need, use, giving and kids depend on the kind only.

## 3. The main statement

**Theorem.** Let `F` satisfy H1 and H3. For every way of selecting `σ` and every start `(w₀, x₀)`:

(a) *Dichotomy.* `NeverEnds σ (w₀, x₀)` exactly where `E` does not exist (a unit outside `F` is formed at every step). If `E` exists: every unit of `w_s` is in `F` for all `s > E`; `¬ Goes (course σ s₀ t)` for some `t ≤ E + 1 + x_{E+1}`; and `¬ Keepable (w_{E+1}, x_{E+1})`, so no way of selecting goes on from there. Nothing bounds `x_{E+1}` below `C`.

(b) *Which.* Add H2, H5 and `1 ≤ n₀`. Then one of `D`, `E`, `R` exists and is at most `t̂`. So for a `σ` that forms `F` first at every step (`R` absent), exactly one holds:
  - (I) `E ≤ D` (or `D` absent): the course ends as in (a);
  - (II) `D < E` (or `E` absent): no `F` unit is in `w_{D+1}` (and none ever after, if the kinds outside `F` are closed under kids too); the course never ends exactly where `E` is absent;

  and which of the two is settled by step `t̂`: by whether the level falls below `F`'s need before `F`'s candidates fill the slots.

(c) *Closed form in a family.* Kinds `T` (need `a`, use `c ≥ 1`, gives 0, kids `k ≥ 2` copies of `T`) and `K` (need `b`, `1 ≤ b ≤ a`, use `d`, gives `d`, kids `[K]`), with `a ≥ 2(c + d)N`; `σ` forms `T` first and then every `K` that fits; start `n₀ ≥ 1` of `T`, `g₀ ≥ 1` of `K`, level `x₀ ≤ C`. Let `S t = c·n₀·(k^t − 1)/(k − 1)` and `t*` the least `t` with `N ≤ n₀·k^(t+1)`. While `T` is met and the slots are not filled, `x_t + c·n_t/(k − 1)` is constant. Then:
  - if `a ≤ x₀ − S t*`: `E = t*` and the course ends;
  - otherwise let `D` be the least `t` with `x₀ − S t < a`, `x_D = x₀ − S D`, `n_D = n₀·k^D`, `g_D = g₀` if `D = 0` and `min g₀ (N − n_D)` otherwise, `ℓ = x_D − (c·n_D + d·g_D)` (truncated), `x' = min C (ℓ + min ℓ (d·g_D))`. The course never ends exactly where `b ≤ x_D` and `max b (2·d·g_D) ≤ x'`.

**Lemma 1 (more level closes nothing, under free selection).** `Viable P Goes n (w, x)` and `x ≤ x'` give `Viable P Goes n (w, x')`. So `Keepable (w, x) ↔ θ w ≤ x`, and a course's step of no return is the first `t` with `x_t < θ w_t` (no `θ` counted as above every level).

**Lemma 2 (the menu only narrows).** The kinds of `w_{t+1}` are among the kids of the kinds of `w_t`. If no kind in the closure of `w`'s kinds under kids gives, and each uses and needs at least one, then `¬ Viable P Goes x (w, x)`: no selection goes on, at any level.

**Lemma 3 (registered alike, selected alike).** If `pick` does not depend on the level, and for every `j < n` the levels of two courses from `(w, x)` and `(w, x')` (also under two uses, givings or caps) meet the same needs among the units of the first course's way, the two ways are the same at every `j ≤ n`.

**Proof sketches.**

- *Lemma 2 and (a), second half.* A way all in `F` stays in `F` (H1; `pick` is a sublist of the candidates). If `Goes (w, x)` then some need `≥ 1` is met, so `x ≥ 1`; `w` is not empty and each unit uses at least one, so `left ≤ x − 1`; `given = 0`; so `level' ≤ x − 1`. The level falls strictly while the course goes, and at nothing no need is met: `¬ Goes` within `x` steps, for every choice of moves.
- *(a), first half.* If a unit is in `w_{t+1}`, the candidates at `t` are not empty, so a unit of `w_t` was met: `Goes` at `t`. So forming something at every step is going on. Conversely if `E` exists the way at `E + 1` is all in `F`, and the paragraph above ends it.
- *(b).* Suppose `D > t̂` and `R > t̂`. For `t ≤ t̂` some `F` unit of `w_t` is met, so by H5 all are, and by H1 and H2 the `F` candidates at `t` number at least `2·n_t`. `FFirst` gives `n_{t+1} ≥ min N (2·n_t)`, so `n_t ≥ min N (2^t·n₀)`, and at `t̂` the `F` candidates number at least `N`: `pick` is all in `F` and `E ≤ t̂`. The two cases then follow from (a) and the definition of `D`.
- *(c).* While `x_t ≥ a`: every unit is met (`b ≤ a`), `left ≥ a − (c + d)N ≥ (c + d)N ≥ d·g_t`, so the level after is `x_t − c·n_t`, and `n_{t+1} = k·n_t` while `k·n_t < N`: the geometric sum gives `S`. At `D` the `T`s are unmet and leave nothing, all units still take, and the `K`s alone hold a level exactly from `max b (2·d·g)` up (below `2dg` what is left is less than what they take and the level halves its margin each step).
- *Lemma 1.* Induction on `n`. `Goes` is monotone in the level. `cand (w, x)` is a sublist of `cand (w, x')`, so the same move is open. `left`, `given` and so `level'` are monotone in the level. The induction hypothesis applies at the same way and the higher level. With a handed-in `γ` it needs `z + γ z` monotone.
- *Lemma 3.* Induction on `n` as in `LineOnAStock.ways_alike` (`after_alike`), with `pick` applied to equal candidates.

## 4. R1 to R7

- **R1, has.** Units leave units (`kids`), and the level's coming back is the doing of units that can be lost.
- **R2, has, with a chosen form.** The level is capped, runs down, comes back by at most what is left, and only by met units of the model. The form `min left given` and the numbers `give u` are handed in.
- **R3, half.** Keeping costs only as a relation between parameters: a giver leaves fewer kids than a taker, which is what makes a yield-only selection form `F` first. There is no budget inside a unit.
- **R4, half.** There is a selecting among candidates, and the candidates are the kids of what was formed, so who can be favoured next is set from who was. The exclusion of the rest comes about (part (b)) and is not a premise. But the favouring itself is fixed: no rule is re-read from a share, as `RuleCapture`'s `h` is.
- **R5, has.** Moves, `Keepable`, a step of no return (`E + 1` in (a); `θ` in Lemma 1).
- **R6, first half.** What is registered decides the outcome in built instances, and `R` puts any reading in (b) as a third first step. Revision of the reading by where its act failed: left out. The only response to the fall is that unmet units leave nothing.
- **R7, left out.** Lemma 1 says more level closes nothing under free selection; nothing here says more can go on after.

The cut: the lock and the turn as two first steps of one course, with the giver inside the model. I judged R4's "comes about" and R2's giver to be what every earlier try lacked, and left the reading's revision and R7 for a model that has those two first.

## 7. Where it is weakest

1. **Part (a) is thin.** Once `F` is assumed unable to go on alone (H3), "never ends exactly where something outside `F` is formed at every step" is immediate. The content is the model, the bound in (b), the closed form, and Lemma 1. A reader may fairly call (b) "an exclusion lemma, then the old ending", new lemmas in a row.
2. **No rule is set again.** If the lock means a rule re-read from its share, R4 is not had; what stays is a fixed favouring that empties the menu.
3. **The turn in (b) is by loss.** The favoured whose need is unmet leave nothing and the rest go on. On 19 November that was read as a matter of grain. A turn by reading enters only as `R`, and what the course does after `R` is not characterised; three simple readings each keep under half of what can be kept.
4. **T5 fails on the choice side**, and on the lock side gives one small case.
5. **The line on a stock's convention** that every unit takes whether or not the level bears it makes (c) need its side condition.
6. **The form of what comes back and the cost are chosen**, not derived. (a), (b) and Lemma 1 survived the other form tried; (c) would change.
7. **Lemma 1 is a threshold**, one for each way. If the brief's T6 is read as forbidding that too, it is refuted by nothing I found, but it would fail with a handed-in giving for which `z + γ z` falls.

What would refute it: a system meeting H1 and H3 where a course goes on past a step that forms nothing outside `F`; one meeting H1 to H5 with none of `D`, `E`, `R` by `t̂`; a disagreement with (c) inside its side condition; a law already in the Lean of exclusion by slots, or of keepable being upward closed in the level under choice; or a reading of (b) as `LineOnAStock.need_unmet` with Price's `share_step` in other names.
