## 1. Verdict in five lines

Design A survives as a built case worth keeping, with a local law of transfer.  
Law E derives when control can pass from F to O in its two-kind model.  
Corollary L and Theorem M(i–ii) survived tests beyond the designer’s box.  
The strongest limit is that graded setters can lose control while some live on, and discarding those survivors can reverse the verdict on keeping.  
The readable Theorem M(iii) also lacks two existence conditions on its stated domain.

The claimed change of reading changes the organism.** At `hi=4`, `g(1)=1`, needs `(4,1)`, from `(1,1,3)`, the F rules `(2,0), (2,1), (2,2)` gave **ended / kept / ended**. **LESSER**

- The independent `hi=4` ablations reproduced **120** changed starts with F fixed, **16,716** changed worlds with every rule available, and **15,792** worlds with a kept locked start after adding hold. In the first witness, share control holds `(0,2,3)` fixed while fixed-F control goes to `(0,4,3) → (0,8,0)`. Allowing *all* rules also enlarges the menu. Removing positive needs gave `(1,0,1) → (2,0,0) → (4,0,0) → …`, still guarded. Multiplication, the need test and who sets each do work. A reading does no work in single-start M.
- The designer’s “share” program observes the ordered pair of counts. I checked the fraction separately. At `hi=5` both reproduced **1,266** of **1,270** individually kept pairs at horizon ten, equal to full observation; the constant reading kept **978**. The designer’s reported failing pair `(1,1,3),(1,1,5)` was individually kept and jointly lost even under full observation, which still has to take a common first move. These are finite-horizon results.
- `Exit` leaves its move variable free in the displayed definition; I used the adjoining existential gloss. `CarrierCounts.raises` has a positive other count in both of its `CarrierCounts` arguments. The zero-count and zero-offspring cases of E use direct arithmetic. Also, L’s “F set at every step” requires an F-setting start.

## 4. Does a general law do work that the old laws do not?

**Yes, locally.** The old share inequality compares reproduction coefficients, and the old need test tells which units leave offspring. E uses both to rule out every F-to-O exit while all present F are met. L also gives a converse witness, deciding exactly when the whole F-setting region is closed. An old declaration giving that exit characterization was **not found in what I read**. M(ii) then cuts a kept course at its first exit, using bounded multiplication to force one. Grading the setters separates those contributions: the cut survives, but the O-only state that made it readable disappears. The design gives no unchanged old law from each side as required by T5, and no law of more becoming possible after the turn.

## 5. The specific questions

**(a) Graded setters.** Give low- and high-need F their own same-kind descendants, the same F multiplier, and count both in the share. **Yes**, some *present* setter must miss its need at an exit. If all are met, their summed count is exactly `kF·f` and O’s next count is at most `kO·o`. Of **59,904** F steps, **5,880** exited, **1,512** retained setters and **0** violated this weaker E.

**M(ii)’s equivalence with a kept whole successor survives.** While F keep setting on a guarded course, a present F type of least need doubles each step until its need fails. At that point every remaining F need fails, so staying F empties the population. This forces a finite first exit on a kept course. The order consequence is `nO` below the need of *some* setter lost at that exit. There were **0** equivalence or deadline failures on **44,928** graded starts. With needs `(1,6,2)`, F rule `(2,2)`, O hold and `H(z)=hi` for positive residuals up to the cap, the output below is in order `(F_low,F_high,O,x)`.

| Cap | Course from the same start | Whole successor kept | O-only projection kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | `(1,1,2,5) → (2,0,4,7) → (2,0,4,7)` | true | true |
| 6 | `(1,1,2,5) → (2,0,4,6) → (2,0,4,0)` | false | true |

**(b) Giving by a third kind.** **Yes if only the stock update changes.** I gave G a positive need, unit draw and same-kind children. Each met G adds two units when a positive residual remains, capped at `hi`. With G outside the vote, E had **0 failures in 13,824 F steps, 576 exits**. It compares counts before the new level is used. G nevertheless determines survival. In order `(F,O,G,x)`, `(0,1,1,3)` holds, `(0,1,0,3)` drains to zero, and from `(0,1,1,2)` the giver misses its need of 3 and disappears.

If G votes with O, the comparison must cover G’s offspring too. With multiplier triple `(2,1,3)` and needs `(1,1,3)`, the output was `(1,0,1,5) → (2,0,3,5)`. F lose the vote while every setter is met. There were **113** such all-F-met transfers in this variant, **0** when `kF ≥ max(kO,kG)`. With that dominance, an exit still removes F and requires a reproducing member of the other voting side, which may be G. The counterexample changes the electorate as well as the giving.

**(c) Quotas, bounds and excluded rules.** The legal E/L sweep included quotas `1/9, 2/5, 1/2, 3/4, 16/17, 97/101, 1`, counts through 97 and levels through 40. At `16/17`, needs `(19,9)`, `(16,1,9) —(4,1)→ (0,1,0)` exits. At unanimity a mixed state cannot start under F. E is independent of the cap. L is a closure assertion on its specified levels: with `hi=4`, needs `(9,7)`, there is no in-band exit, but `(1,1,7) → (0,1,5)` exits above it.

M survived larger legal caps **8, 14, 25**, with counts through `hi+2`. A kept exit can occur at **T=2**: `(1,1,12) → (2,1,10) → (4,1,7) → (0,1,2) → (0,1,2)`, needs `(9,1)`, `g(1)=1`. Dropping `x₀ ≤ hi` breaks both M(i) and M(ii). With `hi=1`, equal needs 1, `H(z)=z` through 1 and `4z` above 1, F rule `(2,0)`, the locked course begins `(1,0,8) → (2,0,28) → (4,0,104) → (8,0,400)`. The inequality `x ≥ 2f` persists and the course lives forever without an exit.

A rule with `kO>kF` is legal when **O** set. Admitting it when **F** set breaks E, L, the need ordering and M(iii)’s mark. With `hi=9`, equal needs 1, `H(z)=9` on positive residuals in band, F rule `(2,3)` and O hold, `(1,1,9) → (2,3,9) → (2,3,9)`. On **112,464** such relaxed F starts, M(i) and the whole-successor equivalence had **0** failures; `Kept → nO<nF` had **53**. Multiplication still forces departure on a kept course, but unmet need need no longer be its cause.

**(d) O-only upward closure.** The larger monotone results are in part 2. The required monotonicity is that of `H`. Dropping it breaks the threshold even with only hold available to O. At `hi=7`, `g(1)=2`, needs `(7,1)`, the kept levels for two O were **[3,5,7]**. From 3 they stay at 3. From 4 the levels go **4 → 2 → 0**. This also refutes the relaxed M(iii) on a real exit: `(1,1,6) —(2,2)→ (0,2,4)`. The first three exit clauses hold, `PNR(2)=3 ≤ 4`, and the start is lost.
