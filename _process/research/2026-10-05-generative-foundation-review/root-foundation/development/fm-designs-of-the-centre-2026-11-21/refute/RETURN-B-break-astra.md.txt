## 1. Verdict in five lines

Design B survives as a law about one-step populations and as a built case worth keeping.  
Its standing-population criterion does real work.  
It does not establish the proposed centre.  
A closing course can lose the formation and preserve the ability to restart it indefinitely.  
With lives of two or three steps, genuine compulsory change survives, but the equivalence that identified it breaks.

## 2. Statement by statement

## 3. MUST, then LESSER

**MUST**

1. Separate uninterrupted keeping from the ability to restart. The proof of B5(b) has established backward propagation of viability across a **live** predecessor. Its next sentence drops that condition. In the counterexample, even a closing choice from a viable start can reach a state where the formation is absent and the means of restarting it are intact.
2. Keep B4's existential quantifier in the telling of the main result. Reaching a standing population puts a keeping move within reach. The current selection may still choose a different move. B4 also gives no rule for when the selection itself changes.
3. Replace eventual periodicity in B6 by repetition of a state. One free kind with `R=c=1, C=2` admits all four transitions among counts 1 and 2. Choose 2 exactly at positive powers of two. The program prints `1,2,2,1,2,1,1,1,2,…`. The gaps are unbounded and 2 recurs infinitely often.
4. Qualify T7's coverage. The designer's `main_search.py` skips the free-support check whenever the costly kernel is empty. For the reported partner formation, my rebuild found **11,156** such laws with a viable free course in A and **29,872** in B. Checking the free conclusion on every law in both families gave zero counterexamples. The reported one-mechanism finding reproduces inside those families. It fails as a description of case (iii) outside them.

**LESSER**

- The hard weighted example needs its ground cap raised from 2 to 4. Doubling every cap is sufficient. Raising the ground cap alone to give `C=(4,2,1)` produced `standing [(4,2,1)]`.
- Ranking by share enforces closing only if the allocation excludes zero-share kinds. With mutual unit recipes, unit capacities and caps, a greedy rule ranking *all* kinds by share gives `(1,0) → (0,1)`. The designer's reported closing-rule experiment explicitly excludes absent kinds and reproduces.
- R1's claim about where tokens come from needs an exception for initial tokens and permitted zero recipes. R2's permanent loss of the ground is a feature of a ground that requires itself. The law permits other recipes. Formally `Config` also permits an over-cap initial state. All successors are capped, which suffices for the proofs.
- T5 already records the failed bridge. One further qualification is that `GuardHorizon.closed_viable` concludes viability at every finite horizon. The infinite course at a standing state here is supplied directly by its self-loop.

## 4. Does a general law do work that the old laws do not?

Yes, at the level of this model. A generic viability certificate can use a population already known to stand. B3 goes the other way and **constructs** a standing population from persistent presence on a course. B6 constructs one under two changes to the resource rules. Those constructions use the recipe inequalities and can fail when the lifetime changes. That work was not found in what I read of the older statements. The design gives no instantiation deriving an unchanged old lock law or October's renewal theorem from its construction. Closing supplies the restriction on what can be selected from the outset. The resulting law distinguishes ways to keep a population, but it does not derive a lock, irreversible resource loss, or growth of the future.

## 5. The specific questions

**(a) Persistence.** I used exact remaining lifetimes. Every currently live token can supply its per-step capacity. Survivors age compulsorily, births enter the youngest cohort, and closing forbids a birth of a kind absent across all current cohorts. I tried both meanings of the original cap that coincide at lifetime one: a bound on births per step and a bound on total living tokens per kind. Standing means a self-loop of the full age configuration. A young and an old token have different possible futures.

**Genuine case (iii) survives.** In the following exact finite graphs, `P` means some formation kind can stay present forever. Each law has one ground kind and a ring of formation kinds. The ground's recipe needs `r` ground acts. Each ring birth needs `h` ground acts and one act from its predecessor around the ring. Ground capacity is `c₀`. All other capacities and all caps are one.

| Lifetime | Cap | Ring kinds | `(r,h,c₀)` | States | V count | H count | S count | `P` | Witness period |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 2 | Total stock | 2 | (1,2,2) | 27 | 12 | 0 | 0 | false | 4 |
| 3 | Total stock | 3 | (1,2,2) | 256 | 180 | 0 | 0 | false | 9 |
| 2 | Births | 3 | (1,1,1) | 256 | 182 | 0 | 0 | false | 6 |
| 3 | Births | 2 | (2,2,1) | 512 | 234 | 0 | 0 | false | 6 |

For the first row, writing youngest cohort first, the program returned the cycle `001|100 → 100|001 → 010|100 → 100|010 → 001|100`. Closing cannot keep either partner. The unmodified designer's L* behaves differently at lifetime two. With a stock cap it has `(|V|,|H|,|S|)=(16,6,0)`. With a birth cap it has `(45,26,1)`.

**B3, B4 and B5 do not transfer together.** With a stock cap of one, a single self-replacing kind can alternate young and old forever. The output for life two, under both costly and free support, was `states/V/H/S/P = 3/2/2/0/True`. Its count is always one. This refutes B3, B4's equivalence and B5(a–c) under the formal definition of case (iii). With stock caps even B4's up-sets can fail. For the first ring above, `101|000` is viable and `111|000` is not. For rows `((0,1),(1,1))`, capacities `(3,1)`, caps `(2,1)` and `D={0}`, `11|00` admits closing keeping and `21|00` does not.

Birth caps remove that crowding effect, but the equivalences still fail. At life two, take rows `((0,2),(1,1))`, unit caps and capacities, and `D={1}`. Output was `16/9/0/0/True`. Kind 1 remains present on `01|01 → 10|01 → 01|10 → 01|01`, while the helper disappears and returns. Here every closing selection ends, yet the formation kind never has to disappear. At life three, rows `((0,2),(0,2))` and `D={0}` gave `64/24/24/0/True`, refuting even the first-exit claim for formal case (iii). B4 can fail with a standing state elsewhere: at life two, both rows `(0,1)`, all parameters one, `D={0}`, the closing cycle `01|10 ↔ 10|01` cannot reach the only standing state `11|11`. Both birth-cap up-sets do survive, since a larger start can replay the same births until the extra tokens expire. B5(d) survives both cap conventions: summing a cycle preserves the age-shift equalities as well as the resource inequalities. All four displayed ring folds passed.

**B6 depends on which meaning of case (iii) is intended.** Without caps, a keeping course still gives an exact age-stationary population. Apply Dickson to the age vectors and sum a self-covering stretch. Write `x₀,…,xₗ₋₁` for the summed source cohorts, youngest first. Compulsory aging and self-covering give `x₀ ≥ x₁ ≥ … ≥ xₗ₋₁`. Summed births dominate `x₀`. Putting `x₀` in *every* age cohort therefore supplies at least the original summed acts and asks for at most the summed births. It stands and is live. The original use of downward closure cannot be copied because survivors cannot be discarded.

With free support and birth caps the maximum around a cycle respects the age shifts and birth caps, so an exact standing state exists. With free support and **stock** caps the unary young/old cycle refutes that conclusion. Nevertheless, **genuinely compulsory change still requires both a finite cap and a cost** for these common fixed lifetimes. Under free stock support take the kinds recurring on a live cycle, start them together at their stock caps and renew them together at expiry. Each recurring kind had a birth on the cycle, each required supporter recurred there, and the synchronized full stock supplies every required threshold. This gives a closing course with constant kinds. Its ages can rotate indefinitely.

**Until used.** I let a token depart only when it supplies an act. Zero acts and zero births are allowed. Every live state then has a closing self-loop. All 972 law–formation pairs checked per cost convention gave `V=H=S=Live`. B3 and B4 hold immediately, B5 has no case (iii), and both B6 conclusions follow by idling. A compulsory maintenance drain would be an additional rule.

**(b) First failing proof steps.** In original B3(2⇒1), different coordinates may attain their minima at different times. That is harmless: for each supporter `j`, choose its own minimizing time `tⱼ`. The same vector of minima lies below *every* successor on the tail. That proves the budget inequality for row `j`. The held kind's minimum is positive, and one capped successor on the tail already bounds the minimum. In the age version the first failure is that a kind present at all times may have minimum zero in **every age cohort**. For original B6, the uncapped argument works as written. In the free argument the first false step is eventual periodicity. Two equal states at distinct times suffice to fold the intervening cycle. The powers-of-two course in part 3 separates these claims.

**(c) How rare, and a second mechanism.** I reproduced **16/21,952** and **36/59,584** in the designer's three-kind families, all with the reported recipe support pattern. With four kinds I also exhausted all nonempty Boolean recipe rows, caps one, capacities one or two and `D={1,2,3}`: **376/810,000** laws were in case (iii), about **0.046%**. Uniform nonempty Boolean recipe draws with caps one or two gave **0/6,000** at each of four and five kinds. Mixed-weight draws with entries up to three gave **0/4,000** at each size. Those zeros depend on how laws were drawn.

A second mechanism is a ground that recovers between expensive births. In a four-kind example it doubles up to four tokens. The first marker costs seven ground acts, and each later marker requires only its predecessor. With all three markers in `D` the cycle is `(1,1,0,0) → (2,0,1,0) → (4,0,0,1) → (1,1,0,0)`. Output was `|V|=23, |H|=|S|=0`. Removing **any one** marker from `D` gave `|V|=0`. At five kinds, ground cap eight and first-marker cost fifteen give the four phases with ground counts `1,2,4,8`, `|V|=103`, and the same deletion result for all four markers. There is no return edge from a later marker to an earlier one. In the weighted relay grids the frequencies were **32/720** and **33/720**.

Even dependence *between* formation kinds is dispensable. With two independent doubling grounds, each capped at two, each can issue its own terminal marker for three acts. Alternating which ground spends gives `(2,1,0,1) ↔ (1,2,1,0)` in order `(ground₀,ground₁,marker₀,marker₁)`. All 36 states gave `|V|=9, |H|=|S|=0, P=false`. Neither marker supports anything.

**(d) Every start and every closing course.** Yes for a first exit from `V`. I repeatedly removed states of `V` with no remaining closing successor. A nonempty remainder would exhibit a closing course staying in `V` forever. There were **zero** such courses across all 200 configurations of A's case-(iii) laws, 640 of B's, 6,016 of the exhaustive Boolean four-kind cases, and 1,648 and 3,440 of the four- and five-kind relay cases. The largest first-exit times in A, B and the two relay grids were respectively **2, 3, 6, 7**. Under closing, an absent formation stays absent, and a live predecessor of a viable successor is viable. Thus every later state on that closing course also lies outside `V`.

No for permanent loss of the capacity to restart. Take three kinds `(g,a,b)`, rows `((1,0,0),(3,0,0),(0,1,0))`, capacities `(2,1,1)`, caps `(2,1,1)`, and `D={a,b}`. The program returned `|V|=5, |H|=|S|=0` and the keeping cycle `(1,1,0) ↔ (2,0,1)`. From its first state a closing choice leads to `(2,0,0)`. This has a closing self-loop and an open move **back to `(1,1,0)`**. Wait any number of steps before restarting. The original uninterrupted course is lost at the first wait. The ability to start another survives every wait. All 65 case-(iii) laws in the two relay grids admitted indefinite waiting with restart available.

**(e) Never previously present.** In B, at most `|K| − |supp(n₀)|` kinds can appear for the first time. A particular start can require a first-ever birth: L*'s only viable successor from `110` is `101`. The only viable move back is `101 → 110`, after which both kinds are familiar. At the law level even one first-ever introduction cannot be required from *every* viable start, since the full cap vector dominates a viable in-box state and already contains every kind that can be born. Dropping finite `K` changes the answer. For integer-indexed kinds, finite populations and one unit act of kind `i` required to form `i+1`, the only keeping course from a singleton keeps shifting to a fresh index. The finite prefix printed indices 0 through 12 and 13 distinct kinds. The unbounded conclusion follows from the shift rule and is outside B.
