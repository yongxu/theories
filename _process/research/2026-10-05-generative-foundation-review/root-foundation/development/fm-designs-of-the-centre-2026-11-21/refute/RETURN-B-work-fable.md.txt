## 3. MUST, then LESSER

**M1. Case (iii) exists only where D leaves out what it rests on, and that part stands by itself.** The design does not say this, and it changes what case (iii) is.

Claim (mine, on paper, not in Lean): from any cycle of non-empty configurations of `Move W`, caps and cost both on, a standing population can be built.
- Let S be the kinds present somewhere on the cycle, and B ⊆ S a class of kinds that need each other and nothing outside B. One exists, since S is closed under needs.
- For k, j in B with R k j > 0, k present at t+1 forces j present at t. Going round a cycle of needs in B, the sets of steps at which each kind is present are exact shifts of one another.
- Put y_k = the least count of k over the steps where k is present, for k in B, and 0 elsewhere.
- For j in B take a step t where j has count y_j. Every k in B that needs j is present at t+1 with count at least y_k, so Σ_k y_k R k j ≤ Σ_k s(t+1)_k R k j ≤ c_j y_j.
- So `Move W y y`, y ≤ C, y ≠ 0.

Consequences:
- With every kind designated there is no case (iii): a course that never empties gives a standing population, with caps and cost.
- In case (iii) for D, every such class B on a keeping cycle is disjoint from D, and y is a standing population of kinds outside D.

## 5. The specific questions

**(a) Closing as a premise.** The lock is handed in: `Closing` is a condition on the class, and "kinds present never grow" is its restatement.
- What is concluded that is not in it: only B3(3⇒1). That conclusion does not need closing, since "a kind of D present from some step on" (B3(2)) gives the same standing population, and closing is one sufficient condition for (2).
- What closing does not give: any rise of use, any level that falls, any using up (M4).
- Where the model itself gives the lock (a kind that needs its own act, `Net.siphon_empty`'s case), case (iii) is excluded (L1).

**(b) Is B6 known mathematics.** Yes. All names below are from memory; no source was opened and no network used.
- *Uncapped half.* An endless course in ℕ^K has a stretch whose end is at or above its start (Dickson's lemma, 1913). This is the self-covering sequence of Karp and Miller (1969) for vector addition systems, whose length Rackoff (1978) bounds. Since the moves add, the sum over the stretch is a whole-number x ≠ 0 with x(R − diag c) ≤ 0.
  - In Petri-net structure theory that is the criterion for a partially repetitive net (Memmi and Roucairol 1980; Murata's 1989 survey).
  - In matrix terms it is a sub-invariant vector: on a class of kinds that need each other, the spectral radius of R·diag(c)⁻¹ is at most 1 (Perron and Frobenius; Collatz and Wielandt).
  - In economics it is the productive Leontief system (Hawkins and Simon) and von Neumann's growth factor of at least 1.
  - Chemical organisation theory (Dittrich and Speroni di Fenizio 2007) calls such an x a self-maintaining flux, and "closed" there is the design's closing.
- *Free half.* The births allowed are a monotone function of the configuration, a standing population is a post-fixed point, and the max over a cycle is one (Knaster and Tarski). For sets of kinds it is the union-closure of autocatalytic sets (Hordijk and Steel 2004).
- *B1's min.* Constraints with one positive coefficient each (c_j n_j − Σ_k b_k R k j ≥ 0) cut out a set closed under pointwise min (Cottle and Veinott 1972). B3's min over a tail is the same closure for an infinite family in ℕ.
- *M1's argument* is the cyclic structure of an irreducible non-negative matrix (Frobenius).
- *What I do not know under a name:* that with caps and cost together a designated set can be kept with no whole standing population (case iii), and that this needs both. By M3 it is a gap between fractional and whole solutions, closed by taking turns.

**(c) Old laws missed.**

  - `closed_viable` (`GuardHorizon.lean:112`) then gives `∀ n, Viable P_W (Live D) n x`, statement unchanged. That is the horizon form; the endless course of B3 is `Viability.Keepable P_W (Live D) x` by the constant run.
  - The design is a case of the old law here; the design's general law (B1, B2) is not used. The designer's "used, not cases" is right.
- *"The kept set is an up-set" as a comparison of two starts.* It is `Viability.keepable_step_iff` (`KeepableStep.lean:50`) used in both directions.

  - For n ≤ n': `Live D n'` (a count not less) and `Move W n' b` (c_j n_j ≤ c_j n'_j). The other direction then gives `Keepable P_W (Live D) n'`.
  - So it is two one-line facts about `Move` and an old law. In October's model the statement is there as `renewable_of_more`.
  - A general law carrying `Keepable` along a relation between two starts: not found. I searched the package for theorems naming `Keepable`, `Viable`, `Kernel`, `KeptFor` outside `Viability`, `GuardHorizon`, `Keeping`, `JointKeeping`, `KeepableStep` and found none; `ReachableRanges.Simulation` is of one built process.

**(d) Against October's law.** The statement is `renewal_closure_theorem (law : ChannelLaw Kind) (U : List Kind) (complete : ∀ k, k ∈ U) : RenewsForever law ↔ ∃ B, SelfRenewing law B` (`RenewalCharacterization.lean:682`).
- `RenewsForever`: some history from some start has a live token at every time, of any kind.
- `SelfRenewing law B`: B is non-empty, each kind of B has a supporter in B of span at least 2, and `formation law B ≤ spanOpening law B`.
- *Case (ii) and the theorem itself.* Read with every kind designated (D = K), the design's counterpart is "some course never empties ⇔ a standing population exists". That holds in the design's model with caps and cost both on (M1). So against `renewal_closure_theorem` itself there is no case (iii): case (ii) is its right side, case (i) its failure, and the theorem's shape carries over whole.
- *Case (iii).* It stands against `renewal_keeping_theorem` (line 703: `KeepsForever law K ↔ ∃ B, SelfRenewing law B ∧ ∃ k, k ∈ B ∧ K k ∧ 0 < law.span k`), which the design does not cite. Case (ii) is its right side with D for K. Case (iii) is its left side true and its right side false: the direction "kept ⇒ a population containing a designated kind" fails in the design's model, and only where the designated kinds sit wholly above a base that stands. B6 is that direction proved again at two corners of another model.
- *Neither is a case.* October's carrier is a reserve and tokens with remaining spans, support is a witness that is not spent, and there are no caps.
- October's nearest to the split of base and designated kinds is `lapsing_correction_cannot_be_kept` (line 982: `¬ KeepsForever lapsingCorrection (fun k => k = true)`). There the closure renews for ever (per the README) and the designated kind cannot be kept at all.

**(e) B5 against Ren's sentence** ("not holding exactly the same, dynamics, forms changes over time, but the continuation may continue, and creates more and more future").
- *States:* in a law with no standing population containing a kind of D, any course that keeps D has every kind of D absent again and again, and forms an absent kind again and again. That is "not holding exactly the same" as a necessity, in one corner.
- *Assumes, does not state:* "the continuation may continue". That some course keeps D is half of the definition of case (iii).
- *Does not state that dynamics change.* The law W is fixed, the same finitely many kinds come round, and the mean of the cycle is a standing population in fractions.
- *Does not state that forms change in the whole.* The base under D can hold the same form at every step (M1), and in L\* it does.
- *Does not state "more and more future".* V_D does not grow, no kind arises, and nothing measures what can go on after; the design says this.
- In case (ii), 10,780 laws against 16 in family A, holding the same is enough and change is never needed, so the design's general picture is the opposite of the sentence's.
