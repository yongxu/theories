## 4. The question

Does a general law of the design do work that the old laws do not? No, in what I read. The design has two candidates.

The comparison between guarded processes is true, needs no premise beyond itself, and is not in the package in that generality. But each use the design makes of it is degenerate in a different way:
- Left a point: it unfolds to `Closed` and repeats `closed_viable`'s induction.
- Right a clock: it is an invariant indexed by the step count, and the form needed is the endpoint one.
- One deterministic process under a preorder: it is iterating a step that keeps an order.

In each, the work is in the local condition, which is the old content.

The nursery law is real mathematics about a new step function. Its general part (a step keeping the order gives ordered runs, endings closed downward, an exact threshold by a finite check) is what `RegimeMargin` already states for any law keeping the order. What the old laws do not have is one arithmetic fact, that this nursery keeps the order when q + ⌈H/q⌉ ≥ N, and the built instance with moves in which a step that still feeds is past return.

## 5. The specific questions

**(a) The comparison over `GuardHorizon.Viable`.** Written, not compiled:

```lean
def Carries (P : Process S A) (g : S → Prop) (Q : Process T B) (h : T → Prop)
    (R : S → T → Prop) : Prop :=
  ∀ s t, R s t → (g s → h t) ∧
    ∀ a, Enabled P s a → ∃ b, Enabled Q t b ∧
      ∀ t', Q.step t b t' → ∃ s', P.step s a s' ∧ R s' t'

theorem Carries.viable (c : Carries P g Q h R) :
    ∀ n s t, R s t → Viable P g n s → Viable Q h n t
  | 0, s, t, r, kept => (c s t r).1 kept
  | n + 1, s, t, r, ⟨within, a, enabled, all⟩ =>
    match (c s t r).2 a enabled with
    | ⟨b, enabled', back⟩ => ⟨(c s t r).1 within, b, enabled', fun t' st' =>
        match back t' st' with
        | ⟨s', st, r'⟩ => Carries.viable c n s' t' r' (all s' st)⟩
```

The induction goes through with no premise beyond the comparison. Nothing has to be decided. `Viable` on the right hands over a successor that occurs (`Q.step t b t'`), and the comparison answers with a left successor that occurs. The premise of decided steps arose because `KeptFor` quantifies over every value a reading could keep; `Viable` quantifies only over outcomes that occur.

The direction of the matching is essential. With the ordinary forward simulation (each left successor matched on the right) the transfer fails in 559,711 of 10,252,445 checks. Smallest found: left a point with a loop, right a move from 0 to {0, 1} with the guard false at 1.

**(b) `closed_viable`.** Take P := the one-point process, g := True, R () t := K t. Then `Carries` is `Closed P guard K` up to trivial terms (29,606 sets K on random processes: all agree, 7,354 of them closed). The derivation needs the point, its own induction that the point is viable at every horizon, the unfolding, and the call. The old proof is a four-line induction with the same three moves: guard from `closed`, move from `closed`, successors by the hypothesis. So it is `closed_viable`'s induction said again with a point put in and taken out.

The comparison is a strict generalisation: between a 2-step chain and a 4-step chain, keeping carries at horizons 0 to 2 where the right has no closed set at all. That generality is not used here.

**(c) `locked_ends_rising`** (TurningInTime.lean 1499–1520).

Premises:
- `raises : Raises P favours fA`
- `monotone : ReadsShare P M hA`
- `upper` (favouring kept by a rule not lower)
- `outside : AloneOutside marked (windowAlone fA hA reads) after`
- `needs : 0 < need`
- `un : ∀ x, need ≤ x → ¬ marked x`
- `nothing : FromNothing giving`
- `keeps : Keeps giving 0 hi`
- `high : x ≤ hi`
- `locked : Locked M favours hA (w.1, w.2.1)`
- `more : ∀ v, P.le w.1 v.1 → M.le w.2.1 v.2.1 → use v < use (windowAlone fA hA reads v)`

Conclusion: `∃ n t, n ≤ hi + 1 ∧ «迭» (net need after (fun w x => RegimeMargin.step giving (use w) x)) n (w, x) t ∧ (∀ v, ¬ net … t v) ∧ t.1 = Law.orbit (windowAlone fA hA reads) w n ∧ Locked M favours hA (t.1.1, t.1.2.1)`.

The relation in full, (v, l) to clock time i:
- v is the orbit of `windowAlone` from w at i;
- `Locked` at v; `P.le w.1 v.1`; `M.le w.2.1 v.2.1`;
- i ≤ use v; l ≤ hi; i ≤ hi + 1;
- i = hi + 1 → l = 0.

Start: `locked`, reflexivity, `high`. A step enabled at (v, l) has need ≤ l, so l > 0 (`needs`), so i ≤ hi. Then:
- `un` and `outside` give the next way `windowAlone v`.
- `turn_locked` (from `raises`, `monotone`, `upper`) gives the lock and both order bounds.
- `more` gives i + 1 ≤ use v′.
- `keeps` gives l′ ≤ hi.
- At i = hi, hi ≤ use v and l ≤ hi leave nothing, and `nothing` gives l′ = 0.

Every old premise is used. By program: 300,000 instances, relation never broken, no run longer than hi + 1, bound attained in 1,859. Dropping `more`, `keeps`, `nothing`, `high` or `needs` breaks the relation in 9,785, 12,950, 17,150, 60,000 and 60,000 of 60,000 instances.

Which way: keeping (with both guards true, merely being able to step) is carried from the lock to the clock and read backwards. The clock cannot take hi + 2 steps, so the lock cannot. Every enabled move of the lock must be matched (it has one), which is the one-sided condition itself. The other half would fail: in 298,141 of 300,000 runs the net stops while the clock can still step.

Is the old conclusion obtained? On paper yes, but not from the keeping-transfer alone. That gives only "no hi + 2 steps". The witness needs three more things:
1. A bridge from `Viable` on a wrapped process to `«迭»` of the relation `net`.
2. A bounded search for the first disabled step (`first_of_decided`, as the old `ends_of_below` does).
3. The orbit equality and the lock at the end, which need a second lemma: deterministic steps keep related endpoints.

Lemma 3 alone already gives the bound: the endpoint is related to clock time n ≤ hi + 1, and at hi + 1 the level is 0. So the keeping-transfer is idle in this derivation.

Shorter? No, by my count. About 31 lines are specific to this law today (`locked_ends_rising`, `ends_with_net_rising`, `rising_takes_all`, `takes_at_hi`, `rises_from`, `kept_within`). The rest (`ends_of_below`, `agrees_until_marked`, `locked_alone`, `locked_alone_not_below`) serve sibling laws and would stay. The new route needs the two lemmas, the wrapper and bridge, the clock and its bound, the relation and its local condition (which redoes `turn_locked`, `kept_within`, `more`, `nothing` step by step), the search and the assembly. My estimate is about 90 lines; it is an estimate, not measured.

**(e) Does the nursery's law use the general comparison?** Only an order argument of its own.
- The comparison half is: one step keeps the preorder (the new arithmetic), then iterate. That is `orbit_mono`'s shape with the allowance varying by season.
- The nursery has one successor per move, the two processes are the same, and the guards are true. Nothing of the general form (two processes, successors matched back) is exercised.
- The exact-fate half uses the finite table and one comparison with the constant largest allowance. The `Viable`-form applied to the nursery would give only that the any-course kernel is closed upward, not that the largest allowance is the witness.
- The two old laws use a point and a clock and never touch the nursery or its order.

The three do not meet. What the nursery law does meet is `RegimeMargin`'s family for laws keeping the order (MUST 2), which the design does not name.

Known under other names:
- The comparison is an alternating simulation carrying a safety objective for the mover (Alur, Henzinger, Kupferman and Vardi 1998; in control, Tabuada's alternating simulation relations), in its finite-horizon form.
- The nursery law is the comparison principle for an order-preserving system, with the viability kernel an upper set found by backward iteration on a finite lattice.
- The clock is a ranking function.
