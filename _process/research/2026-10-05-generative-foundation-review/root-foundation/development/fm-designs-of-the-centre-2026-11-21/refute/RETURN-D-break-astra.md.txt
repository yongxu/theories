## 3. MUST, then LESSER

**MUST**

- **Withdraw the inference that selecting among futures precludes keeping-widening.** From an empty root choose either an absorbing state carrying guard `b`, or state 0 on the line `0→1→2→⋯`. Line state `j` carries exactly the numbered guards `0,…,j`. On the line course, `b` ceases to be possible at the first choice. Yet `Kept = Held` is empty at the root and gains one numbered guard at every step forever. The prefix calculation gives sizes `[0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8]`. The formula at `j` gives the infinite result. Part 2 rules out losing a *currently kept* guard on the widening suffix.
- **Withdraw “the adding unit must be kept at a cost” (§7), and qualify R1.** In I3, form both classes at `(1,(1,0))`. Output is `(1,(1,0)) -> (1,(1,0)); K=1`. Class 1 forms for free, adds 1, fails its need of 2, and is absent at every recorded configuration. Its contribution pays for class 0 forever. Also, a `base` class requires no parent or supporter. The first successful T4 move forms class 0 from an empty configuration.
- **Qualify “with forming free there is no such configuration.”** The reported 1,080 models impose *both* zero fees and nonnegative contributions. A reachable counterexample with zero fees has life 1, cap 2, all classes base, weights `(-1,-1,1)` and needs `(1,1,0)`. Form all from empty level 2. Output is `(1,(1,1,1)); K=7, joint=False`. Either drawer can persist with the giver. Keeping both drawers drains the level. The design's narrower claim about its no-draw twin survives.
- **Distinguish initial configurations from configurations a course can enter.** I3's only incompatible configuration has class 1 present at level 1 despite its need of 2. It has zero predecessors. More strikingly, I reproduced all **438** pairwise-but-not-triple configurations on the full three-class grid. **432** meet all current needs, and **zero** have even one predecessor. All 438 are legal starting states in §2. None provides evidence that an RS move creates that particular predicament on that grid.
- **Keep the initial-guard qualifier in the short telling of part 3.** A course can preserve everything initially kept while every course must lose something it subsequently acquires. In the four-state process `s→u→p` or `s→u→q`, with `p,q` absorbing and respective guard masks `1,7,3,5`, the two possible kept sequences are `[1,7,3]` and `[1,7,5]`. Both retain the initial guard. Neither is lossless from the start in part 2's sense.
- **Keep the T6 zero confined to the reported search.** It reproduces there. As a statement about RS keepability it is **REFUTED**, including for reachable starts with positive needs and free formation. See 5(d).

Its successful course is also the exact state sequence of the constant rule “form all.” The T4 contrast establishes dependence on the selected moves. Forgetting action labels gives **1,991** distinct state lassos. Also, a life counter records presence and refresh. It cannot by itself identify individual units across two configurations.
- T1 says each proposed result needs induction. Part 3 uses the very run in its premise and its tails. T5's `Keepable.each` projects a jointly keeping run onto each guard at the **start**. Obtaining separate keepability at **every** step also uses the corresponding tail. In the Lean source the declaration is `Becoming.Viability.Keepable.each`. `JointKeeping` is the file name. Likewise `keepable_step_iff` lives in `Becoming.Viability`.
- Section 2 determined the RS transition unambiguously with the vector index as priority. It leaves the starting level and lives free of any reachability requirement. Section 2 also unambiguously defines keeping by the existence of a run. Guaranteeing safety against all outcomes introduces another quantifier.

## 4. Does a general law of the design do work that the old laws do not?

**Part 4 does comparison work, but the design does not supply a general law joining selection to depletion and continuation.** Its domination premise lets one run witness the kept and eventually held guards of many rival runs. Part 2 needs only the two inclusions around its middle predicate. Part 3 uses one common witness throughout. Algebraically, for the monotone, contractive, idempotent operation `K` of keepability, `K(⋂_i K G_i)=K(⋂_i G_i)` already follows from those three properties. The old `keepable_and_iff_of_kernel_left` directly pairs the witness for one guard with the kernel guarantee for the other. Part 3 can be used there, but supplies no new reason that the witness keeps both. An older declaration stating the entire part 4 was **not found in what I read**. Neither side of T4 is predicted by parts 1–3, and the design gives no instance of a lock law derived by part 4.

**(b) Several outcomes per move.** Under the definition in §2, **nothing in part 3 fails**. A `Run` already picks its outcomes. If a guard stays existentially keepable, it holds at every state on that very run. The tail witnesses joint keeping for all such guards simultaneously.

For the guarantee reading, let `s` have moves `a→{s,p}` and `b→{s,q}`, with `p,q` absorbing. Guard `g` holds at `s,p`, guard `h` at `s,q`, and guard `j` only at `s`. Always choosing `a` guarantees `g`. Always choosing `b` guarantees `h`. No first move guarantees both. On the realized path `s,s,…`, both stay individually guaranteeable forever.

```text
                              g     h     j     jointly keep g,h
some run from s               true  true  true  true
one policy, every outcome     true  true  false false
held on the realized s-loop   true  true  true
```

The first invalid step is offering the observed tail as a **policy** witnessing joint safety. It witnesses one outcome sequence. Guard `j` also disproves `Held ⊆ Kept` in the mixed reading, and this constant path disproves part 2 there. The **nested** part 3 equivalence survives if both keeping operators mean guaranteed safety. A strategy preserving the separate winning regions preserves the guards, and a jointly winning strategy has a winning continuation after every compatible history. All seven nonempty bundles agreed in the program. Thus the single-path formulation fails while the consistently quantified bundle equivalence survives.

**(c) Weakest numerical condition for the dominance premise.** For §2's pointwise order and “form all,” the following finite arithmetic condition is **necessary and sufficient**. It handles saturation, priority, fees, negative contributions, needs, and unavailable classes. It requires no course search or comparison of all ordered pairs of starts.

At each `(L,a)`, let `E` be the eligible classes, `D={i:a_i>1}` the units that would survive ageing, and `(T_L,T_a)` the result of greedily requesting all classes in priority order. For every `F⊆E` with total fee `c(F)≤L`, compute

`z(F) = clamp(L − c(F) + Σ {wᵢ | i ∈ D ∪ F}, 0, C)`.

1. Require `z(F) ≤ T_L`. For each `i∈F` with `nᵢ ≤ z(F)`, require `T_a[i] = ℓ`.
2. Raise just the level or one life coordinate of the starting state by one, within its bounds. Require that no component of the greedy result decreases on any such increment.

Every affordable eligible subset can be formed by requesting precisely it. Thus the first check is necessary. It also suffices at a common start because it covers every rival's level and every freshly formed survivor. Every other survivor only ages, and the higher greedy level meets its need. The second check is necessary by comparing top moves at adjacent starts, and sufficient across arbitrary ordered starts by chaining increments. Together the checks give exactly `Dominates.dom`. The remaining fields follow from deterministic RS and the presence guards. For a different proposed fixed move, use its greedy result in the same checks. Enumerating moves decides whether any dominates. The arithmetic criterion and direct enumeration of `dom` agreed in all 36 models.

**One unit outside.** With life 1, cap 2, two base classes, weights `(1,1)`, needs `(0,0)` and fees `(0,1)`, form-all dominates despite the positive fee. Raise only the first fee to 1. At `(1,(1,1))` the two classes are individually kept. Form-all leaves `(1,(1,0))`, while requesting class 1 leaves `(1,(0,1))`. The program gives `dominates=False, K=3, joint=False`. On the top run `Held=1`. Priority spends the one available unit before class 1 can form.

Dominance is stronger than the **conclusions** of part 4. A single base class of weight −1, fee and need zero, life and cap 1 gives `dominates=False, conclusions all starts=True`. For those conclusions to hold from every start in finite RS, the exact condition is that `Kept` never decreases on a top edge, `Possible` stays equal on every top edge, and `Kept = Possible` at one state of each top cycle. Part 2 gives the first conclusion and the common witness. On each cycle the two sets are constant, so the last two checks are exactly eventual holding of everything initially possible.

**(d) More level can leave strictly less kept.** Keep all parameters within the designer's two-class grid except for **life 3**. Use cap 4, both classes base, weights `(0,−2)`, zero fees, needs `(1,2)`, and fresh lives `(3,3)` at both starts.

The output again gives `K=1` and `K=0`. At the higher start every two-move sequence reaches level 0. This refutes monotonicity of keepability itself, independently of which rule is later chosen.
