## 4. The question

**No general law of the design does work that the old laws do not.**

- Part 1 is a row of package and scratch lemmas at `adm := Keepable`.
- Part 2 is the fact that `⋂_{k≥n} Holds_k` is the greatest nondecreasing sequence below `Holds`. It is true of any sequence of sets, and my program confirms that keepability can be replaced by `Holds` or by any chain between the two without changing the truth.
- Part 3 is the same statement with `Holds`, and its equivalent form is idempotence of `Keepable`.
- Part 4 is the one law whose premise does work, through an induction. That law is the standard simulation lemma: a forward simulation into a deterministic process gives a greatest course, the premise `greatest` of `Reaching.widens_iff_of_greatest` (ReachableRanges.lean l.387). From it, "the greatest course forgoes nothing" is immediate.

## 5. The specific questions

**(a) Is anything left of part 2 that is about keepability?**

No.
- The proof uses `Kept` only through `Held ⊆ Kept` (the run's tail) and `Kept ⊆ Holds` (`Keepable.start`).
- With either bound replaced, the statement stays true for `Holds` and for random state-read chains between the two: 0 violations in `check_general.py`. It fails only for a chain above `Holds` (`Poss`: 198,872 violations).
- Part 2 does not need runs: it holds for any sequence of sets `A_n`, with `Held_n := ⋂_{k≥n} A_k`.

Keepability changes which courses meet the left side, so it changes what "forgoes" means. It does not change the law: the two left sides differ in truth in 29,324 (run, N) pairs, while the equivalence holds in all of them.

**(b) Part 3 against `Keepable.each`, `keepable_and_iff_of_kernel_left` and KeepableStep.lean: new, or an unfolding?**

An unfolding.
- **First form.** The set R is `Held N` (shown above), and the run's own tail witnesses `Together`.
- **Equivalent form.** It is `Keepable.mono` with `Keepable.start`, together with the scratch `keepable_of_run`. For one guard it is idempotence of the existential viability kernel: Aubin's `Viab_F(Viab_F K) = Viab_F K`, where `Keepable` is Aubin's viability kernel and `Viability.Kernel` the invariance kernel.
- **`Keepable.each`** (JointKeeping l.115) follows from part 3's right-to-left direction at step 0 by `Keepable.start`. Its converse, each to together, fails (`ChoicePoint.each_not_joint`), and part 3 does not touch that.
- **`keepable_and_iff_of_kernel_left` (l.121), instantiated:**
  - `X := Bool`, `G true := g₁`, `G false := g₂`.
  - From a `g₂`-run `r` from `s`, `Kernel.run` gives `g₁` at each step, and the tails give each guard keepable at each step.
  - Part 3 then gives `Together s (fun _ => True)`, and `Keepable.mono` converts that to `Keepable P (g₁ ∧ g₂) s`.
  - It goes through with the statement unchanged, but `Kernel.run` already gave the run in `g₁ ∧ g₂`. Part 3 is used and then cancelled.
- **KeepableStep.lean.** `keepable_step_iff` is the one-step form of the same tail and cons argument. Part 1's last sentence is its `.mpr` contraposed. The idempotence is not stated there, nor anywhere in what I read, but it is the all-steps form of that file's tail argument.

**(c) Is `Dominates` a one-sided comparison of processes, and is it in the Lean already?**

Yes, it is a one-sided comparison. `le` is a forward simulation of `P` by the deterministic process `Law.process nextTop`:
- every move from the lower configuration is matched by the top move from the upper (`dom`);
- the guards are carried upward (`up`, inclusion, not equality);
- the identity is inside `le` (`refl`).

In the Lean:
- **`DeferredBranching.Simulation`** (ReachableRanges.lean l.1804) has exactly this one-sided shape: "related configurations admit alike or more on the right, every step on the left matched on the right into related configurations". It is defined for that one witness process (`Config`, moves `Unit`) and used only for `late_simulates_early` and `early_not_simulating_late`.
- **`Labelled.Bisimulation`** (Labelled.lean l.293) is two-sided, matches each move by the same move, and asks equal claims. `Dominates` is not a case of it. `Bisimulation.trace` (l.364) is the same induction, on finite traces.
- **A general one-sided simulation on `Process`** was not found in what I read. A grep of the package for `Simulation` and `Dominat` found only these two.
- **What part 4 uses of it** (the top course includes every course from below, at `adm := fun s x => G x s`) is the premise `greatest` of `Reaching.widens_iff_of_greatest` (l.387). The package obtains greatest courses only from uniqueness (`greatest_of_unique`, l.410) and from one witness (`KeptAlternative.greatest_not_unique`, l.1298).

Known name: simulation preserves existential path properties (here EG of a guard closed upward) from the simulated system to the simulating one (Clarke, Grumberg and Long, model checking and abstraction, 1994). It is also the comparison principle for monotone control systems with a greatest input (Angeli and Sontag, 2003).

The Lean statement it suggests: "if `R` is a forward simulation from `P` to `Q` carrying `g`, and `R s s'`, then `Keepable P g s → Keepable Q g s'`". Part 4 is then that lemma at `Q := Law.process nextTop`.

**(d) Is the negative for the direction forced, or an artefact of choosing "kept" as the chain?**

An artefact, and definitional.

1. Chapter 07 §2, the theorem at `r-keeping-widening-is-generation-losing-nothing` with its Reading, says the following. Keeping widening is generation that loses nothing, parted from generation "exactly by loss". `Steps.Moving` is generation under the one at every moment and keeps widening from none. And widening "is not its measure" for 生生.
2. `Steps.keepsWidening_iff_generation_losing_nothing` at `Q := Kept r` states that `KeepsWideningFrom (Kept r) N` holds exactly when `(∀ n ≥ N, includes (Kept r) n (n+1))` holds together with 全生成 under the one. Its first conjunct is "forgoes nothing from N". So the negative is that law's first conjunct, and part 2 only rewrites it as `Kept = Held`.
3. The chain decides everything:
   - **At `Q := Poss r`, no course keeps widening.** `Poss` never gains (`Run.possible_of_next`; `Run.not_keepsWidening_of_within`, HeldPossible.lean l.184, with the process itself). So the negative would hold of every way, forgoing or not.
   - **At `Q := Held r`, the first half is automatic, and forgoing does not prevent widening.** Instance (`check_forgo_widen.py`):
     - `S = Sum Nat Nat`, moves `Bool`.
     - `inl n` steps by `true` to `inr n` (absorbing) and by `false` to `inl (n+1)`.
     - The guards are "settle k" (`s = inl k ∨ s = inr k`) and "past k" (`k < index s`).
     - The course always takes `false`.
     - It forgoes "settle n" at every step n: kept at n, not at n+1. Over 34 judged steps it loses a kept guard at all 34, `Held` gains at all 34, and `Held` loses at none.
     - So `KeepsWideningFrom (Held r) N` holds for every N, and the Lean's chain laws apply unchanged.
   - **At `Q := Reaching.PossibleHeld` (HeldPossible.lean l.217), the Lean already has the law of more future while forms change.** It keeps widening only where the held part changes without end (`Run.changes_of_heldKeepsWidening`, l.341).

The design's own test (§7: "an order under which a course that forgoes something still widens and the Lean's law applies to it unchanged") is met.
