## 1. Verdict in five lines

1. The mathematics holds. The stationary criterion and the boxed criterion are correct. I checked the proof step by step, and my own rebuild found 0 disagreements in 939,056 comparisons at the level of single generations (d = 1 to 6).
2. It survives as **one built case worth keeping**, inside a new model next to the others. It is not a centre, and it is not a law that joins.
3. The strongest reason is that its general law is the known nilpotency criterion for a finitely generated matrix semigroup: the chain of images stabilises within d steps. The criterion is applied twice. The design's own notion of choice is put in and then taken out by monotonicity, since choosing every affordable reading dominates every course. As a result no choice, cost, share or lock does work in any proof.
4. No old law comes out as a case of it, as the design itself says. Two of its six "new" definitions are already in the Lean: `Viability.Keepable`, and `NoReturnAt` in form.
5. The case worth keeping is the N3/N5 pair: equal cohort sizes 2,4,0 and different points of no return (2 and 1). That difference holds only relative to the hand-picked catalogue, and the linear algebra does no work in it.

**MUST**

In T4 no cancellation can occur: every map reached is a partial function on the basis (checked, `extra-output.txt` item 4). Cancellation (J²=0) only makes the F_2 verdicts differ from path verdicts.

## 4. The question

Does a general law of the design do work that the old laws do not?

Inside its own model, yes. The boxed criterion is a closed-form decision of keepability, and of the step of no return, from the starting data. `Viability.Keepable`, `GuardHorizon.closed_viable` and `ReadOffInTime.NoReturnAt` are notions and sufficient conditions, and none of them computes it.

But the work is done by three things none of which is a notion of the map:
- monotonicity of set products, which removes the choice;
- the identity that the image of a product is the image of the composed maps;
- stabilisation of a descending chain in a lattice of finite height, which is the textbook nilpotency criterion for a finitely generated matrix semigroup.

The design's own notions do no work in the proof beyond being matrix factors in a product: the choice over repertoires is put in and taken out in step 6, the budget is a filter on C, and the reading is a factor. No old law, from either side, comes out as a case with its statement unchanged. The law could not do that, because it is about one concrete model. So, for the map's join: **no**. It is a known law at two grains in a new toy model.

## 5. The specific questions

**(a) The proof, step by step, and the T4 rebuild.**

1. X_t is the set of nonzero words of length m = 2^t. Valid by induction: a factor of a nonzero product is nonzero, so both halves are nonzero and lie in X_{t−1}. Checked: 1,467 cases.
2. The span of the images of X_t is Σ_A A L_Q^{m−1}(V). Valid, because L_Q preserves sums. The sketch states this for V, but step 7 needs it for every W. The computation is the same; this is a gap in the telling, not in the proof.
3. V ⊇ L(V) ⊇ … descends and is fixed after d steps. Valid: once two terms are equal, the chain stays fixed, and a strict chain has length at most d.
4. If W ≠ 0, then some B ∈ X has BW ≠ 0, since otherwise L(W) = 0. Valid.
5. If W = 0, every word with m−1 ≥ d is zero, which gives h. Valid.
6. Choosing all of C contains every course. Valid, but it needs F to be monotone in **both** Q and the cohort. Monotonicity in the cohort is used without being said.
7. L_{X_t,C} = L_C ∘ L_Q^{m−1}. Valid. Checked on every subspace: 3,272 cases.
8. Apply S1 to the start X_t and the way C. Valid.
9. Unkeepability passes to every successor. Valid.

No step is missing, circular or false.

**(b) Known mathematics.**
- X_t = X·(QX)^{2^t−1} with the zero product removed. The stationary way continues iff the semigroup generated by the finite set QX is not nilpotent, iff (QX)^d ≠ {0}.
- The boxed criterion is the same test applied to the generating set C·X_t = C X (QX)^{2^t−1}.
- The theorem is that a finite set Σ of d×d matrices over a field generates a nilpotent semigroup iff all its products of length d vanish. Its known forms:
  - for one matrix, the image chain of Fitting's lemma;
  - for semigroups, Levitzki's theorem (a semigroup of nilpotent matrices can be triangularised at once);
  - in the joint spectral radius literature, ρ(Σ)=0 iff Σ^d={0}, decidable in polynomial time (Gurvits 1995; Jungers 2009).
- In order terms, it is Kleene iteration from the top of a lattice of finite height, which reaches the greatest fixed point within the height.
- It is **not** mortality. Mortality asks whether 0 is some product (Paterson 1970: undecidable over Z for 3×3). The design asks the dual question, whether nonzero words of every length exist.
- Keepability is Aubin's viability kernel for the set-valued map Y ↦ {F_Q(Y)}. It collapses to a single course because that map has a largest selection, F_C.
- What the Lean statement should be: the lattice form, "a join-preserving map L on a lattice of finite height d: L^n(⊤) ≠ ⊥ for all n iff L^d(⊤) ≠ ⊥", plus domination by monotone set products. Its Boolean case touches `Remainder.no_chain_of_no_loop` and `bounded_iff_no_loop`. That join was not made, and I did not check it against Remainder's exact statement beyond lines 575–640.

**(c) What the map should keep.**
- **The equal-count pair: keep it, as a built case.**
  - Two courses are identical under any reading of counts (2,4,0), and one is still recoverable at generation 1 while the other has passed its point of no return. That is a concrete instance for ReadOffInTime's too-late laws: a selection that reads sizes is blind to the turn. I did not find such a built case in what I read, but I read ReadOffInTime 585–625 only.
  - Keep it in its smallest honest form: partial maps on 5 points (0 to 4; at d=6 the sixth plays no part), the catalogue named, three readings. Say that adding E31 to the catalogue erases the difference.
- **The last turn before extinction as a phenomenon: no.** The Lean already has it in notion: the guard held without being kept (`Viability.within_not_kernel` in universal form, JointKeeping's keepable-not-kernel), and `NoReturnAt`. Here it gets a decision procedure only.
- **The reading as a rank budget: no, as it stands.** It does work in no statement. Its only content is rank(ApB) ≤ rank p, which is one line. It would earn a place only if the budget were shared across pairings. The design's section 7 says that would break domination, and that is exactly where a real choice and a real cost would enter. That is the one direction from this design worth a further try.
