## 3. What the core adds

**With `below` handed in, the core is the per-marking lemma applied twice, plus a short and real quantifier argument.** One invocation extracts a first hit from an arbitrary successful marking. The other supplies a witness. `above_before` is induction on `j ≤ T` using decidable order. No new dynamics is proved between those invocations. The numerical conclusion is not already in `below`: the proof still has to rule out earlier hits, prove the prefix meets the need and produce a realizable marking. [Lines 188–196, 441–453](/Users/ren/theories/tmp/fm/build14-read/LevelAlone.lean:441)

There is a simpler description of that witness than the module gives. **The fixed marking `fun x => x ≠ x₀` works whenever any permitted marking works.** By `rest` it is silent through step `D-1`. By `differs` it marks at `D`. Thereafter `sets`, `stays` and `uses` guarantee the same corrected use regardless of subsequent marks. This is a direct corollary on paper, not a public theorem. It requires no search for levels and no foreknowledge of the number `X D`. It also explains exactly how little freedom in a “reading” the result needs. [Lines 153–161, 373–394, 447–453](/Users/ren/theories/tmp/fm/build14-read/LevelAlone.lean:447)

I would keep the core public. A and B give it mathematical content by deriving `below` in different ways. A uses order preservation of the residual-plus-giving map. B uses a lower bound on use before exhaustion, and permits the level to rise. The two one-premise failures establish the limit of A’s criterion. The reduction over markings, the one-step use criterion and those failures are worth keeping alongside the two dynamical lemmas. S4–S6 are short translations of the result once the earlier package is in place.
