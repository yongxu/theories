## LESSER findings

**L1. "exactly where" for a global equivalence** (five places)
- **Where:** KeepableStep.lean lines 17–18 and 62–63, the root bullet (Becoming.lean:2794–2795), the README row, and the checker SCOPE.
- **Words:** "Every step keeps not being keepable exactly where no step from outside the guard leads to a configuration the guard is keepable from".
- **Against:** `not_keepable_invariant_iff : Invariant P (¬Keepable P g ·) ↔ ∀ s a t, P.step s a t → ¬ g s → ¬ Keepable P g t`. Both sides are closed, global statements. "exactly where" reads as a set of points.
- **Instead:** "Every step keeps not being keepable exactly when no step from outside the guard leads to a configuration the guard is keepable from."

**L2. KeepableStep.lean lines 14–16: definitions told as results**
- **Words:** "For every process and guard, with no further premise: a run read from its step 1 on is a run (`Run.onward`), and a step into a run's start followed by the run is a run (`Run.cons`)."
- **Against:** these are definitions, not theorems. The guard plays no part in them, and `Run.onward` is in `Becoming.Recurrence`, not this module's imports.
- **Instead:** "Its proof reads a run from step 1 on (`Run.onward`, `Becoming.Recurrence`) and puts one step before a run (`Run.cons`, `Becoming.Horizon`)."

**L3. KeepableStep.lean lines 22–25: the witness is unnamed, the key conjunct is left out, and the punctuation is broken**
- **Words:** "a guard that does not hold at `split` and holds at `left` … (`ChoicePoint.keepable_gained`, along the run `ChoicePoint.leftRun` `ChoicePoint.keepable_gained_along_run`)".
- **Against:** `keepable_gained` is stated for `afterSplit` only. Its fourth conjunct, that `closed` fails, is what makes it show the premise is needed, and the header omits it.
- **Instead:** "On `ChoicePoint.process`, the guard `afterSplit` (false at `split` only) is not keepable from `split` and is keepable from `left`, one step on, and not every step keeps its complement (`ChoicePoint.keepable_gained`; along the run `ChoicePoint.leftRun`, `ChoicePoint.keepable_gained_along_run`)."

**L4. "The twin of `kernel_step_iff`" (KeepableStep.lean lines 9 and 48; root bullet "the twin of the kernel's step")**
- **The issue:** the two statements share a shape, with ∃…∧ in place of ∀…→. But `Kernel` is over finite reaches and `Keepable` over infinite runs. At a configuration with no step, the kernel holds where the guard holds, while keepability fails. No theorem relates the two, so "twin" says a little more than shape.
- **Instead:** "the same form as `kernel_step_iff`, with 'some step leads to' for 'every step leads to'".

**L5. The telling of `stops` (wide docstring, TurningInTime header lines 86 and 289, root, README, README.zh, checker)**
- **Words:** "at some step of the course as it was the need is unmet or the level is marked".
- **Against:** the Lean premise is `∃ n, ¬ (need ≤ x ∧ ¬ marked x)`, that is, not both the need met and the level unmarked. Under `[DecidablePred marked]` this equals the "or" form, so nothing is false. But the module takes care about which decisions are used, and the "or" form is the stronger reading without decidability.
- **Instead:** "at some step of the course as it was it is not so that the need is met and the level unmarked", or keep "unmet or marked" and add "(the marks decided)".

**L6. Docstring of `unending_iff_marked_at` (around TurningInTime.lean:765)**
- **Words:** "`unending_iff_first_marked_wide` below is this at the first marked step, found by a search."
- **Against:** the search in `first_of_decided` finds the first step where "need met and unmarked" fails. That step is the first marked step only once the line is known never to end.
- **Instead:** "is this at the first step where the need is unmet or the level marked, found by a search; where the line never ends, that step is the first marked one".

**L7. TurningInTime header lines 85–89: the referent is unclear and one clause is garbled**

## Answers
**(i) Does the general law do the work?** It does. The new proof of `unending_iff_first_marked` only derives `stops` from `asItWasEnds`, in one lambda, and calls `unending_iff_first_marked_wide`. All the search, agreement and transfer happens in the wide law and the two lemmas under it. The derived premise is strictly weaker (the marked, never-below example), so nothing is put in and taken out. One qualification: the wide law's proof is the old 53-line proof, generalised and split into `unending_from` and `unending_iff_marked_at`. It is a re-factoring within the module. It does not make the law a case of a result proved elsewhere before, and the only outside laws it uses were already used by the old proof.

**(ii) Any claim beyond these statements?** I found none about the world: no "placed", "explained" or "shown". Two sentences go a little past the Lean. "The twin of `kernel_step_iff`" relates two statements by their shape, which no theorem does (L4). The counter-case for dropping `asItWasEnds` is argued, not proved, and is labelled as such ("argued here, not a Lean statement"). The headings "one step of keeping along some course" and "whether a step can gain keepability" name what the theorems state.
