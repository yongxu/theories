## Q2. The quantity that decides

**OPENED.** The start's `s.reserve + available k s.renewing s.renewing`, compared with `fee`; at `k = 1` it is `s.reserve + triangle s.renewing` (l. 427). `available` is "a counterfactual ledger, not an additional physical process" (the file's comment, l. 379): what the renewing contracts would pay in if correction never acted, a sum that stops growing after `s.renewing` steps (l. 446).

**Is it a potential that never rises along the unfunded course?** OPENED for what is stated, by hand for the rest.

- Stated: the set `reserve + triangle renewing < fee` is kept by every step with `1 ≤ k` (l. 132); the set `fee ≤ reserve + triangle renewing` is kept by every step at `k = 1` (l. 182). The arithmetic is `r + triangle (r-k) ≤ triangle r` (l. 124), with equality at `k = 1` (l. 107); the ledger alone is conserved at `k = 1` (`unit_ledger_conservation`, l. 418).
- By hand from l. 50 and l. 124: on an unfunded step with `1 ≤ k`, `reserve + triangle renewing` does not rise; it is unchanged at `k = 1` unless the cap cuts. Not found in the file: that inequality stated of `next`.
- For a general schedule the quantity for the rest of the course would be `reserve + available (shifted k) renewing renewing`. Not found in the file: any statement that this is kept along the unfunded course. The file avoids it: it evaluates the ledger from the start at the fixed step `s.renewing` (ll. 510 to 514, 530 to 535).
- On the funded course it is not a potential: the reserve is `fee` and `renewing` rises, so it rises.

**Is it the course's whole memory?** OPENED: no. The state is three numbers and the course from a step on depends on them and on the rest of the schedule (`history_add`, l. 257). The quantity decides one bit (which regime) and, at `k = 1`, that bit is a function of `reserve + triangle renewing` alone. It does not hold the capacity, and the lowest capacity on the way differs between courses with the same regime (ll. 600, 603: nothing, or at least four).

**What runs down.** OPENED. On the unfunded course the number renewing, by `k` a step (l. 52), and with it what can still be paid in. Capacity runs down while fewer than half renew and rises while more than half do (l. 44). The reserve does not run down: unfunded, it rises by `renewing` (`min fee (s.reserve+s.renewing)`, l. 52) and at none renewing stands still (l. 267); funded, it is `fee` (l. 60). So the level that is read is one that only rises to its cap, and the amounts that fall are a count and a capacity that nothing needs.

**Who gives to whom.** OPENED. Each renewing contract gives one to the reserve at every step and one to capacity; each other contract takes one from capacity and, while funded, is charged three into the reserve. The reserve pays `fee` while funded, to no one in the model (`expenditure`, l. 620), and what exceeds the cap leaves (`released`, l. 621; `treasury_accounted`, l. 632). Nothing is given back to a renewing contract by any statement: its payoff is `6 − others` with `others` free.

**What reads what.** OPENED, from `next` (ll. 39 to 44).

- `funded` reads the reserve against the fee, one `Bool`.
- `allocated` reads that `Bool` for its direction, `k` for its size, and the count `renewing` and `N` only to add, subtract and cut. It reads no share (`renewing` over `N` occurs nowhere) and not the capacity.
- `inflow` reads the `Bool`, the count and `N`.
- The capacity's law reads the capacity, the count, `N` and `cap`.
- **Nothing reads the capacity.** It occurs only in its own field and in conclusions (grep: ll. 44, 53, 61 and conclusions). `PopulationRealization` does not read it either: its step is gated by `parts` (l. 34), and `destructive_economy_with_actual_successors` (l. 106) proves successors enabled at every step with capacity at nothing. The destructive course goes on; it does not end.

**Does the reserve ever fall once it covers the fee?** OPENED: under `renewing ≤ N` and `fee ≤ N`, never below the fee (l. 94); a start above the fee is cut to the fee at the first step (l. 60). The rule therefore switches at most once and one way. With a leak it can (l. 646); with `fee > N` no theorem speaks (by hand it falls).

**Is the rule set from a share, a count or a level?** OPENED: from a level, as one bit; the level is fed by a count (one a renewing contract a step). The table's "rule set again from a share" is whole here only if "the reserve that the counts feed" is read as a share; I do not read it so.

**Can it be turned?** OPENED, the instance by hand. `exact_schedule_topup` (l. 516) at `s = ⟨0,B,0⟩`, where `available k 0 0 = 0` by l. 386: correction is funded at some step exactly where `fee − B ≤ extra`; then l. 576 gives `⟨N,fee,cap⟩`. So the absorbing region is left by a transfer of at most `fee`, at any step, and capacity comes back whole. "For good" is under `next` alone. The theory page says the same of capacity (l. 147: "a depleted v can subsequently recover", "not indispensable material support").

## Where this reading could be disputed

- **"Two statements, not one."** One could say the iff of l. 504 with `correction_stays_funded` is the one law, the regimes being corollaries. As derivations, yes; as statements, the iff is of an event and the regimes are two implications.
- **"`k` is a parameter."** l. 141 can be read as "against every schedule". No statement plays a schedule against anything.
- **"The selecting is supplied."** One could say `revised_slot_is_optimal` makes `allocated` the outcome of selecting. It certifies the direction of the changed slots at one step under the flag of that step; it derives neither the number changed, nor its full use, nor `others`.
- **"A level, not a share."** The count feeds the level one for one, so with `N` fixed a count is a share times `N`. But the rule reads the accumulated reserve, not the present count.
- **"Standstill."** The file's own page (l. 169) says the full carrier history has changing generation identities and need not converge. In `PopulationRealization` the generation index and `consumed` rise at both outcomes alike (l. 46); that is a counter, the same in the destructive course.
