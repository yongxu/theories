## 7. The nearest single statement to both ways in one course

Of the five files, the nearest single statement is `ResponseCapacity.exact_no_investment_viability` (`MA/ResponseCapacity.lean` l. 318), read with `optimal_live_history_matches_greedy` (l. 407). Together they say that every course chosen by the immediate-support criterion is the zero-investment course while it lives. So l. 318 is the exact boundary of the selected course itself:
- It lasts for ever iff `0 < support ∧ demand ≤ response`.
- Otherwise response, which the criterion does not read, runs down, then support runs down, then the course stops for good.

It is one course and one iff. There is a selection that does not read what it rests on, a level that is needed, and an end that is absorbing. Other candidates fall further short:
- PC's boundary (l. 504) is about reaching funding, and its two ends are two theorems.
- MarketInstances' two-sided statement (l. 658) is over a parameter, and its law has no level.

What l. 318 exactly lacks:
- (a) The second way. The lasting side is `⟨response, support + demand*t⟩` (l. 249): one number piling up while everything else stands still. Nothing changes form and nothing more goes on afterwards.
- (b) Anything that multiplies, any share, and any rule set again. The criterion is fixed, and the policy is zero.
- (c) That the losing side is a *selection's* doing *while a turn existed*. That needs l. 345 or the witness `investment_has_real_opportunity_cost` (l. 194, `decide`), and neither is in the statement.
- (d) A derived "for good": it is the step's first branch.
- (e) Being more than N2. It compares the start with a threshold (`demand ≤ response`) on a law that is monotone on each side. It agrees in shape with `Stock.repeat_covered_iff` (`B/Stock.lean` l. 319), and I name no map under which they agree word for word.
