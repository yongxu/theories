# The passages the atlas tells, read for roads between dynamics: one found, and what else came of it

*AI-written (Claude Fable, board row FM), 22 November 2026; checked against its sources by a reader who took no part. A record of an evening's read-only survey, for the session that holds the atlas (row DJ) and for whoever next asks how the atlas's dynamics are joined. Nothing here is in the Lean package or on the atlas, and nothing here is a result: every line is a reading of a theorem's statement against two entries, and that a statement is about a dynamic no proof gives.*

## What was asked

The atlas tells passages in two places: a dynamic's exits (329) and its transitions (105). 71 exits name another dynamic as where they lead; the rest tell the far end in words, and no transition names a dynamic at either end. The survey asked, of each passage told in words with a law behind it, and of 39 theorems that a Lean-printed list crossed with the atlas had shown with a notion of one dynamic in the premises and of another in the conclusion (row DJ's aid): is this a road, that is, one theorem whose statement places a course under one dynamic and then, at a later index, at another value of a parameter or with a premise dropped, under another?

The questions were written with what counts as no road first, attacked by two readers before any run, one of whom tried them on twelve real items and found no road, and rewritten in the first attacker's text ([the questions as frozen](QUESTIONS-ROADS.md.txt), [hashes and clock](FROZEN-QUESTIONS.sha256.txt), [the first form](QUESTIONS-ROADS.first-form.md.txt), [the attack on the definition](readers/ATTACK-the-definition-fable.md.txt), [the trial of twelve](readers/ATTACK-try-twelve-opus.md.txt)). The trial's forecast, made for the 252 items a Claude reader would then have read, was put on record in the frozen file before the run: roads that a judge and a checker confirm 0 to 8, more likely under 5; overlaps 10 to 25; blanks 0 to 5; passages inside one dynamic 55 to 65 percent; not a passage or an end that is no place 20 to 30 percent.

## Who read what

- One read-only pass by a reader of another kind of model (Astra 6.2) answered all 393 items in 34 minutes ([its report](astra/REPORT.md.txt), [its answers](astra/ANSWERS.jsonl.txt)).
- Four judges who took no part and were told no answer were given 62 items between them, unmarked and shuffled, each item to one judge: the reader's three proposed roads, three items it had answered "inside one dynamic", and the 56 exits of the atlas that already name a dynamic and have a law. For the three proposed roads the queue named every theorem the reader had cited (three for C21, six each for C30 and C39), a law of the far dynamic among them, without saying which was the item's. They had the atlas's entries with exits and transitions removed (their returns: [1](readers/JUDGE-1.md.txt), [2](readers/JUDGE-2.md.txt), [3](readers/JUDGE-3.md.txt), [4](readers/JUDGE-4.md.txt); [the key](JUDGE-KEY.json.txt); [the verdicts set against the key by script](JUDGE-SCORED.json.txt)).
- A checker opened the three proposed roads against both entries and a sample of 24 of the reader's "inside one dynamic" and "no place" answers (the list is in the checker's material list; that a seeded script drew it beforehand is told, the script not being among the files), 12 of them witness exits; of the 141 witness exits 127 had that one reader and no other ([its return](readers/CHECK-roads-and-sample-opus.md.txt)).
- A second checker opened the reader's 22 overlaps and 5 walls ([its return](readers/CHECK-overlaps-and-walls-fable.md.txt)).

## What was found

**One road that the reader, a judge and the checker each call a road.** `Becoming.Web.optimum_ends_beyond` (`Optimum.lean` l. 740), from `what-an-optimum-does-beyond-what-couples-in` to `three-outcomes-of-a-loss`, by an index (needs met at t; the dependent inactive at t + 1), sufficient. The checker adds a second far end the reader missed, `loss-of-renewal-for-good`, and, among what counts against entering it as it stands: the hypothesis that the dependence persists and the need is no weaker at t + 1 is this law's alone in the near dynamic, so the road starts from a part of the near dynamic; it gives one step and not the far dynamic's law of every later step; and its proof calls neither far-end law. No exit names the pair. The reader also answered it "inside one dynamic"; the judge and the checker do not count that, since the near dynamic carries the far end only through this theorem's own place on its list, and the judge says that on the other reading of the definition it is inside one dynamic.

**Two proposed roads that are not confirmed.** `ReadInTime.kept_once_favoured` (C30): the judge and the checker both place it inside `loss-read-in-time-by-the-others`. `TurningInTime.locked_turns_in_time_share_iff` (C39), from `capture-through-the-rule` to `turning-in-time`: the reader and the judge call it a road, exact; the checker does not: both sides of the iff are the far dynamic's notions and the proof is one application of `turns_in_time_iff`, a passage the far dynamic's own exit and transition already tell (inside one dynamic); the near dynamic's locked course is made the "course as it was" of that law with every lock premise kept and no index quoted at which it leaves the lock (an overlap); and the four lock premises are each taken by laws of four to six dynamics, so that only the notion in the near dynamic's name ties the course to it. It is listed as "reader and judge ROAD, checker otherwise", not dropped. On the three items the reader had answered "inside one dynamic" no judge named a road.

**Overlaps.** Of the reader's 22, the second checker finds: 6 lines (9 items) that stand and that the atlas's store does not yet say, all of them from the candidates, one of the six (four items) told inside one entry's mechanism and law with no link drawn to the other entry; 8 that stand and are already case rows; 5 that do not stand as overlaps between two dynamics. The checker did not read the crosswalk or the records' pages for whether one of them tells an overlap that the store does not. The six are in its return with theorem, file and line; three of them join `every-loss-passes-a-juncture` with `necessarily-drawn-is-not-returning` or `returning-where-the-course-branches` through theorems of `Junctures.lean` that are on no dynamic's list.

**Walls.** All five stand as walls in the statement, three of them more narrowly than "no course under one dynamic's premises is under another's notion": C01 denies returning of the drawn configuration and not of its course, C32 is against the notion of standing late and not against the other dynamic's built courses, and C35 names no course and is against a premise that laws of five dynamics take. Four are already laws of the near entry; the store has no field for a wall.

**No blank**: no far end was found that is a going-on in its own right with no entry.

**The other verdicts**, by the reader alone except for the sample, counted by item, an item counted under each verdict it carries (55 items carry more than one; C21 and ten of the 22 overlaps are among those counted inside one dynamic): 307 items inside one dynamic, 31 whose far end is no place, 16 that state no passage, 27 matches by name, 40 items already drawn. In the sample of 24 the checker would change 7 verdicts, all from "inside one dynamic" to "not a passage" (4) or "no place" (3), six of them among the 12 witness exits; it found no missed road, overlap or wall there. All 793 declarations the reader cites are at the file and line cited and every quoted statement is in its file word for word; whether each entry was read whole cannot be checked by script, and what the reader left out on the three proposed roads suggests the entries' ledgers were not always gone through.

**The atlas's own 56 exits that name a dynamic, as the judges placed them.** Not told what the atlas draws, the judges answered, each exit by one judge: a road between the atlas's own pair for 10 (4 sufficient, 6 witness; 5 by a dropped premise, where no course moves, 3 by an index, 2 by a parameter); a road between another pair for 1; inside one dynamic for 30; an overlap for 12; other no-road for 3. Three answers carry two of these and are counted once: one road between the atlas's own pair is also "inside one dynamic" read from the second list its theorem stands on, the road between another pair is an overlap read from the atlas's own home, and one answer names both "inside one dynamic" and an overlap and is counted as an overlap. This measures the questions against the store and the store against the questions, and is no answer key either way: the judges were shown the theorem and not the exit's home, its words or its far end. It goes to row DJ as a finding.

## What may and may not be said

- Of 393 told passages and candidates, one came out a road by the three readings (the reader, one judge, one checker), which is inside the forecast written before the run for confirmed roads; the reader's share of passages inside one dynamic, 307 of 393, is above the forecast's 55 to 65 percent, which was made for 252 of the items. That is what was found in what was read, and 336 of the 393 items had the one reader and no judge or checker. It is not a count of the map's roads and says nothing of passages the atlas does not tell.
- The passages the atlas tells are, by this reading, mostly inside one dynamic. That is not a shortfall of the atlas: a passage told in words is not an undrawn road.
- If roads between dynamics are wanted, this survey suggests that few are waiting among the passages the atlas already tells in words and the 39 candidates; of theorems the atlas does not tell and the aid does not show it says nothing. That is a suggestion from one pass, one definition and one evening, not a finding about the package.
- The definition is one reading of "road". The judges' answers on the atlas's own 56 show how much turns on it.
