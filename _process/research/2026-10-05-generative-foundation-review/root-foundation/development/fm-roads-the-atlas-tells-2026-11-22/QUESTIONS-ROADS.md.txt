# A survey of passages the atlas tells: which are roads between dynamics, which overlaps, which blanks

*AI-written (Claude Fable, board row FM), 22 November 2026. Second form: the first ([kept beside this](QUESTIONS-ROADS.first-form.md)) was attacked by two readers before any run, one on the definition and one by trying the questions on twelve real items ([the definition](readers/ATTACK-the-definition-fable.md), [the trial](readers/ATTACK-try-twelve-opus.md)); the replacements below are the first reader's text, with the second's additions marked. A read-only survey: no Lean is written, built or changed, nothing is entered on the atlas, and what it returns is four lists on paper (roads, overlaps, blanks, passages inside one dynamic), each line with its theorem, its ends and who read it, for the session that holds the atlas (row DJ) to judge. None of the four is the wanted one. Frozen when its hash stands in `FROZEN-QUESTIONS.sha256.txt`.*

## Why this

The atlas (`theories/becoming/results/atlas/entries/dynamics.json`, 186 dynamics) links its dynamics in two ways that have been told as one. An **overlap agreed** says two dynamics are one law seen twice: a case row, a joining. A **road** says how a course gets from one dynamic to another: where it passes, under what condition, how strongly that is known. On 16 November Ren wrote of an interconnected map "fully connected, with phase transitions, attractors, paths clearly mapped out" ([his words](../../../sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-11-16-no-corners-the-interconnected-map.md); the sentence is conditional). That these words are of the second kind is row DJ's reading of 22 November, which this row takes; he did not say it, nor that a path is a theorem from one dynamic of the atlas to another. Of the three things he names this survey takes one: a transition inside one dynamic it sets aside (N1), and it asks nothing about attractors. His question of 22 November is not a ground for this survey: he did not say what "represented as the map" asks for. By script on main `0db3aaacc`: of 329 exits 71 name another dynamic (56 with a law, 15 with none) and 258 tell the far end in words (256 with a law, 2 with none); of 105 transitions none names a dynamic at either end. Nobody has asked, passage by passage, whether a far end told in words is another dynamic of the atlas. That is a question not yet read, not a shortfall: an exit told in words is not an undrawn road.

## The forecast, on record before the run

The reader who tried the first form on twelve items (five transitions, four exits, three candidates, by a fixed rule) found no road among them: eight passages inside one dynamic, two ends that are no place, one overlap, one match by name. Its forecast for the 252 items a Claude reader would have read: passages inside one dynamic 55 to 65 percent; not a passage or an end that is no place 20 to 30 percent; a match by name for perhaps half of the 39 candidates; overlaps 10 to 25, found mainly where the proof is read and likely the most useful find; blanks 0 to 5; roads that a judge and a checker confirm 0 to 8, more likely under 5. A survey that returns almost no roads is the likely result and is to be reported as that.

## What a road is, for this survey

A **road from X to Y**, X and Y two different dynamics of the atlas, is a theorem of the package in whose statement three things can be quoted.

1. **The near end.** Hypotheses that place a course (a run of a process, an orbit of a law, a population over generations, the optima of an order) under X: premises that X's laws take (the ids in X's `premises[].takes`, with the ledger's Lean names) or the notion in X's name. A premise that the ledger shows taken by laws of Y too, or of three or more dynamics, places the course under none of them by itself.
2. **What changes**, one of: **(t)** an index: the near end holds at a step, generation or date and the far end at a later one or from one on; quote both indices. **(p)** a parameter: the statement, or it and its twin for the other side, gives the far end on one stated range of a parameter and X's own outcome on the other; quote both ranges and name both theorems. **(d)** a dropped premise: a premise X's laws take that the theorem does not take or contradicts; name its ledger id (for an exit, the store's `drops`). In (d) no course moves: the statement sets X beside its neighbour, and the line says so. Where none of the three can be quoted it is no road (N3).
3. **The far end.** The part of the statement that places that course under Y: on the **premise side**, it gives premises that a law of Y takes (name the law, the ids given and the ids not given), so that Y's law goes on from there; or on the **outcome side**, it is what a law of Y concludes (name the law). For a `necessary` law the far end stands among the hypotheses and the conclusion is the condition.

A notion can be carried by several entries. The far end is answered by every dynamic whose name or laws carry it, not by one; where the home is among them the answer is N1. That a sentence of an entry's mechanism mentions a notion, or that its list holds a declaration, does not make the notion that entry's.

Where one theorem takes the course out of X to a state and another, with that state among its hypotheses, places it under Y (the store's `law` and `to_law`), the pair is a **road in two theorems**, listed apart with both named.

## What counts as no road (written first)

- **N0. Not a passage** (added from the trial). The theorem named states no passage at all: a bound, a count, a characterisation of a rest point, a one-step identity. Say what it states. It is not forced into N1, N2a or N3.
- **N1. Inside one dynamic.** The home is among the dynamics that carry the far end. Answered only after the search of Q2. The kind of a transition does not decide it: the two sides of a threshold can be two dynamics.
- **N2. To no dynamic.** No dynamic's name or laws carry the far end. Then: **(a)** an end or a property: the far end has no definition in the Lean, or no theorem takes it among its hypotheses (a bound, a count, "is decided", "outside the region"). **(b)** a **blank**: it has a definition (name it) and a theorem on no dynamic's list takes it among its hypotheses (name it): something goes on from there and the atlas has no entry for it. If a theorem on some dynamic Z's list takes it, it is neither: answer it as a far end on Z's premise side.
- **N3. An overlap, not a road.** Nothing of (t), (p), (d) can be quoted: two notions are said of one thing at one index. An iff between two properties of one course or configuration; an implication with every premise of X kept and nothing later ("every X is a Y"); a theorem that makes one dynamic's case an instance of another's law. Listed apart with both notions and both dynamics. Where the store draws a theorem of this form as an exit (its four `exact` exits to a dynamic are of it), say so; it is still N3 here.
- **N5. A match by name, at either end.** The statement shares a word, a namespace or a declaration with a dynamic, and (a) the entry is about something else; or (b) the declaration is a premise or carrier that laws of several dynamics take, or a structure the statement is stated on, or is said of something other than the course; or (c) the statement's notion and the entry's are two declarations and no theorem carries one to the other. Under (c) name the carrying theorem, or answer N5: a reading that two notions are the same is not a link.
- **N6. Already drawn.** An exit of the atlas, in the home's entry or in the other end's, already names this pair: **(a)** with this theorem as its `law` or `to_law`; **(b)** with another law; **(c)** with no law (argued, or a reading). Say which and cite the exit. Under (b) and (c) the theorem is still listed, as a further law or as a law for an exit that has none, with whether its edge is that exit's edge.
- **N7. Direction.** Say which way the statement goes, quoting it. An iff one side of which is a condition on the start, the rule or a parameter and the other an outcome of the course is one passage, from the condition to the outcome, `exact`; its converse is no second road. An iff of two outcomes at different indices gives two candidates, each answered apart. An iff of two properties at one index is N3. A conclusion that denies a notion is no road to a dynamic that carries the notion; it is answered against the dynamics whose entries are about the denial, and where there is none it is N2a, a boundary of X. A theorem that shows no course under X's premises is under Y's notion is a **wall**, listed apart.

Further, from the first reader, each to be answered under the test named or listed apart as said:

1. **A boundary of X.** A premise of X dropped, or X's own notion denied, and the far end is only "not X" (X001). Answered N2a; never a road by "with one of X's premises dropped" alone.
2. **A wall.** The theorem shows that no course under X's premises is under Y's notion (C09, C35, C32, C01). Listed apart: it says there is no road there while the premises hold.
3. **A shared premise or carrier taken for an end.** A ledger premise that laws of several dynamics take (`Accrues`: C22 and six more), a structure the statement is stated on (C27), a hypothesis said of something other than the course (C07).
4. **Same by a reading.** The statement's notion and the entry's are two declarations with no theorem carrying one to the other. Moved here from Q4.
5. **A case, not a passage.** The theorem makes X's case an instance of Y's law at one index (C38), or contains X with every premise kept in Y (C25, C13). N3, whatever the verb of the docstring ("makes", "forces", "breaks").
6. **A word of Y reached, not Y's premises nor what its laws conclude.** The conclusion matches a phrase in Y's mechanism and gives no premise a law of Y takes and nothing a law of Y concludes (X211 against `three-outcomes-of-a-loss`: a break within a horizon, where the laws about a loss take below-the-need at every later step).
7. **A necessary law with no course shown to pass.** The far end is a hypothesis; no theorem shows it can be met from X. Listed as a condition on a road, under `necessary`.
8. **A witness placed by its name.** A built example in Y's module or with Y's word, and no theorem placing it under Y.
9. **Drawn already from the other end** (N6a), so that a transition filed at the far end is neither answered N1 nor returned as new.
10. **The wrong theorem.** A statement quoted by short name from another namespace; an item so answered is "not read".

## The material

- `INDEX-OF-DYNAMICS.md`: every dynamic's id, name, the start of its mechanism and its declarations, by script. It is a way in and not enough to judge by: 179 of 186 mechanisms are cut in it and it lists no exits or transitions. Every answer is made from the store's full entry.
- `ITEMS.jsonl`, 393 items (by script): **T001 to T098**, the transitions with a `between`, each with its declarations (97 tell two ends in words; T054 tells only the far one); **X001 to X256**, the exits whose far end is told in words and that name a law (38 exact, 50 sufficient, 27 necessary, 141 witness; 17 of them share their law with a transition item); **C01 to C39** … (row DJ's aid: a floor and a list of places to look first. It shows where a listed declaration stands in an implication and nothing more: it shows no direction for an iff; a denied notion counts as the conclusion's; a premise that laws of many dynamics take is filed under one (`ClosedCapture.Accrues`, in seven of the 39); it does not tell whether a hypothesis is said of the course. A candidate has no home: 16 are on no dynamic's list, 11 on the premise end's, 8 on the conclusion end's, 4 on a third dynamic's.)
- The store itself, its ledger of premises (`premises.json`), and the Lean: the package (`experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/becoming/Becoming/`) and, for the 48 declarations named in 44 items that are not in it, the live file elsewhere in the repository, never a frozen copy beside it.
- Out of scope, said so that it is not taken as checked: the 71 exits that already name a dynamic (56 with a law; 15 with none, argued or a reading, for which a theorem would be a first law: not looked for here, except as N6c says); the 2 exits in words with no law; the 7 transitions with no `between` (5 routes that are readings, with no declaration, and 2 with declarations); any passage the atlas does not tell at all and the aid does not show.

## The questions, of each item

**Q1. The statement.** Open the theorem named (the transition's declarations; the exit's `law`; the candidate's theorem). Quote its statement. Is it a passage in the sense above: what moves (a course, a parameter), from what to what? If the declaration named is a definition, or the passage is spread over several theorems, say so and name the one that carries it, if one does. Where a verdict turns on how the theorem is proved (an overlap that only the proof shows), read the proof's text and say what it calls. Give the file, the line and the full name: short names repeat across namespaces. For a transition open every declaration, not the first; where each side has its own theorem, say which gives which side.

**Q2. The two ends.** Both ends are in question for every item: the home is where the atlas files the passage, and can be its far end; a candidate has no home. Without looking for a hoped-for answer, in the store and not in the index: (a) the near end, as the definition's 1; (b) what changes, as its 2, quoted; (c) the far end, as its 3: every dynamic whose name or laws carry it, each entry opened, premise side or outcome side. Answer one of: **ROAD X → Y** with (t), (p) or (d) and the side; **ROAD IN TWO**; **N1**; **N2a**; **N2b** (the definition and the theorem that takes it); **N3** (both notions, both dynamics); **N5** (which of a, b, c, at which end); **N6** (a, b or c, the exit cited); **N7** (which clause); **WALL**. A witness and a necessary law are answered the same way; their strength goes in Q3. **N0** is also an answer. More than one answer may be given where each holds (a passage inside one dynamic that is also a built example).

**Q3. The strength, as the statement has it.** `exact` (an iff one side of which is a condition on the start, the rule or a parameter and the other the passage; an iff of two properties at one index is N3, not `exact`), `sufficient` (an implication to the passage), `necessary` (the passage, far end included, is among the hypotheses and the conclusion is the condition: what passing requires, not that any course passes), `witness` (a built example, with the theorem that places it under Y). Where the store's strength differs from what the statement says, say so. Where the statement is a bound and not a passage, the strength is "a bound" and the answer is N0. A **witness** is answered through Q2 like any item. It is a road of strength `witness` only where a theorem, named, shows the built course under Y (the store's `to_law`). That the example is built in Y's module or carries Y's word is N5.

**Q4. What the atlas would need to say so.** For a ROAD: `to`, `law`, `to_law` (the theorem that places the course under Y; the same as `law` unless the road is in two theorems), `edge` in words taken from the statement and no further, `strength`, `drops` (ledger ids); `records` and `source` are row DJ's. And what would count against entering it: a hypothesis of the theorem that X's entry does not have and the edge does not say (the road then starts from a part of X); a premise of Y's law that the conclusion does not give (the course reaches Y's word and Y's law does not yet speak of it; a break at one step is not a loss at every later step); a far end that is Y's only through a carrying theorem, named. For a transition the fields are `between.from` and `between.to` as ids.

**Every answer is led by OPENED (you read the statement in the Lean and the entry in the store yourself) or TOLD.** An item you could not open is listed as not read, not guessed.

## How

One read-only pass by a reader of another kind of model (Astra 6.2) over all 393 items, answering Q1 to Q4 of each. The trial put a Claude reader's cost at about eight thousand fresh tokens an item and more than one context for thirty items, so no Claude reader reads the items first: the Claude side reads what the Astra pass returns. Then a judge who took no part. Its queue, unmarked and shuffled: every item some reader called a ROAD or a ROAD IN TWO; as many again that every reader answered N1, N2a or N3; and the 56 lawed exits of the store that already name a dynamic. The judge is given each theorem's full name, file and line, the Lean to open, and the entries of the store remade by script with `exits` and `transitions` removed and nothing cut; it is not given the item's home, its words or any answer. It is asked the whole of Q2, and "no road" is a full answer. A road is confirmed where the judge quotes a change, the dynamics it names for the far end include the reader's Y, and it answers none of N3, N5, N7 or WALL. A road not confirmed is listed as "reader ROAD, judge otherwise" with both answers, not dropped; an item no reader called a road and the judge does is listed too. Reported with the lists: on how many of the unmarked non-roads the judge named a road; of the 56, how many the judge places where the store does and how many it answers N3 (not an answer key: where the questions and the store part is a finding for row DJ). Then a checker on the lists as wholes: every ROAD opened against both entries in the store; every blank kept or struck by N2b's two things alone; the 26 theorems that more than one item names (53 items) read for whether their items were answered alike; a sample, its size said beforehand, of N1 and N2a answers opened as the ROADs are, the witness items among them, since those have one reader; the disagreements between the readers and the Astra pass listed, not resolved by vote. Where this paragraph says "reader", the Astra pass is the reader.

Cap, stated before the run and counted in fresh tokens by each run's own footer: about 15 million on Astra's side; about 1.5 million on the Claude side for the judge, the checker and the check of the note. No Lean, no lake, no build, no gate, no core run.

## What the survey may and may not conclude

- It may list: roads, each with (t), (p) or (d), its side, its strength and who read it, confirmed by the judge and the checker; roads in two theorems; overlaps; walls; blanks; passages inside one dynamic. A road by a dropped premise, a `necessary` road and a `witness` road are each listed under that name and not with roads along which a theorem shows a course moving.
- It may not say how many roads the map has, nor that a region has none, nor set families or regions beside each other by how many lines each has: only what was found in what was read.
- It enters nothing on the atlas. Whether a line becomes an exit that names a dynamic is row DJ's, by its own rules and its own readers.
- A ROAD here is a claim that a recognition holds between a statement and two entries; that a statement is about a dynamic, no proof gives.
