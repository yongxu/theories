## Cost

I used about 25 tool calls for the twelve items and the checks, about 13 of them on the items themselves. With batched printing that is about 1 call per item; reading one item at a time it is 3 to 5 calls (statement, full home entry, index search by notion, one to four far-end entries, and the proof for a possible N3). Material per item: statement 0.3k to 1.5k tokens, full home entry 0.5k to 1.5k, far-end entries 0.6k to 0.8k each, index grep output 0.5k to 2k. That is about 5k to 8k tokens read, plus 1k to 2k written, about 8k fresh tokens per item.
- **252 items at 8k ≈ 2.0M fresh tokens.** Add judges for the few ROADs (small) and a checker (about 0.3M), and the total fits 3.5M only if the cap counts fresh tokens. If it counts processed context, cached re-reads included, a reader holding 35 items over about 60 to 80 turns averages more than 100k of context per turn, about 6M to 8M per reader. The cap is then exceeded several times over. The questions should say which count the cap is.
- **A reader given 30 to 40 items** reads about 250k to 320k fresh tokens. One context window cannot hold it unless the reader prints each item in one call (statement, full home entry, entries named), writes each verdict to its file as it goes, and keeps no bulk in context. Batches of 15 to 20 items per reader are safer.
- **Periphery items cost more.** 44 items name a declaration outside `Becoming/`, 21 of them in the 252: X226 X227 T096 T095 T097 T089 X224 T093 X235 T024 X127 T064 T023 T090 T027 X041 T088 X220 X219 X221 T087. Each needs a repository-wide grep (about 1 minute here), and several have multiple frozen copies under `*-checks/`, `*-verification/` and `history/`. The live file has to be chosen as `tools/research/atlas.py` `Repository.declared` chooses it.
