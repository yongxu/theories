## 0. The short answer

- All eleven are possible as worded, as far as paper goes: every bridge and lemma is in the module's import
  closure, every expected-uses name in `members.json` is the name Lean prints, every statement can stay
  binder for binder at a destination that exists, and nothing between an old and a new place uses a moved name.
- One checker term breaks rule 3 as written: member 1, line 2 is 102 characters wide. It fits in 3 lines, or 2,
  once rebalanced (section 3).
- On paper, I expect no member to fail rule 4. The members with the least margin are 7 and 8 (one line each).
- The test is met by the letter in three places while the claim may not be (section 4): member 7's bridge
  `iter_eq_orbit` carries its own `Nat.brecOn` and is dated 22 November. Member 6's checker term reaches an
  induction on traces through `kernel_certificate`, three calls down, which `compare.py` does not see. Member 3
  passes even if member 1 is put back.
- Rule 1 cannot be followed to the letter by any member: every one of the eleven proofs is in equation form
  (`| 0 => …`), so a term proof needs ` :=` added to the statement's last line. `compare.py` strips the
  `:=` and counts the text as the same. The brief does not say this is allowed (section 5).

## 3. Member by member: does the term have the member's type? (on paper, OPENED)

Lemma signatures, read at PredicateIteration:69–152:
`postfixed_in_iter {S} {Φ} {V₀} (mono) {K} (inside : ∀ y, K y → V₀ y) (post : ∀ y, K y → Φ K y) : ∀ n y, K y →
iter Φ V₀ n y` (`omit start in`), and `iter_pred`/`iter_within {S} {Φ} {V₀} (mono) (start : ∀ y, Φ V₀ y → V₀ y)`.

The same remark holds for every `postfixed_in_iter` call. Its output `iter Φ V₀ n y` depends on the explicit
`n y`, so Lean's app elaborator does not push the expected type in before the arguments. `K` and `V₀` are fixed
by the arguments, as Miller patterns at a bound variable, or at `finalize`. Wherever a pattern argument meets a
type headed by a metavariable, the match is postponed until then.

Labelled:340 and Labelled:347. `fun k {s s'} …` stops the implicit lambda; there is a precedent at Likeness:860.
