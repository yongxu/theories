- `MonotoneStep Φ` (l. 82): `∀ {X Y}, (∀ z, X z → Y z) → ∀ y, Φ X y → Φ Y y`.
- `postfixed_in_iter` (l. 146): premises `mono`; `inside : ∀ y, K y → V₀ y`; `post : ∀ y, K y → Φ K y`.
  Proof: `| 0 => inside | n + 1 => fun y h => mono (postfixed_in_iter inside post n) y (post y h)`:
  induction on n, generalised over y; at the step, `post` at y first, then the IH under `mono`.
  `start` is omitted for it (l. 144).
- `iter_pred` (l. 103): `mono`; `start : ∀ y, Φ V₀ y → V₀ y`. Proof `| 0 => start | n + 1 => mono (iter_pred n)`.
- `iter_within` (l. 108): same premises; proof `| n + 1, y, h => iter_within n y (iter_pred mono start n y h)`:
  two nested inductions (iter_within peels one level through `iter_pred`, which is its own induction).
- `iter_iff_stages` (l. 87): `mono`, `zero`, `next` clauses as iffs; one generic induction.

## 1. `Alike.alikeTo` (Likeness:916) as `postfixed_in_iter` through `iter_iff_alikeTo` (1094). OPENED

(a) Premises. `mono` = `alike_mono` (1086). Start V₀ = `agree` = `AlikeTo F G 0` (l. 1092). The
proposed call takes K = the witness bisimulation B; `inside` = `(bisim p.1 p.2 h).1`, `post` =
`bisim p.1 p.2 h` (Bisimulation, Labelled:293, is word for word "B lies in alikeStep F G B"
once `alikeStep`'s first conjunct `AlikeTo F G 0` is unfolded to the claims iff, l. 887).
The old proof's class is not B but **Alike F G itself**: base `| 0, _, _, alike => alike.realized`
(l. 918) is `inside` for K = Alike; the step `⟨alike.realized, … alike.step step …, …
alike.symm.step step' … joined.symm⟩` (ll. 920-923) is `post` for K = Alike, assembled from
`Alike.realized`, `Alike.step` (Labelled:348, 355) and, for the back clause, `Alike.symm`
(Labelled:338, through `Bisimulation.converse`). Every premise's content is in the old proof. The
old proof argues one thing beyond them: it gets the back clause by symmetry of likeness, which the
law does not need (the back clause is in `Bisimulation` directly). The call drops this; nothing is
lost.
(b) Same induction: on k, generalised over the pair (the IH is used at `t, t'`), with the step
"match at the pair, then IH at the outcomes", which is `mono (IH) y (post y h)` fused into one
term. The old proof's K (Alike) differs from the call's K (B); both are instances of the one lemma.
(c) Bridge: `iter_iff_stages` with `Iff.rfl` for both clauses (ll. 1096-1097). No premise, no
restriction (all pairs of S × S'), notion unchanged. No own induction.
(d) "A bisimulation is a class of pairs inside agreement on claims that the matching step
preserves, so it lies in every iterate from agreement, which is likeness to every depth." True to
the Lean.
(e) **CASE** (with a small remainder only in the old proof: the symmetry detour for the back clause,
which the call does not need).

## 7. `Tolerance.withheld_kept` (Tolerance:303) as `iter_within` through `iter_eq_orbit` (238). OPENED

(a) `mono` = `(monotone_iff_keepsInclusion next).mpr keeps` (l. 232: the two are the same up to
binder order). `start` = `empty`: with `allWithheld = fun _ => False` (l. 220), `∀ a, ¬ next
allWithheld a` is `∀ a, next V₀ a → V₀ a` word for word. Both premises are exactly the member's.
(b) **Not the same induction.** Old (ll. 305-307): `| n + 1 => fun a gives => empty a (keeps _ _ (IH …)
a gives)`: state (n+1) = next (state n) ⊆ next V₀ ⊆ V₀, one induction, `mono` applied to the IH
"state n ⊆ V₀". `iter_within` instead peels iter (n+1) ⊆ iter n by `iter_pred` (its own induction)
and then uses its IH: two nested inductions, `mono` applied to `iter_pred n`, not to "iter n ⊆ V₀".
The old proof is the order-dual of `postfixed_in_iter` (a class above the start that the step maps
into itself bounds every iterate), at the class V₀ itself; the law has no lemma stating that dual.
(c) `iter_eq_orbit` is proved by **its own induction** (`| n + 1 => congrArg Φ (…)`, ll. 240-241),
not by `iter_iff_stages`; it gives an equality of predicates, used through `congrFun`. No premise,
no restriction. After the change the induction on n has two homes: `iter_eq_orbit` and the law's.
`Law.run next s).state` reduces to `orbit` by `run_state` (Recurrence:325, rfl).
(d) "Under a step that keeps inclusion, if the step of nothing is nothing, then every iterate from
nothing is nothing." True to the Lean; but "every iterate lies in the start" is reached in the law
through the falling chain, which the old argument does not use.
(e) **CASE WITH A REMAINDER.** The sentence and both premises are the law's exactly; the remainder is
on the law's side: the call brings in `iter_pred` (the chain), which the old proof never argued, and
the old one-induction proof is not `iter_within`'s proof. A record should say "the statement of
`iter_within`", not "`iter_within`'s argument".

## 9. `Branching.fed_keeps` (Branching:164) as `postfixed_in_iter` through `iter_iff_reachable` (Attraction:128). OPENED

(a) `mono` = `pred_mono` (Attraction:124); V₀ = True; K = True; `inside` trivial; `post` = "every s
has a predecessor" = `fed s`. The premise's content (`fed`) is in the old proof.
(b) **Not the same induction.** `Reachable P n s` is `∃ x as, as.length = n ∧ Trace P x as s`
(Recurrence:118), and `Trace` grows at its **start** (`.cons step path`). The old proof
(ll. 165-168) holds s fixed (the IH is used at the same s, `fed_keeps fed n s`), and applies `fed`
to the **start** y of the trace it found, prepending one step. The law applies `post` at the
**endpoint** s (a predecessor x of s) and the IH at x: generalised over the state, recursion at the
other end of the trace. Same premise, used at the other end, in an induction with a different
generalisation.
(c) The bridge's zero clause is short, but its successor clause (Attraction:130-140) is the work of
turning a trace grown at the end into one grown at the start (`trace_append_iff`,
`List.dropLast_concat_getLast`, `ListTests.length_append`). No premise, no restriction, notion
unchanged, no own induction (it is `iter_iff_stages`); but the reversal it carries is exactly what
separates the old argument from the law's.
(d) "Where every configuration has a predecessor, everything is a class the predecessor step
preserves, so everything lies in every iterate, which is being the end of a trace of every length."
True to the Lean as a statement; it is not what the old proof argued.
(e) **DETOUR.** The call is shorter; the old proof's argument (extend at the start, s fixed) is not
the law's (extend at the end, generalised), and the bridge does the turning.

## 10. `Law.kept_along` (GrammarCourse:120) as `Law.KeepsRelation.run` (IdentificationRuns:313). OPENED

the induction is along an orbit of configurations, not on iterates of a step on predicates.

## Across the ten

Same sentence of the law:
- **A class the step preserves lies in every iterate** (`postfixed_in_iter`): 1, 3, 4, 6, 8 as cases;
  9 reaches the sentence only by a detour. 1 and 4 are the one sentence twice (the matching step,
  labelled with claims / unlabelled with E); 3 is 1 at equality.
- **The step takes the start into itself, so each iterate lies in the one before** (`iter_pred`):
  2 and 5, again the one sentence at the matching step and the inquiry step.
- **The same, so every iterate lies in the start** (`iter_within`): 7, statement only.
- 10 is a duplicate within the orbit laws, not of this law.

Not to let stand as written: **9** as "a case of `postfixed_in_iter`" on a board or record. It may
be filed as "its statement is the law's sentence at K = everything, through a bridge that reverses
the trace", not as the old argument. **7** should be filed as "the statement of `iter_within`", with
the note that the old one-induction proof is the order-dual of `postfixed_in_iter`, a lemma the law
does not have; if that lemma were added (a class Q with `Φ Q ⊆ Q` and V₀ ⊆ Q bounds every iterate),
7 would be its plain case at Q = V₀. For 1 and 6 the proposed calls use a different K (B for Alike)
or a different proof of `post` (`kernel_certificate`) than the old proof; both are still the law's
premises and nothing else.

What would count against this reading: if a reader holds that "the same induction" should be judged
only up to the bridge's iff (then 9's end-reversal is the bridge's business and 9 is a case), or that
any proof from the same two premises is "the argument" (then 7 is a plain case). I judged by where
each premise is applied in the recursion. If a build finds that `bisim p.1 p.2 h` is not accepted as
`alikeStep F G K p` without unfolding `AlikeTo F G 0`, that touches 1's call, not this verdict.
