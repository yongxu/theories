### Part H. The losing side of a choice, worded by a disjunction (new; home to be said)

`ScratchEdgeLaw.loseStep P guard X s := ¬ guard s ∨ ∀ a, Enabled P s a → ∃ t, P.step s a t ∧ X t`. The two theorems about it put the undecided proposition into `Enabled`. A reader observed that with a disjunction in place of the implication,

```lean
def lostStep (P : Process S A) (guard X : S → Prop) (s : S) : Prop :=
  ¬ guard s ∨ ∀ a, ¬ Enabled P s a ∨ ∃ t, P.step s a t ∧ X t
```

(each move cannot be taken, or has an outcome that loses sooner), the three-configuration process of `lose_edge_decides` no longer gives a decision: both levels become `¬ q ∨ q`. So:

1. `lostStep` is monotone, takes its start `fun s => ¬ guard s` into itself in the rising sense, and implies `loseStep`; where `Enabled` is decided (`∀ s a, Enabled P s a ∨ ¬ Enabled P s a`) they agree. A level of `lostStep` refutes the same level of `Viable` (`rise (lostStep P guard) (fun s => ¬ guard s) n s → ¬ Viable P guard n s`; mind `Viable`'s universes and check the levels are not off by one: a configuration with no enabled move fails `Viable … 1`).
2. **The question.** With every configuration listed and **every move listed** (`moves : List A`, `everyMove : ∀ a, a ∈ moves`):
   `rise (lostStep P guard) (fun s => ¬ guard s) (all.length + 1) y → rise (lostStep P guard) (fun s => ¬ guard s) all.length y`.
   On paper I expect it to hold with nothing decided, by the argument of `reach_shorten_at_places` (the conclusion as a parameter `G`; a later arrival at a spent place finishes at once), with one difference: the level at `y` is a statement of every move, so its proof needs a shorter witness for every move together, which with the conclusion as a parameter is one continuation inside another, move after move down the list of moves. Try it honestly. If it holds, prove it. If it does not go through, say exactly where.
3. **Without a list of the moves.** Either prove the same from `every` alone, or derive from that edge, for a family of propositions over the moves, a principle the core cannot prove (I expect something of the shape: from `∀ n, p n ∨ q` to `(∀ n, p n) ∨ q`, with moves `Nat`). Either answer is a result; if neither comes in about eight builds, say what you tried.
