## 3. Why H needs the move distinction

**Listed moves.** Removing a state gives two ways to finish. Revisiting that state applies its saved continuation immediately. At a fresh state the proof needs a shorter answer for every move. A disabled move supplies its answer directly. For a move with a losing outcome, the proof shortens that outcome with the remaining state places. `gather_moves` processes the move list with the same final conclusion `G`, so that either an earlier continuation finishes the proof or all the shorter answers reach the caller together. Neither state equality nor enabling needs a decision. The horizon agrees with viability even at a dead end. If every move is disabled, loss holds at level one, where viability would require an enabled move.

**Unlisted moves.** Use three states, root `none`, middle `some false`, and terminal `some true`. The guard is true everywhere. For each natural-number move `a`, the root can go to the middle and can go to the terminal given `q`. The middle can go to the terminal given `p a` and can go back to the root given `q`. The terminal has no moves.

Given `∀ a, p a ∨ q`, the root loses at level four. A level-three witness at the root, inspected at move zero, either takes the terminal edge and supplies `q`, or yields level-two loss at the middle. At the middle every move is enabled by its given disjunction. Its losing response must use the terminal edge and supply `p a`, since the root cannot lose at level one. Thus shortening yields `(∀ a, p a) ∨ q`. Taking `p a := f a = false` and `q := ∃ a, f a = true` gives `LPO` for every Boolean sequence. The checked result is the implication from the proposed edge to this constructive obstruction.
