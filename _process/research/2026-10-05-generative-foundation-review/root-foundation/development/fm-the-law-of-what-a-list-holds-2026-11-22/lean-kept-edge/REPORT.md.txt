## 4. The rising chain

Write `N = all.length` and `W n y = rise Ψ W₀ n y`. In the falling proof, spending the current place uses the positive evidence already in hand at the shorter depth. Reversing the desired arrow gives `W (n + 2) y` where that move would need `W (n + 1) y`. Monotonicity and the rising start carry evidence in the opposite direction.

On the exhaustive list `[()]`, start at `False` and take `Ψ X () := p ∨ (p → X ())` for an arbitrary proposition `p`. This step is monotone and the empty start rises vacuously. Level 1 is `p ∨ ¬p`. Level 2 always holds by the right branch, since a proof of `p` gives the left branch of level 1. Consequently a uniform positive edge even for this singleton decides every proposition. [Checked singleton implication](/Users/ren/tmp/fm/build4/Becoming/ScratchKeptEdge.lean:255)

What follows from the given premises is **`W (N + 1) y → ¬¬ W N y`**. Apply the falling law to `dual Ψ X y := ¬ Ψ (fun z => ¬ X z) y`. Its iterates from the complement of the start are exactly the negations of the rising iterates, pointwise. In the induction proving this, `not_not_all` combines doubly negated decisions across the finite list inside a negation. [Duality and finite edge](/Users/ren/tmp/fm/build4/Becoming/ScratchKeptEdge.lean:275)

**Stability of membership at the queried state and horizon suffices**, namely `¬¬ W N y → W N y`. A decision of `W N y` supplies it. Even `W₀` and state equality can remain undecided. More precisely, under the premise `W (N + 1) y`, stability there is necessary and sufficient for the positive result. Sufficiency is the checked double-negation theorem. Necessity follows because a proof of `W N y` makes that proposition stable. Uniformly over all instances, excluded middle suffices by stability and is required by the singleton. [Repaired law](/Users/ren/tmp/fm/build4/Becoming/ScratchKeptEdge.lean:291)
