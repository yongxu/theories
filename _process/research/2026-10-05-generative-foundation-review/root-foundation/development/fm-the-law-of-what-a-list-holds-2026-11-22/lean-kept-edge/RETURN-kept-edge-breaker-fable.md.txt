## 3. The rising chain

**Classically it holds.** With W0 inside step(W0), rise N+1 was inside rise N for every N from |S| to 2|S|+1: 15, 388, 169,095 and 482,514 (sampled) checks, 0 failures. With |S|−1 it fails 1, 4, 240 and 27 times. The dual chain equals the complement of the chain at every level (0 differences in 6, 144, 64,000 and 192,000 pairs). So the obstruction is about decisions, not truth.

**`singleton_rise_needs_em` in plain words.**
- Take any proposition p. On the one-point type, let the step say: "p holds, or p would give X". More X gives more of this, so the step is monotone.
- Start from nothing. Nothing is in the start, so "the start rises" holds vacuously, and any general rising law would apply here.
- Level 1 is "p, or p gives False", which is "p or not p".
- Level 2 is "p, or p gives level 1". Its second half holds outright: given p, level 1 holds by its left side. So level 2 is proved with no decision.
- The one-point list has length 1. An edge there carries level 2 down to level 1, and so hands over "p or not p" for an arbitrary p.

I found no fault. The term matches this line for line: the monotonicity proof maps the right alternative through the inclusion, and the witness is `.inr fun hp => .inl hp`.

**In models where propositions need not be decided** (`weaker.py`: open sets of a two-world line and of a fork):
- The module's step at "p true later only" gives level 1 = later only and level 2 = now and later. The edge fails.
- On the line it is the only failure among the 20 (monotone step, rising start) pairs; on the fork 10 of 95 fail.
- The doubly negated edge (`rise_not_not`) fails in none. The falling law on one point fails in none (17 and 64 falling starts).
- With two unrelated worlds, where everything is decided, the positive edge fails in none.

**What the theorem does and does not say.**
- It says: a positive rising edge for every monotone step on one point would decide every proposition.
- It does not say the rising law is false. With no axiom, the core can neither prove nor refute that.
- It says nothing against rising steps whose levels are decided. For those, `rise_of_stable` gives the positive edge from stability at the one queried point and level.
- The report's "stability is necessary" is the triviality that a proposition which holds is stable. What is actually shown is that the gap is exactly one double negation at that point.

**Why falling needs no decision and rising does.** Falling, the evidence in hand ("y is kept n+1 steps") is what gets recorded when y's place is spent. Rising, the evidence in hand is one level too high. The only thing to record is the negative "y is not yet reached", so the split comes out under a negation. `iter_dual_iff` and `rise_not_not` do exactly this, calling `iter_persists` once on the dual step, so the two sides are one law up to a double negation. I read both proofs term by term and found no fault.
