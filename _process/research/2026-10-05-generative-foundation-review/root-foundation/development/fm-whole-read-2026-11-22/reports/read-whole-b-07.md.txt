Things the fourteen questions miss that bear on the centre:
1. Continuation states in general a running down in full view. Each formation takes within what the step gives it (WithinOwn) while together they take more (WithinJoint fails). With a gate on the givers, n steps of that excess from a level below n + m end the renewal and every formation that depends on it at a need of at least m, the givers included (joint_excess_ends, family_excess_ends). The built cases are Pair.own_not_joint with Pair.no_web, Exceeding.all_end against Alone.all_continue, and Coupled.
2. Coupled shows that whether the law applies depends on how the conditions are grouped. Read alone, the patch shows no excess and its renewal is not gated on it (alone_within, alone_ungated). Read as one with its neighbour, the pair is gated and in excess, and both formations end (gated, excess, all_end). In Coupled the renewer also gives by drawing down the neighbour it depends on, which is a built case of giving that costs the giver.
3. The point of no return in Continuation is concluded, not assumed: once the level is below m it stays below (stays_below, family_stays_below_at), because the givers depend on the level. held_below and never_returns_below state the same for any exchange that gives no more than it takes below m.
4. In ConditionalWeights the exit bound comes from a level whose rises and falls do not depend on what is left. The opening account (not a theorem) says the bound is void from n = m·m/(p·q), and that persisting longer needs a giving that reads the level. Neither is proved there.
5. ContextArrivalTraces states in general when forgetting the context, or pooling contexts, is exact (erasedLaw_exact_iff_lifts, pooledLaw_exact_iff_joinClosed). Its built cases show a pooled law running a course that no single context runs (CrossingContexts) and a context changing within one course (ChangingContext.joins).
