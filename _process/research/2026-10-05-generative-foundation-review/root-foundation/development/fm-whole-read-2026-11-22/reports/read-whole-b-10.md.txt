Things the fourteen questions miss that bear on the centre:
(1) The module's laws are all of one form. A property of the census counts (n active, c ceasing, a arising) holds of every census of every world meeting a selection of the eight premises exactly when it holds of every Tally meeting listed conditions. Built queues and rings are the witnesses. So the module shows exactly what the premises can and cannot force on what is counted, and the selection S : Premise -> Bool is a choice among premises, not among steps.
(2) A no-return is there in two pieces, both stated, but no single world-level theorem joins them. counting_binds_jointly and counting_under_sound give, for every census, 0 < a t -> 0 < n t (nothing arises with nothing active). EnclosedQueue.empty_stays (l. 1057), though it sits in a witness namespace, is stated for any tally: under that condition an empty count stays empty. Together they say that once every giver has ceased, nothing arises again.
(3) Running down is general, with a sharp boundary. enclosure_bounds_change bounds all change after T by the standing at T. In finite worlds, a change bound is a law exactly when balance, embodiment and dissipation are all selected (finite_change_bound_iff). Outside those selections, endless one-for-one replacement is counted.
(4) An end of change cannot be concluded constructively (lpo_of_finite_ends, lpo_of_census_ends).
(5) binding_contingent shows that every premise binding decides neither rest nor endless replacement.
