What bears on the centre that the questions miss:
- CoupledDrawers states, for every law `G` meeting three gate premises (`gate`, `holds`, `regrows`), a sharp threshold: with two or more drawers in turn, every perfect profile drains when c < m and keeps exactly the need when m < c (`drains_below`, `kept_above`). This is a general law of a selection made by mutual best response, deciding between running a level down and keeping it, by whether the reward for a step at which the need is met exceeds the need. One drawer alone keeps where several drain (`one_keeps_several_drain`). Drawing at once without a pivotal drawer, the drain is perfect at every reward rate (`AtOnce.unpivotal_drain_perfect`, `Shores.drain_perfect`). Its point of no return (`gate`) is a premise on `G`, not concluded, and the level is a number, not a going-on that gives.
- CourseWorlds' `gap_polynomial_iff_no_loop_worlds` and `runaway_worlds` are dichotomies of bounded against runaway difference between two acts' worlds, not of going on against ending.
- In CouplingTraces, `settled_every_iff_steps` states that every coupling process settling is equivalent to a principle of reasoning (`RecursOrStops`), not a theorem of the model.
