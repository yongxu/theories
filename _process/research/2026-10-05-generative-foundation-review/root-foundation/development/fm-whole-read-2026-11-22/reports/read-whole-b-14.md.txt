Four things the fourteen questions miss that bear on the centre:

1. DrawHistory has a draw that runs a level down and a return that depends on what is left. Its no-return (inflow_kept, leaving_needs_a_lower_draw) is about the inflow rest, not about the loss of a giver. The giving g is a function handed in.

2. DrawnBeyond states a general law that selecting by a rise breaks an unseen dependence beyond (favours_breaking_beyond, breaks_beyond_ends). It also states that the share reaching beyond is never selected against (share_not_against, share_not_lower). But no statement has the loss coming back to the selection, and no step-to-step irreversibility is stated: breaks_beyond_ends is for one step only.

3. DrawnShaping proves a general dichotomy in which every course is locked one way or the other (drawn_locked_either), under every schedule of reading. DrawnReplacement and Drift show that, with drift, either lock is left with positive weight (lost_ge, fixed_ge). So a noiseless lock and a drifting chain give different answers to whether something can no longer be turned.
