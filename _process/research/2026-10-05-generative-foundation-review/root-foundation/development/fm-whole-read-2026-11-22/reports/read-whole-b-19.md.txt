What the questions miss that bears on the centre:
(1) Frequency's `Saturation` section is a general law of a coupling whose own passing wears its response down. With strengths f(a)·k, a being what has passed and f not rising, the coupling stops by some step exactly where f reaches zero (`Saturation.stops_iff`, l. 1329; in the formed course, `course_stops_iff`, l. 1443). With f at least one at every amount it recurs and stays inexact (`floored`, l. 1343; `floored_inexact`, l. 1363). This is a both-outcomes law of a giver's use running down what it gives, but the 'level' is a response function, not a stock of the model.
(2) Frequency also shows that 'every schedule settles' is equivalent to a Σ⁰₂ excluded middle (`settled_iff_recursOrStops`, l. 1008; `settled_every_iff`, l. 1024), and that settling even for couplings acting once gives LPO (`lpo_of_settled`, l. 885). So whether a coupling stops or recurs is not decided in the core without such a premise. This bears on any 'point of no return' a design would conclude.
(3) Gated gives, for every process, that a change of a quantity kept by ungated moves passes a gated move, located at one gated step (`change_passes_gate`, l. 64; `change_located`, l. 78). Asked of every process with the gate as a proposition, the origin law is exactly double-negation elimination (`origin_of_propositions_iff`, l. 197).
(4) Gain shows that a finite whole whose rest gives nothing net stays bounded and recurs. Its configuration does not carry the count of its exchanges (`Flow.whole_recurs_of_rest_idle`, l. 312). Endless novelty of a family needs exchange beyond every finite family holding it (`Flow.new_beyond_every`, l. 341).
(5) FrontierWorlds and Frontier state a relay in general: a requirement is served across a handover with no gap (`served_of_handover`). This is a case where the going on continues while the provider changes. Neither module states that the giver can be lost or what its giving costs it.
