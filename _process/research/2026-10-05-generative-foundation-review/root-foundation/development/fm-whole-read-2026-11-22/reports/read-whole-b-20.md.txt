Things the fourteen questions miss that bear on the centre:
(1) Grammar.lean pairs «生生», the greatest family that keeps renewing (l. 259, «生生最大»), with «息», the least property closed under renewing into (l. 654). It proves that they exclude each other («生生不息», l. 660). It does not prove that they exhaust the cases, and «四相之定当且仅当排中» (l. 191) shows that deciding positions in general is excluded middle. So a dichotomy of continuing and ending in this calculus has to be decided per model.
(2) «以核为判据则火无续» (Grammar l. 791) and Decay.history_apart (GrammarCases l. 152) say that under a class whose line ends, the core tells moments apart by how long is left. In GrammarCourse, Law.continues_in_core_iff (l. 217) makes this exact for every determined law: a step continues under the core iff the line under the value class never ends. A running-down is therefore visible to the core and not to the class.
(3) In GrammarConditionals, «不返而有限则非不穷» (l. 287) is a general law: in finite room, a line that never returns is not endless. «线不返则短于所列» (l. 264) is its bounded form. Both are about room counted by a list, not about a stock.
(4) GrammarCases.SharedCondition (l. 317–402) has lines that draw on a shared condition, but its docstring says no amounts are modelled. The contrast between affecting and fed is a reach law, not a running-down.
(5) Gluing.lean is a complete parity obstruction (global_or_contradicts, glues_iff) with a Bell-type bound: m−1 of m contexts met without context, m with context. It bears on readings, where the context carries what no context-free reading holds, and not on levels or moves.
