Points the fourteen questions miss that bear on the centre:
1. Lexical `BasicPriority.same_claims_alike` (l. 367) is a general law close to the centre's first premise. Under `BasicPriority`, two alternatives realizing the same claims are compared alike in every respect: what the claims do not read, the comparison cannot weigh. `Above.agent_adjoined` (l. 384) shows that adjoining a claim restores the order. It is stated for a comparison, not for a selection that runs over steps.
2. Lexical `Above.no_trade` (l. 157): no number of gains trades for a lost basic claim. This is a priority law, not a law of running down.
3. Lifting `lift_continual_improvement_has_no_survivor` (l. 79): where every live response is liftedly extended by a live one, no response survives. It is a general statement that continual improvement leaves no undominated selection.

Likeness `Unbounded.sameBranchingTests` (l. 2548) and `Unbounded.not_alike` (l. 2575), built: a configuration whose one move leads to chains of every finite length, all of which end, passes every branching test that one with an added endless chain passes, yet the two are not alike. Finite tests cannot register, from the start, whether some course goes on without end. `alike_of_sameBranchingTests_outcomes_iff` (l. 2586) states that with only listed outcomes, tests fix likeness exactly under LLPO for decided families. This bears on what a reading can decide about going on versus ending.
5. Likeness `Dependence.dichotomy_of_same_level` (l. 2626) is the one keep-or-lose dichotomy in the batch. It is about one move's breaking of a dependence, with the level after the move as its only extra premise.
