Points the fourteen questions miss that bear on the centre:
(1) Listing.lean gives a general rule for how the dissipation laws reach any family. A law whose conclusion is a refutation holds of a list with repetitions, with no premise (not_of_listsOnce, l. 176). So the non-recurrence and opening laws of Dissipation, Continuation, Roles, Resolution and Standpoints hold however a family is listed. ListingRefutations.lean is the 24 corollaries that follow. A sum law, by contrast, needs the sum taken once per member (Doubled.needs_once, l. 584).
(2) Listing.lean, World.numbers_indiscernible_iff_em (l. 794): when relata are counted through their relations, the count is fixed for every world exactly under excluded middle. Names give one more decided identity than the relations do (probe_named_apart).
(3) ListingValuation.lean is the module nearest the alignment questions, but it is about one decision and states no running down. Judged by the reading formation's own current valuation, a revaluation never raises the worth of holding (own_least, l. 996). It lowers that worth exactly where it decides some listed cell against the current strict weighted preference (revalued_lowers_iff, l. 1008). Some replacement valuation gains, by a reference, exactly where the current answer has a positive residual (revaluing_gains_iff, l. 1050). The account says plainly that it does not show reachability under the supplied update, or any choice by the holding formation. TwoWorlds.not_lumped (l. 2402) shows that the record of answers and the end state do not determine whether the valuation was changed. This bears on "shaping how they select does not become what they select for", but only as a built case with two starts.
(4) The second account inside Listing.lean (CensusWebs, l. 1020) names `listed_over_stretch` and `counts_total`. Neither is declared in that section, which holds only keep_pace, stock_eq, stock_le and indiscernible_numbers_unique. I did not look for those names elsewhere.
