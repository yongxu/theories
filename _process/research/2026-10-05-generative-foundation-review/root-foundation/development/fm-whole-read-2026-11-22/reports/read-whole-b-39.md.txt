Three things the fourteen questions do not quite catch, each bearing on the centre:
(1) Locality.recurs_iff_unmet (l. 137) is a general falling-amount law, stated for any amount r : Nat → Nat and any event E and condition Q. Under Q the event lowers r and r never rises; every rise of r is an occurrence of E. Then the event recurs exactly when it recurs at steps outside Q. change_recurs_iff and exchange_recurs_iff are cases of it. It is a general shape for "a selection cannot go on under a draining condition unless it goes on outside it". Locality's coming_of_ceasing_covered (l. 603) and Covers.ceasing_not_recurring (l. 698) say that ending which keeps recurring needs arising which keeps recurring.
(2) LoopReturn.closing_merges and closing_not_lossless (l. 333, 339) take f 0 = 0 and a closed positive difference. They conclude that the return map merges two differences, and so is not lossless. The account, citing Law.Merges.loses, says that within finitely many values the merge loses a possibility for good. This joins closing a difference to an irreversibility. LoopReturn.gap_course (l. 984) makes the gap between two balanced flows at one condition the orbit of a return map, under the premise RespondsByReturn. The account says which return law a loop follows is not derived.
(3) Loops' runaway (carried_runaway, polynomial_iff_no_loop, gap_polynomial_iff_no_loop) is stated of the most difference that can arrive, and of an upper bound over every law with the given strengths. It is not stated of what each law does. The strengths are supplied. The account says loops form and dissolve by Becoming.Closing and Becoming.Remainder, but no statement here derives that.
