Things the fourteen questions miss that bear on the centre:

(1) ModelStatements.optimum_broken_reading (l. 1270) is the one general statement in the batch where a selection by an order on a family ds uses up what lies beyond it. Its premises: an order containing exceeding on ds, room beyond, the levels after the step unbeaten among the eligible, and each condition beyond required and met. Under them, a dependence beyond is broken exactly where some eligible outcome stands below its need. It is a reading of a row proved elsewhere (Web.optimum_broken_iff).

(2) Novelty.changes_le_stock and rests_of_loses (l. 249, l. 284) are a general law of running down by a count. If a part loses stock at each change of its configuration, its changes number at most its stock at T, and change stops. Novelty.unbounded_of_new (l. 186) is the converse side: going on with something new at every step needs stock without bound, from beyond the part.

(3) MutualDependence.Balances.half_stands and doubling_needs (l. 417, l. 431) state that a draw exceeding everything drawn before leaves more than half the stock standing at every step, while lasting at most about log2 of the stock. The account (l. 73-76) reads this as no warning, as a reading, not a statement. Web.break_not_bounded is named there but not declared in the module.

(4) MoveOutcomeProcesses.summary_hides_loss (l. 279, built) and Narrowing.narrow_class_settles_wrongly (l. 566, built) are cases where what is registered decides wrongly or hides a loss. Neither is a general law.

(5) MoveFormation.TwoClocks.one_course_two_laws (l. 720, built) shows that one course does not fix whose move it is. no_policy_upto_iff (l. 622, general) says a record excludes a policy exactly where a configuration recurs with the move varied.
