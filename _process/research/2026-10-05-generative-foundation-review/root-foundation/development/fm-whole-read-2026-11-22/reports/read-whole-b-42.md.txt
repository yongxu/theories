I read the one module of this batch, Observation.lean (566 lines), whole: its opening account, every declaration's statement and docstring, and some proof bodies, those of apart_only_downstream and Measure.couples_after in part. I opened Loops.lean, Remainder.lean, Propagation.lean, Closing.lean, Counterfactual.lean and Flow.lean only to find where the names the statements use (Upstream, CouplesAt, Conveyed, carried, strengthsBefore, ExchangeLaw, Follows, Balances) are defined; I did not read them further. No question was left unread.

Most of the fourteen questions find nothing here, by the module's own scope: no moves or policy, no share, no cost, no multiplying, no rank, and no level whose return depends on what is left. The formations present are a fixed premise in every statement.

Two things bear on the centre that the questions do not ask about:
1. The module's main law is a noninterference law. It is general over every exchange law that reads into each condition a schedule: what is not downstream of a stock by the routes of reading formed by a step stands alike in two courses that start apart only at that stock. The account says plainly that the converse is not stated, that a downstream condition does make a difference.
2. Reading is not taken as given. It forms in time: a route of reading forms at a step only through a coupling acting at that step (upstream_succ_iff), and once formed it stays formed (Upstream.before_mono). In the built measurement, the loop stock to record to draw forms at T, and the raise of the stock passes through the record with no threshold of the stock involved.

So "what is registered decides" is stated generally here, as one direction: what is not registered by a formed route makes no difference. How the registering comes to form, or fails to form in time, is stated generally only as the closing of a route; which schedule a law reads, and when an instrument starts, are premises, as the account itself says.
