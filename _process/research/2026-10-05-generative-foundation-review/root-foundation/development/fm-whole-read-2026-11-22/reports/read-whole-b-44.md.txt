Both modules were read whole, all statements and docstrings: OpenedCorrection.lean (203 lines, proofs read as well) and Openness.lean (1261 lines, read in three parts to its end). To read OpenedCorrection's built case I opened Revision.lean's `Revising.P` and `read`. No question was left U. Some answers depend on names defined in other modules that I did not open: `RestsFrom`, `World.Premised`, `Flow.RunsDown`, `ClosedAt`, `Amounts.rests_unrefuted`, and the worlds hatch, mutualism, leaking and throughput. Two things bear on the centre that the fourteen questions do not quite catch. First, in OpenedCorrection the starved configuration (link on, no stock) is not a point of no return: replenishing reopens the correction. Rebuilding the reading can use up the last unit the correction needs (`Linked.rebuilt_starved`). So the module has a revision of the reading that draws on the same stock its use needs, but nothing about running down without return. Second, Openness's general laws all point toward rest: under enclosure, balance and dissipation, the total standing is a falling natural-number quantity, and change, exchange and arising stop, though only as not refuted (¬¬). Change going on without end appears only in built worlds that drop a premise (exposed, procession, slow, throughput). There are general one-way laws, `arising_opens` and `CountedWithin.arising_iff_ceasing`, but no statement says, for one model, exactly when it goes on and when it ends. Total standing falls, but no formation pays for its own giving.
