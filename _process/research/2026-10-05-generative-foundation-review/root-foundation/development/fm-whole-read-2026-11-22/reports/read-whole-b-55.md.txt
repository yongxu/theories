Three things bear on the centre that the fourteen questions do not quite catch.

1. ReadOffInTime states a general point of no return on courses. `NoReturnAt` is relative to a supplied set of feasible courses and a supplied `keeps`, so the point is a premise-relative definition, not derived from a step law. It also has `Web.too_late_of_own`: a group that reads only the admissions that are themselves what is kept can never read the loss in time. That is a general law of "running down unseen". Its account states that the direction in which a reading turns a course is not stated (it cites `Clearing.read_not_direction`, which I did not open).

2. ReaderRebuilding is a built family. In its member that is rebuilt from the level, the going-on that keeps the level (the reader) is itself lost, and its loss is absorbing (`«取于储者绝而不复»`). The account says it is not proved that this member ends under a cut from every stock, nor that its horizon is monotone in the stock.

3. Reading's `ExchangeLaw.requirement_beyond_course` states that two laws giving the same course can differ in requirement: what is registered in a course does not fix what the course rests on. This bears on the gap between what a going-on rests on and what makes a difference to it.

ReadStages is a static comparison of four two-stage plans, plus chance draws. It has no step dynamics, no level and no point of no return.
