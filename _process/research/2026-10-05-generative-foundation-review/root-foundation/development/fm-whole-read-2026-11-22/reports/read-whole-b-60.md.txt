Things the fourteen questions miss that bear on the centre:
(1) RenewalFlow gives a general family law in which the giver is itself a formation that can be lost. The sustaining formation gives renewal only while the level meets its need (renewal l.71, renewal_supported l.130, givers_supported l.149). Overdraw beyond renewal ends that formation for good (overdraw_ends l.225). absorbs (l.366) is an exact point of no return with no premise on later inputs: once the level is below both needs, it stays put whatever is later drawn or contributed. This is the closest thing in the batch to 'selection uses up what it rests on'. But it is a fixed two-formation, one-condition family with its inputs handed in. Nothing in it registers or selects, and no share is set again from what is favoured.
(2) Relevance's possibility reading lets which reading is used decide the ranking. Under the possibility reading, a formation drawing within renewal ties with its absence. Under the two `living` readings, its presence ranks above its absence (possible_tie, readings_rank_presence, sharing_presence). possibleFirst counts a rest as a loss (Pause.pause_ranked_below). This is a statement of what a comparison registers deciding what it favours, but courses are fixed alternatives, not moves.
(3) Remainder's cut is a general, exact statement of what a truncated account misses and where: carried_eq_carriedWithin_add, carriedWithin_eq_iff, exact_every_start_iff. A finite order is exact exactly where no loop of the couplings that go on lies upstream, once the schedule has settled (finite_order_iff_no_loop_from). This holds for schedules in which couplings come and go. It is about propagating differences, not about a level that runs down or a selection, but it bears on 'an account taken for the whole'.
(4) RenewalBound's alternation is a built case in which renewal recurs arbitrarily late while every score of configurations stays bounded. Recurrence there does not mean that more goes on after.
