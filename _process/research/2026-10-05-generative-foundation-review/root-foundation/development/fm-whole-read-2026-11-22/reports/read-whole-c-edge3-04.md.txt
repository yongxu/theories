How the letters are used: each field begins with one letter, the strongest thing found for that field anywhere in the module, and the text then says whether it stands in the both-outcomes statement or in another one. So an S in moves_in_it or reading_in_it does NOT by itself mean it is inside an edge statement; read the sentence after the letter.

The claim under test stands in these four. None has, in one statement giving both outcomes with an exact condition, a course with a choice, a reading and a bound or falling quantity. In none is `edge` S.

- PoolFromCourse: edge B. Both outcomes only as built courses with no exact condition between them. In `Drained` the two outcomes (`strong_undone` l.660, `weak_turned` l.685) are BOTH locks kept for good, in opposite favour, each by `window_locked_after_removal`: a closed region on each side and a rank on neither. This is not the pattern the brief describes (region one side, rank the other). The nearest general statement, `window_locked_after_removal` (l.342), has in one statement every schedule of reading steps (`reads : Nat → Bool`, a section variable), a reading (`windowRead`, `Stands K`), a quantity rising past every bound (the loss, under `Accrues`) and a closed region, but it gives ONE outcome (the lock kept) from two premises at a step, not both outcomes with an exact condition. The module's only general iffs (`borne_by_shapers_favours_iff` l.524, `borne_by_others_favours_iff` l.530) are of one rule's favouring (`c + l ≤ s`, `c ≤ s + l`), not of a course.
- ReadingsSpread: edge B. The `Commons` world has both outcomes with a standing population on one side (`keep_unmoved`, `keep_persists`) and a deadline on the other (`clearedBack_ends`: empty from step 6; `overdrawnBack_ends`: from step 3), as five separate theorems over two parameters fixed at the start (`back : Bool`, `σ : Reading`); no iff, no move within the course, readings assigned and inherited. The general statement with a choice and a reading, `standingCount_unselected` (l.455, two acts agreeing on what is coupled into the closure), concludes that the counts are the same in both courses: no two outcomes and no bound. `fixed_of_closedFrom` (l.150) and `revised_out` (l.158) are two directions of keeping a region of readings but not the halves of an iff (the leaver needs a kid); the iff is `kept_iff` in ReadingsPersist.
- Postponement: edge N. Its iffs equate a frame with its postponement (taking a course of moves, realizing a claim after it, passing the same linear tests, likeness). They have moves (`as : List A`) and what tests register, and no ending, bound, rank or falling quantity anywhere in the module.
- Relevance: edge N. `possible_extends` (l.356) and `possible_tie` (l.465) are two sufficient regimes on one `Law` whose premises are not each other's negation (strict overdraw from some step on; within renewal at every step) and whose start premises differ. The `Course` is the fixed pair `.occurs | .absent`; the conditions are on the law's numbers (`draw`, `renew`, `contrib`), not on what is registered; no bound stands in either statement.

At the statements: `turn_locked_at` (l.153) holds for both values of `b : Bool` (the step reads or holds), and every course law holds for every `reads : Nat → Bool`; this is a schedule given before the course, not depending on the configuration, and the module's account calls it "a fixed schedule of reading steps" (l.99).
