A reader meeting proved laws about generation and ending will ask whether they are laws of nature, and the answer should be stated rather than left to the word. **Scope:** texts only. ## In brief

*On laws of nature: in part.* 08 §10 keeps three senses of "law" apart, and Ren's question joins two of them.
- A *law of the theory* holds of every relation, carrier or model its hypotheses admit. A law of the root forbids no relation. A law with content forbids through its hypotheses, and the World's premises are each shown holding in one constructed world and failing in another.
- A *science's law*, such as thermodynamics' first and second laws, claims something of the actual world. It is stated in measured quantities, rules out observations, and stands on evidence.

The account's laws are laws of the theory. Its claims about the actual world are theses (T1–T8), conjectured and partly tested. Thermodynamics' laws enter it as premises in the periphery; the account does not derive them. The ethics rests on two unasserted premises: the continuation condition for scope and C for order. No statement reading only the dynamics entails the first, and no statement reading no order entails the second (07 §1, 08 §7). So neither is a law in either sense.

*On the full picture of evolution: yes for the form, no for the course.*
- **The form.** The aim of stating each mechanism of evolution on the root's models, as an exact law with its hypotheses and a counterexample where it fails, is well begun: Price, Fisher, Hamilton and drift hold in counting form. Some of what evolution names is still open: formation before inheritance, how parts first come to live as one, and statements on amounts.
- **The course.** Given the course of selection terms, some laws decide a family's fate: terms positive at unboundedly many steps force going on, and in Fisher's exact case the terms fix the region. They do not give the course itself. Counting "does not say which happens and supposes no chance of the unfolding" (04 §6), and the possible within a horizon, as the account reads it, is that of a prestated space (08 §8).
- **What closing the gaps needs.** New carriers are formal work (amounts on the root, a model of an organism). The tests need data.

## 2. A law of the theory and a science's law

08 §10 keeps three senses of "law" apart: a law of the theory; `Law`, the spine's name for a determined step; and a science's law, named with its science. The first and the third answer the question.

| Sense (08 §10) | What it holds of | What it forbids | In the account |
|---|---|---|---|
| A law of the theory, of the root | every carrier, relation and class | no relation | the root's laws (01; 02 §1) |
| A law of the theory, with content | every relation, carrier or model its hypotheses admit | through its hypotheses | most laws; the World's premises each hold in one constructed world and fail in another |
| A science's law (thermodynamics' first and second laws) | the actual world, in measured quantities, as a claim on evidence | observations: a machine of the second kind, heat flowing up unaided | the theses T1–T8, conjectured and partly tested; thermodynamics' laws as periphery premises |

**An analogy** [AI reading]. A law of the theory with content is a theorem under modelling premises. Statistical mechanics also states theorems under modelling premises, and argues from them to thermodynamics' laws. Two limits keep this an analogy. First, for thermodynamics the account proves no theorem from which its laws follow; it takes them in as premises. Second, thermodynamics' laws stand on evidence as impossibility statements, whatever their reduction, which is contested. For a law of the theory to become a science's law for some domain, the domain must be shown to meet its hypotheses. That runs through a stated correspondence reading the model's terms (discrete steps, natural-number amounts, Prop-valued activity) in measured quantities. 08 §12's open programme names that work. Thermodynamics' quantities (energy, entropy, temperature) lie at the root's edge (item 2).

So the reading is: **the account states laws of the theory. Those with content would be a science's laws for a domain once the domain is shown, through a correspondence, to meet their hypotheses. It does not yet state a science's law of its own.**

**OPENING's test.** OPENING denies that the struggle for power, competition or the sovereign self is a law: "each fails in a world the laws admit". That is the test of a law of the theory. The spine's balance and dissipation, which 08 §12 relates to thermodynamics' first and second laws, each fail in a constructed world (`World.independent`: `unbalanced`, `lossless`), so the same test would not admit them either. So the test shows that a feature is not a law of the theory. It does not show that it is not a science's law; that needs evidence about actual systems. OPENING's note says as much: "The readings are this note's and test none of those theories against its evidence." For OPENING's owner: the body's "a law of nature" and "None of them is a law" join two of 08 §10's senses. Naming the sense would close the gap.

## 3. The picture of evolution

**What is begun** [AI reading]. The aim is every mechanism of evolution stated on the root's models as an exact law, each with its hypotheses, a counterexample where it fails, and a map of which hypotheses hold where. In counting form, Price, Fisher, Hamilton and drift are there, with mutation and its balance (item 7). EL states where selection terms decide a family's fate (item 8). Still open:
- reconstruction and development as laws;
- formation before inheritance, and how parts first come to live as one (item 7);
- why a descendant comes to depend on what its giver drew on;
- the evolution rows on amounts (item 2).

What this would give is one place where the mechanisms stand together, joined to balance, renewal and thresholds. Each joint prohibition found so far is known in some neighbouring field, and the line adds no new population-genetic prediction (item 5). So the gain is the joining, and a statement of exactly what each mechanism needs, rather than prohibitions no field has.

**What is not given, for three reasons.**

1. **The course is not given by the laws.** Given the course of selection terms, some laws decide a family's fate: terms positive at unboundedly many steps force going on, and in Fisher's exact case the terms fix the region (item 8). Which terms occur, which variants arise and in which conditions, is not given. Counting does not say which history happens, and it supposes no chance of the unfolding, so it makes no probabilistic prediction either (item 7). The possible within a horizon, as the account reads it, is that of a prestated space, not the one that cannot be prestated (item 10). Explanation can still be complete in form: given the history, it can say why the course went as it did and what was ruled out.
2. **Completeness would be a premise about couplings.** By 03 §1 a selection, or a group's pooled selection, separates exactly what its couplings separate (item 9). A complete picture of the course needs couplings that separate every relevant history. The account gives no reason to expect a formation within the web to meet that premise, and item 10 points the other way. A picture of the forms does not need it. So "we", read as a group's pooled coupling, can hold the forms without holding every history.
3. **Theorems on the present carriers are not the bottleneck.** New carriers are formal work: amounts on the root (U1b's interface) and a model of an organism. The theses' tests need data and runs (items 2, 11).

**The aim restated.** Not "the full picture of evolution", but *one account in which each mechanism of evolution is stated with its exact hypotheses and counterexamples, joined to the others on one root, saying what a given history allows and forbids, and saying what the account cannot predict and why.*

## 4. Where Ren's words seem wrong

- **"Our work is also laws of nature, as thermodynamics."** In 08 §10's senses, the account's laws are laws of the theory, and its claims about the actual world are theses (§2). What it has in common with thermodynamics is theorems under modelling premises (the analogy in §2), not a science's law's standing. For reach, 08 §8 says the account is level with thermodynamics, or states less, and it is level only because it takes thermodynamics' laws in.
- **"The full picture of evolution ... if we push far enough."** The form can be stated, with the open items of §3. The course is not given by the laws. Pushing further on the present carriers does not reach the gaps, which need new carriers and data.
- **"And how it can be understood."** This part holds up best. Stating exactly what each mechanism needs and where it fails, on one root, is a way of understanding evolution that no single field's statement gives.
