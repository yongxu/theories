## The owner's reading of the proposal

The proposal is taken with two amendments, and one placement.

1. **"For life" says whose way it is, not whose losses count.** The scope stays the condition's: every continuation's losses count, including losses that return to no one. Read otherwise, the stance form would narrow the scope to life's own losses. That would change the ethics, which LW does not propose.
2. **The standing names what is open exactly.** The NOTE's "P4 and the reason for C are open" leaves out two things. First, §4.6 names P2 and P4 together as the crux: P4 is the first step, and P2 is one regularity that carries it on. Second, the step of invariance stays open for what §4.6's path does not reach: others counted beyond their part as a condition, present beings beyond every closure, and whatever no chain joins. T8 still says "counted for everything", so the step is still needed for that remainder. It is given up only as a demand on every mind.
3. **The stance rests on P1, not on a want.** Ren's message has "if we want life to continue". That is provenance, and it is a weaker ground than the one the chapter has. Acting presupposes, held de se, that one's own continuation has significance (§4.6's P1, §4.1). A wish that life go on gives only a guard: keeping off the courses that end on every branch (05 §10, a plan keeping a guard). It does not give that the wider course is better, and both readings in the source record agree.
4. **Placement.** The note's §3.2 retitles 07's open item, "A universal practical warrant", as a question, and §3.4 restates the goal. These go with part (b). So does 08's mapping sentence, "a stronger practical warrant is item 12", and its bullet "A stronger universal practical warrant". While the goal stands, the target it authorizes stands. Part (a) says only what holds either way:
   - no warrant binding every possible mind is claimed;
   - none comes from the dynamics or from logic;
   - the ethics does not wait on one;
   - what still needs argument for life is named.
