## 2. Better structures for each of Ren's four uses

**What is native to the account.** [A] The account's objects are relations, closures, classes and formations; a dynamic is a region of courses that holds under named premises and is left where a premise fails under a stated condition, with a strength. That is a *premise-indexed exit graph*: nodes are dynamics (and the released states), edges are exits labelled by the premise dropped and the law that shows it, typed by strength and base. The Lean already has this shape (hypotheses are named structures: `Unread`, `Accrues`, `ReadsShare`); the data layer flattened it into prose. Premises, not dynamics, are what an aligning process can check against its own case, so premises must be first-class.

**Storing.** Keep JSON in git (the gate, the receipts and the "generated, never hand-edited" rule all depend on it; a database would move the truth out of the receipts). Add three entry kinds the plan named and never built, and structure `hypotheses`:

```jsonc
// entries/premises.json (new; names from Lean, prose curated)
{"id":"unread","lean":["Becoming.ClosedCapture.Unread"],"en":"neither the next share nor the rule read reads what lies beyond",
 "zh":"下一份额与所读规则皆不读份额之外者","kind":"on how a rule reads","met_in":["Becoming.ClosedCapture.Beyond.closedRead"],
 "fails_in":["Becoming.ClosedCapture.Beyond.lossRead"],"world_evidence":[]}

// dynamics.json: closed-capture, re-expressed (abridged)
{"id":"closed-capture","family":"C","base":"any type",
 "about":{"objects":["share","rule","reading of the rule"],"relation":"selection read from the share","closure":"the share's own"},
 "premises":["unread","accrues","reads-share:rule","reads-share:mind","raises","favouring-kept-upward","locked-start"],
 "records":["closed-capture","rules-that-change-themselves-in-the-grammar"],
 "exits":[{"drops":"unread","via":"Becoming.ClosedCapture.turned_by_loss","to":"capture-through-the-rule","strength":"sufficient",
           "condition":"a reading favouring at no loss above a bound, after a reading step past it"}],
 "composes":[{"with":"readers-drawn-from-the-selected","as":"the reading as a pool of readers"},
             {"with":"loss-read-in-time-by-the-others","as":"the exit under a deadline"}],
 "level_of":"capture-through-the-rule",            // the same loop one level up (ZB's reading, typed)
 "forbids":[{"text":"such a metric dropped by its selectors on evidence alone","test":"LA2"}],
 "touches":[{"kind":"test","id":"LA2","outcome":"mixed","frozen":"…"}, {"kind":"proof","receipt":"…"}]}
```

What becomes possible: "what holds if actual rules do not read the share" is a query, not a reading of 177 mechanism paragraphs. Cost: premise names can be extracted from `lean-index.json` for the 2,101 declarations the atlas already names (a day of scripting); naming and curating ~190 premises in en/zh, with met/fails witnesses, is the real work (the record `counterexamples` of 377 records and the K3 witnesses seed it). Keeping in step: the validator already diffs hypotheses against records; it would diff premise lists against the Lean binder names at each receipt. What it would get wrong: Lean names coincide across modules with different meanings (`ReadsShare` on a rule and on a mind are two premises); a lattice built from names alone would merge them. Make the premise id carry its module.

**Indexing.** Today's indexes are by family, by record id, by chapter. Add: by premise (above); by grammar concept (the 41 concepts are nodes; `shared-laws.json` already computes which central declarations reach which grammar law, so dynamics inherit concepts through their declarations at no curation cost); by base (so "any type" results, the universal-form ones, are one list); by what a dynamic forbids and what tested it; and plain full-text search over the en/zh pairs of the 5.7 MB `atlas.json` with SQLite FTS, a generic tool that is right here. The atlas is English: 6 of 177 dynamics have a `zh` name and no correspondence has Chinese, while every record has zh for every prose field. For a map of 共识 the index must be bilingual; the records show the cost is bearable.

**Visualizing.** The existing pictures draw chapters (spine), standings (squares), and folders×chapters (connections.svg, map.html). None draws a dynamic. Three pictures from the structure above, all generated:
1. *The exit graph*: 177 nodes coloured by family, sized by records, bordered by base; exits as edges labelled by the premise dropped, solid for sufficient/necessary/exact and dotted for the 151 witnesses (a witness shows a course *can* leave and gives no condition; drawn solid it would overstate). Needs the 263 null targets filled or named as released states.
2. *Premise × dynamic matrix*: which premise releases which locks; the column "reads what lies beyond the share" would show the C and D families' exits converging on one premise, which is GOALS' condition 1 made visible.
3. *Exact phase diagrams* for the 41 exact exits and 29 exact thresholds whose conditions are decidable integer inequalities (plan §8.2), the grid checked against Lean at sample points. What they hide: bases. An "any type" exit and a World exit drawn alike read as the same strength of claim; colour by base, every time.

**Understanding.** The unit a person understands is a *course through a case*: the lake, the judge, `Clearing`, `Diversion`, `Drawing`, `Forgoing`. The Lean has 220 modules with witnesses and the records 377 counterexamples, but no data kind is a case. A `cases.json` ({model, module, dynamics it instantiates, premises it meets and fails, test if any, one-page sentence}) lets a reader walk from the one page's fishers to the dynamic, to its premises, to the Lean witness and the test outcome. This is the structure that carries forming/persisting/ending as content rather than listing it: a case shows a course entering a region and leaving or not. Cost: moderate (the witnesses are already named in records' `counterexamples.detail`); risk: a case read as evidence about the world when it is a model, so each case carries `kind: model | run | field`.

Where generic tools are right: FTS for search, D3 force layout for 177 nodes, SVG from Python; a graph database is not needed at 3,000 nodes and would break the receipt-bound gate.

## 3. The map and the world

**What couples to something actual.** [M] Proofs: every recorded-checked record binds to a receipt by sha (`proof_bindings`), the strongest typed coupling in the repository. Tests: 66 rows, 36 folders; 5 passed, 4 failed, 20 mixed, 2 refused, 11 not run, 10 with no prediction; trained systems in LA2 (T7, mixed) and LA4 Stage A; field data in MQ3 (113 fish stocks, 0 of 4 passed) and the yeast regimes. Everything else is reading: the 1,183 correspondences (all AI; 328 from AI memory with no stored text), the chapters' readings (marked `[AI reading]`), the records' `correspondence` prose.

**How the structure can carry that relation.** [A] Two moves. First, every dynamic and record gets a `touches` list with kind ∈ {proof, model-witness, run, field-data, trained-system, reading}, object, outcome, frozen hash and date; `tests.json` rows gain `records` and `dynamics`. Today 152 of 177 dynamics have no test and 166 no `forbids`; a reader of ATLAS.md's family tables sees Exits and Open counts but not that. Add Tests and Forbids columns and colour the exit graph by touch state (proof-only grey; tested, by outcome; refused; reading-only). Second, the premises are the world interface: "that actual rules read the share is the premise" recurs in the records. The premise object's `world_evidence` is where a field observation, a trained judge's behaviour or a refused run attaches, and its emptiness is the honest default. The theory graph's row "classified, and read against nothing yet: 1,330" is the right model for showing where the map does not touch: say the count of what is untouched, in the view, not in a footnote.

**How it would show being wrong.** A failed test must appear on the dynamic it bears on, not only in TESTS.md: MQ3's failure belongs on `margin-of-a-regime`'s entry, LA2's mixed verdict and the T7 case that occurred (the judge turned by a loss read only by the disfavoured) on `closed-capture` and `readers-drawn-from-the-selected`. Keep the plan's rule that a failed frozen prediction removes no theorem and say which premise the failure bears on (`bears_on: "reads-share"`), so a reader sees whether the world failed the law or the bridge.

## 4. The map and alignment, "even universal"

**What "universal alignment" can mean here.** [A] Not a universal order: `ReadsNoOrder.not_entails` and the open record `universal-practical-warrant` say no order follows from the dynamics and no warrant binding every mind is shown. Not a rule installed in every selector: that would be the map becoming the captured rule. It can mean a universal *form*: the selection laws hold at base "any type" for every relation and class (36 dynamics at that base, 15 at "any process"), so the conditions under which *any* selection, human, institutional or trained, keeps a reader of what lies beyond its share and leaves a lock before the loss passes its point of no return are stated once for all of them. That is universal in scope of application, with content conditional on the premises being met and on a scope (the continuation condition) and an order (C) that are held, not asserted. Say it that way and it is within the map; say "aligned for every mind" and it is a warrant the map refuses.

**Structure for an aligning process to use the map within its own selection.** [A] Three affordances, each a data kind: (i) *check*: the premise checklist per dynamic, instantiable on the process's own case (does my rule read the share? who reads the loss, and before which deadline? what lies beyond my couplings?), which is why premises must be objects; (ii) *add*: a write path for a case, a run or a correspondence that lands through the same gate as records, so the map grows from use and not only from AI sessions; (iii) *see itself*: the map's own standing shown on every view, AGENTS.md's "patterns within the map". Against becoming a lock: the atlas is itself a selection whose readers are AI sessions drawn from what it selected. Its "beyond" is measurable: 312 uncited live records, 1,330 unread texts, 263 exits with no named target, the wide-sense meta-attractor with no record. Keep those counts on the front of the view; keep divergence (325) and contrary reading (25) as prominent as agreement; keep `fresh-reading` revisable. A map that shows its own loss beyond its couplings is, by its own law, the one that can be turned.

## 6. Where the question or the direction may be wrong or have holes

1. **The limit is coupling, not representation.** [M, A] Ren asks for better data structures; the measured shortfall is that 25 of 177 dynamics touch a test, 11 say what they forbid, and 328 correspondences come from AI memory with no text. A better structure will not add contact with the world; its best service is to show the absence plainly. If the hold on runs lasts, the map's world edge stays where it is whatever is built.
2. **"Universal alignment" risks the dualism AGENTS.md left behind.** [A] If it means an alignment every mind must accept, the account's own theorem says no such order follows, and the open record says no warrant is shown. The word is safe only as universal form (§4).
3. **The direction's central object is the least mapped.** [M, R] Ren's alignment problem names the meta-attractor in the corpus's wide sense (reproducing across carriers). The map's typed content is the glossary's narrow sense (closed capture); the wide sense is one open entry with no law, and the atlas plan placed WM behind CW and MC. The map for alignment is strongest where the alignment problem is narrowest.
4. **The atlas re-lists records.** [M] 107 of 177 dynamics have exactly one record; the atlas adds exits and transition kinds, but with 22 targeted exits it is a typed catalogue, not a map of a landscape. "177 dynamics" overstates what has been related.
5. **The typed dependence is AI-assigned and unchecked.** [R] THEORY-GRAPH.md says nothing checks a `formal-reuse` edge against the Lean; the spine's solid curves are 1,189 such edges. A picture generated from them reads as more than it is.
6. **The "ledger of premises" is not a ledger.** [M] The one page says what survives the known-results review is "a ledger of premises, each shown holding in one model and failing in another"; in data that ledger is 17 items on 17 coverage rows, and every other premise is a paragraph.
7. **The map of wisdom is English in its data.** [M] Six Chinese names among 177 dynamics; no Chinese in 1,183 correspondences. 道可道 is on the front; the atlas cannot be read in the language of the texts it reads.
