## Why order is routed through the grammar and balance's arithmetic is not (F13)

DESIGN item 3 forbids routing a proof through the grammar only to raise the measure. Order and balance fall on opposite sides of that rule, for a reason that holds of the laws themselves:
- **Order.** The grammar's inclusion, exceeding and widening (`«则»`, `«逾»`, `«广»`) are the general laws, and Web's and Weighting's comparisons are their instances. What a path or course admits is read as a coreflexive relation, the standard representation of a set by a partial identity (`Web.admitted`, `Weighting.admittedOn`). Proving `Includes.trans` from `«则之传递»` therefore proves the special law from the general one, as rule 9 asks.
- **Balance.** The grammar's form of balance is a cardinality encoding of natural numbers by unit lists. Under the one, a pairing is exactly equal count (`«一之对»`). A consumer of `Flow.Balances` that needs only the count would gain a detour through lists and no generality.

The grammar's balance laws (`«对之接»`, `«对之族»`, `«对之程»`) are general where the relation is not the one. They say how pairings under a relation compose, gather over a family and carry over a stretch under its closure. No model's balance is yet read under a relation other than the one. That is where a derivation of balance, rather than a representation, would begin.
