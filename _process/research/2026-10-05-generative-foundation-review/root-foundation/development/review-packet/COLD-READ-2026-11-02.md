## Where the readers ended

The readers' two-sentence answers, in full in their files:

> It might not add much if the common vocabulary mainly repackages known results and the connections to actual systems and ethical concern remain unestablished. (GPT)

> It might not matter because, by its own account, it has no new mathematics, no human review and no shown fit to real systems, several of its predictions failed, and its ethics rests on premises it declines to assert and goes silent on mixed cases, so what it adds may be only a tidy vocabulary. (Claude)

Their case for it is the same on both sides: one vocabulary in which a selection can be seen destroying what it cannot register, with the premises named that an ethics must add. The Claude reader's word is an audit, "one organised audit, not one deduction". The GPT reader's is "a connected research programme".

**This reading is correct.** It is the account's own verdict ([08 §8](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/08-METHOD.md#known-results)). Better text can make a reader see what this is; it cannot make the reader see more than this. The same section names what would change the verdict: "A theorem a specialist would not foresee, a prediction that could fail and does not, or a premise derived from something weaker". The nearest of these in hand is a frozen test of how a trained system drains as it nears its reward's optimum. [T3's home](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/05-COLLECTIVES.md#t3) says "No test yet varies how near a trained system comes to its reward's optimum"; LA4 is designed for that and has not run.

Both readers also asked for the same next thing, one argument carried end to end. The GPT reader wants "one complete chain from assumptions through theorem to a trained-system test". The Claude reader wants the optimum law with T3's test and its refuter, and one real mixed case worked through. Both would gather the fishery, now split across §§2, 3, 5 and 7 of the overview, into one early passage.

## What failed

### 1. What would show it wrong

The one page says "Eight theses about actual systems name what refutes them" and names none. The overview's table has Claim, Standing and Home. So neither reader could say, even after an hour. The GPT reader: "The text does not say what would refute the account as a whole." The Claude reader's T3 refuter is marked as its own inversion of the thesis.

Three further points came from the same question:

- **Refuters differ by kind of claim, and no front says so.** The GPT reader offered a counterexample to a root theorem as a way of showing the account wrong. Nothing on a front says that the root's laws hold of every relation and so forbid nothing; only [08 §10](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/08-METHOD.md#law) does. UE's owner, reading the same fronts separately and not cold, reached the same result and listed the kinds with their homes (see the work list).
- **What a failure costs is unsaid.** Both readers asked what a failed test changed. The answer exists: 08 §12 says "A frozen test failing where a thesis forbids would move the thesis", and T3 shows it, its first wording failing where the learner reads the condition. No front says so.
- **The material is ready.** 08 §9 has a Forbids column for T1 to T8.

### 2. The whole

Both readers retold the line correctly, and their outlines agree with each other and with the account:

- **The GPT reader's three questions:** what makes something continue; what sustains continuation and what can detect its loss; which continuations should matter and what should be done.
- **The Claude reader's picture:** root, then models (the draw on a renewal, what a selection reaches, life, the order of widening), then a gap by construction, then the two premises, then the direction, with the theses, Lean and the classical texts beside the line.

Both say "partly", for the same reasons, which the Claude reader names as three joints: root to models ("not yet restated on the grammar"), models to the world ("not shown"), dynamics to ethics (premises, with significance "not lifted onto the root").

**Against the account, "partly" is mostly a right reading.** The joints are real and the text is right to state them. The defect is order: the joints appear in §10, so the reader builds the picture alone and meets the gaps last. A map that shows the three questions and the three joints at the start would give a reader what these two had to build.

Two things neither outline shows:

- **How actor, knower, evaluator, capacity and responsibility differentiate within the relation.** Each section says its part; no front shows them as one differentiation.
- **The lock.** Both readers would move the meta-attractor paragraph out of the way, and both lost its sentence on readers drawn from the selected. Yet T7 and the line on the joint selection rest on the lock, and the line of the argument leaves it out. It should either be one plain sentence in the line, or be plainly a branch.

## The work list

**For the fronts, no new claim** (the one page and overview are their owner's to read before any landing):

1. **What would refute it.** A Forbids column in overview §9, copied from 08 §9. A short table of what refutes each kind of claim: a theorem fails only by a failed check or a statement that does not say what the prose says; a law of the root forbids no relation; a law with content reaches a domain through a stated correspondence, and that is what an observation can refute; a thesis by its Forbids; the premises by no observation. One example on the one page, and the sentence that the root's generality is why it forbids nothing.
2. **What a failure did.** One sentence with T3 as its example.
3. **The fixes in tables 3 and 4.** The one page is over its ten-minute budget (1,176 words against 1,100), so what it gains has to come from these cuts.
4. **The map** (item 3), built from the three questions and the three joints, each part with its standing and what would refute it.
5. **The fishery gathered once**, stock, boats, seabirds and the premises each step takes, carrying readers through the map. 07 §5's table on restraint and overdraw already has a comparison C ranks under one reading and not under another, a start on the case the Claude reader asks for.
6. **One path.** A line from the overview to the pages that hang off it (the free-energy principle, a collective's course, the dossiers). UE's owner found that none of them is reached from README, the one page or the overview.

**For item 4, the crux.** Both readers stopped at the same join, and neither saw an argument yet.

- The Claude reader: "'Has significance' has become a connectivity fact, so this looks like a different proposition under the same name", and the path "reads to me as redefining the self, not as an argument".
- The GPT reader: the two treatments' "present proximity makes it easy to mistake a change of interpretation for a derivation", and "[I] do not yet see why the absence of a dynamically fixed boundary supports extending significance across boundaries".
- Both named 07 §4.6 and the step as what they would read next, so this is where the fronts have to carry the argument, or say plainly that it is not yet one.

**For the account itself: not wording, and each needs arguing.**

- **The central explanatory claim has no named defeater.** That reading a subject as continuation relative to a class explains how it goes on is "argued", and nothing says what would defeat it (UE's owner; 08 §1's item 5 asks for this of every claim). This is the gap behind the GPT reader's "what would refute the account as a whole". Placed with UE's owner, who sends the fronts' wording.
- **The philosophical thesis's stated refuter may be unmeetable** (the Claude reader). "Two configurations that no test or relation tells apart and that go on differently": within the model this cannot occur, since configurations that are alike pass the same tests (`Alike.tests`, 01). Outside it, any difference in how they go on looks like a test that tells them apart, the same reasoning 08 §9 uses to dismiss an intervention. So the thesis forbids explanations, not observations, and should say that. U4's owner checked this against the Lean and confirms the conclusion, correcting two steps. "Within the model this cannot occur" holds only for what alike configurations can do (`Alike.tests` is existential): on a run, where the law does not fix the outcome up to likeness, alike configurations do go different ways (`DeterminedAt.goAlike` needs that premise; `Decay.time_not_in_core`, `Decay.both_open`). And "any difference is a test" holds for a difference that recurs (01 §4). Placed with U4's owner, whose home text in 08 §9 comes first.
- **How evaluation forms is defined and read, not derived.** The fronts open with how evaluation takes shape; 07 §1 gives a center's region, a supplied parameter and a reading, and §10 says significance's central laws are not derived from the root. Either the fronts say that much or the work shows more. SJ's owner, asked to decide, says neither "the fronts promise it whole" nor "it is only defined": some of it is shown under stated conditions, significance is read or supplied, and no statement reading only the represented dynamics fixes it. Its sentence goes to the fronts.

**Placed for the fronts.** C's exact role ("in general alone") and "from every standpoint", on the first screen, in 07 §4, the glossary and the test that pins it: LN. The one argument carried end to end: AJ's fork, on a page of the path. README's first screen, which is generated: MV.
