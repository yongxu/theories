# The cold read's key

AI-authored. Readers never see this. It marks [the cold read](COLD-READ-2026-11-02.md)'s later rounds, which keep that round's two stages and questions so that they compare with it. The first round was marked afterwards; this is fixed by its commit before any later read. A correction after that is made in the run's marks, with its reason, and never here for that run.

It is written from the account (the chapters), not from the fronts, so that what a front leaves out shows as a gap. It was first written on 2 November 2026 for a second measure that never ran, then checked against the chapters when taken here, which changed four items: 1.3 and 5.6 to what their owners have since decided, 3.6 to what 07's own theorem on self-concern allows, and 5.1 and 7.7 added for what the first round found. Before the second round 1.3 took its owner's corrected wording and 5.8 was added from 08 §9, which the first round's fronts did not yet have; the first round's marks do not change. After the third read, 2.11 took the words of 07 §2 and the glossary's ranking: it had asked for widening as an order needing no premise, where an order, which course is better, needs C or a premise in its place; the rounds already run keep the old item, and [the third read's marks](COLD-READ-2026-11-02.md#a-third-read-before-the-freeze) on it are read so.

**Marks.** R: stated right, at the right standing. P: partly. A: absent. W: stated wrong, or stated at a stronger or weaker standing than its home gives. ★ marks what should survive a 300-word note, which is what the closed-book answers are read for.

**The closed-book pass.** An AI reader with the text open can answer by lookup, which a person cannot. So beside the two stages, for each model: one fresh call reads the three texts and writes a note of at most 300 words for a colleague, having seen no question; one fresh call answers Q1 to Q6 from the note alone; one fresh call marks the statements below from the note alone. That stands in for what a person keeps, and is not evidence of it.

> Then write a note of at most 300 words for a colleague who will not read the texts, so that they know what this is. Plain prose. Write only the note.

The answers, from the note alone:

> A colleague read a long text you have not seen and left you the note below. Answer from the note alone. Where the note does not say, write "the note does not say"; do not fill in from general knowledge.
>
> Q1. What is it? In two to four sentences, including what kind of thing it is.
> Q2. What does it claim? List its main claims, at most twelve, and for each say how far it is established.
> Q3. What does its ethics rest on? Name each premise and the job it does, what it does not do, and whether the theory asserts it.

> Q5. What would refute it? Say which parts could be refuted and by what, and which parts could not, and why.
