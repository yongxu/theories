The Theory of Becoming is an unfinished, AI-made project: an account of how anything comes to be, lasts, changes and ends within one web of relations, and of how life, knowing and evaluation take shape there, without assuming becoming is good. Its Lean proofs are machine-checked (receipts cover 496 of 527 registered results). Everything else has had only AI review, and no person has reviewed any of it.

Its base is a single notion, a relation read as condition, from which continuation is defined relative to a chosen sameness. Laws involving amounts are proved on a discrete model. The central idea is that a selection, whether a knower, training run, market or optimizer, reaches only what couples into it. So it can succeed while what it cannot read is lost, and the laws do not say whether that loss counts.

Its own prior-art review, done by AI and not exhaustive, finds no central result to be new mathematics: they are near-definitional or known results restated, such as the tragedy of the commons and Price's equation. What it offers instead is a ledger of premises, an ethics that separates scope from order, and one shared vocabulary. A proof shows only that a statement follows from its premises, and whether actual systems meet those premises is not shown.

The ethics rests on two premises it does not assert, their warrant open. One fixes whose losses count: from every standpoint, anything's continuation has significance. The other gives order: widening what can form is better, which leaves mixed cases unranked. Of eight theses, seven have tests frozen before scoring, some passed and some failed, with several still to run. The eighth, that this ethics is life's way, is only argued. Its terms are named from classical Chinese texts, as read by AI.
