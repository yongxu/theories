## R5. Does it agree with the shorter texts?
I found no flat contradiction. The differences are of strength, and in most of them the shorter text claims more.

1. **T3.** One page: "T3, on trained systems: conjectured, partly supported", with a defeater about optimality gaps. Overview: "No test yet varies how near a trained system comes to its optimum".
2. **T1.** One page: "T1: three settings passed, four failed". Overview adds that one setting's passes were "testing the measured quantities and not the thesis".
3. **The root.** One page: "most laws below add amounts and a model's premises". Overview: "most use none".
4. **Frozen tests.** First screen: "Empirical theses face tests frozen before scoring". Overview, T3 row: "ran before its first wording". The overview's own §9 also says "T1 to T7 have tests frozen before scoring".
5. **The condition's wording.** Shorter texts: "for anything, its continuation into the future". Overview: "the continuation of anything has significance at every step", while significance is defined "for a standpoint, a step and a formation". So "anything" may be narrower than it sounds.
6. **Authorship.** One page: "Made with AI, the Lean proofs included". The overview mentions AI review and AI classification but never says who made the theory or the proofs.

Here the overview claims more, or the shorter text is harsher on itself:

7. **Commons.** The overview's table says takers can "end it". The one page keeps "enough such steps in a row from a low enough level", as does §5 with "from a level below `n + m`".
8. **T7.** One page: "a further case it forbids occurred". Overview: "a case it forbids at that reading, though the frozen rule for it, which reads the end, did not support it".

Within the overview itself:

9. **Lock-in's standing.** "Each claim carries one standing", yet the lock is "Proved in the model" in the table and "Proved for any type" in §2.
10. **Order.** "An order with no premise" sits beside "Order: C". "A ranking of courses needs C or a premise in its place" sits beside "in general C alone orders".
