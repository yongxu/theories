The Theory of Becoming is an unfinished philosophical project, made with AI, that tries to describe persistence, change, life, knowledge and ethics within one relational unfolding. Its starting point is that something continues when it conditions something alike in a specified respect. Looking separately at a pattern and its parts distinguishes renewal that sustains the pattern from its eventual ending. Classical Chinese texts supply names and interpretive connections, not the mathematical operations.

Its central concern is that selection can succeed while destroying conditions its selector cannot register. Fisheries, evolution, markets and AI training provide applications or illustrations: individually sustainable demands can exhaust a shared resource, and a metric reinforced by its own results can persist despite losses it ignores. The project connects these familiar problems through relational language and explicit mathematical models.

Its proposed ethics makes two separate moves. The “continuation condition” says every thing’s continuation has significance from every standpoint; it determines whose losses count. Premise C says a course is better if it preserves every possibility of formation another admits and adds more. Neither premise follows from the dynamics, neither is asserted as established, and C leaves gains involving losses unresolved. A central philosophical question is whether everything affected by one’s actions belongs to one’s own continuation, potentially extending self-concern downstream.

The project’s distinctive offering is a ledger of assumptions, conditional results, interpretations and tests, rather than new mathematics. It reports Lean receipts for 496 of 527 registered results; these establish consequences of premises, not their truth or applicability. Empirical tests have mixed results, and correspondence with actual systems remains unestablished. No human has reviewed the work. Its ambition is a unified account and a direction for life, without claiming an ethical verdict binding every possible mind.
