**The question.** Every law of the regime so far (RM1–RM4) reads amounts in whole numbers. Two of its strongest laws rest on that: a run within a band comes to rest within `3 hi + 3` steps (RM3, [study §300](#300-where-a-run-of-a-regime-comes-to-rest-rm3)), and a state's fate is decided at that step (RM4, [study §301](#301-the-threshold-in-the-plane-of-level-and-recent-giving-rm4)). Renewal is usually modelled in real amounts. So the question is which of these laws are artefacts of whole numbers, and what holds in any amounts.

**What was built** (new laws of `Becoming.RegimeMargin`, pure core, every axiom report empty; the periphery module `RegimeReals`, with Mathlib).

- *Amounts with a draw* (`Drawn`). The core's `Amounts`, with a linear order, antisymmetric, and a truncated difference: `sub a b` is all of `a` but `b` where `b` is within `a`, and nothing where it is not. `Drawn.step` is the adjusted step there. In whole numbers it is RM2's step, and its rising adjustments and order are RM3's (`Drawn.whole_run`).
- *The order.* Where the adjustment rises with what is left and with the recent giving, the step keeps the order of states (`Drawn.step_mono`, through `Drawn.sub_mono`). So a run that raises (lowers) both at one step does so at every later step (`Drawn.rising_on`, `Drawn.falling_on`).
- *The shape of an unordered step.* After a step the recent giving is within the level (`Drawn.given_le_level`). From such a state, a step that neither raises nor lowers both either climbs or sinks, whatever the adjustment (`Drawn.climbs_or_sinks`). A climb raises the level strictly while the recent giving falls strictly and stays strictly above the draw. A sink lowers the level strictly while the recent giving rises strictly and stays strictly below the draw. At or above the draw the level rises exactly where the new giving meets the draw (`Drawn.level_up_iff`, `Drawn.level_down_iff`). Below it the draw takes everything and the level is the new giving (`Drawn.level_of_low`). The step after a climb (sink) that neither raises nor lowers both climbs (sinks) too, since the recent giving cannot cross the draw while unordered (`Drawn.climbs_next`, `Drawn.sinks_next`). `Drawn.run_shape` states the five laws on the run.
- *Within a band, at rest.* A run from a state within a band the step keeps stays within it (`Drawn.run_within`). The step leaves a state as it is exactly at the regime's point (the recent giving at the draw and the adjustment giving the draw from what is left) or within the draw with level and recent giving equal, the adjustment from nothing left giving it again (`Drawn.rest_iff`). A run is still from a step exactly where its state there is such a rest (`Drawn.still_iff`).
- *Over the non-negative reals* (`RegimeReals`). The non-negative reals are amounts with a draw (`Drawn.reals`). Every run of a rising adjustment from a state within a band converges (`Drawn.converges`). Either some step raises or lowers both, after which both coordinates are monotone; or none does, and from the first step every step climbs, or every step sinks, so the level and the recent giving are each monotone. In both cases they are bounded by the band. Where the adjustment is continuous the limit is a rest of the step (`Drawn.limit_rest`), so the regime's point or a state within the draw with level and recent giving equal (`Drawn.limit_fates`). Limits keep the order of the states run from (`Drawn.limits_below`). So a state at or below one whose run converges below the draw converges below it too (`Drawn.falls_of_below`). At one level, a larger recent giving whose run converges below the draw has every smaller one converging below too (`Drawn.falls_given`). The fates are down-closed, the boundary a falling curve, as in RM4.
- *Witness* (`Drawn.Relaxation.never_rests`). Giving that moves halfway from the recent giving to a draw of one rises in both and keeps a band up to one. From nothing the level and the recent giving stay equal and fall short of one by `(1/2)^n` (`Drawn.Relaxation.course`). So the run converges and is still from no step.

**Against the prior form** ([significance review, amounts check](BECOMING-SIGNIFICANCE-REVIEW.md#amounts-check)). The mathematics is known: orbits of cooperative (order-preserving) planar maps are eventually componentwise monotone, so bounded orbits converge (Smith 1998; Hirsch and Smith 2005, from memory). The general theorem asks for regularity conditions (a one-to-one map, positive determinants). The adjusted step needs none of them, because of its own structure: the level moves by the new giving less the draw. That makes every unordered step move the recent giving toward the draw from the side it stands on, with no property of the adjustment. The spine states this shape constructively in any amounts with a draw. The periphery adds the convergence, which needs completeness, over the reals.

*Reading* [AI reading, D45]. What whole numbers add to the regime's laws is that a run reaches its rest in finitely many steps and that its fate is decided at a fixed step. The structure (order kept, unordered steps toward the draw, rests at the regime's point or below the draw, fates down-closed and falling with the recent giving) holds in any amounts. Over the reals runs converge rather than rest, as the relaxation witness shows. T2's account of the state does not depend on amounts being counted.

**Self-review.** The first plan stated convergence in the core over any amounts. It was dropped: a bounded monotone sequence converging is an omniscience principle constructively, and "converges" needs a topology the core does not have. So the core states the shape, which is all a constructive proof gives, and the periphery adds what completeness gives. `Drawn` asks for antisymmetry only for the rest law: with the order a preorder, as the core's `Amounts` allows, a rest would only be equivalent to the draw, not equal to it. The shape of an unordered step asks nothing of the adjustment, and that the recent giving is within the level holds from the first step on, which is why `run_shape` starts there. The periphery's `Falls` takes the limit by existence, so no choice of limit is defined; limits are unique in the reals. The connectivity check was planned before the first checker run: every new core law is stated on a run or read by one that is. Limits: discrete steps remain; the regime case `H + y` and a computed threshold over the reals are not stated; coupled conditions and a further lag are not stated.
