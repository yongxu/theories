## A check before building: one reader, several conditions (21 October 2026, RS1)

An AI check (one session, 21 October), made before the Lean and completed while it was written. The [oversight check](#oversight-check) found the oversight theorem known as supervisory control under partial observation and delay. It left one thing open, the reader's capacity. `Regulation` reads one condition at a time, and an overseer reading one condition a step can keep how many? **Verdict: the mathematics is known. This is pinwheel scheduling, and its origin is a monitoring problem: a ground station reading satellites, each within its window. What the spine adds is the correspondence, and one consequence that alignment work does not state.** The windows come from the margin law: `a` is the largest with `(a + ℓ) d ≤ m`. Keeping a condition is exactly reading it within its window, in every balanced flow (`ReadingSchedule.keeps_iff_within`). So oversight capacity is a pinwheel density, and the rule for it is the pinwheel rule. Summed rates at most one are necessary. Summed rates below one are not sufficient: windows 2, 3 and any `M` cannot be kept together, though the reader is idle a share `1/6 - 1/M` (`ReadingSchedule.spare_not_enough`). Summed rates at most one half suffice, proved constructively here (`ReadingSchedule.keeps_of_half`). Summed rates at most five sixths suffice, which is Kawamura's theorem, cited and not proved.

**What is claimed, and what is not.** No new mathematics: the counting bound, the `(2, 3, M)` instance and the one-half theorem are from 1989. The step from a margin to a window is the margin law with a draw a step, and keeping as reading within the window is the oversight check's theorem. The module adds three things. First, the correspondence on the spine: the window is read off balance, the draw bound and the lateness, so each step of lateness shortens every window by one and raises the density a reader must carry. Second, the exact form on the base, with a witness flow for necessity. Third, the half-density theorem constructively, with empty axiom reports. The consequence for oversight is the one a reader of the AI-control literature would not foresee from its own terms. Spare attention does not show that a set of conditions can be kept: an overseer idle a sixth of its time can fail three conditions. The exact rule is density at most five sixths, from scheduling. Whether actual AI oversight has diffuse draws with known margins and draw bounds is not checked. That is the premise under which the rule applies.
