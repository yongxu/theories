# The map's store: a design, 11 November 2026

*Written by AI for Ren, on his question: "is the approach optimal for human understanding, and right representation? is the data structure that project onto the map pareto frontier?" Six independent designers answered it, each from one discipline, five of them blind to my own view and one set to break it. Their reports are in `designs/`. This is my decision from them and from the data. A working note, not part of the public work.*

## The answer

No. The first map's picture can stay. Its store cannot.

The first map stored two things: a place, and an arrow between two places. Both turn out to be drawings, not the thing drawn.

- **A place is a combination.** The 15 places are answers to about eleven independent questions. "Judged only from inside" is four answers at once. A flat list cannot say that, so it could not show overlap, and it had to file the law that leads *into* the hold as "inside" it, because "from any start" is not a place.
- **An arrow carried five different things.** Of the 38 arrows, 9 are borders where nothing moves, 8 are single built examples, 3 are readings, and several happen only if something the laws take as given is changed from outside. About 18 are a change that a general law shows the course itself making. A reader takes all 38 as "this leads there".

All six designers reached this, by different routes. None was told the others' answers.

## What is stored instead

One collection, beside the atlas and anchored to it.

1. **Questions.** Each is one distinction a law draws, about one relation among a small cast (the rule, those ahead, the others, those with a say, what is used up, those who live on it). Each has two to four answers, tied to the Lean predicates and ledger premises that state it.
2. **Laws.** One row per Lean law, on one question, of one of three shapes: the answer **stays**, the answer **changes**, or a **border** between two answers. Each row keeps what it takes as given (answers to other questions), what the model compares, how far it is seen, and which Lean module it is proved on.
3. **Places.** A name for a set of answers. The fifteen names stay. Overlap and containment are computed.
4. **Cases.** A real situation, or a Lean witness, is the same record: the questions answered, with "not known" allowed.

## What is drawn from it

- **Who is who:** six roles and the relations between them. Every place is this one figure lit differently.
- **The board:** the questions in rows, the answers in cells, each law drawn by its shape. Position is computed.
- **Where a case stands:** answer the questions for something you know; the page says which places it is in, which laws apply, and which are one answer away.
- **The first map** can be regenerated from the same store. It is a view.

## What the designers warned of, and I accept

- **Whether it is good is yours to say, and only then anyone else's.** You said yes to boxes and arrows, so the map look stays. Nobody outside is asked to read it until you find it good; asking people to review a 4 would waste their attention.
- **"Pareto frontier" has no single point.** What can be shown is that the first store is dominated: the new one redraws the first map and more. Which picture to show first is a choice, not a proof.
- **The store is not the binding limit.** The laws sit in about 108 Lean modules joined by 10 bridges, 6 of them proved. No structure can draw a more connected map than that truthfully. The best it can do is show the joins, which also says which bridges to prove next.
- **More unity than the Lean has** is the main risk. One question gathers predicates from different models. Each such joining is marked proved or a reading.
- **The vocabulary does not shrink.** Eleven questions and 28 answers for this region, against 15 place names. Each page gets shorter; the number of words to learn does not.

## What I did not take

- **Hexagrams.** The idea fits (a few aspects, one changes given the others); the six-line figure does not. Most combinations have no law, the relations depend on one another, and the account already has the 64 hexagrams as a named model, so the glyph would confuse the two.
- **Seven global questions for the whole theory.** Each region needs its own; the regions join where they share a question, not through arrows.
