# The map's foundation, after the test (11 November 2026)

*Written by AI for Ren. A draft foundation was put to ten readers set to break it: one for each of seven families of the atlas, one on the tests on record, one on the words, one against the whole. Their reports are in `round3/`. This is what stood, what gave way, and what I now think the foundation is. A working note, not part of the public work.*

## What gave way

I had proposed that the map's whole vocabulary is the root's lines and four marks: of every line, what makes it begin, last, change, end. It does not hold as the whole.

- Across 462 laws read from their Lean statements in seven families, 86 fit "if these marks, then that mark" cleanly. About 100 more are a comparison of amounts or times. The rest say something else: two courses go alike; an order is kept; two models are the same; nothing of this kind can happen.
- A mark has no meaning without its sameness (01 §3: "the answers depend on the class"). "Ends" alone stood for eight different Lean predicates in one family.
- Two of 193 laws in the first region are stated in the root's own words. For the rest a mark word would be the map's reading of the Lean, and the map would show less of what was checked.

## What stood

- **One map.** No family needs a map of its own. The pattern, its stock and a rule appear in all seven. Two families are the same roles asked two questions: one asks what makes it last, the other what ends it. 91 Lean laws are filed in more than one family.
- **Reasoning, situation, and where they meet** are already the grain of the record: the atlas keeps laws, one case written as a web, and tests bound to laws with each premise assessed.
- **The four questions** stand as the reader's way in, each said with its sameness and its extent.

## The foundation

**1. The ground is one web of kinds.** A few kinds of role and a few kinds of tie between them. A role is a place in a tie, not a thing: one thing can fill two roles.

| Roles | Ties |
|---|---|
| what goes on (a pattern) and its parts | passes on through new parts, or keeps the same |
| its stock, and what renews the stock | draws on; renews; needs |
| the other drawers; what depends on it | draws together; depends on |
| a rule; what it favours and the rest; its readers | selects and favours; is set from; reads, or lies beyond |
| a reading, and what it is of | is revised by what it meets |

**2. The unit is a statement.** Of a role or a tie, under a named sameness: that it begins, lasts (and how: at rest, returning, passing on, growing), changes or ends; or that one amount is at least another. With when (at a step, from some step on, again and again) and on which courses (this one, some, every).

**3. Reasoning says statements in general.** A law is proved on a model, in that model's roles. It has a frame, which says what a case must supply to meet it: one course; two courses that differ in one thing; the best of what is open; every course. Two models' roles are one place on the map only where a proof joins them; otherwise two places with a seam between.

**4. A case says statements in particular.** An actual web, in its own words: here, then, this was seen. Each statement is built (set up so) or found.

**5. Where they meet is a bridge and a test.** A bridge pairs a case's things with a law's roles. A test compares what the law then predicts with what was seen. On the record a proved law has never failed and cannot: a case falls under it or outside it. What fails is the bridge, the measure, the scoring rule, or a thesis, which is a bridge claimed for a whole kind of case.

| | the "then" was seen | the "then" was not seen |
|---|---|---|
| the "if" is met | they agree; ask whether a rival predicts the same | the bridge, the measure or the rule is wrong |
| the "if" is not met | it came about another way: a sighting no law covers | the law is silent: the case marks its border |

**6. The words follow the same cut.** A law's sentence uses role-words only. A case uses its own words. The bridge is the one place both appear. My 人 came from writing laws in one case's words.

## What the record shows today

- Of 2,073 laws in the atlas, 27 are named by a test that ran.
- No law has been met whole, every "if" met, by a case with a scored prediction.
- In the three field bindings every premise is "not checked".
- Of 11 bindings with a result, 6 mark the bridge and none marks the law.

## The reading is on the map too

One family of the account is its own laws about a reading: what it is of, what reaches it, what it tries, whether it accords. The account is itself such a reading, and its tests are what reaches it. So the relation between reasoning and data is not a layer above the map; it is one part of the map applied to the map.

## Not settled

- **The words.** The role-words are the account's own (carriers, share, lock, readers, what lies beyond). They are free of persons and not all plain. The proposal is that plainness comes through cases, not by rewording laws.
- **How many forms of law.** The readers found seven or so. That number comes from reading, not from a rule.
- **The picture.** One web is honest at the grain of roles and ties. Drawn truthfully it shows a few thick places and much that no case has met.
