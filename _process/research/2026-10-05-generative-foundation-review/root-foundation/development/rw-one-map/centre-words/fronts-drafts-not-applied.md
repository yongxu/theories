# The questions of a selection on the fronts, second version: old and new (drafts, not applied)

*Working sheet for readers (board item WM2), AI-written, 13 November 2026. V1 was checked by six readers; NEW is written from their findings. "Four questions" now names the four questions for a sentence or a picture (AGENTS.md; chapter 08 §1), so the questions asked of a selection are called "four things to ask".*

## 1. README's first screen, "What it is for" (`_process/navigation/README-FIRST-SCREEN.md`, line 10)

OLD: **What it is for.** Its centre: a selection can meet its own criterion while what it cannot read is lost. Whether that loss counts forms within becoming too, in what people and AI come to read; the theorems, which read only the dynamics, do not settle it (07 §1). Four questions for any selection, a reward, a judge, a market or a vote: what does it read or need; what lies beyond that; can it do better on what it reads by lowering what lies beyond; can its rule lock on what it already selected. A judge has a fifth: who reads the loss, and when. The work proves, on its models, when doing best on what is read must drain what lies beyond, as far as any allowed outcome does, and when a rule read from the share of its own winners, favouring them, locks on them, without noise, while a loss it does not read grows. A ranking whose next rule is refit to the clicks its own top results draw, its success read from those same clicks, may be such a loop; that an actual ranking is one, meeting the theorem's conditions, is conjectured (02, T7). To use them today: the five questions as a sheet for a model-as-judge pipeline, worked on one, and its uses in six fields. No test designed from it has yet run on a deployed pipeline.

NEW: **What it is for.** Its centre: a selection can use up what sustains it while how much is left makes no difference to it. Whether that loss counts forms within becoming too, in people and AI; the theorems state the dynamics and do not settle it (07 §1). Of any selection, a reward, a judge, a market or a vote, ask four things: what can make a difference to it; what makes none; whether there is room, every allowed outcome that leaves more of what makes none being beaten on what makes a difference; and whether its rule, set again at each update from the share it already favours, can lock. Of a judge ask a fifth: who counts the loss, and when. The work proves, on its models, when a selection at its best, with room, leaves what makes no difference to it as low as any allowed outcome does, and when a rule set again from the share it already favours stays locked, without noise, however large a loss grows that makes no difference either to what is selected next or to the rule. A ranking whose next rule is refit to the clicks its top results draw, its success measured by those same clicks, may be such a loop; that an actual ranking is one, meeting the theorem's conditions, is conjectured (02, T7). To use them today: the five questions as a sheet for a model-as-judge pipeline, worked on one, and its uses in six fields. No test designed from it has yet run on a deployed pipeline.
