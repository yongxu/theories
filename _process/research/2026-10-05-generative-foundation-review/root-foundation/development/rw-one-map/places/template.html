When a rule is rebuilt from its own results

Each box is a place: a condition something can be in. A shared word, a habit, a currency and a monopoly can all sit in the same one. Each arrow is a change, and its line shows how far that change has been seen: from proved exactly, on a model, down to a reading with no law. Select a place or an arrow to see what is behind it.

How to read the lines

  • Happens exactly when its condition holds
  • The condition is enough for it
  • Leaving needs this (hollow head)
  • One worked example; no general condition shown
  • A reading or an open question; no law
  • No arrowhead: one place is a case of another, by definition

A number on a line counts the laws behind it. This region holds {{items}} recorded exits, thresholds and joins. {{arrows}} are drawn as changes between places, on {{pairs}} lines; {{unconditioned}} of them have no general condition. {{stays}} say something stays where it is, {{inside}} are thresholds inside one place, {{out}} lead out of this region, and {{unplaced}} are not placed.

Every law here is a theorem about a stated model, checked in Lean. Whether an actual market, ranking or institution meets a law's conditions is a separate question, and the examples are illustrations. Places were named and the laws assigned by AI readers on 11 November 2026, each assignment checked against the theorem's statement by a second reader. Drawn by script from the atlas's data.
