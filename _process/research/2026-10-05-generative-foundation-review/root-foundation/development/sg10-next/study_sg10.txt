**The question.** SG8 ([study §295](#295-an-order-weighting-later-draws-less-sg8)) and SG9 ([study §296](#296-rebuilding-from-below-sg9)) found what every optimum of a far-sighted, weighted order does to a renewing base: it ends the base at once where the weighting falls faster than the base renews, and otherwise holds it or rebuilds it. Every one of those laws read what stands to be drawn at a step as the level with its renewal. Item 10 of §8.1 left open several optimizers ranking courses. Two questions: do the laws need the renewal form at all, and what becomes of a far-sighted optimizer's course when others draw from the same base?

**Against the prior forms** ([significance review, drawn-first check](BECOMING-SIGNIFICANCE-REVIEW.md#drawn-first-check)). The mathematics is known in its kind: under open access the rent of a common stock is dissipated, since what one harvester leaves another takes (Gordon 1954), and in the dynamic commons each player's far-sight is cut by the others' draws, the equilibrium stock falling as players are added (Levhari and Mirman 1980; Clark 1990). The spine's form is one optimizer's side of it, exact: the threshold for ending the base at once moves from what the base renews to what the others' leaving renews, for every order rising with the weighted draw, not one sum; where the others leave no more than renews back to the level, any falling weighting ends it, and where they leave less, even an undiscounted order does. The generalization below is the spine's own step: the far-sighted optimum laws hold over any law of what stands, so decay, renewal changing from step to step and others' draws are cases, not new models.

*Reading* [AI reading, D25, D45]. SG8 and SG9 said what far-sight does with a base an optimizer draws alone: it ends it only where its weighting falls faster than the base renews, and otherwise keeps or rebuilds it. SG10 says this keeping is not the optimizer's alone to give. What it leaves stands for it only through the law of what stands, and others drawing first are part of that law: where they take what the remainder would renew, nothing it leaves comes back larger, and any preference for sooner over later ends the base. So the same order that rebuilds a depleted base when alone ends it at once among others: the destruction comes from the relation, what one leaves being drawn by the others, not from any change in the optimizer. For §7 this is the course form of what SG3 found in one step, several optimizers draining as one: over a course, far-sight does not protect a shared base unless what is left is held, by the whole relation, against being drawn.
