**The question.** Every optimum of an order rising with what is drawn holds a level of greatest renewal through the horizon and draws everything at its last step (`Optimum.frontier_holds`, SG5, [§292](#292-courses-ranked-over-a-horizon-sg5)). The drain at the end is the order's: nothing after the horizon counts. The far-sighted objection's natural repair is to value what is left at the horizon. §8.1 item 10 listed that order as open: does a terminal value keep the base, and what it depends on?

**What was built** (new laws of `Becoming.Optimum`, pure core, every axiom report empty).

- *The bound with what is left* (`Optimum.drawn_add_left_le_most`, `Optimum.most_left_iff`, `Optimum.open_left_le_most`). From SG5's identity, what a course draws across `n + 1` steps, with what stands after them and everything the renewal falls short of `r` after the first step, is the start with what is given there and `r` a step. So what is drawn with what is left is at most that, exactly where `r` is given at every step after the first; what is left is not fixed.
- *The order* (`Optimum.byDrawnLeft`): courses ranked by what they draw across `n + 1` steps with what they leave after them, valued alike.
- *Keeping is an optimum* (`Optimum.holdingTo_same`, `Optimum.keeping_left_most`, `Optimum.keeping_open`, `Optimum.keeping_frontier_left`). The course standing at `y` at every step after the start draws and leaves the most. It is open over any horizon where the capacity covers what the first step draws down to `y` and `r`, since after the first step it draws only what is given.
- *Draining is one too* (`Optimum.holding_frontier_left`). SG5's course, drawing `y` to nothing at the last step, draws `y` more and leaves `y` less. Valued alike, the last step's draw is indifferent.
- *Without the value, keeping is none* (`Optimum.keeping_not_frontier`). Where `y` is positive, the course keeping it is no optimum of the order by the draw alone, every optimum of which leaves nothing.
- *Every optimum, exactly* (`Optimum.frontier_holds_left`, `Optimum.frontier_left_iff`). Every optimum of every order rising with what is drawn and left stands at a level of greatest renewal at every step from the first to the `n`-th. The optima of `byDrawnLeft` are exactly the open courses doing so.
- *On a web* (`Web.frontier_kept_left`, `Web.frontier_broken_left`). From a start `s`, as in SG6's web laws, a dependence every level of greatest renewal meets stands through the horizon under every such optimum. One above every such level is broken at the first step and unmet through the horizon.
- *Witnesses* (`Optimum.Holding.gated_left`, `Optimum.Holding.logistic_left`). Under a gate renewing one unit at every level from one, from the dweller's need, with capacity three, the course keeping the source at the need through and past the horizon is an optimum and keeps the dweller at every step. Draining at the end is an optimum too. The optima are exactly the courses standing where the gate renews, and keeping is no optimum by the draw alone. Under the logistic law, every optimum breaks the dweller at the first step.

**Against the prior form** ([significance review, left check](BECOMING-SIGNIFICANCE-REVIEW.md#left-check)). A value on the state left at the end is a terminal or salvage value. With none, a finite-horizon harvest ends by drawing everything (Clark 1976, from memory). The spine's form is exact for every order rising with what is drawn and left. Valued one for one, the end's drain is no longer forced, and the steady yield at a level of greatest renewal is an optimum through and past the horizon. Valuing what is left removes the drain and nothing else, so what depends on the condition above those levels is broken as before.

*Reading* [AI reading, D45]. A far-sighted optimizer that values the stock it leaves keeps its own base. It does not thereby keep what lives on that base: the value attaches to the stock, not to the dependences the stock's levels meet. The objection "add a terminal value" is met exactly. It removes the end effect SG5 found and leaves the first-step break, which is the alignment gap.

**Self-review.** Premises were checked for necessity. The keeping course draws only `r` a step after the first, so its capacity premise is `r ≤ k`, not SG5's `y + r ≤ k`. It needs no lower bound on the horizon, so `frontier_holds_left`, `frontier_left_iff` and `Web.frontier_kept_left` hold over any horizon. The draining course keeps SG5's premises. The broken law keeps `0 < n` for the step after the first.
