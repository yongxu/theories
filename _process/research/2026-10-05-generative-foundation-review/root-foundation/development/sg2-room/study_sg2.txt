**The question.** SG1 ([study §283](#283-what-optimizing-through-couplings-does-beyond-them-sg1)) proved that every order rising with what couples in has its optima at the least level beyond exactly where lowering a condition beyond leaves room for what couples in to rise (`Optimum.least_iff`), and that without room an optimum need not lower anything (`Optimum.Drain.room_needed`). Room was supplied. Thesis T3 rested on it: its standing called room "a supplied premise", its claim said the drain lies where what lies beyond shares units with what is rewarded, and its forbidden drives included a systematic drive on a condition beyond that shares no units with anything rewarded. Chapter §8.1 item 10 kept the derivation open. The question was whether room follows from a law of exchange, and if it does, where every optimum then stands beyond, exactly.

**What was built** (new laws of `Becoming.Optimum`, pure core, every axiom report empty).

- *The outcomes open to a part.* A part is a Boolean on formations, with a capacity for each member at each condition. Across the step from `t`, a flow `L'` acts within the part's capacity where the same formations take part as in `L`, the formations outside the part take and give as in `L`, and each member takes from each condition at most its capacity, gives nothing outside the listed conditions, and gives the listed conditions no more in total than it takes from them (`Flow.ActsWithin`). The outcomes open to the part are the levels after every such step that balances from the levels before (`Flow.Outcome`), the step that happened among them (`Flow.Outcome.of_balances`). What the rest takes, what it gives and the part's capacity at a condition are totals over the formations taking part (`Flow.restTaken`, `Flow.restGiven`, `Flow.capacity`).
- *Room, read off the exchange.* Among the outcomes open to the part standing at least at given floors beyond (`Optimum.Above`), an outcome above another at a condition beyond is exceeded on what couples in by an outcome open to the part (`Optimum.Above.room`). The other step reaches the lower level there either by taking more there or by giving less there; outside the part both steps exchange alike, so some member differs (`Optimum.member_below`, from `exists_lt_of_total_lt`). If it takes more, this step's member has capacity to spare and can take one more unit there and give it to a condition of what couples in (`Flow.ActsWithin.moveUnit`, `Flow.moveUnit_balances`); if it gives less, this step's member gives a unit there that it can give to what couples in instead (`Flow.ActsWithin.redirect`, `Flow.redirect_balances`). Either way the levels after are the transfer of one unit (`Optimum.Above.transfer`), above every floor (`Optimum.transfer_above`). The only premises are listings without repetition, what couples in apart from what lies beyond and holding a condition.
- *Every optimum beyond, exactly.* So the drive of SG1 applies, and more: above its floor, at every step reaching an optimum of every rising order, each member takes its whole capacity at a condition beyond and gives nothing there (`Optimum.Above.full`), since otherwise the transfer beats it; so the optimum stands at its floor or at what the rest leaves less the capacity (`Optimum.Above.floor_or_full`). With no floor (`Optimum.above_nothing`), every optimum stands at each condition beyond at `l c + restGiven − restTaken − capacity`, natural subtraction, so at nothing where the capacity reaches all that stands (`Optimum.optimum_beyond`).
- *No drive where no units are shared; the drain exactly where they are.* Where no member taking part may take from a condition beyond, every optimum leaves it at `l c + restGiven − restTaken`, where the rest leaves it (`Optimum.optimum_untouched`); an optimum lowers it below that exactly where the part's capacity there is positive and the rest leaves something there (`Optimum.optimum_lowers_iff`). On a web whose levels after the step are an optimum, each condition beyond depended on at a need it meets, a dependence beyond is broken exactly where its need exceeds what the rest leaves less the capacity (`Web.optimum_broken_iff_capacity`).
- *Witness* (`Optimum.Drain`, on `Diversion`). The taker, with capacity `k` at the source and none at the store, draws `k` from the source into the store, the dweller exchanging nothing (`drawing`, within its capacity by `drawing_within`); for `k ≤ 2` this is an optimum of the store's order among the outcomes open to the taker (`drawing_optimum`), and the dweller's dependence on the source at need two breaks exactly where `0 < k` (`drawing_broken_iff`, derived from the general law).

**Against the prior forms** ([significance review, room check](BECOMING-SIGNIFICANCE-REVIEW.md#room-check)). The mathematics is known. Zhuang and Hadfield-Menell's Theorem 1 assumes a resource constraint strictly increasing in each attribute, so that lowering an unmentioned attribute frees resources for a mentioned one; that assumption is room, supplied. At a maximum flow every arc leaving a minimum cut is saturated (Ford and Fulkerson 1956), and an optimum of a monotone objective over capacity constraints binds every constraint through which the objective can still rise (corner solutions; the greedy solution of the fractional knapsack). On the spine the members' capacities are the arcs from what lies beyond into what couples in, and the saturation is `Above.full`. What the spine's form adds is where the constraint comes from: conservation per member (a member gives no more than it takes) with capacity, and the rest exchanging as it does. Read so, the shared resource is a law of exchange, not a premise; the optimum's level beyond is exact, not only least; and an attribute that shares no units with the part is untouched, which their theorem leaves outside its hypotheses.

*Reading* [AI reading, D25, D45]. Where an optimizer's order rises with what couples in, how far it lowers what lies beyond is set by its capacity there and by nothing else in the order: the drain at a condition beyond is the part's capacity there, or all that the rest leaves, whichever is less. What the optimizer registers of the beyond does not enter, and counting the beyond after the reward changes nothing (SG1); what it can take does. So of the levers on the drain at an optimum, the capacity at what others depend on is the exact one: an optimizer whose capacity at a condition is nothing leaves it where the rest leaves it, whatever its order. T3's clause that the drain lies where units are shared, and its forbidden drive on a condition sharing none, hold for optima at one step; what stays conjectured is that trained systems near their optimum do the same, and what stays open is a course of steps in which the rest responds.

**Self-review.** The laws are close to definitional once the outcome set is stated: room is the existence of one unit's move, taken where capacity is spare or redirected where a member gives beyond, and the exact optimum is saturation. Their content is that SG1's substantive premise is now a theorem of one step of exchange, with the optimum's level beyond exact, and that a clause and a forbidden drive of T3 become theorems for optima. Five limits. One step: an optimizer choosing again at each step of a course, the levels before moving with it, is not stated, nor renewal between steps. The rest is fixed: where the formations outside the part respond to it (the pool laws of §7.1, `Becoming.PoolResponse`), the outcomes open to the part differ, and the laws here do not reach them. Capacities are supplied, as the levels before are; which capacity a formation has is not derived from a law of the formations. A member may give what it takes to any listed condition; a member able to give only to some, a store only it fills, is not covered. The `DecidableEq` premises on formations and conditions decide equality, used to state the unit moves; they add no premise about the unfolding.
