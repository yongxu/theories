**The question.** SG5 ([study §292](#292-courses-ranked-over-a-horizon-sg5)) found that far-sight keeps the resource base of an optimizer's own draw and nothing else: every optimum of every order rising with what is drawn holds a level of greatest renewal, and a dependence whose need lies above every such level is broken at the first step. Its reading named what could keep such a dependence within the law's terms: a coupling bringing it into what the order rises with. That leaves the quantitative question every coupling, penalty or side constraint meets: how much must the coupling be, and does the amount stay fixed as the optimizer sees further?

**Against the prior forms** ([significance review, keeping check](BECOMING-SIGNIFICANCE-REVIEW.md#keeping-check)). The mathematics is known in each part. That a coupling of a constraint into an objective enforces it exactly when it exceeds what the constraint takes from the objective is the exact-penalty principle (Zangwill 1967), here with a lump-sum penalty whose threshold is the value gap itself; that leaving forage for predators lowers yield is the multispecies and ecosystem trade-off (May and others 1979; Pikitch and others 2012); that a side-effect penalty's coefficient trades task reward against impact is the impact-measure literature (Krakovna and others 2019; Turner, Hadfield-Menell and Tadepalli 2020). The spine's form: the value gap is exact, the renewal given up at the need for each step and the need left at the end, for every renewal law bounded by `r` and `q`; the thresholds hold for every order rising with the worth, not one objective, and are attained on both sides; the dependence's break is a law of the web, not a valuation; and where the need lies above every level of greatest renewal the gap grows linearly with the horizon.

*Reading* [AI reading, D25, D45]. What keeping a dependence forgoes for a far-sighted optimizer is no fixed sum: it is the renewal given up at the need for each step of the horizon, with the need left at its end. A coupling of the dependence into the order keeps it exactly where it exceeds that amount, so a coupling fixed in total is outweighed by a long enough horizon wherever the need lies above every level of greatest renewal, and an optimum keeps it only where the coupling is at least `(r − q) n + m` for a horizon of `n + 1` steps (with a capacity able to draw everything at the last step). The further an optimizer sees, the larger the coupling needed to keep what depends on the condition it draws: far-sight, which SG5 showed keeps only the optimizer's own resource base, also erodes every fixed coupling for anything else. For §7 this sharpens SG5's answer to the far-sighted objection: a fixed penalty or side constraint set once does not stay sufficient as horizons lengthen; what keeps a dependence for every horizon is a coupling that grows with it at the rate of the renewal forgone, or the dependence entering what the order rises with at every step, which is the alignment question itself rather than a parameter.
