**The question.** SG6 ([study §293](#293-what-keeping-a-dependence-forgoes-over-a-horizon-sg6)) found what keeping a dependence forgoes over a horizon: the renewal given up at the need for each step and the need left at the end, `(r − q) n + m`. A coupling of the dependence into the order keeps it exactly beyond that amount, so a coupling fixed in total is outweighed by a long enough horizon wherever the need lies above every level of greatest renewal. Its reading named the other form: the dependence entering what the order rises with at every step. Does a coupling added for each step the dependence is kept escape the erosion, at what rate, and what does the end of the horizon do to it?

- *Generalized below SG6* (rule 9). A course from a start meeting `m` either keeps it through the horizon or falls below it, at some step to the `n`-th, after standing at `m` or above at every step to it (`Optimum.keeps_or_falls`); SG6's `keeps_or_drops`, which records only the level just before the fall, is its case.
- *What met steps forgo.* Where what is given at each of the first `j` of `n` steps is at most `q`, `j r` is at most what is given falls short of `r` across the `n` steps with `j q` (`Optimum.shortfall_ge`, from `le_sub_add_of_le`). So, with SG5's identity after the first step, a course standing at `m` or above at every step to the `j`-th, `j ≤ n`, draws across `n + 1` steps, with `r` for each of those steps, at most its start with what is given there, `r n` and `q` for each of those steps (`Optimum.met_drawn_le`); on the flow, under balance, what is taken there likewise (`Flow.Balances.met_taken_le`). Each met step forgoes at least `r − q`. The holding course stands at `m` or above at every step to the `n`-th whatever its end (`Optimum.holdingTo_met`).

**Against the prior forms** ([significance review, stepwise check](BECOMING-SIGNIFICANCE-REVIEW.md#stepwise-check)). The mathematics is known in each part. That a finite horizon's end changes the last period's choice is the end-game effect of finitely repeated play, where cooperation sustained by later play fails at the last period and unravels to the first by backward induction (Luce and Raiffa 1957; Selten 1978); that an owner with a finite horizon depletes the stock at the end is terminal depletion (Clark 1976); that a side-effect penalty may be applied at each step is the stepwise form of impact measures (Krakovna and others 2019; Turner, Hadfield-Menell and Tadepalli 2020). The spine's form: the rate a step coupling needs to keep the dependence at every horizon is exact and free of the horizon, `r − q`, the renewal forgone at the need a step, for every renewal law bounded by `r` and by `q` at or above the need; the thresholds hold for every order rising with the worth and have optima in every case; the end effect is exact, below the need, and stays at the last step wherever the coupling exceeds the renewal forgone a step, because each met step's coupling is direct rather than sustained by what later steps do, so nothing unravels; and the break is a law of the dependence web.

*Reading* [AI reading, D25, D45]. A coupling fixed in total erodes with far-sight (SG6); a coupling added at each step the dependence is kept does not. What it must match is a rate, the renewal given up at the need a step, and that rate does not grow with the horizon: once the coupling a step covers it and exceeds the need, every optimum keeps the dependence at every horizon. What remains is the end of the horizon. There the last step can draw the need itself, so a coupling a step below the need loses the dependence at the end; but where the coupling exceeds the renewal forgone a step the loss stays at the last step and does not reach back, unlike the unravelling of strategic play, because nothing in the earlier steps depends on what the last step does. For §7 this makes SG6's reading exact. What keeps a dependence for a far-sighted optimizer is the dependence entering the order at every step at a rate covering what keeping forgoes there. The rate is fixed by the renewal law and the need, not by how far the optimizer sees. The horizon's end then needs either a coupling a step above the need or no end within the optimizer's horizon. A coupling sized to a horizon is outrun by a longer one; a coupling sized to a step is not.
