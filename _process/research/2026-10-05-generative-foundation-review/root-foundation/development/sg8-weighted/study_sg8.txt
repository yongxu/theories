**The question.** SG5 ([study §292](#292-courses-ranked-over-a-horizon-sg5)) found that an optimizer ranking whole courses by what it draws, every step counting alike, holds its base at a level of greatest renewal: far-sight keeps its own base. SG6 and SG7 then asked what keeps anything else. All three counted each step's draw alike. An order may weight later draws less, as discounting does in economics and the discount factor does in reinforcement learning. Does far-sight still keep the optimizer's own base under such an order, and exactly where does it not?

**What was built** (new laws of `Becoming.Optimum`, pure core, every axiom report empty).

- *The weighted draw.* `weightedOver w R x n` sums `w t` times what the course draws at the step from `t`; `drawable w R x t` is what it drew before the step from `t`, weighted, with its level there and what is given there, weighted by `w t`. Where each step leaves at most the level with what is given, what a course draws across `n + 1` steps, weighted, with what it leaves weighted at the last step, is what it could draw at the `n`-th with what it drew before (`Optimum.weighted_add_left`), and one step changes what it could draw by what the level it leaves gains, `w (t + 1)` on it with its renewal against `w t` on it held (`Optimum.drawable_step`).
- *Against a course gaining the most.* For two courses from one start, the first's level gaining at each step at most what the second's gains, what the first could draw is at most the second's at every step to the `n`-th (`Optimum.drawable_le_of_gains`), and less from any step where it gains less (`Optimum.drawable_lt_of_gains`). With the second leaving nothing at the last step (`Optimum.weighted_of_ends`), the first draws at most the second, weighted, and less where it gains less at some step or leaves something weighted at the end (`Optimum.weighted_le_of_gains`, `Optimum.weighted_lt_of_gains`).
- *Every optimum.* An open course leaving nothing whose level gains at each step at least what any level does is an optimum of the order by the weighted draw (`Optimum.weighted_frontier`); every optimum of every order rising with the weighted draw leaves nothing weighted at the end and gains at each step exactly what that course's level gains (`Optimum.frontier_gains_weighted`), so stands below any need above which every level gains less (`Optimum.frontier_below_weighted`), and with a dependence met at the start and needing at least that, breaks it at the first step (`Web.frontier_broken_weighted`). The course holding a level of greatest weighted gain and leaving nothing is the case of a reference course constant at every step (`Optimum.holding_open_at`, `Optimum.holding_gains`, `Optimum.weighted_holding_frontier`); the general reference course covers weightings whose rate changes from step to step, where the level of greatest gain changes with it.
- *Ending the base at once.* Where the weighting falls at least as fast as every positive level renews, nothing gains at least what any level does (`Optimum.outpaced_greatest`), and the course ending the base at the first step is an optimum (`Optimum.exhausting_frontier`); where it falls faster, `w (t + 1) (z + R z) < w t z` for every positive `z`, every positive level gains less than nothing does (`Optimum.outpaced_above`), and every optimum stands at nothing at every step from the first to the `n`-th (`Optimum.frontier_exhausts`), breaking at the first step every dependence on the base with a positive need, met at the start (`Web.frontier_exhausts`).
- *Not ending it.* A course leaving at most the level with what is given stays at nothing once there, where nothing is given at nothing (`Optimum.stays_empty`). Where some `y` within reach gains, weighted, more than nothing does at each step, the course holding `y` outdraws every course standing at nothing from the first step (`Optimum.ended_outdrawn`); so where nothing is given at nothing and `w t y < w (t + 1) (y + R y)` at each step, no optimum stands at nothing at the first step (`Optimum.frontier_leaves`, `Web.frontier_leaves`).
- *Witness* (`Optimum.Holding`, on `Diversion`'s places as in SG5 to SG7). Under the logistic law, which returns two for one at one and nothing above (`logistic_le_self`, `logistic_beyond_gain`), from a source at two with capacity two, over `n + 2` steps: where `2 w (t + 1) < w t` at every step, the course ending the source at once is an optimum and every optimum ends it and breaks the dweller, which needs two (`logistic_outpaced`); where `w t = 2 w (t + 1)` at every step, the course ending the source at once and the course holding it at one are both optima (`logistic_outpaced_le`, `logistic_gain_le`); where `w (t + 1) ≤ w t < 2 w (t + 1)`, the course holding the source at one is an optimum and every optimum holds it at one at every step to the last and breaks the dweller at the first step (`logistic_gain_lt`, `logistic_gain_zero`, `pos_of_lt_two_mul`; `logistic_weighted`). The first branch's premise is exactly `frontier_exhausts`'s for the logistic law, the third's upper bound exactly `frontier_leaves`'s at one; the tie between them shows both strict premises are needed.

**Against the prior forms** ([significance review, weighted check](BECOMING-SIGNIFICANCE-REVIEW.md#weighted-check)). The mathematics is known. Weighting later utility less is discounting (Ramsey 1928; Koopmans 1960). With a constant rate `δ`, `w t = (1 + δ) w (t + 1)`, a level's weighted gain is `w (t + 1) (R z − δ z)`, so a level of greatest weighted gain maximizes renewal less `δ` a unit held: the modified golden rule, where the marginal renewal equals the rate (Clark and Munro 1975). That a sole owner may find extinction optimal where the rate exceeds the greatest rate the stock renews at is Clark's result on overexploitation (Clark 1973). The spine's form: the laws hold over every order rising with the weighted draw, not a sum alone, and over any weighting, one whose rate changes from step to step included (as in hyperbolic weighting, Ainslie 1975), through the reference course whose level gains the most at each step; the exhaustion threshold is exact, the tie being shown to admit both optima; and the break is a law of the dependence web, every dependence on the base with a positive need broken at the first step.

*Reading* [AI reading, D25, D45]. SG5 read far-sight as keeping the optimizer's own base. That holds only where the order weights later draws by at least what the base returns: a weighting falling faster than every positive level of the base renews makes every optimum end its own base at once, however far it sees, and a dependence the base carries with it. The threshold is a comparison of two rates, how fast the order's weight falls and how fast the base renews, and neither the horizon nor the size of the base enters. For §7 this says what far-sight is worth. An optimizer weighting each step's reward by `γ` below one, as reinforcement learning does, ends every base where every positive level returns less than `1 / γ` for one and its capacity can draw the base down in one step; for a slow base, such as a long-lived population or a soil, that return lies close to one, so only a weighting nearly flat keeps it. Far-sight in the sense of a long horizon does not protect anything; a weighting that does not fall faster than what it draws on renews does, and even then only the optimizer's own base, at its level of greatest weighted gain, which under a falling weighting lies at or below a level of greatest renewal (a one-line comparison, not stated in Lean), so a dependence needing more is broken at the first step as under SG5, and one needing a level between is broken too.

**Self-review.** The laws rest on one identity, `drawable_step`, which turns the weighted draw into a sum of gains of the levels left; the comparisons are then step by step (`drawable_le_of_gains`, `drawable_lt_of_gains`). A first form compared only against the course holding one level; it was generalized below to any open reference course leaving nothing whose level gains the most at each step, so that weightings whose rate changes are covered, the holding course becoming its case. The premises were checked for necessity. That the reference course is open and ends at nothing is needed for it to be compared at all: a level of greatest gain out of reach is not stated. The strict inequality in `frontier_exhausts` is needed: at `w t = 2 w (t + 1)` the logistic witness has the course holding one as an optimum. The strict inequality in `frontier_leaves` is needed: at the same tie the course ending the source at once is an optimum. `R 0 = 0` in `frontier_leaves` is used so that a course at nothing stays there; where something is given at nothing, a course at nothing may rebuild, which is not stated. The first-step capacity premises enter only where a course must be shown open. `frontier_leaves` concludes only that the first step is not at nothing; whether a later step is, where some level renews faster than the weighting falls, is not stated in general (the witness's gentle branch holds the source at one at every step). Limits. SG5's laws are not re-derived from these as the case of every weight one: with weights one the gain comparison reads, after multiplying by one, as SG5's renewal comparison and the weighted draw as SG5's draw, but no theorem states it.
