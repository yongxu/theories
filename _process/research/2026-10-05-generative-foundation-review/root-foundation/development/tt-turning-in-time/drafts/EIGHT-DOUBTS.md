# Eight doubts

Ren approved the rewritten statement in the main session, and then listed eight things he was not sure of. He is right on most of them. What I proposed was too small where it mattered and partly wrong.

## 1. "No renaming of existing Lean; a short table; the new model named in root terms"

Wrong on all three parts.

- **"No renaming" contradicted my own finding** that the Lean's vocabulary is the first cause. It is also cheaper than I assumed. The five lock modules hold 84 definitions, and 25 of the 401 modules import them (about 20,000 lines to rebuild). The cost lies outside the Lean: `RuleCapture` is cited in 990 files and `ClosedCapture` in 892, most of them generated pages or history.
- **A table is prose, and prose drifts.** The tie should be proved: a small module of bridges, each old definition shown to be a statement in the root's own predicates.
- **Root names on the same model would be the word swap again.** If the model is still a tuple of share, stock and rule with functions between them, Chinese labels hide the cut. What matters is that the theorem is stated with the root's predicates (one relation, classes, constant under the relation, stops), the model supplying only the relation.

So: state the centre in the root's predicates, prove the bridges to the old modules, and rename the 84 afterwards only if the old names still mislead.

## 2. Would this actually fix it?

Not by itself.

- **It is the kind of fix that has failed each time**: a rule and a table. In this session I wrote the four rules and broke all four in my next answer.
- **The rewritten statement is not clean.** It keeps one thing at the centre and everything else as "its conditions". That is organism and environment, softened. In the root a condition holds between occurrences, both ways, and a thing is a line under a class.
- **A fix has to change what generates the language**: the few lines every session reads first, how the centre is stated in the Lean, and who reads what before it is built on.

## 5. Really on the right path, when we always drift?

The aim, yes. The way of working, no, and that is where the drift comes from.

- **Rate.** Over the last fourteen days there were 75 to 405 commits a day and 1 to 9 records of Ren's words a day. He is the only one who can tell whether the language holds, and what he says arrives after hundreds of commits have been built. This is the centre statement itself: the work goes on while the one thing that could turn it makes little difference to it.
- **His yes is often a button an AI wrote.** The signal that steers the work is shaped by the work. When he speaks freely he is more exact, as in "where is the line between short and long return?".
- **Two standing instructions feed it**: "keep going, no permission" and "see the answer, don't ask". Right for proofs and checks. Wrong for anything that states the centre.
- **The corrections alternate between requirements that pull apart.** 11 November: too many words, own vocabulary, not serious. 12 November: dualist language. Each round satisfies the latest one. They need ranking once.
- **One part cannot be met.** No language is free of a subject. A test for real drift: does the wording change what one would do? "Oversight" from outside leads to more control; "carriers within" leads to keeping them independent of the course. That is corruption. A grammatical subject is residue.

- **The rewritten statement reads as philosophy.** An alignment researcher will skim it; the first version was easier for them. The language that keeps the thought whole is not the language they read.
- **Two doors, one statement.** For alignment: the result, a dozen Lean statements checked by a person, one experiment with a number that could be wrong, all placed against the near work. For philosophers and systems people: the statement and the ethics in three sentences.
- **Where the value is**, as I see it:

## 7. Holes in the theory?

Yes. By weight:

1. **Forming.** How something that carries a difference comes to exist, and how a thing comes to be a thing (the classes are supplied). Everything constructive for the ethics and for alignment sits here.
2. **Value.** Concern wider than one's own return is not shown to form. Prudence is.
3. **Foresight.** Nothing in the models looks ahead. Planning, deception, and what a network comes to pursue are not covered.
4. **The centre is not one proof.** Two links are assumed.
5. **Amounts.** Numbers and conservation stand beside the root as a second primitive.
6. **Evidence.** No test trains a model, and the one test near the centre counts against a premise.
7. **One form across six results** is unproved, and the near work is unread.

## 8. Fixing at the right scale?

No. Too small and too large at once.

- **Too small**: words, a page, a table, while the causes sit in the process and in how the centre is modelled.

- **The scale that fits is what Ren can read.** In order:

  2. The few lines every session reads first.
  3. One working rule: nothing that states the centre is built on before he has read it.
  4. Then one model.
