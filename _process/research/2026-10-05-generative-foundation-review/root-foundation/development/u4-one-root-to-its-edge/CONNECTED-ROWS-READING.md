# The 31 connected rows, read against the grammar's laws

*An AI reading, for U4 ("one root to its edge"); read-only, nothing compiled. Checkout: `wt-u4` (branch `u4-edge`, package identical to main at 4ac8cf27e). Lean paths below are relative to the package, `experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/becoming/`.*

## Method

I took the 31 rows of `boundary-table.json` with `"standing": "connected"`, located each declaration by name and namespace, and read its statement and proof, then the proofs of the lemmas it calls, down to where each reported grammar constant enters. A row is **case** only where an existing theorem Y of a `Becoming.Grammar*` module, read at the model's relation, *is* the row's content: the remaining steps only read the model's notions as the grammar's. Those steps are definitional unfoldings, or short representation lemmas that already exist (for example an orbit read as `«及»`), plus predicate logic or a prelude fact. A row is **steps** where grammar laws are used inside reasoning that needs a model fact no grammar law gives: amounts, step indices, preorders, valuation premises, or a model's own induction. **Case, Y not in the grammar** marks one row whose Y has grammar-only content but is declared in a model module. The definition of "derived" does not let that row count until Y moves into the grammar. Where I was unsure between case and steps, I chose steps. I checked the walk's grammar constants against the proof text and list the discrepancies at the end. I did not build the package; nothing here is a new Lean claim.

## Where the walk's grammar constants look wrong or misleading

1. **`onLoop_of_closing`** lists `«及».brecOn` among its grammar *theorems*. It is the auxiliary definition Lean generates for structural recursion on the inductive `«及»`, used by `Coupled.of_reach` (`Closing.lean:254`). It is not a law and belongs with the definitions, beside `«及».below`.
2. **`Learnable.learnable_iff` and `LearnableRuns.learnable_iff_tracks`** list `GrammarLaws.gap_of_lt`, `le_succ_cases`, `revisits_within`, `skipAt_le`, `skipAt_lt` and `skipAt_ne` (and the definition `GrammarLaws.skipAt`) as grammar theorems. `GrammarLaws.lean:258–264` says these are "Lemmas on numbers and lists, not statements of the grammar" (pigeonhole and its index surgery). Of the eight constants listed, only `«迭之定»` and `«迭之接»` are grammar laws; they enter through `lasso_of_held` (`Learning.lean:495`). A walk that counts every constant of a `Grammar*` module overstates these rows' reach. Their standing (steps) does not change.
3. **Misleading, not wrong.** In the rows that use the closure to carry a closed region along a run, the walk reports `«所闭之传递»`, `«所闭之自反»` and `«缘在所闭»`: `ClosedCapture.lock_outlives_loss`, `Pace.rule_until_reading`, both `RuleCapture` rows, four `RegimeMargin` rows (through `orbit_mono`) and `Valuation.significant_iff`. These enter only through `reach_iff_withinClosure`/`withinClosure_of_trace`, which turn a run's reach into `«所闭»`. In `Coupled.Indexed.ends_downstream` they enter through `downstream_within`, which reads the model's chain as `«所闭»`. The law the rows actually rely on is the content of `«闭含所闭»`, that a closed region holds what lies within the closure. It never appears as a constant, because it is used by applying `«所闭»`'s definition to the region (`Frame.lean:255–257`; `RegimeMargin.lean:2242`).
4. Every other row's reported grammar theorems match the proof text I read. I did not trace every leaf of `Identification` for `le_succ_cases`; it is plausibly reached through `Identification.lean:1025`.
