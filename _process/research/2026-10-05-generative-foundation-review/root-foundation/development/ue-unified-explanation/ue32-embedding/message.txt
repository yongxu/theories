UE32's design landed: T2's named state against the field's fitted delay embedding, frozen before any series is read by it

MQ3 to MQ5 read T2's state in assessed fish stocks against rivals of its own family: regressions in chosen columns, fitted in sample, and a frozen law's forecasts. UE32 reads it against the field that reads a state from a series without naming it in advance, empirical dynamic modelling, by that field's own criterion, out-of-sample skill. On MQ5's stocks, its spawning-biomass reading, its share of the catch and its split, T2 gives only the coordinates (the escapement, the escapement a year back and the recent giving), as T2 and MQ5 write them, and the rival is the delay embedding of the same two series, its dimension from one to four chosen by the S-map's own leave-one-out error on the training years. Both are read by the same S-map, its nonlinearity chosen the same way, and scored by the held-out squared error of the next giving. T2 fails if the field's embedding removes a tenth of T2's held-out error in the median stock and is better in significantly more stocks (two-sided sign test, p < 0.05); the converse is "T2 better"; otherwise not separated. How a "T2 fails" is read is frozen too: the longer history carrying more, T2's first forbidden item out of sample, where the stocks whose embedding chose a longer history carry the loss, and otherwise the lag not paying its way. The run checks its stocks against MQ5's committed fits before any rule is computed, and stops if they differ.

The answer expected is stated before the run: not separated. MQ5's own readings of these stocks place them between its synthetic worlds: at one year its forecast with the lag had the larger pooled error, as in its age-structured world, and S3's share of stocks above the tenth, 39%, lies between the 12% of T2's world and the 99% of the age world. So the dry runs add a mixed world, each stock age-structured with probability 1/3, where the test says not separated in 94% of 200 archives and never T2 fails. Across 200 archives for each of ten worlds, the test fails T2 in 47.5% to 78% of archives where an age structure holds in every stock and in 44.5% under a second lag, each fail read as the longer history carrying more; it fails T2 in one of the 1,200 archives of the other worlds; under T2's own law it says T2 better in 33% (22% with noise). Both states trail persistence in most worlds, and that is reported. Each stock's r is reported beside its MQ5 in-sample S3 excess, which can show a minority of stocks carrying memory where the median-stock verdict cannot. The verdicts with N in the balance's other form and with its form chosen per stock are reported; the per-stock choice costs N in nine worlds of ten, so the verdict reads the form T2 named. The dry runs took about 13,700 seconds of CPU.

With the design: the field read source by source, each source with how it was read, and what it would count as a fair test (FIELD-READING.md); the field's published per-stock dimensions tabled against T2's 277 stocks, one table seen, 28 stocks matched, no failure of T2 named (STOCKS.md); and four proposals for T2's owner, documents only (T2-PROPOSALS.md): the second forbidden item not to apply in a series shown, by an upper bound, to sit at the regime's-step end; the horizon at the grain the three numbers are read; "as far as anything measured does" kept until a frozen run fails it; the lag's place named before the data. Two readers, each a separate agent that had not written what it read and opened no data, read the work before the freeze: one the documents (16 findings, all taken, its second reading's five slips fixed), one the design (15 findings, all taken; a second reading found that choosing T2's form per stock cost T2 in nine worlds of ten, that the S3 pairing's power and the expectation were overstated, and that the sign test's defence rested on the wrong comparison; a third reading recomputed every number and found one error and two stale sentences, fixed).
