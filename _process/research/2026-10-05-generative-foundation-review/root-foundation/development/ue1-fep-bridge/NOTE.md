## 1. Why first

The free-energy principle is the most developed attempt to give one principle for every self-organizing system, from a cell to a brain. Its proponents now also offer it as the basis of collective intelligence of humans and AI. Containing it as a case is the sharpest available test of whether this theory reaches farther. The test can fail, and on the first pass it has, in the sense the plan defines: a containment that forbids nothing the principle leaves open is a restatement.

## 2. The principle in its own terms

- **Its start.** Random dynamical systems with Langevin dynamics. States are partitioned into internal, external, sensory and active, by conditional independencies the principle derives from sparse coupling; the sensory and active states form the Markov blanket. The derivation is disputed (below). The classical formulations assume a nonequilibrium steady state.
- **Its step.** Under the partition, internal states are read as parameterizing beliefs about external states. Their flow is read as descent on variational free energy, an upper bound on surprisal. To persist is then to gather evidence for one's own model: *self-evidencing*.
- **Its process theory.** Discrete-state active inference:
  - a generative model, with perception and learning as belief updating;
  - the posterior over policies is a softmax of the log prior over policies, less the free energy of past outcomes under each policy and the precision-weighted expected free energy (SPM12 uses the prior's expected log, ψ(e) − ψ(Σe));
  - the prior over policies has Dirichlet counts that add the posterior over policies at the end of each trial (§4);
  - in the 2016 paper a habit is also a special state-action policy whose transitions are learned.
- **Its status.** Its proponents present the principle as a method of description rather than a claim that could be refuted, and its process theories as the part that makes predictions.
- **What its critics found.**
  - Biehl, Pollock and Kanai: definitions of the blanket differ between works and are not equivalent; rewriting the equations of motion under a blanket needs assumptions that are not stated; the original free-energy lemma fails as stated, by counterexample. Where the lemma holds, it implies that the variational density equals the ergodic conditional density; the Bayesian reading hinges on this, and they conclude that it is not sufficiently justified.
  - Aguilera, Millidge, Tschantz and Buckley: in weakly coupled linear stochastic systems the blanket condition and the bound on solenoidal flow hold only in a narrow range of parameters, and taking the dynamics of average states for the average of the dynamics fails in general. Sparse coupling does not in general give the blanket condition.
  - Bruineberg, Dołęga, Dewhurst and Baltieri: the blanket as a statistical tool is conflated with the blanket as the boundary of a thing.

## 4. What the kit proves, and how it stands to active inference

`Becoming.ChoiceCountCapture` ([kit tree](tree/Becoming/ChoiceCountCapture.lean)): the scratch build compiled 35 theorems in 45 declarations, every axiom report empty, against the pure core ([log](scratch.log)). It is a leaf, an instance module of three spine laws: selection through couplings, capture through the rule and closed capture.

- **A chooser reading what couples in selects through its couplings.** The choice made from a fold of the prefix of what couples in, whatever the fold and whatever the choice, is a selection through the chooser's couplings (`selectsThrough`, by `Web.selectsThrough_iff_prefix`). So histories alike through them are chosen alike (`chosen_alike`), and a part acting only beyond the coupling closure does not change the choice, under the hypotheses of `Flow.unselected_beyond_coupling` (`unchosen_beyond_coupling`). Choosers sharing their folds select through their pooled couplings (`pooled_selectsThrough`, `pooled_unchosen_beyond`). This part is near-definitional: the principle's sparse coupling, internal states reading external states only through blanket states, already says as much. The blanket condition, conditional independence in the steady-state density, is a further property that sparse coupling does not in general give. Active states are not among what couples in, and a sampled action must be listed among what couples in for the laws to apply. Their use is that every law about selections through couplings applies to an agent so represented.
- **A rule read from the count of its choices locks.** The chooser takes the policy its rule weighs more, ties to the first, and that policy's count rises (`choose`). The rule is the counts weighted by a standing score, `κc` and `κo` (`weighted`). Choosing raises the share it reads, strictly (`choose_raises`, `choose_rises_strictly`), and the reading is monotone (`weighted_reads`). Under a standing score, with the rule in force read from the counts at the start, the threshold `κo · other ≤ κc · carrier` divides the counts into two regions the course never leaves:
  - at or above it, the first policy is taken at every step along every schedule, and neither share nor rule falls (`counted_locks`, `counted_chooses`), also where the score favours the second policy;
  - below it, the second policy is taken at every step, the rule favouring it strictly, and neither share nor rule rises (`counted_locks_down`, `counted_chooses_down`).

  Ties go to the first policy by `choose`; with `κc = 0` or no count of the first policy, the second is taken for good.
- **Its edges, proved.** What carries the course across is a change of the counts, of the score, of how the rule reads or of the choice rule. One count moved, three to one becoming three to two under the score one to two, and the course locks the other way (`Witness.moved_locks_down`). The in-sync start is a hypothesis: counts above the threshold under a rule left from an older score lock the other way (`Witness.Stale.above_but_stale_locks_down`), and a change of score read one step late leaves the old lock in place where read at once it would turn (`Witness.Stale.devalued_read_late_locks_up`, `devalued_read_at_once_locks_down`). `RuleCapture.uniform_locks` does not apply, since the weighted reading favours only at or above the threshold.
- **Counted from the posterior at the time of choosing, the share falls.** Selection by the fixed rule `byScore` (the counts scaled with the posterior's weights added) lowers the first policy's share strictly at every step where the score favours the second, carriers present (`posterior_count_falls`, `posterior_course_falls`, by `RuleShaping.falls_strictly` and `present_along`). A falling share need not cross soon: in the witness the weighted rule still favours the first policy after one step, by a tie, and the second from the second step (`Witness.after_one_still_first`, `Witness.after_two_turns`). With additive counting the crossing can take very long: from counts 17:1 and 48:1 against a score of one to two, first at trials 208 and 1710 (the review's direct computation, not Lean).
- **The lock outlives a loss it does not read** (`counted_outlives_loss`, by `ClosedCapture.lock_outlives_loss`).

**How it stands to active inference's scheme.** The 2016 paper (Friston, FitzGerald, Rigoli, Schwartenbeck, O'Doherty and Pezzulo, §2.4) counts "beliefs about the initial state and selected policy", with the learning updates at the end of each trial. SPM12's `spm_MDP_VB` adds the end-of-trial posterior over policies to the habit's counts, `MDP.e = MDP.e + u(:,T)`, as the review read in the code. Where the trial's outcomes reveal the policy pursued, that posterior is concentrated on it, so the count is close to the count of the executed policy, `choose`'s count. Policies that explain the trial equally share it, which the kit does not model; the paper places the habit's advantage exactly there. `byScore`, the posterior at the time of choosing, is a contrast case that no implementation cited here uses.

The paper's simulation of devaluation (§5.2): devalued at trial 48, after the habit has formed, the habit persists and strengthens itself with repeated execution; devalued after 16 trials, before it was fully acquired, the agent switches at once and later forms a habit matching its new preference. Under any counting, the decision at a change of score is the posterior's argmax, so the outcome turns on the counts against `κo/κc` at the change, where the rule is read again under the new score before the next choice; read late, the counts at the first reading after the change decide (the stale witnesses). What counting the choice adds is that, under a standing score, the decision never reverses; the simulation shows persistence over the trials it runs, which is consistent with a lock and with slow decay alike. The threshold is exact in this model: the prior is the counts themselves, where SPM uses ψ(e) − ψ(Σe), and the model has no free-energy term, no sampling and no split among equally good policies. For example, counts 3:1 under a score of one to four lie below the model's threshold, while SPM's prior weights favour the first policy (the review's computation).

**Known results it restates.** The lock is the lock-in of increasing returns (Arthur 1989): past a lead, every later choice follows the leader whatever the standing preference. Counting the action taken is the value-free habit of Miller, Shenhav and Ludvig (2019), habits formed by strengthening recently taken actions rather than by encoding outcomes. In rats, extended training makes responding insensitive to devaluation (Adams 1982; Dickinson 1985); five attempts to induce this in humans failed (de Wit and others 2018).

## 5. Readings [AI reading]

1. **The blanket is the boundary of one closure among several.** What feeds a pattern can lie beyond what couples into it. The principle's active states can reach what never returns through its sensory states, so the gap exists in its terms; with one dependence structure it has no separate place for the gap, and its steady state presupposes that what feeds the thing goes on feeding it. 05's commons and the optimum's drain live in the gap.
2. **Closed self-evidencing is capture through the rule.** Gathering evidence for one's own model, with the model read from what it selects and nothing read beyond it, locks and outlives what it does not read. It is the meta-attractor of 02 only where the score too is formed from what it selects (`ClosedCapture.both_reads`), which the kit does not state. Curiosity opens the loop only within a fixed model's lumping.
3. **A chooser with set points escapes the optimizer's drive at its set point, not the drain below it, nor the commons.** This was first written as an escape from the drain; UE1b ([kit](../ue1b-set-points/NOTE.md)) proved it wrong. An order greatest at a set point is not rising, so the optimum law does not reach it as such. But below its set point it rises, and its optima drain as a maximizer's do. At its set point, on a step that loses nothing, it draws exactly its shortfall; where the step can lose what it takes, nothing protects what lies beyond. Joint excess holds for choosers held at set points. A term counted after the set point keeps the most beyond, while after a rising order it keeps nothing. Many of active inference's preference vectors are monotone, rewards among them, and those orders rise.
4. **Belief sharing pools couplings, at most.** Choosers that exchange what they keep tell apart at most what some member tells apart (05). Sharing a generative model, a common structure, pools nothing by itself. Where the rest follows one law of exchange reading through coupling, a collective of self-evidencers is blind beyond the union of its members' closures, its own acts included ([05](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/05-COLLECTIVES.md#r-what-pooled-coupling-reaches)). Where the rest's draw outruns the renewal, no later reading selects restraint ([05](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/05-COLLECTIVES.md#r-the-rest-outruns-the-renewal)): coupling is necessary and not sufficient.

## 6. Where the bridge does not reach

- No surprisal, free energy or expected free energy is stated in the kit. The score enters as two whole-number weights, the fold as any value; UE2 carries conditioning and surprisal in counting form, and the variational bound in the periphery.
- `selectsThrough` and its corollaries are near-definitional.
- The model counts the executed policy between two policies of distinct behaviour. The 2016 scheme counts the end-of-trial posterior: close to the executed policy where the outcomes reveal it, and split among policies that explain the trial equally. Its habit is also a state-action policy whose transitions are learned.
- `choose` is the posterior's argmax, the limit of high action precision; the count is of the policy executed, which is what conditioning on the trial's outcomes gives. At finite precision a sampled choice can differ from the argmax, which is a change of the counts.
- That actual agents, brains or trained systems count their choices so is not shown.
- That the principle's partition is a coupling structure of the World model is a reading. No representation theorem joins a Langevin system with a blanket to a web.
