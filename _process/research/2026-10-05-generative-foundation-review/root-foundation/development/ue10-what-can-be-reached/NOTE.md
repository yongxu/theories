## 1. The question

07's open item asks for "a comparison of a range whose widening keeps widening with one merely wider now, never reversing widening and counting neither novelty nor scale" ([07 Open](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/07-SIGNIFICANCE.md#open)). [UE9](../ue9-comparing-courses/NOTE.md) read the comparison on what courses admit, where an admitted range carries no alternatives, and left two candidates: what a course keeps reachable, and what carries its openings. UE10 reads the first, candidate B of [UE4's design](../ue4-second-order/DESIGN.md#32-candidate-b-capacity-widening-of-what-can-be-reached), on any process, through what each configuration admits; the courses from a configuration are the runs starting there.

The answer, in short: compared at the same offsets, covering implies inclusion of what each configuration admits now, so widening what can be reached never ranks a configuration above one that starts a course and admits now what it does not, whatever the first's range does later; on a determined process widening what can be reached is the strict part of range inclusion of the two courses. With a lag, the lagged order, built as UE9's is but on families of courses, decides the witness's pair, though not every such pair, and C leaves that pair unranked, so the lag's verdict is a premise beyond C. What it adds to UE9 is alternatives: covering is the lower (Hoare) preorder of the families of course ranges, and on a branching witness realized and reachable widening rank a pair oppositely, so no strict partial order there contains both. **[AI reading]** Holding both needs a rule of precedence, a premise.

## 2. The laws

Throughout, `adm : S → X → Prop` says what each configuration admits: a frame's realized claims, or what a dependence admits at a configuration (`Dependence.AdmitsAt`, where each condition a formation requires stands at least at its need). A course's range is what it admits at each offset from its start.

### Law 1. Same-offset covering is an order that implies inclusion now

- *Ranges of courses.* A course's range includes another's where what the other admits at an offset it admits at that offset (`Run.RangeIncludes`); it exceeds it where at some offset it admits what the other does not (`Run.RangeExceeds`), and widens it where it includes and exceeds it (`Run.RangeWidens`). These are the grammar's inclusion and exceeding of what the courses admit, as claims on (offset, formation) pairs (`Run.admitted`, `Run.rangeIncludes_iff`, `Run.rangeExceeds_iff`), and with fixed conditions 07's inclusion and widening (`Run.rangeIncludes_conditions_iff`, `Run.rangeWidens_conditions_iff`, by `Iff.rfl`). Range inclusion is a preorder and range widening is irreflexive and transitive (`Run.RangeIncludes.refl`, `.trans`, `Run.RangeExceeds.not_included`, `Run.RangeWidens.irrefl`, `.trans`).
- *Covering and widening what can be reached.* `s` covers `s'` where every course from `s'` is included by some course from `s` (`Reaching.Covers`). `s` widens what can be reached over `s'` where it covers it and some course from `s` is included by no course from `s'` (`Reaching.Widens`). Covering is a preorder; widening what can be reached is irreflexive and transitive, composes with covering on either side and is not covered back, with no premise (`Reaching.Covers.refl`, `.trans`, `Reaching.Widens.covers`, `.irrefl`, `.trans_covers`, `Reaching.Covers.trans_widens`, `Reaching.Widens.trans`, `Reaching.widens_not_covered`).
- *Inclusion now.* A configuration covering one that starts a course admits now all that one admits now (`Reaching.Covers.now`); inclusion now does not give covering (`WiderNow.inclusion_now_not_covering`), and a configuration that starts no course is covered by every configuration (`Reaching.covers_of_no_course`). So where `s'` starts a course and admits now what `s` does not, `s` does not widen what can be reached over `s'` (`Reaching.not_widens_of_wider_now`); with a course read as realized from each configuration (`course : S → Run P`) and the two compared courses starting at their configurations, the lexical order (law 5) does not rank `s` above `s'` either (`Reaching.not_above_wider_now`). A range that keeps widening is never ranked above one wider now that starts a course, and the premise is needed: where the configuration wider now starts no course, widening what can be reached ranks a climb that keeps widening above it (`EndsNow.keeps_widening_above_wider_now`).
- *Greatest courses.* Where each configuration has a course including every course from it, widening what can be reached is the strict part of range inclusion of those courses (`Reaching.widens_iff_of_greatest`; the direction from range widening needs only the second greatest course, `Reaching.widens_of_rangeWidens_greatest`). Such a course can exist where courses differ (`KeptAlternative.greatest_not_unique`).
- *Determined processes.* Where any two courses from one configuration agree, every course is greatest (`Reaching.greatest_of_unique`), so widening what can be reached is the strict part of range inclusion of the two courses (`Reaching.widens_iff_of_unique`, `Reaching.widens_of_rangeWidens`): a first-order comparison.

### Law 2. With a lag, the lagged order decides `WiderNow`'s pair, though not every such pair

- *Covering with a lag.* `s` covers `s'` with lag `d` where every course from `s'` is included `d` offsets later by a course from `s` (`Reaching.CoversLater`), UE9's `Web.IncludesLater` read on courses. Lag `0` is covering, and lags add (`Reaching.CoversLater.zero`, `Reaching.CoversLater.trans`).
- *The lagged order.* Widening what can be reached with a lag is covering with some lag and covered back with none (`Reaching.WidensWithLag`), and the lagged order is widening what can be reached or widening it with a lag (`Reaching.WidensOrLater`). It is a strict partial order containing widening what can be reached, and it never ranks a configuration above one covering it, so it never reverses widening what can be reached (`Reaching.WidensOrLater.irrefl`, `.trans`, `.not_covered`, `.not_reversed`), proved directly, as UE9's `strictJoin_trans` is, at configurations of any universe.
- *On a determined process.* It is the strict part of range inclusion of the two courses joined to the strict part of their inclusion with some lag (`Reaching.coversLater_iff_of_unique`, `Reaching.widensOrLater_iff_of_unique`).
- *Where it decides.* `WiderNow` is a determined law: `none` stays and admits formations `0` and `1`; `some n` climbs to `some (n + 1)` and admits the formations up to `n`. What the climb from `some 0` admits keeps widening, in 07's form, `none` admits now what the climb does not, and each course exceeds the other. Same-offset widening of what can be reached and the lexical order are silent both ways (`WiderNow.silent`). With a lag of one step the climb covers `none`, and `none` covers the climb at no lag, so the lagged order ranks the climb above `none` and not conversely (`WiderNow.decided`).
- *Where it does not.* On the same process with other admissions it need not decide such a pair. Where `none` admits the first formation of one side and every formation of the other, and `some n` the first side's formations up to `n`, the climb keeps widening, `none` admits now what the climb does not and each course exceeds the other, yet the lagged order is silent both ways, neither covering the other at any lag (`WiderNow.TwoSided.silent`, UE9's `Sides.silent` read on courses).
- *Where same-offset widening decides.* Where `none` admits every formation, `none` widens what can be reached over the climb, so the lagged order ranks it above the climb and not conversely, as it must (`WiderNow.Full.not_silent`, `Reaching.WidensOrLater.not_reversed`). Same-offset widening is not silent there, so the pair is not of `WiderNow`'s kind; the second re-check's Q5 (in [`Recheck2UE10.lean`](recheck2/tree/Becoming/Recheck2UE10.lean)) shows that on this process, under any admissions, the lagged order never ranks `none` above a climb whose course exceeds `none`'s.
- *A premise beyond C.* Widening what can be reached and the lagged order both satisfy C on the frame of reachable ranges (`Reaching.comparison_widens`, `Reaching.comparison_widensOrLater`), and on `WiderNow` the first leaves the climb and `none` unranked while the second ranks the climb above (`WiderNow.comparison_undecided`). C leaves that pair unranked; ranking it is a premise beyond C.
- *Its standing.* **[AI reading]** Its definition follows UE9's widening or widening with a lag, read on families of courses. The re-check's probes (R17, in [`RecheckLaws.lean`](recheck/tree/Becoming/RecheckLaws.lean)) give the relation exactly: on a determined process with fixed conditions its lag part is, by definition, UE9's widening with a lag of the two courses' levels, its first part is the strict part of their inclusion, and UE9's order lies inside it; the converse holds where exceeding back is decided (the second re-check's Q6c, in [`Recheck2UE10.lean`](recheck2/tree/Becoming/Recheck2UE10.lean)). The module does not import UE9's, so those comparisons stay probes. UE9's other laws about its order (it reads names across steps, and on the own-condition web it and eventual widening rank a pair oppositely) are not re-proved here.

### Law 3. Nothing is added to the whole possible along a course; on a determined process a rise is the course one step on strictly including it

- *The possible.* What some configuration reachable from `s` admits is the whole possible from `s` (`Reaching.Possible`); what some configuration reached in at most `k` steps admits is the possible within `k` (`Reaching.PossibleWithin`). They are realizability of the one-formation bundle within some bound and within `k` moves, Labelled's `WithinMoves` (`Reaching.possible_iff_withinMoves`, `Reaching.possibleWithin_iff_withinMoves`). The possible within a horizon is possible, grows with the horizon and contains the present (`Reaching.PossibleWithin.possible`, `.mono`, `.present`).
- *Nothing is added.* What is possible from a reachable configuration is possible from the first (`Reaching.Possible.of_reach`, from `together_after_reach`). So along every course nothing is added to the whole possible (`Run.possible_of_next`, `Run.possible_of_later`, `Run.possible_from_start`), a step losing a possibility has no way back (`Run.not_possible_returns`, from `lost_together_not_returns`), and what is possible within `k` of the next configuration is possible within `k + 1` of the present one (`Run.possibleWithin_moves`, from `WithinMoves.before`).
- *Covering and the possible.* A trace into a course's start gives a course whose states from some step on are the other's (`Run.tail_of_trace`, the strong form of `Onward.run_of_trace`; `Run.tail_of_reach`). So where every configuration reachable from `s'` starts a course, a configuration covering `s'` has all that is possible from it (`Reaching.Covers.possible`), and a step of a course whose later configuration covers the earlier leaves the whole possible unchanged (`Run.possible_same_of_covers_next`).
- *The premise is needed.* In `NoCourse`, `true` stays and `false` has no step. `true`, admitting nothing on its one course, widens what can be reached over `false`, which starts no course and admits every formation; everything is possible from `false` and nothing from `true` (`NoCourse.widens_reversed`).
- *The rise.* In `Rise` every step leads to `true`, which admits formations `0` and `1`, and `false` admits `0`. On the course from `false`, the next configuration widens what can be reached over the present one (`Rise.rises`), while the whole possible, and the possible within every horizon beyond the present, are the same from both (`Rise.possible_same`, `Rise.possibleWithin_same`): only what each admits now rises. The rise is the same-offset reading of the course's own delay. With a lag of one step every configuration of a course covers its successor (`Run.coversLater_next`), and on a determined process the next configuration widens what can be reached over the present one exactly when what the course admits at each offset it admits one offset later, and not conversely (`Reaching.widens_next_iff_rising`). On `Rise` the rising step widens what can be reached and widening with a lag does not rank it (`Rise.rises`, `Run.not_widensWithLag_next`), UE9's `Delayed.widens_not_withLag` read on courses. Widening with a lag never ranks a configuration of a course above its predecessor (`Run.not_widensWithLag_next`), so along a course the lagged order ranks a successor above its predecessor exactly where same-offset widening does (`Run.widensOrLater_next_iff`): the lag adds nothing there.

### Law 4. Keeping widening needs an unlisted range

- *For chains.* A chain of predicates keeps widening where nothing held at a step is lost at any later step and after every step something not held there is held later (`Reaching.KeepsWidening`): 07's form, read from the chain's first step. It gathers, for every `n`, `n` distinct members held at one step (`Reaching.KeepsWidening.gathers`), so no list holds it (`Reaching.KeepsWidening.not_listed`).
- *Along a course.* The possible within `k` along a course does not keep widening where a list holds what is possible within `k` at every configuration of the course (`Reaching.not_keepsWidening_of_listed_along`), which a list holding the whole possible from its start gives (`Reaching.listed_along_of_listed`, `Reaching.not_keepsWidening_of_listed`).
- *By name on a web.* Keeping widening by name from step `T` is exactly keeping widening of the chain of what the levels admit from `T` on (`Web.KeepsWidening.chain`, `Web.KeepsWidening.of_chain`, `Web.keepsWidening_iff_chain`), so the landed law that over a listed type of formations no level path keeps widening by name (`Web.KeepsWidening.not_listed`) follows from the law for chains (`Web.KeepsWidening.not_listed_via_chain`).
- *Witnesses.* On the climb (`Climb`: each step climbs one, and `n` admits the formations up to `n`) the possible within every horizon keeps widening and no list holds the whole possible (`Climb.keeps`, `Climb.not_listed`). The converse fails: from one configuration admitting every formation no list holds the whole possible, and along no course does the possible within any horizon keep widening (`AllAtOnce.converse_fails`).
- *Determined witnesses.* `Rise`, `Climb` and `WiderNow` are determined laws (`Rise.process_eq_law`, `Climb.process_eq_law`; `WiderNow.process` is `Law.process next`), whose runs from one configuration agree (`Law.runs_agree`, the two-run form of Branching's `law_run_state`). On them every notion here is a property of single courses, so they witness nothing about alternatives.

### Law 5. Realized and reachable widening rank a pair oppositely, on a branching witness

- *The witness* (`KeptAlternative`). The course from E runs through configurations `e t`, each admitting formations `0` and `1`. From `k` there are two branches, the realized one (`real t`, admitting `0`) and a wide one not realized (`wide t`, admitting the formations up to `t + 1`); from `single` only the realized branch. `k` and `single` admit `0` and `1`. The course read as realized from each configuration starts there (`KeptAlternative.course_start`).
- *Disagreement.* E's realized course widens `k`'s, while `k` widens what can be reached over E through its course not realized (`KeptAlternative.disagree`). So on this witness no strict partial order contains both realized widening and widening what can be reached (`KeptAlternative.no_order_contains_both`), nor satisfies C both on the realized courses and on the reachable ranges (`KeptAlternative.no_comparison_both`). On a determined process, with the realized courses starting at their configurations, realized widening gives widening what can be reached (`Reaching.widens_of_rangeWidens`), so there the conflict needs branching.
- *The lexical order.* Realized widening, or keeping the same realized range and widening what can be reached (`Reaching.Lexical`), is a strict partial order containing realized widening and never reversing it (`Reaching.Lexical.irrefl`, `.trans`, `.of_rangeWidens`, `.not_reversed`). It lies inside the package's two-tier lexical order (`Lexical.Above`) read on the realized claims and then the reachable ranges (`Reaching.realizedClaims`, `Reaching.realizedFrame`, `Reaching.lexical_above`), and the two-tier order gives it back where the inclusions are decided (`Reaching.lexical_of_above_decided`): `Lexical.Above` reads the negation of inclusion where the lexical order reads exceeding.
- *The tie.* `k` and `single` have the same realized range, so realized widening ranks neither above the other, while `k` widens what can be reached over `single` and the lexical order ranks it above (`KeptAlternative.tie`).

### Law 6. Covering is the lower preorder of the families of course ranges, and widening what can be reached is C's extension on them

- *Covering.* Covering is inclusion of the ranges some course includes (`Reaching.reachableRanges`, `Reaching.covers_iff_included`), the lower (Hoare) preorder of the families of course ranges. It is dominance for every criterion on ranges closed upward under inclusion (`Reaching.covers_iff_criteria`), and carrying the profiles of the courses (`Reaching.coursesFrame`, `Reaching.covers_iff_carries`), so preserving every bundle of their (offset, formation) admissions (`Reaching.covers_iff_preserves`).
- *Widening what can be reached.* It is extension, every claim living, on the frame of reachable ranges (`Reaching.reachFrame`, `Reaching.widens_iff_extension`, both ways and with no premise). UE4's "C read on the frame of reachable courses" is therefore the definition `Comparison (Reaching.reachFrame P adm) (fun _ => True)`, no longer a reading.
- *Linear time* (`DeferredBranching`). `late` steps to `mid`, which steps to `a` or to `b`; `early` steps at once to `ma` or to `mb`, and `ma` steps to `a`, `mb` to `b`; `a` and `b` stay, `a` admits formation `1`, `b` formation `2`, the rest formation `0`. `late` and `early` cover each other, so neither widens what can be reached over the other (`DeferredBranching.cover_both`). One step on, the next configuration of every course from `late` widens what can be reached over the next configuration of every course from `early` (`DeferredBranching.widens_after_one_step`). `late` simulates `early`, and no simulation relates `late` to `early` (`DeferredBranching.late_simulates_early`, `DeferredBranching.early_not_simulating_late`): covering cannot see when a commitment is made.

### Law 7. The possible does not certify what can be reached together

- *The witness* (`KeptTogether`). `both` stays; `split` steps to `zero` or to `one`, each staying. `both` and `split` admit formations `0` and `1`, `zero` admits `0` and `one` admits `1`.
- *The law.* `both` and `split` have the same whole possible and the same bundles realizable together within every bound (`KeptTogether.same_possible`, `KeptTogether.same_bundles`), yet `both` widens what can be reached over `split` (`KeptTogether.widens`): from `both` one course holds the two formations together at every offset, and every course from `split` holds them together only at its start (`KeptTogether.split_courses`). So on this witness no comparison read off a description that the bundles realizable within every bound determine, comparing no description with itself, satisfies C on the reachable ranges (`KeptTogether.no_comparison_through_bundles`), an instance of `Comparison.separates`, as `Regimes.no_comparison_through_marginals` is.

## 3. What it shows, and what it does not

**Shows**, on any process with what each configuration admits given:
- Candidate B in its same-offset form is not 07's second-order comparison. Covering implies inclusion now, so wherever one configuration starts a course and admits now what another does not, it ranks the other no higher, whatever the other's widening does later; on a determined process widening what can be reached is the strict part of range inclusion of the two courses, a first-order comparison.
- With a lag the lagged order decides `WiderNow`'s pair, on which same-offset covering is silent, though not every such pair (`WiderNow.TwoSided`); where the configuration wider now also widens what can be reached over the climb, it ranks that configuration above, as it must (`WiderNow.Full`). Its definition follows UE9's lagged order, read on families of courses, and on `WiderNow` C leaves the pair unranked, so ranking it is a premise beyond C.
- Along a course nothing is added to the whole possible, and a step whose later configuration covers the earlier leaves it unchanged where reachable configurations start courses. On a determined process a rise of same-offset widening along a course is exactly the course one step on strictly including the course (`Reaching.widens_next_iff_rising`); on any process the predecessor covers its successor with lag one, and along a course the lag adds nothing.
- Keeping widening of the possible within a horizon needs a range no list holds, and a range no list holds does not suffice.
- On a branching witness realized and reachable widening rank a pair oppositely, so on that witness no strict partial order contains both. **[AI reading]** Holding both needs a rule of precedence, a premise; the lexical order is one, inside the package's two-tier order.
- Covering is a known order, the lower preorder of the families of course ranges, and widening what can be reached is C's extension on the frame of reachable ranges.
- The bundles realizable from a configuration within every bound do not determine what can be reached together along its courses.

**Does not show:**
- that the lagged order, the lexical order or any comparison here is the second-order direction of 生生, or ranks a course better. Ranking needs C, and a rule of precedence between realized and reachable widening, or the lag's verdict, is a premise beyond it (`WiderNow.comparison_undecided`);
- a branching witness for keeping the possible within a horizon widening: `Rise`, `Climb` and `WiderNow` are determined, so on them every notion is a property of single courses;
- anything about learning or quasi-option value: covering cannot see when a commitment is made (`DeferredBranching`);
- the adjacent possible that cannot be prestated, or a process that changes itself: one process, one type of formations and one reading `adm` are fixed throughout;
- anything about what carries a course's openings, 07's remaining candidate.

## 4. Standing and neighbours

Each law is near-definitional or a witness. The neighbours, as the review graded them and as their publication records were checked on 26 October 2026 by web search:

- **The adjacent possible** (Kauffman 2000, *Investigations*). Law 3 restates it for a prestated space: within one fixed process the possible within a horizon moves with the actual and nothing is added to the whole possible. The adjacent possible that cannot be prestated, where the space of possibilities is not given in advance (Longo, Montévil and Kauffman 2012, arXiv:1201.2069), is outside the module, as is any process that changes itself.
- **Irreversibility and option value** (Arrow and Fisher 1974, *QJE* 88(2):312–319; Henry 1974, *AER* 64(6):1006–1012; Dixit and Pindyck 1994). Law 3 is their irreversibility premise, restated: a step can lose a possibility, and nothing lost returns. Quasi-option value, the value of deferring an irreversible commitment until information arrives, is not captured: it needs learning, which the module does not have, and deferral, which covering cannot see (`DeferredBranching`).
- **Preference for flexibility** (Kreps 1979, *Econometrica* 47(3)). Covering is exactly dominance for every criterion on ranges closed upward under inclusion (`Reaching.covers_iff_criteria`), the lower (Hoare) preorder of the families of course ranges (`Reaching.covers_iff_included`). **[AI reading]** That is the ordinal, unanimous form of a preference for flexibility, which values a set of options through its best member under each possible future preference: unanimity over the indicators of upward-closed criteria gives covering, and every monotone valuation's level sets are such criteria. The step from Kreps's representation to unanimity is argued, not proved here.
- **Opportunity sets** (Jones and Sugden 1982, *International Review of Law and Economics* 2:47–65; Pattanaik and Xu 1990, *Recherches Économiques de Louvain* 56:383–390; Bossert, Pattanaik and Xu 1994, *JET* 63:326–345). Covering is the dominance ordering of the families of ranges, inclusion of their down-closures. With options unordered it reduces to set inclusion, the monotonicity the opportunity-set rankings share; Pattanaik and Xu's own ranking counts options, which UE9's condition of keeping what the other keeps excludes.
- **Achievement and freedom** (Sen 1985, *Journal of Philosophy* 82:169–221). The lexical order is the package's two-tier lexical order read on realized claims, then reachable ranges (`Reaching.lexical_above`), near-definitional; the split between what is realized and what can still be reached is Sen's distinction between achievement and freedom, a neighbour.
- **The linear-time and branching-time spectrum** (van Glabbeek 1990, CONCUR '90, LNCS 458:278–297). Covering compares traces, not branching: a configuration that branches a step later and one that branches at once cover each other, though only the first simulates the second (`DeferredBranching`), the shape τ.(a + b) against τ.a + τ.b. Covering reads infinite courses only, so a configuration that starts no course is covered by every configuration (`NoCourse`).
- **Finite recurrence and open-ended evolution** (Bedau and others 2000; Taylor and others 2016; Adams and others 2017). Law 4 is the pigeonhole of finite recurrence, already in the package for keeping widening by name (`Web.KeepsWidening.not_listed`) and in what keeping widening needs; its converse fails (`AllAtOnce`). The open-ended evolution papers pose open-endedness as open problems and are neighbours, not containments.
- **Assembly theory** (Sharma and others 2023). An analogy only: the assembly space read as a fixed process of construction; no assembly observable is stated.
