## 1. Summary

Causal set theory takes spacetime to be a locally finite order, and its classical sequential growth adds one maximal element at a time, born with a down-set of the existing elements as its past. The module reads both on the root and on processes. Its laws fall under two records: [precedence and its links](#law-1-precedence-and-its-links), homed at 01 §1, and what can be born next against what continues, homed at 07 §2.
- **Precedence and its links.** A link of the root's reach `«及» R` is a step of `R`, so every net whose reach is a relation contains that relation's links, its covering pairs. On a naturally labelled transitive relation with decidable precedence, a causal set among them, precedence is the reach of its links, and the links are the least net whose reach it is. Deciding what lies between two elements suffices and cannot be dropped: that every pair of every causal set is reached by links is excluded middle, and so is that every pair on every configuration the growth reaches from the empty set is. On `ω + 1` the links never reach the top.
- **Growth holds the past.** Every birth keeps the precedence below the existing elements and makes the new element maximal, so the possible with the past held is the whole possible. On every course from a causal set what each configuration admits next, its stems, keeps widening, inside a whole possible that never grows.
- **What can be born next reverses the inclusion of precedence.** Of two courses from the empty set, one includes the other's stems at every offset exactly where its precedence is included in the other's at every offset, and strictly exactly where strictly; it widens them where, besides, the other has a pair it lacks, and with precedence decided only there. For every pair of such courses, finding that pair is excluded middle. Widening adds no continuation of any class read on precedence.
- **Two readings of widening, reversed.** Here every course starts from the empty set. Read on what can be born next, the course of births with empty pasts is above every course with a pair, and nothing continues in it. Read on what continues within the one class, the single chain is above every course lacking one of its pairs. No strict order contains both widenings; read on both together, neither of two courses widens the other, and courses differing by a pair are mixed.
- **The courses' start.** Most of these comparisons fail for courses from other configurations, on witnesses.
- **No configuration renews without end.** On every causal set of the growth every element ceases, so the grammar's `«生生»` holds on no configuration. In the limits the extremes part: on the antichain's nothing renews without end, on the chain's an element does under a class pairing neighbours.
- **A finer class on links** can gain or lose continuation by widening, on witnesses.
- **Up to relabelling** the pasts still reverse the inclusion of precedence, while the kinds of birth, births up to relabelling, do not: at offset 2 neither extreme's kinds of birth are among the other's. The order on courses stays labelled: two courses growing configurations of one kind are mixed.

**[AI reading, D45]** On causal-set growth from the empty set, what widening ranks depends on what it is read on: on what can be born next the course in which nothing conditions anything is highest, and on what continues the single chain. Under the continuation condition every continuation's losses count, and a widening of what can be born next loses continuation; so with that scope the reading is on both together, and there C, calling widening better, ranks no two courses: courses differing by a pair are mixed, and ordering them needs a measure, a premise beyond C and the condition. Read with D1, 生生 as generation generating generation, it is not the order read on what can be born next, since the widest course transmits no condition; that it is not the order read on what continues needs a further premise, that admitting at each offset the fewest ways to go on also falls short (§4).

## 2. The laws

### Law 1. Precedence and its links

- *A link of the reach is a step.* The links of a relation, causal set theory's term, are its covering pairs: the pairs it relates with nothing it relates between (`links`, `links_le`); two relations relating the same pairs have the same links (`links_of_iff`). A link of `«及» R` is a step of `R`, since a chain of two or more steps has a point between (`link_of_reach`). So every net whose reach is a relation contains that relation's links, on any carrier (`links_least`).
- *Every pair is reached by links* on a naturally labelled relation on the numbers, each element preceded only by smaller labels, with decidable precedence (`reach_links`). The proof is induction on the gap between labels with a bounded search for an element between (`searchBelow`, `reach_links_within`). No finiteness is needed: on all the numbers in their order, the limit of the chain, every pair is reached by links (`nat_reach_links`). What the proof uses is that each element has finitely many predecessors, all with smaller labels.
- *With transitivity, precedence is the reach of its links*, both ways (`reach_links_le`, `reach_links_iff`), so on a causal set with decidable precedence (`ordered_reach_links_iff`), and by `links_least` the links are the least net whose reach is the precedence. Without transitivity only one inclusion holds: the links of the path `0 → 1 → 2` reach `(0, 2)`, which it does not relate (`path`, `path_not_reach_of_links`).
- *Deciding what lies between.* The natural labelling bounds the search and decidability lets it be made. Deciding, for each pair and each element, whether the element lies between them suffices, with no `Decidable` instance (`searchBelow_or`, `reach_links_within_of_decided`, `reach_links_of_decided`). It cannot be dropped: that every pair of every causal set is reached by links is excluded middle (`reach_links_all_iff_em`), already on three elements whose relations through the middle are a proposition (`Undecided.third`, `Undecided.third_ordered`, `Undecided.third_not_from_two`, `Undecided.third_no_reach_from_two`, `Undecided.em_of_reach_third`). For the unfolding this says: telling a link, a direct condition, from a longer chain is deciding whether anything lies between.
- *No cycle.* Reach along a naturally labelled relation raises the label (`labelled_of_reach`), so a causal set's precedence is irreflexive and has no cycle in the root's sense (`Causet.Ordered.irrefl`, `Causet.Ordered.acyclic`), and the root's `«无环则及为序»` makes its reach a strict order.
- *Not on `ω + 1`.* On `Option Nat`, with the numbers in order and `none` above them all, the order is strict (`OmegaPlusOne.prec`, `OmegaPlusOne.prec_irrefl`, `OmegaPlusOne.prec_trans`) and every positive number lies between `some 0` and the top (`OmegaPlusOne.between`). No link ends at the top (`OmegaPlusOne.no_link_into_top`), every reach of links ends at a number (`OmegaPlusOne.link_lands`, `OmegaPlusOne.reach_lands`), and so `some 0` precedes the top while the links never reach it (`OmegaPlusOne.not_reach_top`).

So the root's reading of precedence as the reach of conditioning, with conditioning read as the links, holds on naturally labelled orders with decidable precedence, a case of causal set theory's discreteness, and can fail where the order is not locally finite. Three limits: causal set theory's discreteness is local finiteness, which is weaker than natural labelling, so a locally finite order with no natural labelling, such as the integers, is not covered (classically the law holds there, but it is not proved); `ω + 1` shows that the links reading can fail without local finiteness, not that it needs it, since an order with infinite intervals can be the reach of its links; and on `ω + 1` precedence is the reach of conditioning when conditioning is precedence itself, a transitive relation being its own reach, so only the links reading fails. This answers in part question 4 of the logic review's L-02 ([LOGIC.md](../../../../../../theories/becoming/review/LOGIC.md)), whether precedence as the transitive closure of a condition relation is a sound basis for an order of time compared with causal sets: on the orders causal set theory grows, with precedence decided and the links read as the conditions, it is, and the links are the least such conditions; without deciding, that every pair is reached by links is excluded middle (`reach_links_all_iff_em`), on the grown orders too (`reach_links_courses_iff_em`, law 2).

### Law 2. Sequential growth holds the past

- *The process.* A configuration is a labelled finite causal set (`Causet`, `Causet.Ordered`: transitive, each element preceded only by smaller labels, on the elements). A stem is a set of elements closed downward (`Stem`). A move chooses a stem and adds the element `size`, preceded exactly by it (`birth`, `process`), starting from the empty set (`empty`, `empty_ordered`).
- *Births keep a causal set a causal set* (`birth_ordered`), *keep the precedence below every existing element* (`birth_holds`) *and make the new element maximal* (`birth_maximal`).
- *Internal temporality, with the parent kept as it was, is holding the past.* Every move holds the past: no new element is born below an old one, and the old order is unchanged (`HoldsPast`, `step_holdsPast`). So the possible with the past held, UE11's possible relative to a held part, is the whole possible (`possibleHeld_iff`).
- *On every course from a causal set, what each configuration admits next keeps widening.* Every stem stays a stem across a birth (`Stem.after`); after each birth the stem of all elements is new (`all`, `stem_all`, `not_stem_all_birth`). So on every course from a causal set (`ordered_of_run`) the stems keep widening in UE10's sense (`keepsWidening`). The start must be a causal set: from the configuration with no elements whose precedence relates every pair, every past empty, no stem holds an element, and nothing keeps widening (`Starts.full`, `Starts.full_stem_empty`, `Starts.keepsWidening_needs_start`). What keeps widening is what each configuration admits; every stem a course admits is possible from its start (`stem_possible_from_start`, by UE10's `Run.possible_from_start`), and the whole possible never grows (UE10's `Run.possible_of_next`): the widening is the actual moving within a fixed possible. The adjacent possible is read here as the children, one for each stem, what the present admits (UE10's `Reaching.PossibleWithin` at `0` moves); UE10's possible within one move would be the stems of the children.
- *Links on the growth.* That every pair on every configuration of every course from the empty set is reached by links is again excluded middle: the growth reaches law 1's `Undecided.third P` at offset 3 (`Undecided.thirdPast`, `Undecided.thirdPast_stems`, `Undecided.thirdCourse`, `Undecided.thirdCourse_prec_iff`, `reach_links_courses_iff_em`).

### Law 3. What can be born next reverses the inclusion of precedence

- *Of two configurations of one size*, every stem of the first is a stem of the second exactly where the second's precedence is included in the first's (`stems_included_iff`). One direction takes the principal stem, an element and what precedes it (`principal`, `stem_principal`). The other is closure under the larger precedence. Equal sizes are needed: the stem of all elements of the larger fails in the smaller.
- *Of two courses from the empty set* (`size_of_run`, `ordered_of_empty`), one includes the other's stems at every offset exactly where its precedence is included in the other's at every offset (`rangeIncludes_iff`), and strictly exactly where strictly, with no decision (`rangeIncludes_strict_iff`). It exceeds the other's stems where the other has a pair it lacks (`rangeExceeds_of_pair`), and only where at some offset the other's precedence is not included in its own (`not_included_of_rangeExceeds`). Hence it widens them where its precedence is included in the other's at every offset and the other has a pair it lacks (`rangeWidens_of`), and a widening has its precedence included and not conversely (`rangeWidens_strict`).
- *With precedence decided*, a failed inclusion gives an explicit pair by a bounded search over pairs (`pair_of_not_included`), so a course exceeds another's stems exactly where the other has a pair it lacks (`rangeExceeds_iff_pair`). For every pair of courses, this converse is excluded middle (`pair_converse_iff_em`). Take the course bearing `2` above `0` exactly where a proposition holds. Wherever that proposition is not refuted, the antichain exceeds this course's stems, and a pair of this course gives the proposition. For `P ∨ ¬ P`, which is never refuted, the pair decides `P` (`Undecided.onlyIfPast`, `Undecided.onlyIf_two`, `Undecided.onlyIf`, `Undecided.onlyIf_prec`, `Undecided.antichain_exceeds_onlyIf`, `Undecided.notNot_em`, `em_of_pair_converse`, `pair_converse_of_em`, with `stems_of_two`); asked only of widenings, the converse already gives excluded middle (`em_of_widening_pair_converse`). So which pair a widening loses is found by deciding.
- *Widening never adds continuation* of any class read on precedence, of two courses from the empty set (`continuation_of_rangeIncludes`). Within the one class continuation is precedence (`continuation_one_iff`), and on links too where precedence is decidable (`continuation_links_one_iff`), so there too including the other's stems adds no continuation (`continuation_links_one_of_rangeIncludes`).
- *Compared by both, two courses from the empty set have the same precedence.* Where one includes the other's stems and continues at least as much within the one class at every offset, the two have the same precedence at every offset (`prec_iff_of_both`). So of two such courses, neither widens the other's stems while continuing at least as much within the one class (`not_widens_and_continues`). Within the one class what continues is precedence itself, so this is one relation read twice, not two quantities: continuation becomes a second quantity only for a finer class on links (law 7).

### Law 4. The extremes

- *Courses given by their pasts.* The configuration after births with given pasts (`grown`, `grown_size`) has `i` before `j` exactly where `j` is born by then with `i` in its past (`grown_prec_iff`). Given that each past is a stem when chosen, the births are a course from the empty set (`course`, `course_start`).
- *Births with empty pasts* (`antichain`, `antichain_prec`): the course includes the stems of every course from the empty set (`antichain_rangeIncludes`) and widens those of every such course with a precedence pair (`antichain_rangeWidens`). In it every element begins and ends in every class, on precedence and on links (`antichain_begins_ends`).
- *Births above every element* (`chain`, `chain_prec_iff`): every course from the empty set includes the chain's stems (`rangeIncludes_chain`), and every such course lacking a pair of the chain widens them (`rangeWidens_chain`). The chain has the most continuation of every class read on precedence (`continuation_le_chain`). In it each element is linked to the next (`chain_link`), and every element after the first abides in the one class, on links, once the next is born (`chain_abides`); at every offset with an element the last element ends, on precedence and on links (`chain_last_ends`).

The start matters here too: a course from one element has the stem `{0}` at its first offset, which is no stem of the antichain's empty configuration, and so widens the antichain's stems with the same, empty, precedence (`Starts.fromOne_widens`, after law 5).

### Law 5. Two readings of widening, reversed

- *Read on what continues.* Of two courses from the empty set, what continues within the one class, read as what a configuration admits (`continuing`), is included exactly where what can be born next is included the other way (`rangeIncludes_continuing_iff`). Widening it is exactly inclusion of precedence with a pair the other lacks, with no decision (`rangeWidens_continuing_iff`), and it narrows what can be born next (`rangeWidens_stems_of_continuing`). So the chain widens what continues in every course from the empty set lacking one of its pairs (`chain_rangeWidens_continuing`). That narrowing what can be born next always widens what continues is again excluded middle (`em_of_stems_converse`, `stems_converse_of_em`, `stems_converse_iff_em`).
- *No strict order contains both widenings* (`no_order_contains_both`): widening what can be born next ranks the antichain above the chain, widening what continues the chain above the antichain.
- *Read on both together* (`formsOrContinues`), of two courses from the empty set neither widens the other (`not_rangeWidens_formsOrContinues`), and two such courses one of which lacks a pair of the other are mixed: each exceeds the other (`mixed_of_pair`). 07 §2 says that ordering a mixed part needs a measure ([07](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/07-SIGNIFICANCE.md#r-four-cases-of-a-part)).
- *07's requirement, read on stems.* An order never reversing widening what can be born next ranks no course from the empty set with a precedence pair above the antichain (`not_above_antichain`), and ranks the chain above no course from the empty set lacking one of its pairs, since every such course widens it (`chain_not_above`, by `rangeWidens_chain`).

**The courses' start.** Each comparison of two courses in laws 3 to 5 takes both from the empty set, and most fail without it. Courses from a given configuration (`Starts.grownFrom`, `Starts.grownFrom_size`, `Starts.grownFrom_prec_iff`, `Starts.grownFrom_empty_prec`, `Starts.fromEmptyPasts`) give the witnesses.
- From one element, every past empty (`Starts.one`, `Starts.fromOne`, `Starts.fromOne_prec`, `Starts.fromOne_size`, `Starts.fromOne_stem_of_antichain`, `Starts.fromOne_stem_zero`, `Starts.not_stem_antichain_zero`), a course widens the antichain's stems (`Starts.fromOne_widens`) while each one's precedence, empty, is included in the other's. Without the start this refutes `rangeIncludes_strict_iff`, `rangeWidens_strict`, `not_included_of_rangeExceeds` and `rangeExceeds_iff_pair` (`Starts.strict_needs_starts`). Against the antichain, in which nothing continues (`Starts.antichain_not_continuing`), it also refutes `not_widens_and_continues`, `rangeIncludes_continuing_iff` and `not_rangeWidens_formsOrContinues` (`Starts.not_widens_and_continues_needs_starts`, `Starts.rangeIncludes_continuing_iff_needs_starts`, `Starts.fromOne_widens_formsOrContinues`, `Starts.not_rangeWidens_formsOrContinues_needs_starts`).
- The course bearing `1` above `0` (`Starts.onePast`, `Starts.onePast_stems`, `Starts.oneAbove`, `Starts.oneAbove_prec`) lacks at offset 0 the pair `(0, 1)` of the course from the two-chain with every past empty (`Starts.twoChain`, `Starts.fromTwo`, `Starts.fromTwo_prec_iff`), and exceeds it on neither reading. This refutes `rangeExceeds_of_pair`, `rangeWidens_of` and `mixed_of_pair` (`Starts.rangeWidens_of_needs_starts`, `Starts.mixed_of_pair_needs_starts`). An order never reversing widening what can be born next can rank that course from the two-chain, which has a pair, above the antichain (`Starts.fromTwo_stem_all`, `Starts.not_stem_antichain_all`, `Starts.not_above_antichain_needs_start`).
- The chain from one element (`Starts.chainFromOnePast`, `Starts.chainFromOne_prec_iff`, `Starts.chainFromOne_labelled`, `Starts.chainFromOne`, `Starts.not_continuing_chain_one`) includes the chain's stems, continues at least as much within the one class, and has a pair the chain lacks. This refutes `prec_iff_of_both`, `continuation_of_rangeIncludes` and `rangeWidens_stems_of_continuing` (`Starts.prec_iff_of_both_needs_starts`, `Starts.continuation_of_rangeIncludes_needs_starts`, `Starts.rangeWidens_stems_of_continuing_needs_starts`).
- Law 2's widening needs a causal set at the start (`Starts.keepsWidening_needs_start`).

### Law 6. No configuration renews without end

- Where a relation raises the label and relates only below a bound, every element ceases (`«息»`) under every class (`ceases_of_bounded`). So on every causal set of the growth every element ceases, in every class, on precedence and on links (`ordered_ceases`), and the grammar's renewal without end (`«生生»`) holds on no configuration (`ordered_not_renews`).
- In the limits of the extremes the two part. The antichain's limit (`antichainLimit`) has nothing renewing without end in any class (`antichainLimit_not_renews`). On the chain's limit (`chainLimit`, `chainLimit_iff`: `i` before `j` exactly where `i < j`), `1` renews without end under the class pairing `2m` with `2m + 1` (`chainLimit_renews`; `Halves.half`, `Halves.pairs`, `Halves.pairs_class`, `Halves.double_succ`, `Halves.half_double`, `Halves.half_double_succ`, `Halves.half_lt`, `Halves.odd`): each odd element conditions the next even one, which begins its pair, and the pair's line goes on to the next odd element.

Renewal without end and growth of what is admitted come apart here as on UE11's witnesses: every course from a causal set keeps widening what can be born next, while no configuration renews without end.

### Law 7. Read on links, a finer class can gain or lose continuation by widening

The wider course bears `2` above `0` and every other element with the empty past (`Finer.widePast`, `Finer.wide_stems`, `Finer.wide`). The narrower bears `1` above `0` and `2` above `0` and `1` (`Finer.narrowPast`, `Finer.narrow_stems`, `Finer.narrow`). The class sets `1` apart from every other element (`Finer.classOf`, `Finer.classOf_class`).
- The wider course widens the narrower's stems (`Finer.widens`).
- In the wider course, at offset 3, `0` and `2` are linked and alike, so the class continues from `0` to `2` along links (`Finer.continues`).
- In the narrower, `1` lies between them and is of the other class, so the class does not continue from `0` to `2` along links (`Finer.not_continues`).
- Widening can also remove it: the antichain widens the wider course's stems, and the class continues from `0` to `2` on links in the wider course and not in the antichain (`Finer.loses`).

So law 3's monotonicity holds for continuation read on precedence, and on links within the one class where precedence is decidable (`continuation_links_one_of_rangeIncludes`); for a finer class read on links widening can add continuation or remove it, so there the two are independent.

### Law 8. Up to relabelling: the pasts, and the kinds of birth

Causal set theory takes labels to carry no physical meaning (discrete general covariance), and the root reads an element only by how it relates, equality on the carrier being a commitment of the model (01 §1). Laws 3 to 5 compare two courses at the same offsets, identifying their elements by labels, so the order on courses is labelled: two courses growing configurations of one kind at every offset can be incomparable (the last bullet). This law reads what can be born next up to relabelling.
- *A relabelling* carries one configuration onto another: two maps, inverse between their elements, with the precedence read through the first (`Kinds.Relabels`). Two configurations are of one kind where a relabelling carries one onto the other (`Kinds.Isomorphic`), and the kinds of birth from one configuration are included in those of another where each birth from a stem of the first is of one kind with a birth from a stem of the second (`Kinds.Included`).
- *Up to relabelling the pasts still reverse the inclusion of precedence.* Wherever the elements of the first configuration are carried one to one into the second's, as by the inverse of a relabelling, every stem of the second, carried over, is a stem of the first exactly where the first's precedence, carried back, is included in the second's (`Kinds.stems_carried_iff`; the two need not have one size, `Kinds.stems_carried_unequal`). So law 3's core does not depend on identifying elements by their labels: under any identification fixed for the comparison, one configuration includes the other's pasts exactly where its precedence is included in the other's.
- *The kinds of birth do not.* At offset 2 the antichain's births above `0` and above `1` are of one kind, by exchanging `0` and `1` (`Kinds.swap`, `Kinds.swap_fixed`, `Kinds.swap_swap`, `Kinds.swap_below`, `Kinds.antichain_two_stems_one_kind`): two pasts, one kind of birth. At offset 2 neither extreme's kinds of birth are among the other's: the antichain's birth with the empty past has no pair, while every birth from the chain has `0` before `1` (`Kinds.not_included_antichain_chain`); the chain's birth above `0` has `0` before two elements, while in every birth from the antichain each pair ends at the new element (`Kinds.not_included_chain_antichain`). So read on kinds of birth at offset 2, the antichain, which includes the pasts of every course from the empty set, does not include the chain's kinds of birth. At offset 0 each extreme's kinds of birth are among the other's (`Kinds.included_at_zero`).
- *The order on courses stays labelled.* The course bearing `2` above `1` (`Kinds.swappedPast`, `Kinds.swapped_two`, `Kinds.swapped`) is `Finer.wide` with `0` and `1` exchanged (`Kinds.swap_eq_two_iff`, `Kinds.swap_eq_one_iff`, `Kinds.wide_prec_iff_swapped`). From offset 2 on the two grow configurations of one kind (`Kinds.wide_swapped_isomorphic`), and each exceeds the other's stems (`Kinds.wide_swapped_mixed`). This law compares configurations up to relabelling; no order on courses up to relabelling is defined.

**A count, not a law.** Counted by brute force over every causal set of one to five elements ([count/kinds_count.py](count/kinds_count.py), output in [count/kinds_count.txt](count/kinds_count.txt)), the kinds of next birth are fewest for the antichain and the chain, `n + 1` each and tied with others, and most for configurations mixing precedence and unrelated elements:
- at three elements the two-chain with a point has six kinds, every other causal set four;
- at four the most is nine, a single pair with two points; the antichain and the chain have five;
- at five the most is sixteen, a configuration with three pairs and no symmetry; the antichain and the chain have six.

The enumeration finds 1, 2, 5, 16 and 63 causal sets up to relabelling, the known counts of finite orders. It is not proved in Lean: an upper bound on the kinds of birth needs the stems decided, since pasts given as propositions cannot be sorted. The re-check reran the count byte for byte and recounted it by an independent method ([probes2/kinds_recount.py](probes2/kinds_recount.py)).

## 3. What it shows, and what it does not

Shows:
- Causal sets are a case of the root: on the orders sequential growth produces, read with decidable precedence, precedence is the reach of its links, and the links are the least net whose reach it is; telling a link from a longer chain is deciding whether anything lies between; and `ω + 1` shows the links reading can fail where the order is not locally finite.
- Sequential growth is a process holding its past, and on every course from a causal set what each configuration admits next keeps widening, inside a whole possible that never grows.
- Of two courses from the empty set, including what can be born next is having precedence included at every offset, strictly where strictly; widening it is that with a pair the narrower has and the wider lacks, which is found where precedence is decided and, for every pair of courses, only by deciding. Read on what continues within the one class, the order is reversed; no strict order contains both, and read on both, neither widens the other. Most of these comparisons fail without the start.
- The grammar's renewal without end holds on no configuration of the growth, and of the extremes' limits only the chain's has it.
- Up to relabelling the pasts keep the reversal, and the kinds of birth do not; the order on courses stays labelled.

Does not show:
- that reach of links fails on every order that is not locally finite (it need not), or holds on every locally finite one (classically it does; the Lean proves the naturally labelled case);
- anything about transition probabilities, Bell causality, discrete general covariance as a condition on a dynamics, or the continuum limit;
- that widening and continuation are opposed on every process: the reversal is shown on causal-set growth, labelled configurations compared at the same offsets from the empty set;
- how kinds of birth are ordered beyond the witnesses: the count is a computation;
- which course is better: the laws state what each reading ranks and leave the ranking to premises.

## 4. Readings

**[AI reading, D45] What the model bears on.** It bears on what C is read on, a first-order matter, and not on the second-order direction 07 leaves open. Among the courses from the empty set, read on what can be born next, C ranks highest the course in which nothing conditions anything; nothing continues in it in any class, and even in its limit nothing renews without end. Read on what continues within the one class, C ranks highest the single chain. No strict order contains both. The continuation condition counts every continuation's losses, and every widening of what can be born next loses continuation, so with the scope it fixes the reading is on both together; there no course widens another, two courses one of which lacks a pair of the other are mixed, and ordering them needs a measure, a premise beyond C and the condition. The model says nothing for the second order: every course from a causal set keeps widening what can be born next, so a range whose widening keeps widening and one merely wider now are not told apart here; 07's requirement that the direction never reverse widening, read on stems, ranks nothing above the antichain and confines any weighing to pairs widening leaves unranked, and read on both together it constrains nothing here.

**[AI reading, D45] Which 生生.** In the grammar 生生 names only renewal without end: on this growth it holds on no configuration and, of the extremes' limits, only on the chain's, under a class pairing neighbours (law 6). **[AI reading, D45] Kauffman's fourth law.** As two reviews report *Investigations* (2000), Kauffman's tentative law says that biospheres may, on average, enter their adjacent possible as fast as they can sustain (p. 22), and that they enlarge their workspace, the diversity of what can happen next, on average as fast as they can (p. 209); his Edge essay "The Adjacent Possible" (2003), read by the review, glosses the limit on pace as what exploring too fast would destroy of the system's own organization.
