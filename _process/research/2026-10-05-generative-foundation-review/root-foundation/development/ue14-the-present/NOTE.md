## 1. Summary

The root leaves one question open ([01, Open](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/01-ROOT.md#open)): whether a condition must still be there when what it conditions arises is not expressible, since the grammar has no relation of presence in time, and adding one is a choice of model. Causal-set growth ([07 §2](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/07-SIGNIFICANCE.md#r-what-can-be-born-next), UE13's `Becoming.CausalGrowth`) is a model in which the question can be asked exactly. Each birth is an event of becoming, the past is held, and the labels, the order of births, carry no physical meaning (discrete general covariance, Rideout and Sorkin 2000). How the question is answered there depends on what presence is read as. Read as being in the configuration, a condition is there when what it conditions is born, on every course, since the growth holds its past; read as Dowker's present, the birth of each element, it is past by then; read as a maximal antichain of the whole configuration, it is never in one present with what it conditions. None of these varies with the course. The module reads the present at a stage of a course as the maximal elements of what the course has born, a reading on which presence does vary with the course, and asks what of it every course keeps.
- **The orders of birth.** The renumberings of a causal set keeping precedence, its natural labellings, are exactly the orders of birth of the courses from the empty set growing a configuration of its kind (one theorem). Births of unrelated elements commute, and only they.
- **Born before.** With precedence decided, `i` is born before `j` on every course exactly where `i` precedes `j`; so what is born no later than an element on every course is its past, itself included, the largest relation every course respects: the relation Stein's theorem singles out, here as a maximum and not by uniqueness. With precedence weakly decided, `i` is born before `j` on some course exactly where the two differ and `j` does not precede `i`: two different unrelated elements are born in either order, so no order of birth of unrelated elements is common to all courses.
- **The present at a stage** is where the grammar's ending holds on what has been born. It is an antichain, and with precedence weakly decided every antichain given by a Boolean test is the present of some course at some stage. Two elements are present together on some course exactly where neither precedes the other (precedence weakly decided), and on every course exactly where they have the same future (precedence decided).
- **Presence of a condition when what it conditions is born.** `i` is present at `j`'s birth on some course exactly where `i` conditions `j` directly, a link (precedence weakly decided), and on every course exactly where `j` is the one element `i` is linked to (no decision). Between the two bounds presence is a choice of course.
- **The decision.** With no decision, being born before on every course is precedence not refuted, being present together on every course is the same future doubly negated, and presence at a birth on every course is exactly the one link. Asserting precedence, the same future, or that everything a condition precedes is at or above what it conditions is, asked of every causal set, exactly excluded middle. The laws on some course need precedence only weakly decided, and asked of every causal set each is exactly weak excluded middle.
- **On the courses themselves.** Each main law is also stated on the courses of the growth, the runs of its process from the empty set, read through the orders of birth.
- **Witnesses.** On three elements presence at a birth, and the present after two births, differ between courses. On Wüthrich and Callender's four elements their relation is kept by every automorphism but is not what every course has born by a birth; on the diamond it is the past with the pair present together on every course. Off the diamond that join need not be their relation, and need not be transitive.

**[AI reading, D45]** In this model 01's open question has an answer for each reading of presence. Read as being in the configuration, a condition is still there, on every course; read as Dowker's present, the birth, or as a maximal antichain of the configuration, it is not, whatever the course. Read as being among the maximal elements of what has been born, the answer varies with the course, between two bounds that do not: present on some course exactly at a link, on every course exactly at the one link. Whatever every course keeps, or some course has, is defined from precedence, since the natural labellings are (`natural_congr`); a relation of presence read on one course is a choice of course. So, given covariance, a premise of the model, the grammar's condition relation defines every relation of presence the growth keeps, and its lack of a primitive relation of presence in time loses no covariant content here (§4).

## 2. The laws

The module has 164 theorems in 190 declarations, every axiom report empty ([scratch-14.log](scratch-14.log)). Names are in the namespace `Becoming.Presence`. Precedence is decided where a law says so, by an instance `∀ a b, Decidable (c.prec a b)`, and weakly decided where a law says so, by a hypothesis `∀ a b, WeaklyDecided (c.prec a b)` (the core's `WeaklyDecided`: whether it fails is decided).

### Law 1. The orders of birth

- *Natural labellings.* A natural labelling of a causal set `c` numbers its elements `0` to `c.size - 1` again, keeping precedence: a renumbering of the first `c.size` numbers (the core's `Reindexes`) under which every element is placed after what precedes it (the core's `KeepsOrder`) (`NaturalLabelling`, `NaturalLabelling.at`, `NaturalLabelling.back`, `NaturalLabelling.before`). The labels as they are form one (`natural_identity`).
- *A natural labelling gives a causal set of the kind.* Reading the label `a` as the element `σ a` gives a causal set (`relabel`, `relabel_ordered`), and the labelling carries `c` onto it, a relabelling in UE13's sense (`natural_relabels`, `CausalGrowth.Kinds.Relabels`). Conversely, every relabelling of `c` onto a causal set of the same size is a natural labelling (`natural_of_relabels`).
- *Every causal set is grown from the empty set.* Bearing the element `t` at offset `t` with its past in `c` (`pastIn`, `pastIn_stem`, `courseOf`) gives a course from the empty set whose configuration at offset `c.size` has `c`'s elements and precedence (`courseOf_grows`) and is of `c`'s kind (`courseOf_isomorphic`).
- *The orders of birth, as one theorem.* The natural labellings of `c` are exactly the numberings of its elements by a course from the empty set whose configuration at `c`'s size is of `c`'s kind; on such a course `c`'s element `i` is born at offset `π i` (`natural_iff_course`, with UE13's `ordered_of_empty` and `size_of_run` for the converse).
- *What depends only on precedence.* The natural labellings depend only on the size and the precedence (`natural_congr`), and so does whatever holds on every course or on some course, whatever is read on a course (`every_congr`, `some_congr`).

### Law 2. Births of unrelated elements commute, and only they

Two elements are comparable where one precedes the other (`Comparable`, `comparable_symm`), and natural labellings order every comparable pair alike (`natural_orients`). From a natural labelling, a renumbering is reached by exchanging consecutive births of incomparable elements exactly where it is a natural labelling (`rearranges_iff_natural`). This is the core's theorem of Mazurkiewicz for arrangements (`rearranges_iff` in `Becoming.OrderNormal`), which needs no decision, with joining read as comparability: an instance, not a reproof. The step from it is `Rearranges.orients` and the natural labelling's order.

### Law 3. A down-set first, and tables (lemmas)

- *Counting.* How many numbers below `m` pass a Boolean test (`count`, `count_succ_of_true`, `count_succ_of_false`, `count_le_succ`, `count_le_add`, `count_mono`, `count_le`): what passes is counted before what comes later (`count_lt_of`), two passing numbers with as many passing below each are one (`count_inj`), the count takes every value below it (`count_reaches`), and what passes and what fails make all (`count_add_not`), with two Boolean lemmas (`not_false_of_true`, `bool_absurd`) and the prelude's (`ListTests.or_true_iff`, `ListTests.and_true_iff`, `ListTests.false_of_not`, `ListTests.not_eq_true_of_false`). `count` computes the numbers `Frequency.count` does, and its first five laws restate Frequency's; it is kept here so that the leaf imports only `Becoming.CausalGrowth` and `Becoming.OrderNormal`, where importing `Becoming.Frequency` would add six modules to its closure.
- *The stable partition* places what passes first and then the rest, each in label order (`front`, `front_of_true`, `front_of_false`, `front_lt`, `front_true_lt_false`, `front_lt_count_iff`), and its inverse is found by the core's bounded search `firstWhere` (`back`). It is one to one and onto (`front_inj`, `front_onto`), so a renumbering of the first `n` numbers (`front_reindexes`, with `lt_zero_add` and `of_lt_zero_add`). Where the test is closed downward under a relation it keeps that relation's order (`front_keeps`), and so on a causal set it is a natural labelling (`front_natural`).
- *Tables.* A Boolean `f` tabulates a proposition `Q` on the first `n` numbers where each passes exactly where `Q` holds (`Tabulates`, with `beq_false_of_ne`, `table_yes`, `table_no`, `table_false`). Every test has a table doubly negated (`table_not_not`), a decided one has a table (`table_of`), and a table of a test closed downward gives a natural labelling (`natural_of_table`, with `down_closed`). With `decided_true` and `weakly_exists_below` (whether some number below a bound has a property is weakly decided where it is for each), these carry the laws below that need no decision or a weak one.

The stable partition is the one new construction: counting and its intermediate values, no pigeonhole, no decision beyond the Boolean test.

### Law 4. What a course has born

What a natural labelling has born by stage `t` is closed downward (`born_closed`), a stem (`born_stem`). Every stem with decided membership is what some course has born first: bearing the stem first is a natural labelling whose first births, as many as the stem has elements, are exactly the stem's (`stem_born_first`). So the stages of the courses are exactly the stems with decided membership. This is Brightwell and others' characterization of their stem sets (a completed causet has a stem of a kind exactly where the first elements of some natural labelling form it), here on finite causal sets. Their stem sets, not stages, are the covariant events, proved to generate them up to sets of measure zero for generalized percolation.

### Law 5. Born before

- *Down-sets of an element.* The elements preceding `j`, with `j` (`downTo`, `downTo_closed`, `downTo_self`, `downTo_other`); bearing them first is a natural labelling that bears `j` before everything not preceding it (`downTo_before`).
- *On every course.* With precedence decided, `i` precedes `j` exactly where `i` is born before `j` in every natural labelling (`prec_iff_every`, for `j` an element): Szpilrajn's extension theorem in Dushnik and Miller's intersection form, a finite order being the intersection of its linear extensions, read on growth. So what is born no later than `x` in every natural labelling is `x`'s past with `x` (`past_every_iff`, for `x` and `y` elements). This is the relation Stein's theorem singles out, here as the largest relation every course respects (`past_largest`), not by uniqueness under invariance. Invariance under relabelling, the counterpart of Stein's condition, admits Wüthrich and Callender's relation as well (`Diamond.kept_not_agreed`). With no decision, being born before on every course is exactly precedence not refuted (`every_iff_not_not`, by `every_not_not`).
- *On some course.* With precedence weakly decided, `i` is born before `j` in some natural labelling exactly where they differ and `j` does not precede `i` (`before_some_iff`, for elements, by `before_of_weakly`: bearing first everything not refuted to lie at or above `j`); two different unrelated elements are born in either order (`either_order`), so no order of birth of unrelated elements is common to all courses. Which elements have been born by a stage is a choice of course; how many is not.

### Law 6. The present at a stage

- *The maximal elements of what has been born.* What a labelling has born by stage `t`, with the precedence among it (`bornBy`); an element is maximal among what is born by `t` where it is born by then and nothing it precedes is (`MaximalBorn`), read as being in the present at that stage. This is the grammar's ending under the one class, `«亡»` under `«一»`, read on what has been born (`maximalBorn_iff_ends`).
- *Presents are antichains, and every antichain is one.* Two elements maximal among what is born by one stage are unrelated (`maximalBorn_unrelated`). Bearing first what a downward-closed test passes, the present after as many births as pass is the passing elements that precede nothing passing (`maximalBorn_front`). With precedence weakly decided, an antichain given by a Boolean test is exactly the present of the course bearing first the elements not refuted to lie at or below it (`antichain_present`, by `weakly_above`).
- *Present together.* With precedence weakly decided, two elements are in the present at some stage of some course exactly where neither precedes the other (`together_some_iff`). With precedence decided, at some stage of every course exactly where they have the same future, each preceding what the other does (`together_every_iff`, by `future_of_together_every`); with no decision, exactly where the same future holds doubly negated (`together_every_iff_not_not`, by `together_not_not`). Present together is not the root's co-presence, 俱有, mutual reach, which no two elements of a causal set have.
- *The present is a choice of course.* On the vee, `0` below `1` and `2`, the present after two births is exactly `1` on the labels as they are and exactly `2` with the births of `1` and `2` exchanged (`Vee.maximal_after_two_identity`, `Vee.maximal_after_two_swap`).

### Law 7. Presence of a condition when what it conditions is born

- *Presence at a birth.* `i` is present at `j`'s birth in a labelling where `i` precedes `j` and nothing `i` precedes is born before `j` (`PresentAt`); in a natural labelling, exactly where `i` precedes `j` and is maximal among what is born by `j`'s stage (`present_iff_maximalBorn`).
- *On some course: a link.* A condition present at a birth on some course is a link below what is born, conditioning it with nothing between (`link_of_present`, no decision; UE13's `CausalGrowth.links`). With precedence weakly decided, every link is: `i` is present at `j`'s birth in some natural labelling exactly where `i` is a link below `j` (`present_some_iff_link`, for `j` an element), the course bearing first the elements not refuted to lie at or below `j`.
- *On every course: the one link.* With no decision, `i` is present at `j`'s birth in every natural labelling exactly where `j` is the one element `i` is linked to (`present_every_iff_only_link`, by `only_link_of_present_every`, `present_every_of_only_link` and `above_of_only_not_not`). With precedence decided this is exactly where `j` is the least element above `i`, everything `i` precedes being `j` or above `j` (`LeastAbove`, `present_every_iff`); the least element above is a link (`link_of_least`) and, with precedence decided, the one link (`least_iff_only_link`, by UE13's `ordered_reach_links_iff` and `reach_links_le`). With no decision, presence on every course gives that everything `i` precedes is at or above `j` only doubly negated (`above_of_every_not_not`).
- *Witnesses on the vee* (`Vee.vee`, `Vee.ordered`): `0` is not present at `2`'s birth on the labels as they are, `1` being born between (`Vee.not_present_identity`), and is present with the births of `1` and `2` exchanged (`Vee.natural_swap`, `Vee.present_swap`, with `swap12`, `swap12_three` and `below_three`); so `0` is a link below `2` (`Vee.link`) and `2` is not the least element above `0` (`Vee.not_least`).

### Law 8. What the decisions are (calibrations)

Asked of every causal set at once, each decision a law uses is exactly a principle of logic, and the principle says what deciding is in the unfolding. Each is a lemma of its law, stated with that meaning.
- *Excluded middle, for the laws on every course in their decided forms.* Born before on every course gives precedence (`every_iff_em`, by `every_needs_decision` on two elements whose one precedence is a proposition: `pairIf`, `pairIf_ordered`), and so does `past_every_iff` from left to right (`past_every_iff_em`, by `past_every_needs_decision`); present together on every course gives the same future (`together_every_iff_em`, by `together_every_needs_decision` on `apartIf`, `apartIf_ordered`); presence on every course gives the least element above (`present_least_iff_em`, by `present_every_needs_decision` on `leastIf`, `leastIf_ordered`), and so does the one link (`only_link_least_iff_em`). For the unfolding: agreement across all courses settles that one element conditions another, or that two share their future, only up to double negation; asserting it is deciding whether it does.
- *Weak excluded middle, for the laws on some course.* `either_order` (`either_order_iff_weakly`, by `either_order_deMorgan` on `middleIf`, `middleIf_ordered`, `middleIf_not_two_zero`, `middleIf_not_zero_two`, and `weakly_of_deMorgan`), `before_some_iff` from right to left (`before_some_iff_weakly`), `together_some_iff` from right to left (`together_some_iff_weakly`, by `together_some_deMorgan`), `antichain_present` (`antichain_present_iff_weakly`, by `together_of_antichain`) and `present_some_iff_link` from right to left (`present_some_iff_weakly`, by `present_some_deMorgan` on `linkIf`, `linkIf_ordered`). For the unfolding: finding a course that bears two unrelated elements in either order, or shows a link present at a birth, is deciding, for each pair, only whether its precedence fails.

### Law 9. Wüthrich and Callender's diamond, and off it

Wüthrich and Callender (2017) show that Stein's theorem fails for a single causal set: on four elements, `a` below `b` and `c`, both below `d`, the relation taking each element to itself and to its past and `b` and `c` to each other is reflexive, transitive, nontrivial, not universal and kept by every automorphism, `b` and `c` being a pair with the same past and the same future. Here `0` is `a`, `1` and `2` are `b` and `c`, and `3` is `d` (`Diamond.diamond`, `Diamond.prec_below`, `Diamond.ordered`).
- *Their relation* (`Diamond.PastOrPair`, read `b` definite as of `a`; `Diamond.pastOrPair_refl`, `Diamond.pastOrPair_below`) is transitive (`Diamond.pastOrPair_trans`) and kept by every automorphism (`Diamond.pastOrPair_kept`, by the middle elements, those preceded and preceding: `Diamond.Middle`, `Diamond.middle_iff`, `Diamond.pair_of_middle`, `Diamond.middle_of_pair`, `Diamond.auto_middle`, `Diamond.auto_middle_back`, `Diamond.auto_inj`). It is neither the past read reflexively nor universal (`Diamond.pastOrPair_pair`).
- *It is not what every course has born by a birth*: `2` is definite as of `1` in it, while on the labels as they are `2` is born after `1` (`Diamond.kept_not_agreed`), and exchanging the births of `1` and `2` is a natural labelling (`Diamond.courses_disagree`, with `swap12_four` and `below_four`). By `past_every_iff`, what every course has born by a birth is the past.
- *On the diamond it is the past with the pair present together on every course*: on the diamond's elements, `PastOrPair a b` holds exactly where `b` precedes `a` or `a` and `b` are in the present together at some stage of every course (`Diamond.pastOrPair_iff_together`; with the join named, `PastOrTogether` and `Diamond.pastOrTogether_iff`), since `1` and `2` have the same future (`Diamond.future_one_two`) and no other two different elements of the diamond do.
- *Off the diamond the join need not be their relation.* Being present together on every course needs only the same future, and their pairs share the past as well. On four elements, `0` below `1` and `3`, `1` and `2` below `3` (`SameFuture.sameFuture`, `SameFuture.prec_below`, `SameFuture.ordered`), `1` and `2` have the same future (`SameFuture.future_one_two`) and so are present together on every course, while `0` precedes `1` and not `2` (`SameFuture.together_different_pasts`). There the past joined with being present together on every course relates `2` to `1` and `1` to `0` but not `2` to `0` (`SameFuture.pastOrTogether_not_trans`): it is not transitive, so not a relation of becoming in Stein's sense, while their relation, the past with its non-Hegelian pairs, is on these elements the past alone.

### Law 10. On the courses themselves

By `natural_iff_course`, what holds in every natural labelling of `c` holds on every course of the growth from the empty set, read through every relabelling of `c` onto the course's configuration at `c`'s size, and conversely; what holds in some natural labelling holds on some such course (`every_course_iff`, `some_course_iff`). The main laws are stated on the courses themselves, each with its law's decision: births of unrelated elements commute and only they (`rearranges_iff_course`); every stem with decided membership is what some course bears first (`stem_born_first_course`); born before on every course is precedence (`prec_iff_every_course`), what is born no later than an element on every course is its past with itself (`past_every_course`), the largest relation every course respects (`past_largest_course`), and with no decision born before on every course is precedence not refuted (`every_course_not_not`); born before on some course is differing and not being preceded, and two different unrelated elements are born in either order (`before_some_course`, `either_order_course`); every antichain given by a Boolean test is a present (`antichain_present_course`); two elements are present together on some course exactly where unrelated, on every course exactly where they have the same future, and with no decision exactly where that holds doubly negated (`together_some_course`, `together_every_course`, `together_every_course_not_not`); and a condition is present at a birth on some course exactly where it is a link, and on every course exactly where it is the one link (`present_some_course`, `present_every_course_only_link`). Each is its law composed with `natural_iff_course`. Their statements are on the growth's process (`Run process`), so the laws they use connect to the base the repository's connectivity check measures (§6).

## 3. What it shows, and what it does not

Shows:
- In one model of becoming in which it can be asked exactly, 01's open question has an answer for presence read as being among the maximal elements of what has been born: whether a condition is present when what it conditions is born varies with the course, holding on some course exactly at a link and on every course exactly at the one link.
- The same holds for being born before (on some course, differing and not being preceded; on every course, precedence) and for being present together (on some course, being unrelated; on every course, the same future). Every present is an antichain, and with precedence weakly decided every antichain given by a test is a present; no order of birth of two different unrelated elements is common to all courses.
- Each law's decision is exact: none for presence on every course read as the one link, weak decidedness for the laws on some course, decided precedence for the decided forms of the laws on every course; each calibrated decision is exactly its principle of logic when asked of every causal set (membership in `stem_born_first` and the decision of `past_largest` are not calibrated).
- The past is the largest relation every course respects. On the diamond, Wüthrich and Callender's relation is the past joined with being present together on every course; off it the join need not be their relation.

Does not show:
- anything about continuous time, the present of a center or of experience, or presence on the theory's other models (the World, processes in general);
- anything about transition probabilities, Bell causality, the continuum limit or infinite causal sets;
- that presence must be read as being among the maximal elements of what has been born: the other readings, being in the configuration, Dowker's birth and a maximal antichain of the whole configuration (Wüthrich and Callender's kinematic present), answer 01's question without varying with the course, and are stated here, not proved in Lean, beyond the growth holding its past (UE13's `step_holdsPast`);
- a uniqueness theorem of Stein's kind: the past is singled out as a maximum;
- which presence or course is better.

## 4. Readings

**[AI reading, D45] Presence in time.** The open entry asks whether a condition must still be there when what it conditions arises. On causal-set growth a condition, once born, stays: the past is held. Read as Dowker's present, the birth, a condition is past at every later birth; read as a maximal antichain of the configuration, it is never present with what it conditions. Read as being among the maximal elements of what has been born, the answer depends on the course: a condition can be present when what it conditions is born exactly where it conditions it directly, and must be exactly where that is the one thing it conditions directly. Being born before and being present together go the same way. Whatever every course keeps, or some course has, is defined from precedence (`every_congr`, `some_congr`); some of it goes beyond precedence itself, since being present together on every course relates elements neither of which precedes the other, and a relation read on one course, a present at a stage or a foliation, is a choice of course. So, given covariance, a premise of the model, the grammar's condition relation defines every relation of presence the growth keeps, and its lack of a primitive relation of presence in time loses no covariant content here; the entry stays open for continuous time, for the present of a center and for growths whose courses are not all equal.

**[AI reading, D45] Stein, Wüthrich and Callender, Dowker.** Wüthrich and Callender find Stein's theorem false for a single causal set, since invariance under the automorphisms of one configuration admits a relation beyond the past. They grant a local becoming defined from the causal order and ask instead about a global becoming, in which only the number of elements born is determinate. `past_every_iff` is that local becoming read on courses, the largest relation every course respects; it agrees with them and leaves their negative result standing. On the diamond their relation adds to the past being present together on every course (`Diamond.pastOrPair_iff_together`); elsewhere their pairs share the past as well, and the join with being present together need not be their relation (on the vee it is, the pair there sharing past and future). What has been born by a stage is a stem, and every stem with decided membership is what some course has born first, in as many births as it has elements (law 4): this states in general what their example of two candidates shows. Dowker reads the birth of each atom as the passage of time: the present is a birth, not a collection of atoms, what has been born is past, and births of unrelated atoms have no physical order. Law 5's `either_order` states the last of these on the growth, and the vee's two presents after two births agree that a present read at a stage is a choice of course. Her present is not law 6's: read her way, a condition is past at every later birth, on every course.

**[AI reading, D45] The continuation condition's future.** The condition reads: for anything, its continuation into the future has significance ([07 §3](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/07-SIGNIFICANCE.md#condition)). In the grammar continuation is continuation under a class; on the growth, under the one class it is precedence, and under a finer class it is part of precedence, read on precedence or on links (UE13's `Finer.continues`, `Finer.loses`). Each is defined from precedence and the class, never from a course, so on a covariant growth the condition's scope is the same on every course for every class given on the elements; a class given on the order of births, the first born alone say, is itself a choice of course. Read instead as what comes after a present stage, "the future" is a choice of course (`Vee.maximal_after_two_identity`, `Vee.maximal_after_two_swap`), and a significance read on what is present at a stage, a now, is a choice of course. This bears on scope, not on order: which continuation is better stays with calling widening better (C).

## 5. Standing

Near-definitional laws on linear extensions of a finite order, two lemmas (the stable partition, and tables of a test), witnesses, and calibrations that are lemmas of their laws. Known in order theory: the linear extensions of a finite order are connected by exchanging adjacent incomparable elements, Mazurkiewicz's traces, here an instance of the core's theorem; a finite order is the intersection of its linear extensions (Szpilrajn 1930; Dushnik and Miller 1941); the prefixes of linear extensions are the down-sets; the maximal elements of a finite down-set form an antichain, and every antichain is the set of maximal elements of its down-closure. The bounds on presence at a birth (a covering pair; the one upper cover) and on being present together (incomparable; the same strict up-set) are elementary consequences. In causal set theory, natural labellings as linear extensions, links, partial stems and their correspondence with antichains are Rideout and Sorkin's; stem sets through the first elements of natural labellings are Brightwell and others'; and the order of births of unrelated elements as gauge is theirs and Wüthrich and Callender's. So law 2, law 4 and the correspondence of stems and antichains (law 6) restate known results. The bounds of laws 6 and 7 are not stated in these sources, as the review found reading them and part of Rideout's thesis; Wüthrich and Callender's co-presentness of non-Hegelian pairs is the nearest, and it is narrower than being present together on every course. New is the reading: these as the covariant content of presence on the growth, the answer to 01's open entry in this model for one reading of presence, the exact decisions, and Wüthrich and Callender's relation on the diamond as the past joined with being present together on every course.
