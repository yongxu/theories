## 1. His view, at its strongest

Ren's two messages hold seven claims that should be kept apart, because they stand or fall separately.

**(a) Evaluation is a kind of dynamics.** A judgment of right or wrong is a higher-order pattern: a formation that reads formations. It is made of the same stuff as what it reads (couplings, readings, selections), formed by the history that formed its bearer: environment, past environment, the body selection built. There is no second kind of fact beside the dynamics for it to track.

**(b) Evaluation acts.** These patterns are not epiphenomenal. A judgment held is a selection; acted on, it changes what forms next ("cascading changes, that leads to a future that becomes"). So evaluations are real in what they do, and are themselves selected by what they lead to.

**(c) Ethics needs no ranking.** The unit of the ethics is not an order over courses. Once an order is made the unit, "which order is right?" returns, and that is the dualist question again. What there is instead: systems within systems responding to systems, and from some of those responses, from human or AI standpoints, right and wrong emerge, and "seeing the way" emerges.

**(d) Intersubjective ethics is a formation.** The shared moral reality of a community (norms, codes, "seeing the way" together) is an interconnected network formed between formations, within becoming. It is real and it is formed; neither word cancels the other.

**(e) Widening and narrowing are within becoming.** "More or less future" are descriptions of how continuation goes, not grades conferred from outside. "There is just is; no should beyond the becoming."

**(f) Order and method are patterns that change.** No absolute order, no absolute ranking, no better or worse as such. Choice emerges from the situation.

**(g) The account's scope.** The theory covers "what makes sense within all the situations, but not necessarily sufficient": it gives what can be made intelligible across situations, and does not claim to settle them.

Behind these is a stance on selves (message 1): no permanent self; life that does not go on toward more future ceases, and there is nothing right or wrong in that; ethics is a "useful way" of holding the condition, which in the account is C.

**Put as precisely as I can.** The account has, since 07 §1 and 08 §1, placed evaluation inside the unfolding: "positions, regions and standards form within the unfolding; none outside it owns value" **[shown in the text]**. What Ren adds is a change of grammatical mood for the whole ethics. The present text has a descriptive layer (laws) and a normative layer (two premises, "warrant open", hypotheses wherever used). Ren's view says there is no normative layer to warrant: there are only evaluators, formed, each reading through its couplings, each holding an order or a scope, each selecting by it, and each then selected by what it brought about. The account's job toward ethics is therefore the same as its job toward selection in chapter 3: say how such patterns form, what they read and miss, how they couple into shared codes, how they lock, and what they lead to. Scope and order stop being premises of the account and become *properties some formations have*: the continuation condition describes the widest standpoint (one that counts every continuation), C describes an order monotone in admission. Whether anything holds them, and how what holds them fares, are questions about the unfolding. "For life, the way is 生生" is then not a ranking offered under premises but a report: this is the stance of those whose line runs through life, seen from inside it. The theory can show what that stance reads and misses. It cannot, and on this view should not try to, show that one must take it.

**Where it sits.** Nearly every element has a home.

- *Spinoza* is closest in structure: good and evil are "modes of thinking" relative to us; "we do not desire a thing because we judge it good, we judge it good because we desire it" (E3P9S); conatus is continuation. *Deleuze's* reading of Spinoza, ethics (immanent evaluation of modes of existence) against morality (transcendent judgment), is Ren's "no should beyond the becoming" almost word for word.
- *Nietzsche's genealogy* gives (a): values have a history and serve life; "there are no moral phenomena, only moral interpretations of phenomena." But Nietzsche ranks (ascending against descending life). Ren refuses the ranking and keeps "for life".
- *Buddhist dependent arising with the two truths* gives the shape of the whole: conventionally (世俗谛) ethics is real, useful, binding within a community; ultimately (胜义谛) there is only dependent arising, no self, no standard standing apart. "Useful way" is 方便 (upāya). The tradition also carries the warning Ren needs: Nāgārjuna's "emptiness wrongly seen destroys the dull-witted" (MMK 24.11); the nihilist reading of "no right or wrong" is the classic error, and the two-truths structure exists to block it.
- *Zhuangzi* gives (c) and (f): 是非 relative to standpoints (齐物论), 以道观之，物无贵贱 (秋水), and then a practice without a ranking, 因是, going along with what is so. 天地不仁 gives the cosmos with no verdict; 道法自然 the method (already in 06 and OPENING, read as "no commander outside").
- *Dewey* gives (g): valuation as an activity within a problematic situation, ends-in-view, no fixed ends, inquiry revisable. *Enactivism* (Varela, Weber, Di Paolo) gives (a) and (b) at the level of organisms: sense-making, norms enacted by an autonomous system's own viability, with no norm beyond the system. Note that enactivism keeps an intrinsic norm *within* each living system, which Ren's "no should beyond the becoming" allows.
- *Expressivism and quasi-realism* (Blackburn, Gibbard) give the semantics: moral talk expresses attitudes formed in creatures like us, and earns the right to "true" and "right" from inside the practice, with no external fact. *Street's* Humean constructivism (already named in the 10-30 record) and her Darwinian dilemma give the debunking half of message 1. *Mackie's* error theory is what message 1 sounds like when "there is no right or wrong" is said flat; message 2 moves it to quasi-realism.
- *Luhmann* gives (d) and the "higher order": morality as a code within communication, ethics as second-order observation of it, no observer outside the system, and the system's own description of itself included in what it describes.
- *Evolutionary ethics* (Ruse, Joyce) gives the genealogical part of message 1 and shares its hazard: a debunking argument that proves too much debunks the debunker's standards as well.

**What is not already there.** Not the stance. What no neighbour has is the stance joined to a formal ledger in which evaluation is a free slot, with proofs of what the represented dynamics do and do not fix (`ReadsOnlyDynamics.not_entails`, `ReadsNoOrder.not_entails`), of what every formed order that counts everything shares (`Weighting.widens_iff_every_positive`), of what self-concern alone ranks with no further premise (`Flow.coupled_ending_ranked_below`), and of how a rule read from its own share locks (02 §9). That lets Ren's view be more than a stance: evaluators can be treated as selectors and the chapter-3 and chapter-2 laws applied to them. The other thing not already there is the setting: standpoints of any substrate, human and AI, in one account. Whether either is *new* by 08 §8's standard (a theorem a specialist would not foresee) is a separate question, and 08 §8's verdict so far is no.

## 2. Holes, and where it may be wrong

**2.1 Denying ranking while choosing.** Every reader, and the account itself in §5 and §7, chooses among courses. Is "no ranking" coherent? Yes, if stated carefully. A selection is a response, and 03 §11 shows that choices can be *read* as showing a strict partial order under conditions **[shown]**; but the reading is a formation's act, and no law says the order read off is "the" order. So the coherent form of (c) is: *there are orders, formed; there is no order that is the order.* That is what `AGENTS.md` already says ("better… is a relation formed by whatever compares"). The hole is not incoherence; it is cost. The account's ethics is at present ranking content: "C ranks it higher" (one page), T8 "compared by widening… ranked by what every valuer who counts everything shares", the direction of §7, practical judgment in §5. Drop ranking from the account and T8 loses its only refuter ("a mixed case argued better from every standpoint counting everything positively"), which is a ranking refuter. A thesis with no refuter is, by 08 §9's own rule, not a thesis. Ren's correction is right about what the ethics need not be, and silent about what then stands where T8 stood. (Section 4 below proposes a dynamical refuter.)

**2.2 "What makes sense within the situation" is an order under another name.** It is a standard, in the glossary's sense ("what something is read against, so that it can be met or missed"). Some responses make sense in a situation and others do not; that is an evaluation. What Ren has is not "no should" but *no should from outside the situation*. And the situation is drawn: 07 §4.1 says it in its own case, "admitting every closed region holding the center as a drawing is a substantive choice" **[shown in the text]**. Who draws the situation (which formations are in it, which losses are its) does the work the order used to do. This is the account's recurring finding: each time a premise is removed it reappears in a boundary, a marking or a class ("monotonicity at the standpoint is C's premise relocated", §4.4; "the theorem moves the whole question into the class", §4.7) **[shown]**. Ren's move relocates it into "the situation". It does not vanish. **[argued]** This is not fatal; it means the account must say, each time, who drew the situation and what the drawing leaves out, as it already asks of every analytic split.

**2.3 Can a theory that is itself "a pattern that changes" be wrong?** Yes, and the account already has the distinction that saves it. Laws are theorems under hypotheses; they do not change. What changes is which hypotheses actual systems meet, and the readings and theses laid over the laws. 08 §1's investigation "proceeds in an order that can be revised"; 08 §10's law "holds of every relation its hypotheses admit". So "order and method are patterns that change" is true of the account's *method and readings* and false of its *theorems*, and Ren should not let the first dissolve the second. Truth under monism is also already in the account: a reading is kept or refuted by what it reads (the reading loop, 03 §12), and a frozen prediction can fail (T1 failed in four). A pattern can be wrong by the account's own measure. The self-reference is Neurath's boat and Luhmann's autology; it is consistent. **[argued]**

**2.4 Does "no should" erase the losses of whoever bears them?** This is the serious one. Separate two things the dynamics keep apart. That the dweller ends (`Clearing`), that the bed dies under fifty, that a lock outlives a growing loss: these are *facts of the dynamics*, and monism does not touch them **[shown]**. That the loss *counts* is not a fact of the dynamics (`ReadsOnlyDynamics.not_entails`) **[shown]**. Ren's view removes the external verdict, not the loss. And it does not even remove the counting: the bearer's own standpoint counts its own ending (self-significance; "a self acting for its own continuation presupposes that some continuation has significance", §4). What remains open is whether *another* standpoint counts it, and under Ren's view that is a formation: coupling, sympathy, an installed scope (T4). The continuation condition then reads as a description of one standpoint, the one that counts every continuation; the step of invariance as what a standpoint does when it stops privileging its own drawing, which `boundaryInvariant_iff_conditional` already states as a conditional, not a derivation **[shown]**. So the formal content survives and the modal word changes. The residual hole: losses with no bearer who is a center (the bed as a formation but no standpoint; the generations that "occur nowhere on the course taken", §4.7). Under Ren's view these count only where something forms that counts them. The account must say that plainly, and it half does (T8: "no verdict binding every possible mind"). The danger is a reader's inference from "no right or wrong" to "nothing is lost". The text must keep the loss as fact and move only the verdict. **[argued]**

**2.5 "More or less future" is a measure, of what, from where.** Widening is inclusion of what is admitted at each step: a strict partial order, no number, no premise **[shown]**. But it is relative to a frame (which formations are listed, what counts as admission) and the frame is drawn by a formation. Worse for the phrase "more future": 07 §2 and the Open list show that what widening is read *on* decides which course widens which. On causal-set growth, read on what can be born next, the course in which nothing continues widens every other; read on what continues, the chain does; "no strict order contains both widenings" **[shown, on witnesses]**. So the dynamics give no one "more future" relation; they give several, which disagree, and C ranks no two courses from the empty set when both are read together. Ren's "more or less future" smuggles a scalar and a single direction that the account has proved, on witnesses, do not exist. The account's "widening", deliberately not a scalar and still frame-relative, is the honest word. And mixed cases, which is most of life, need a measure that is a further premise ("Ordering a mixed part needs a measure", §2). **[shown]**

**2.6 "Systems reacting to systems": does it explain anything?** As a stance it admits every outcome, and by the account's own rule (08 §10: a root law forbids no relation; content comes through hypotheses) it explains nothing alone. It does work only when joined to hypotheses about *which* couplings an evaluator has, what it reads, what lies beyond its closure (03), when its rule locks (02 §9), when codes form between evaluators (05). The account has all of these as laws about selectors. Reading evaluators as selectors, which 07 §1 and the glossary's "ranking" entry already do ("acted on it selects… read from the share it raises it can lock"), turns Ren's view into a research programme with content: the dynamics of evaluation. Then it forbids things (section 4). Without that join, "just dynamics" is as empty as "just relations". **[argued]**

**2.7 "Useful" hides a good, and selection does not give widening.** The coordinator's question "useful for what?" stands unanswered. Ren's best reply is selectional, not evaluative: continuation is not a good, it is the condition of there being anyone to ask; judgments that do not keep their bearers going stop being held. But the account's own results break the easy version of "if life doesn't want more future it ceases". Things persist by narrowing: the clearer persists and ends the dweller; a locked rule persists while a loss it does not read grows (02); an optimum drains what lies beyond what couples in (05) **[shown, on models]**. Only under gated renewal and joint excess does narrowing end the narrower (05 §joint excess) **[shown, on models]**. So "persistence requires widening" is false as a law and true as a hypothesis under stated couplings, which is exactly where Ren's view could become testable (section 4). **[argued]**

**2.8 The flat negative.** "There is nothing right or wrong about it" (message 1) is a verdict from nowhere, the same kind of claim as a universal ethics with the sign reversed; the coordinator was right. Message 2 half-retracts it ("from human, or your perspective, emerges right or wrong") but keeps "there is just is". Under Ren's own view the sentence should be: from no standpoint outside becoming is it right or wrong; from within, it is counted as those who count it count it. The 10-30 record already has astra making this point against the earlier message, and Ren did not take it up then either. **[argued]**

**2.9 The two 生生.** Message 1: "arise, generate, continue, die, the cycle of life"; ethics' 生生 in the glossary: "life in the whole and its continuing generation", compared by widening, which excludes degeneration. If 生生 names the cycle including ending, it is the whole dynamics, and "for life, the way is 生生" says "the way is whatever happens", which is empty. If it names the direction, it is not the cycle. His words blur what the glossary keeps apart (`«生生»` renewal without end; 生成 generation; the ethics' 生生). This needs his decision (section 6). **[argued]**

**2.10 Standpoints as units.** "No permanent self" against `sig : Pos → Nat → F → Prop`, where a standpoint `p` persists across every step `t`: the free-significance layer builds a persisting standpoint into its type **[shown in the Lean]**. The root does not: there a self is a line under a class (01 §2; 07 §4.5–4.7), and "which occurrences are centers" is open. So the formal side of "no permanent self" is already in the root, and the free `sig` with a persistent `Pos` is the older layer, a fact about the representation. Nothing to prove; something to read differently (section 3). **[shown]**

## 3. What it changes in the account as it stands

Nothing proved changes. What changes is what the prose says the proofs are for, and several lines are, on Ren's view read honestly, "supposed but not really". Superseded text goes to history, not away.

**The status of the two premises.** One page, "The ethics": "The ethics adds two premises, neither asserted, warrant open, each a hypothesis wherever used"; 07 Bases: "Evaluation enters through the ethics' two premises"; `AGENTS.md`: "Two unasserted premises carry it". Under Ren's view these are not premises *of the account*; they are the content of one standpoint (the widest counting) and one kind of order (monotone in admission), which some formations hold. "Warrant open" supposes a warrant is the kind of thing being sought; that is the residue of the universal-metaethics line (10-10's one axiom, then "stated, not asserted", then 10-30's "for life"). The Lean already fits the new reading better than the prose: §4.4 reads C "as a condition an order can meet or fail" (`Comparison` is a predicate on `Better`), and `condition_reads_no_order` shows the condition itself reads no order and holds on a web whatever order is read **[shown]**. The 07 Open item "Whether a universal practical warrant exists" and 08's "Philosophical warrant" list ("a reason for C", "the step of invariance") would move from goals to questions the ethics does not wait on, which astra proposed on 10-30 and the text half-adopted ("does not wait on it"). 08 §12's ranked open items I did not read and would need the same pass.

**"C ranks it higher"** (one page, the mussel-bed paragraph). This personifies a premise as a ranker. What is proved is `Weighting.widens_iff_every_positive`: every order counting every listed item positively ranks the wider course higher **[shown]**. T5 already has the honest form ("The order is shared by every valuer who counts everything positively"). The one page's sentence should take it: *every valuer who counts everything, the bed included, ranks thirty above fifty; one who does not count the bed need not.* That is a description of formed orders, and it says where disagreement can fall. Same for the one page's row heading "Which continuations count, and what should be done?", whose "should" is the account's, and for T8's "the way is", which states a stance and should say so in its first words.

**"Significance."** Ren called it a dualist word on 10-24, and the 10-24 record reads the condition's source as "to keep the work going, toward continuation, not as a further value predicate". The glossary gives `sig` as a proposition, not a value, and 07 §4.5 ties it to the own-closure reading ("lying within what a center continues with along a relation"). That reading is the one Ren's view wants: significance as a relation of continuation from a center, not a value conferred. The free `sig` remains as what the representation leaves open, "a fact about the representation" (§1) **[shown]**. Names are renamed only where they state a wrong meaning; `sig` states none. The prose gloss does: "its continuation into the future has significance" (one page, glossary, 07 §3) would read, under Ren, "is counted from that standpoint". Section 4 proposes the word.

**"Better."** `AGENTS.md` already has it right. 07 uses "better" freely as C's content, which is fine wherever the sentence names whose order it is; the sentences that do not ("widening is better") are the ones to touch.

**The step of invariance.** Framed now by Gewirth and Parfit as a step to be *warranted* (§4.2, "the strongest argument for the step… the strongest case against"). Under Ren's view it is a description of a standpoint widening its drawing, and the Lean already says only that: "logically A ⇔ P ∧ (P → A), given A → P, which locates the contested step and derives nothing beyond it" (§4.1) **[shown]**. Whether any formation takes the step is a question about formations (mutual dependence gives it: where every center's closure is the whole, the de se presupposition is the condition with no step, `ownClosure_unrestricted_of_whole`) **[shown]**. The warrant framing moves to history; the laws stay.

**"For life" (T8).** Fits Ren's view exactly: it says whose way it is. Keep. What does not fit is the rest of the sentence, "compared by widening… ranked by what every valuer who counts everything shares", if Ren means no ranking; see 2.1 and section 6.

**生生.** The glossary's three senses stay; T8 must say which it names (2.9).

**Self and standpoint.** Read `Pos` as a line under a class wherever the prose speaks of a standpoint over time; the root's treatment (§4.5–4.7) is the one that fits, and "which formations hold standpoints" stays open as it is.

**What already fits and stays.** 08 §1 whole ("fact and value are each drawn within the unfolding, along relations, together with what they leave out"; "Nonduality is a requirement of method; it proves neither the ontology nor an ethical necessity"); 07 §1's four questions (influence, recognition, warrant, followed); the glossary's "ranking" entry, which already describes a formed order that selects and can lock; 07 §6, alignment as what a selection reaches, with no premise; 03 §11; 07 §2, how courses differ with no premise; the premise ledger; "no verdict binding every possible mind"; 08 §8's verdict that none of this is new mathematics; the one page's centre sentence, "a selection can succeed while what it cannot read is lost, and the laws do not say whether that loss counts", which is Ren's view in one line.

**Lean results unchanged, read differently.**
- `ReadsOnlyDynamics.not_entails`, `ReadsNoOrder.not_entails`: from "the dynamics cannot ground the ethics, so the warrant is open" to "the representation leaves the evaluative slot to the evaluator, as evaluation is the evaluator's formation".
- `bounded_rival`: from "a perspectivist consistently denies the condition" to "every bounded standpoint is a consistent standpoint", the normal case.
- `Weighting.widens_iff_every_positive`, `mixed_either_way`: from "C is what positive valuers share" to an invariant of any population of evaluators that count everything: their agreement is exactly widening and their disagreement falls only on mixed pairs. That is a law about formed orders, which is what Ren asked for.
- `Weighting.Restraint` (the prisoner's dilemma read as a menu): from "a witness against C" to "a case where several choosers' formed orders and the widening order part": systems reacting to systems.
- `Flow.coupled_ending_ranked_below`: from "self-concern ranks one case with no C" to "how an order emerges from a situation under gated renewal".
- Closed capture and the lock (02 §9), applied to moral codes: a code read from the approval it raises cannot drop a norm whose costs fall on those it disfavours. Unchanged as a theorem; new as a reading of intersubjective ethics.

## 4. Going further

**What the view forbids** once joined to the account's hypotheses (each **[shown]** as a law about selectors; their application to evaluators is **[argued]**):

1. No evaluator counts a loss that does not couple into it (03): a community's moral code cannot count what no member couples to. Told nothing of the bed, the fishers kept it in no season; this is T4's content, already frozen and run.
2. A code whose keeping is selected by the share it raises locks and outlives a growing loss it does not read (02 §9), under the model's hypotheses. For moral systems: a norm maintained by the approval it produces is not dropped on the evidence of costs borne only by non-carriers. This is T7's shape on norms rather than metrics, and the one T7 case ran against trained judges.
3. "Persistence requires widening" is false in general and true under stated couplings (05's gated renewal and joint excess). That is Ren's "if life doesn't want more future it ceases" put where it can fail.

**What it predicts that could fail**, named and not designed under the hold: (i) under coupling into a renewal they depend on, populations holding admission-monotone orders outlast populations holding narrower ones, and *not* otherwise (the coordinator's opening, with its negative half added); (ii) moral lock-in: in institutional records, norms whose costs fall only on those the norm disfavours are dropped at lower rates than norms whose costs reach carriers, holding the evidence fixed; (iii) frame-dependence of "more future": human and AI valuers given the same two courses under two readings of what is admitted reverse their verdicts in the predicted direction. Each would be a T-thesis with a refuter, and (ii) and (iii) replace the ranking refuter T8 loses.

**What could be proved** (my **[conjecture]** on tractability): (a) a general form of the witness in 07 Open: for any two readings of admission that are not nested, there exist courses that widen in opposite directions, so no frame-free "more future" relation exists; the witnesses suggest it is short. (b) The step of invariance as a formation: state a widening relation on standpoints' regions and prove under which couplings it reaches the whole; `ownClosure_unrestricted_iff` and mutual dependence are most of it. (c) A second-order lock: a selector over evaluators whose rule is read from the evaluators' shares, with 02 §9's lock lifted one level; this is Luhmann's point as a theorem. (d) "Makes sense within the situation" as a frame-relative order, with the frame's drawing shown to carry the content, closing 2.2 formally.

## 5. The coordinator's first reply

**Where it was wrong.**

- "Ranking courses still needs an order, and the dynamics still don't supply one. That's proved." What is proved is narrower and, in the text's own words, "immediate, since 'better' is free: it shows that a frame fixes no order" (§4). It does not prove that the account must rank. The reply slid from the conditional (if you rank, you need an order premise) to the account ranking. Ren's correction hits this exactly.
- "So C stays a premise for any ranking." False as stated. A ranking needs *an* order; C is one condition among orders, and the account proves that self-concern, graded, priority and sufficiency orders rank without meeting it (§4.4) **[shown]**.
- It half-saw Ren's view ("C is an order that some formations hold") and then reverted to "C stays a premise… what changes is where it sits". Those are two different accounts; the reply holds both.
- "'Just dynamics' is a reduction (dynamics real, ethics illusion), and that's a dualism too." Uncharitable to the "just": Ren denies a second layer, he does not assert one layer real and the other illusory. Against message 1's flat "there is no right or wrong" the point stands; against the view it does not.

**Where it was right, and Ren's correction passed over it.** Selection gives continuation, not widening (2.7). "Useful for what?" (2.7). Whose losses count when nothing counts them (2.4). "Progression" brings direction back. "No right or wrong", flat, is a verdict from nowhere (2.8). None of these was answered in message 2.

**Where Ren's correction was itself too quick.**

- "It doesn't need to be ranking/comparison" is right about the ethics' unit and silent about T8, §5 and §7, which are ranking content, and about T8's refuter, which is a ranking refuter. Dropping ranking without putting a dynamical thesis in its place leaves the ethics as stance plus description, with nothing the account says to the fishers about the bed. He may want exactly that; he should see the cost first.
- "There is just is, no should beyond the becoming": the second clause is right, the first drops the shoulds inside, which the view itself says are formed and real. Zhuangzi's 齐物 did not stop him from 因是.
- "More or less future": a scalar and a direction the account has, on witnesses, shown not to exist as one relation (2.5).
- "Choice are emergent from the situation": true, and incomplete without his own point that a choice is a formation that changes the situation, and without the account's addition, that a situation's selections cannot read what lies beyond their couplings, which is a reason to look if one counts what lies there. Said alone, it reads as quietism.

## 6. Three points for Ren to decide

1. **Does the account still rank courses at all?** Either C stays as the order of one stance, with T8 keeping its ranking refuter and every "C ranks" sentence rewritten as "every valuer who counts everything ranks"; or the ethics goes descriptive, orders and scopes as formations, and T8 becomes a thesis about how widening-holding stances fare under stated couplings, with a new refuter named (section 4's (i) or (ii)). The second is his view read honestly; it costs the account its only present word to the fishers.

2. **Do "premise" and "warrant open" give way to "the widest standpoint, which some formations hold"?** This retires the universal-warrant goal as a goal (astra's 10-30 version, kept as a question), moves the Gewirth–Parfit framing of the step to history, and leaves every theorem where it is. It also commits the text to saying, each time, who drew the situation and what the drawing leaves out, since the premise reappears there (2.2).

3. **Which 生生 does T8 name?** The cycle that includes ending, in which case T8 says nothing, or the direction compared by widening, in which case message 1's "cycle of life" is not the ethics' 生生. The glossary already keeps them apart; his words do not, and the account cannot have both.
