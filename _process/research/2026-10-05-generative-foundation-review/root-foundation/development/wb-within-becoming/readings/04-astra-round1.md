**1. His view, at its strongest.**

**Interpretation, argued.** Ren is moving away from a picture in which the world first unfolds without value and then receives an ethical instruction. Living, feeling, discriminating, judging, objecting and coordinating are differentiated activities within that unfolding. Evaluation changes what happens: a prohibition redirects conduct; an expectation reorganizes cooperation; an institution reproduces particular sensitivities and silences others. The ethical activity and the conditions it evaluates change together.

Several distinct claims belong here.

First, this is a claim about **where evaluation exists**, not yet about whether any evaluation is correct. Right and wrong can be real features of practices without existing independently of all practices. “Higher order” should mean that some activities respond to, organize or revise other activities—including their own evaluative habits. It must not imply superior worth or a separate ontological level.

Second, action need not begin by representing alternative courses and ordering them. Care can be a cultivated responsiveness; a promise can establish a requirement; a procedure can allocate turns; a confrontation can change the available alternatives. Ranking sometimes occurs within these processes. It need not constitute them.

Third, intersubjective ethics is itself a formation. Participants do not necessarily arrive with finished interests and negotiate a common score. Through encounter they acquire commitments, identities and ways of perceiving what matters. An ethical institution can therefore have a history and causal organization without being either an illusion or an agreement between permanently bounded selves.

Fourth, “more or less future” concerns changes in what can happen within becoming. It names no reservoir outside the world. Its strongest interpretation is presently plural: opportunities for continuation, capacities for repair, possibilities for new living relations, and the losses particular changes impose. Whether those dimensions admit one comparison remains a question.

Fifth, methods and orders are revisable formations too. The account participates in what it describes. Its role would be to make situations intelligible, expose dependencies and omissions, and support responses whose adequacy remains answerable to consequences. “Not necessarily sufficient” acknowledges that a grammar of situations does not decide every response.

This has substantial predecessors. Spinoza’s *Ethics* III.9 locates judgments of goodness in striving and desire rather than treating goodness as their prior external command. The affinity is clear; Ren need not inherit Spinoza’s account of essence. [Spinoza, III.9](https://spinozaatlas.com/en/text/ethics.III.P9)

Dewey is particularly close: habits, purposes and intelligence form through biological and social interaction, and moral inquiry belongs within that activity. Nietzsche’s genealogy likewise makes evaluations themselves objects of historical investigation and criticism. Neither move licenses treating a value’s origin as its sufficient justification or refutation. [Dewey, *Human Nature and Conduct*, Preface and Conclusion II](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/41386/pg41386-images.html); [Nietzsche, *Genealogy*, Preface §6](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/52319/52319-h/52319-h.htm)

Enactivism supplies a close account of the intersubjective claim: De Jaegher and Di Paolo describe meaning changing through interaction that acquires organization of its own. Their insistence on preserving participants’ autonomy also warns against dissolving the affected person into “the whole.” [“Participatory Sense-Making”](https://hannedejaegher.net/wp-content/uploads/2009/11/dejaegherdipaolo07participatorysensemaking.pdf)

Madhyamaka offers a useful discipline rather than a warrant for monism. Dependent designation and the two truths resist both independently existing essences and the erasure of conventional distinctions. An ultimate substance called “becoming” would itself require criticism. Nāgārjuna’s chapter 24 does not infer from emptiness that suffering, practice or ordinary distinctions disappear. [《中論》〈觀四諦品〉](https://dlbs.liberal.ntu.edu.tw/BDLM/sutra/html/T30/T30n1564.htm)

Expressivism and quasi-realism are nearby where ethical language expresses commitments while retaining disagreement, reasoning and talk of error. Ren’s causal account alone does not decide that semantic question: explaining how judgments arise does not establish what their sentences mean. [Blackburn, “An Ethical Dialogue”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/think/article/an-ethical-dialogue/901508E3FC0E9F8F98802A30BAF575B1)

I find no demonstrated new philosophical thesis in this central move. The distinctive opportunity is a disciplined synthesis: relating the formation of standards, their consequences and their revision within one checked vocabulary. That opportunity is not yet an accomplished discovery.

**2. Holes and where it may be wrong.**

**Argued: rejecting necessary ranking is coherent; rejecting every distinction in practical adequacy would undermine the project.** Choosing does not logically entail carrying a fixed preference order. A lottery chooses; a rotating procedure chooses; habits respond differently as circumstances change. Chapter 03 §11 already states the relevant limitation: its strict choices admit an according strict order exactly where their shown relation has no cycle. Choices required only to be maximal force no positive comparison.

But “what makes sense” needs unpacking. It can mean causally explicable, intelligible to participants, effective for an aim, or justified. Cruel conduct can satisfy the first three while remaining open to ethical criticism. These meanings are not all orders, but moving between them silently would conceal the very evaluative commitment Ren wants exposed.

**Argued: being formed does not settle being warranted.** A reason and a threat are both causally effective. Their effects alone do not distinguish persuasion from coercion, understanding from conditioning, or correction from successful domination. Those distinctions can themselves be formed and contested; they do not need an external realm. The account nevertheless owes an explanation of how they work.

There are two coherent projects here: explaining ethical practices, and participating in their justification. Ren can pursue both. He cannot obtain the second merely by completing the first. Conversely, a naturalistic origin does not disqualify a justification. Arithmetic beliefs also have histories.

**Argued: “no should beyond becoming” does not entail “no universally applicable should.”** Ontological location and scope are different questions. A norm formed in some practice can claim application to people who reject it. Whether that claim succeeds requires argument. Monism alone neither establishes nor refutes it. The newer view should therefore reject an unexplained external source without pretending to have disproved every naturalistic moral realism or universal practical argument.

**Argued: continuation without wanting is possible; wanting without continuation is possible.** Ren’s sentence that life without wanting “more future” simply ceases is false if “want” means desire. It becomes nearly tautological if “want” means whatever mechanisms sustain continuation. A formation can also persist through restriction, exploitation or reduced diversity. The coordinator should not have answered “That’s true.”

Selection can explain the persistence of some dispositions under specified conditions. It supplies neither a general tendency toward wider possibilities nor the continuation condition’s scope. This hole is closable by separating desire, maintenance, reproduction and selection, then making bounded causal claims about their connections.

**Argued: “more future” is not yet a measure.** More duration, more possible branches, greater resilience, more distinct formations and more realized lives differ. Counting possibilities depends on individuation, temporal grain and the possibilities admitted by the model. Two infinite possibility sets need not differ in cardinality even when one strictly includes the other. And what is possible for one formation can close possibilities for another.

The repository already contains stronger warnings than the phrase suggests. Chapter 07 §2 distinguishes realized, admitted and reachable content; §4.4 retains the wider-menu witness where each chooser’s equilibrium payoff falls. Calling all these “more future” would lose information. A defensible use must identify what continues, under which class, across which alternatives and horizon.

**Argued: a changing theory can be wrong.** A belief’s becoming obsolete is different from its having misdescribed the situation it addressed. A prediction fixed yesterday can fail today even if today’s method changes. A method can improve because it exposes errors its predecessor concealed. None requires an observer outside becoming.

The danger is retrospective accommodation: every failure becomes another instance of change, while the theory keeps its claimed adequacy. Chapter 08 §9 correctly distinguishes the unrestricted language, which forbids nothing, from claims about specified relations and classes. Ren should preserve that distinction. “Everything is dynamics” explains little unless a proposed organization of dynamics excludes something.

**Argued: nonduality cannot make the bearer of a loss disappear.** A village’s poisoning remains a loss to its inhabitants even when actor and inhabitants share conditions. Their dependence does not make their experiences interchangeable. Chapter 07 §4.7 already identifies the further problem: causal reach follows occurrences, whereas destruction can prevent the future occurrences whose absence matters. The more completely an act destroys, the less downstream life there may be to include.

“No should beyond becoming” need erase none of this. “Nothing counts except where someone currently counts it” would be a much stronger—and unsupported—claim. An ethical commitment may include unrecognized losses, while recognition and response remain causally limited.

Finally, “systems reacting to systems” risks beginning with the bounded systems that the relational method promises to explain. “Cognitive structures” can also underdescribe bodily affect, material arrangements and institutional practice. These are repairable omissions if systems and cognitive functions are treated as identified organizations within relations, with their boundaries and explanatory limits stated.

**3. What it changes in the account as it stands.**

**Shown:** much already fits. `AGENTS.md`, “Method,” requires evaluation to differentiate within relations. Chapter 07 §1 says standards form within the unfolding and distinguishes their influence, recognition, warrant and being followed. Chapter 08 §1 explicitly says: **“‘Supplied’ is local: a law supplied to a model need not be external to reality.”** These passages should stay. They directly contradict the coordinator’s claim that the present distinction between dynamics and premises is necessarily leftover dualism.

The two premises need **clearer ownership and speech act**, not automatic abolition. Three assertions differ:

- A formal conclusion follows if the continuation condition and C hold.
- A particular formation holds commitments corresponding to those premises.
- We endorse those commitments and offer reasons for adopting them.

The first is conditional mathematics; the second requires correspondence and evidence; the third is ethical participation. A formation can hold an order without its order being the account’s endorsed order. A premise of an ethical argument can be endogenous to the world without becoming a mere description of someone’s conduct.

The front’s **“The ethics adds two premises, neither asserted, warrant open”** is therefore not inherently false. It becomes misleading when read as either an external addition to reality or a complete account of ethical activity. The continuation condition fixes scope; C gives an ordering commitment. Neither is necessary simply for evaluation to occur. [ONE-PAGE.md, “The ethics”](/Users/ren/theories/theories/becoming/ONE-PAGE.md:32)

Likewise, **“C ranks it higher”** in the mussel case is accurate conditional shorthand. The account should retain the inference: if thirty admits everything fifty admits and also the bed, every comparison satisfying C puts thirty above fifty. It should not imply that the fishers’ response must be produced by such a comparison. They might adopt a constraint, accept a claim, or establish a practice that protects the bed. Those responses still need investigation and, if endorsed, justification. [ONE-PAGE.md, paragraph following “The ethics”](/Users/ren/theories/theories/becoming/ONE-PAGE.md:34)

**Argued:** “significance” is not intrinsically dualist. Its current free predicate is an abstraction. The trouble arises when causal connection, being taken into account, and having reason to count are treated as interchangeable. Chapter 07 §4.7 already calls the own-closure substitution “a different proposition under the same name.” Relational connection does not by itself establish ethical relevance. Nor does a free formal parameter prove that relevance is ontologically detached.

“Warrant open” should remain wherever justification actually remains unresolved. Warrant can consist in situated arguments about suffering, undertakings, dependency and fair participation. It need not arrive from outside becoming. Ren’s proposal changes the possible account of warrant; it does not discharge the question.

A sharper textual problem occurs in 07 §3.3: **“‘should’ is what a center does once its represented boundary matches what it depends on.”** This conflates dependence, accurate representation and action. The paragraph later qualifies its claim, but the opening still promises too much. Accurate dependency knowledge need not motivate preservation; an agent may accept an ending. This is a candidate for correction independently of Ren’s change of view.

The step of invariance remains a substantive proposal. Chapter 07 §4.2 acknowledges that its argument is not proved sound. No permanent self does not imply that every defensible scope must include everything. Relationally formed boundaries can matter without being absolute: bodily organization, dependence, consent and undertakings distinguish situations. The absence of an ultimate boundary does not erase those reasons.

The most consequential tension is in T8. It says **“This stance starts from what acting presupposes”**, and then claims that the shared verdict **“binds those whose own line runs through life.”** That is stronger than offering a way from within life. P1’s inclusion in the continuation condition avoids an additional formal assumption once that condition is assumed; it does not establish that all acting actually presupposes P1 in the philosophically required sense. [07, §8, T8](/Users/ren/theories/theories/becoming/chapters/07-SIGNIFICANCE.md:778)

The thesis labels its binding claim as argued, so it is not presented as a theorem. Nevertheless, Ren’s current position appears to withdraw its proposed basis. “For life” should identify an offered orientation or its participants, not attribute one stance to life collectively. Life has no demonstrated single deliberating standpoint.

生生 should keep its glossary sense—life in the whole and its continuing generation—while its role is clarified. It cannot simultaneously mean every actual transformation and a direction that distinguishes support from damage. Nor should it collapse into persistence, novelty, widening, or the grammar’s `«生生»`. Ending a harmful organization may support life; that conclusion needs the relevant account of losses and commitments.

Centers and standpoints may remain useful local units. A fixed type in a theorem does not assert permanent selves. But the account must distinguish a supplied index from an explanation of how a standpoint forms. The own-closure reading is particularly limited: 07 §4.5 reports that on an acyclic relation its unrestricted condition forces centers to coincide. That is a constraint on this reading, not an argument that actual plurality dissolves.

**Shown in the inspected Lean:** `ReadsOnlyDynamics.not_entails` varies the untied significance predicate; `ReadsNoOrder.not_entails` varies the free comparison and uses an extension; `bounded_rival` supplies a bounded counterexample. They stand unchanged under Ren’s proposal. They do not show that a fuller dynamics cannot determine an evaluator’s judgments. Once a model connects judgments to its dynamics, arbitrary variation of the judgment parameter may cease to satisfy the model.

The conditional rankings, widening laws and closure counterexamples likewise retain their statements. Their philosophical role would become more modest: they expose what particular representations and commitments entail. No result needs deletion; any superseded interpretation belongs in history.

**4. Going further.**

**Argued:** the immediate opportunity is to explain differences among ways evaluations form. “Both are dynamics” leaves untouched the difference between a practice that discovers neglected losses and one that trains participants not to notice them.

The view should forbid at least these explanatory shortcuts: treating present endorsement as sufficient justification of the process producing it; inferring normative convergence merely from shared causal membership; and claiming a response distinguishes situations that its specified history and couplings cannot distinguish. The last already has formal support. The first two are methodological commitments requiring argument.

**Conjectures worth naming, without designing tests:** ethical judgments may depend on histories of coordination even when immediate descriptions and payoffs agree; widening an available range may reduce jointly realized continuation where choices change one another’s conditions; and a norm maintained through its beneficiaries’ selection may cease responding to losses that remain visible elsewhere. Each needs an independently specified mechanism and a possible failure. None follows merely from monism, and the third overlaps existing capture work rather than automatically constituting a new result.

A useful formal target is the **relation between endogenous revision and stable comparison**. Given a specified process that changes both responses and evaluative standards, characterize when its choices admit one order across occasions, when only situation-indexed orders suffice, and what information those representations discard. Chapter 03 §11 already provides the cycle criterion; simply restating it would be no new result. Further content would have to come from restrictions on how standards form and change, justified independently of the desired conclusion.

Another target is preservation of identifiable losses across changing classifications. Chapter 07 §2 already treats regrouping. Extending that work to revisions that alter both the evaluator and its categories could ask when an apparent ethical improvement is merely the disappearance of a bearer from the representation. This would connect Ren’s philosophical concern to an exact failure mode.

A possible stance sentence is: **“From within life, we offer 生生 as a direction, while keeping its commitments and the losses it cannot resolve explicit.”** Chinese: **“从生命之中，我们提出以生生为方向，同时明说这一方向所持的承诺，以及它尚不能裁决的损失。”**

Here “we” needs actual ownership. “Situated adequacy,” “emergent normativity,” “higher-order dynamics” and “more future” would all be new expressions, not harmless replacements for glossary terms. Each could conceal the evaluative criterion it appears to explain.

**5. The coordinator’s first reply.**

It correctly recognized that continuation does not yield widening, that scope exceeds self-maintenance, and that selection alone does not justify C. It also correctly preserved the formal non-entailments.

Its central mistake was to diagnose **analytic separation as ontological separation**. “Warrant open” does not imply an external source; a supplied hypothesis need not be external to the unfolding. Chapter 08 explicitly says so. The proposed repair was already substantially present.

“Ranking courses still needs an order” is true as a clarification of ranking, but misplaced as an answer to Ren’s account of evaluation. The Lean theorem concerns an independently variable order; it does not prove that ethical response must rank. Ren’s correction is decisive here.

Several other objections were too quick. A reductive “just” is not automatically dualism: reduction can be monist. “Useful” does not automatically conceal a supreme good: usefulness can describe a function without recommending it. “Progression” can mean temporal succession rather than improvement. And saying the bed counts only where something counts it moves from a claim about the formation of judgments to a stronger claim about ethical standing.

Ren’s correction is itself too quick if it treats ranking as necessarily reinstating absolute right and wrong. A comparison can be local, incomplete, revisable and causally formed. The correction should remove ranking’s monopoly, not prohibit it.

The reply also accepted too readily the claim linking survival to wanting continuation. And both parties risk treating “within becoming” as explanatory completion. It establishes a proposed location. The difficult work is showing the organization, its consequences, its omissions, and why a particular response deserves support.

The three points I think matter most for Ren to decide are:

1. **Is the account explaining ethics, offering an ethics, or doing both?** If both, its own commitments need a clear voice. Their formation does not replace their justification.
2. **Does “no ranking needed” mean ranking is optional, or that comparative justification is being abandoned?** The first is defensible and fits existing results. The second would relinquish much more than his messages establish.
3. **What remains of “for life”?** An offered, revisable commitment to 生生 can stand without a universal mandate. A verdict binding everyone whose actions touch life needs an argument that the current view does not supply.
