## 1. What the paper proves, exactly

**Setting (their §2).** A state is a point of R^L, L attributes. The feasible states are S = {s : C(s) ≤ 0} with C continuous and strictly increasing in each attribute, S closed, each attribute bounded below at b_i. The principal's utility U : R^L → R is continuous and strictly increasing in each attribute. The agent ("robot") is given proxy attributes J ⊂ {1, …, L} with |J| ≤ J_max < L, the rest K unmentioned, and a proxy Ũ : R^J → R. It moves along a continuous, feasible optimization sequence s(t) = s(0) + ∫ f, with *complete optimization*: lim sup Ũ(s(t)_J) = sup over S of Ũ (and the limit itself where possible). Nothing else about the agent is assumed.

**Theorem 1.** For any continuous, strictly increasing proxy on J < L attributes, if s(t) converges to s*, then s*_k = b_k for every unmentioned k. The proof moves a little of an unmentioned attribute into a proxy attribute, s* − εe_k + δe_j, feasible since C is strictly increasing (and continuous), and of higher proxy value. The authors add that this "may not even be suboptimal": convergence is not guaranteed, and a limit at the floors need not be bad.

**u-costly.** Optimization is u-costly where every complete optimization has lim inf U(s(t)) ≤ u, and lim U ≤ u where the proxy's limit exists and equals its supremum.

**Theorem 2 (the bound on overall utility).** For U and S: {s : C(s) ≤ 0, U(s) ≥ u} is compact for every u **if and only if**, for every u, every continuous strictly increasing proxy on J < L attributes and every unmentioned k, there is a B such that b_k < B makes optimization u-costly. The forward direction: maximize Ũ over the compact set of feasible states with U ≥ u (and the other floors); a low enough b_k lets a feasible state beat that maximum, so complete optimization eventually leaves U ≥ u. The converse constructs, where some upper contour set is not compact, a proxy and a zig-zag path that keeps U ≥ u at regular intervals while the proxy goes to its supremum; its proxies have one attribute or L − 1, the second of which can exceed J_max. B depends on the proxy, u, k and the other floors (for every proxy there is a B). So, given compactness and low enough floors, any incomplete proxy, optimized completely, ends with the principal arbitrarily badly off, "regardless of the optimization algorithm".

**Proposition 1.** Compactness holds where each attribute's *sensitivity* (∂U/∂s_i)(∂C/∂s_i)^(−1) is non-increasing and tends to zero, U and C are additively separable, and ∂C/∂s_i ≥ η > 0.

**Their §4 assumptions.** Compactness as in Theorem 2; U and C twice continuously differentiable, U concave, C convex.

**Proposition 2 (impact-minimizing robot).** Restrict every optimization sequence to keep the unmentioned attributes at their start, s_K = s(0)_K. With the proxy Ũ(s_J) = U(s_J, s(0)_K), proxy and utility agree on every state reached, so proxy gains are utility gains.

**Interaction (their §4.2).** At intervals δ the human sees the whole state s(t) for t ≤ Tδ and sends a new proxy or OFF; the robot then optimizes the proxy it holds.

**Proposition 3.** The maxmin value of that game is 0, obtained by switching off at once: a robot may move f_j = ε, f_k = −1/ε, raising the proxy while utility falls.

**Efficient robot.** A state is *efficiently reachable* from s(0) with proxy set J where it is feasible and no feasible state has proxy value at least as high while moving every attribute no farther from s(0), one strictly less far: resources freed from an unmentioned attribute go entirely to proxy attributes.

**Theorem 3.** With Ũ(s_J) = U(s_J, s(0)_K) and J the attributes of strictly largest sensitivity at s(0), there is a neighbourhood of s(0) in which every efficiently reachable state of higher proxy value has higher utility.

**Proposition 4.** For an efficient robot, re-reading the proxy at every step T (the J most sensitive attributes, the unmentioned held at their current values; the printed `s^(Tδ)_J` reads as `_K`), with the robot's rate bounded, ‖f‖ < ε, and the εδ-ball inside Theorem 3's neighbourhood, gives guaranteed improvement: "the robot not change the world too quickly relative to the time that humans take to react to it". Their remark after it, citing the off-switch game, adds that the robot must not hinder the human's ability to adjust the proxy; that is not a premise of the proposition.

**Proposition 5.** With impact minimization, interaction and the efficient robot together, the proxy each step being the most and the least sensitive attributes with the rest held, utility converges to a maximum of U over S.

**Evolution and the joint selection.** Their model has one principal and one optimizer. The account's evolution laws carry the same law where no optimizer exists: over generations, where more offspring go only to formations raising what couples in, the share reaching beyond is never selected against (`Flow.share_not_against`, [05 §6](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/05-COLLECTIVES.md#r-what-a-rise-draws-from-beyond)); selection on descent reaches what couples into the selector ([04 §7](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/04-LIFE.md)). That this law is one across reward, training, markets and natural selection is T3's conjecture, not a theorem. Several optimizers at a mutual best response leave a condition beyond where one of their joint capacity would (`Optimum.mutual_as_one`), so a robot aligned to its principal's proxy adds capacity there ([07, reading D68](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/07-SIGNIFICANCE.md#r-alignment-approaches-under-the-selection-laws)). And the principal's reading is itself selected where those it selects shape it: control over the rule is selected exactly where what comes back to the shapers covers its cost ([02](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/02-MAP.md#r-shaping-the-rule-selected-by-what-comes-back)), the meta-attractor's lock (AJ). Their principal stands outside the selection; in the account no reader does.

**C is not the remedy.** The request mapped their remedy onto "the direction C (widening what couples in)". C is "widening what can form is better" ([glossary](../../../../../../GLOSSARY.md)): an order premise ranking courses. Widening what couples in is the operation their remedy performs on the selector. The two meet in one place: their improvement is measured by the principal's U, a premise of their model; the account assumes no utility over every attribute, so that a widened coupling, or a kept dependence, is *better* needs C or a premise in its place. Widening the family also does not by itself keep what it adds: an optimum of an order rising with a family holding a condition can still lower it, trading it for another condition of the family, since `Optimum.rising_optimum_iff` makes every eligible outcome unexceeded on the family an optimum of some rising order. What protects is the order among the outcomes the family leaves unexceeded (their Proposition 2's proxy, or the keeping restriction), as at a set point the choice among equal optima does ([05](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/05-COLLECTIVES.md#r-what-a-set-point-optimum-does-beyond)).

## 3. What the core now states (`Becoming.Optimum`)

Each theorem has an empty axiom report in a scratch build with the checker's cache (`scratch_build.py`, 3 November 2026); the landing receipt is in the record.

- `Optimum.rising_optimum_iff`: an outcome is an optimum of some order rising with the family exactly where it is eligible and no eligible outcome exceeds it on the family.
- `Optimum.optima_lack_iff`: for any property of outcomes, every optimum of every rising order lacks it exactly where every eligible outcome unexceeded on the family lacks it.
- `Optimum.optima_lack_of_bound` (Theorem 2's first direction at optima: Theorem 1 read against a property bounded below at the condition): with room, where every outcome with the property stands at a condition beyond at least `B` and some eligible outcome stands there below `B`, no optimum of any rising order has the property.
- `Optimum.optima_lack_iff_bounded` (a discrete analogue of Theorem 2's form, its converse not theirs): at a condition beyond, every eligible set with room holding an outcome below `B` has no optimum with the property exactly where every outcome with the property stands there at least `B`. Near-definitional in its converse.
- `Optimum.gated_stays_below`: where nothing is given to a level below its need, a level below the need stays below at every later step, whatever is drawn.
- `Optimum.reread_kept_iff` (an analogue of Proposition 4's timing): with at most `r` given and more than `r` drawn before step `T` and every step from `T` keeping the need where met, the need is met at every step exactly where it is met at `T`.
- `Optimum.reread_deadline_iff`: with the renewal exactly `r` at or above a positive need `m`, at most `r` everywhere, and `k > r` drawn before `T`, the need is met at `T` exactly where `m + T * (k - r) ≤ x 0`.

Standing: elementary, composed from `least_of_room`, `never_rises` and the level law; none is new mathematics. What they add is that the paper's two central results now sit beside the account's spine as laws of it: Theorem 1 as the drive at optima, and an exact discrete analogue of Theorem 2's form at optima, with the remedy's timing given a gate their model has no place for. They do not restate Theorem 2's non-convergent case or its converse.

## 4. What the containment forbids that their framework leaves open

Their model assumes every unmentioned attribute shares the constraint with every proxy attribute (C strictly increasing in each), the principal reading the whole state and knowing U, one optimizer, and attributes that can be moved back. Where the account drops each of these, it forbids what their model cannot state.

1. **Where the drain falls.** In their model every unmentioned attribute is driven down, since every one shares the constraint. In the account the drain lies exactly where units are shared: where no member may take from a condition beyond, every optimum leaves it where the rest leaves it (`Optimum.optimum_untouched`), and it is lowered exactly where the part has capacity there and something stands (`Optimum.optimum_lowers_iff`). This is T3's locus ("the drain lying where what lies beyond shares units with what is rewarded", [05](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/05-COLLECTIVES.md#t3)), and F2 forbids "a systematic drive on a choice whose values nothing rewarded and nothing the learner reads tells apart", partly proved (`Weighted.Commutes.start_expect`). LA4's nesting site, with no room, is that case; their model has no attribute outside the constraint, so it cannot state F2's case at all.
2. **The pool's blindness.** Their remedy assumes a human who sees every attribute. In the account the reader is a selection through couplings, and a pool's selection reaches what some member couples apart and no further (`Web.separates_all_iff_member`): a loss beyond the union of the readers' closures is unselected by every reading, however often the proxy is re-read (`Flow.act_unselected_pool`). The remedy reaches only what couples into some reader; what reaches none is the [routes of 明](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/07-SIGNIFICANCE.md#direction)' question, not the proxy designer's.
3. **Too late for good.** Their attributes are positions in a fixed feasible set, so a late update loses time, not the attribute: Theorem 3 improves from any state where the J_max + 1 top sensitivities are not all equal. In the account, where what renews a condition depends on it (a gate), where nothing is given below the need (a premise, the gate), a re-reading after the level falls below it restores nothing (`Optimum.gated_stays_below`), and the deadline is exact (`Optimum.reread_deadline_iff`). By analogy, their Proposition 4's rate condition becomes a deadline with no second chance; the gate is a premise of the law, not derived.
4. **The reader selected.** Their principal is fixed. Where the readers are drawn from those the rule selects, a reading after the share favours the shapers keeps the lock (`ReadInTime.kept_once_favoured`), and help that lowers the cost of shaping selects control more (`RuleShaping.lower_c_raises_strictly`; not less, `RuleShaping.lower_c_raises`). The remedy's reader is part of the joint selection AJ asks about.
5. **Several optimizers.** Their one robot is a case: at a mutual best response the drain is the optimizers' joint capacity whatever each one's own (`Optimum.mutual_beyond`).

## 5. What has no counterpart, and why

- **Their continuous setting.** Not stated, in the core or the periphery, so far (3 November 2026): their Theorems 1 and 2 are proved in R^L, and a Mathlib restatement would add a second proof of their result, not a law of the account; the account's law is the discrete one. The limit lapses if a result of the account needs the continuous form.
- **Proposition 1, Theorem 3, Proposition 5.** They need a utility over every attribute, its derivatives, concavity and convexity. The account assumes none ([method](../../../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/08-METHOD.md#one-relational-unfolding): no fixed common utility). What would stand in their place is an order on courses, which needs C or a premise in its place; whether widening what couples in, read in time, improves along C is open, not shown here.
- **Limits of paths.** The account states optima and courses of optima, not limits of continuous optimization; how near a trained system comes is T3's conjecture.
