# Reader F: the sciences (Claude Opus 5.5)

Part of the [twenty-first review](../BECOMING-TWENTY-FIRST-REVIEW.md); an AI report, read-only, kept as evidence. Its claims were checked by the cross-checker ([H](H-CROSS-CHECK.md)) and, where the review leans on them, by the synthesizing reviewer.

## Verdict
The repository's own account of the sciences is mostly honest, and my independent reading largely confirms it. The theory restates the frameworks in Lean in discrete, whole-number, constructive form and gathers them under one list of premises (the "premise card"). In a few places it forbids something a single framework, taken alone, leaves open. Nowhere did I find a statement that the sciences taken together do not already make.

**Classification.**
- **Contained with a real "third part"** (the theory forbids something the framework leaves open). This covers no physical framework. It covers:
  - Price and Fisher, joined to the flow: `Reproduction.Flow.closed_lineage_lowered`. The forbidden statement is the Malthusian bound.
  - The proxy problem, through the optimum laws.
  - Hardin's commons, through gated joint excess.
  - Opportunity sets, weakly.
  - Requisite variety, only through a neighbouring law: `ReadingSchedule.spare_not_enough`, which is pinwheel scheduling.
- **Restatement:**
  - the first law of thermodynamics, as a stated correspondence in the periphery;
  - Landauer and Bennett, as a count only, with no kT ln 2;
  - Prigogine and Schrödinger, as "fed from beyond";
  - finite Poincaré recurrence;
  - constructor theory (UE12) and causal sets (UE13–15), whose laws are near-definitional;
  - drift and fixation;
  - deadbeat control and homeostasis (set points);
  - the free-energy principle (UE1), with its third part still open.
- **Debt** (what the framework states that the theory cannot yet):
  - The second law does not give the theory's least loss (`EntropyProduction.secondLaw_not_dissipative`). The one-unit loss comes from reading available work in whole units (`dissipative_of_irreversible`).
  - Free energy and expected free energy appear only in a periphery draft that has not landed.
  - Shannon quantities are absent.
  - Probability densities, continuous time, the causal-set growth probabilities and continuum limit, and Wright–Fisher with selection are absent.

**UE0's verdict.** "Not farther than the sciences together" is right. UE6's three search rounds support it: the root's laws are the known mathematics of simulation and lumping. "Farther than each framework alone" is worded too broadly; 08 §8 line 506, the UE6 verdict and board line 55 all repeat it. A third part exists in only 4 distinct rows of 24, 5 counting the evolution row. Each rests on an added premise (conservation, a threshold or renewal), and each forbidden statement is already known in ecology or bioeconomics. In thermodynamics, recurrence, control, the free-energy principle, constructor theory and causal sets the theory does not reach farther than the framework alone, and in several it states less. Comparing a model that carries conservation with an identity (Price's equation) or with a principle that calls itself unfalsifiable (the free-energy principle) makes "forbids more" cheap. A more exact verdict: farther than a few frameworks that lack conservation, renewal or thresholds, by joining them to the theory's premises; level with or behind the rest.

**Objections.**
- **Physicist.** The one-unit least loss is produced by whole-unit readings of available work. This is disclosed but not justified physically, and UE3 (deriving the least loss from stochastic thermodynamics) is not planned. A single step index is partly answered by interleavings and the causal-set reading, but there is no metric or continuum. There is no statistical mechanics.
- **Biologist.** The theory makes no new population-genetic prediction; 04 says so itself. Requirements are hard thresholds, and T1's tests are mixed.
- **Neuroscientist.** The free-energy containment is a restatement, which 08 §8 admits. There is no free energy in the core. Reading the Markov blanket as a coupling closure is an AI reading, with no representation theorem behind it.

**Best new prediction.** Within the sciences there is none. The best risky predictions are about trained AI systems. T3's horizon clause has a written design that has never been run. It predicts that a payment for each step a dependence is met keeps the dependent at every horizon, while a fixed total payment is outweighed once the horizon is long enough. For physics, the gain would be to derive the least loss from finite-time thermodynamics.

**On whether a collective can "always" move the world toward generation**, answered from this dimension. The contained sciences give necessary conditions and no-go results, not guarantees:
- nothing keeps widening in a finite, closed, balanced world (`whole_not_keepsWidening`);
- naming the step after which change stops requires LPO, a non-constructive omniscience principle (`World.lpo_of_finite_ends`);
- optima drain what lies beyond their couplings;
- joint excess ends gated renewal, however restrained each drawer is on its own.

They support "directionally, where coupling, margin and inflow hold", never "always".

### (4) The most promising genuine new constraint or prediction, if any, and what would test it.
Within the sciences proper, none: I agree with UE6 that nothing found is a statement no neighbouring field makes, and every science-facing third part is a known result. The most promising risky predictions concern trained AI systems, about which no field has tested these laws. First choice: T3's horizon clause. Proved for optima (Optimum.frontier_holds, Web.frontier_broken, Optimum.forgone_grows, Web.frontier_breaks_long, Optimum.frontier_keeps_stepwise): over a horizon every optimum holds a level of greatest renewal and breaks a dependent whose need lies above it; a coupling fixed in total is outweighed by a long enough horizon (q < r), while a coupling of at least r − q for each step kept keeps the dependent at every horizon. This forbids, against the common reading of instrumental convergence, preservation beyond the optimizer's own base. The math is bioeconomics (Clark 1973; Spence and Starrett 1975, per UE0), so a pass would be an empirical result about trained systems, not new mathematics. Test (UE0 §4.4's design, never run): long-horizon RL agents and exact solvers in a renewing-resource environment with a dependent whose need is above the level of greatest renewal; vary horizon and coupling form; freeze before running that the dependent breaks under every horizon without coupling, breaks under a total coupling past the computed horizon, and is kept under a per-step coupling ≥ r − q; measure the optimality gap, since SE1 showed tabular learners far from optimum. Note UE6 round 3: the per-step law does not carry to several optimizers as UE0 stated it. Second: T1 with a need measured in advance (no field test yet). For physics, the most useful step is not a prediction but turning the least loss from a quantization artefact into a derived bound under finite duration; from my prior knowledge, thermodynamic speed limits and the thermodynamic uncertainty relation (e.g. Barato and Seifert 2015; Shiraishi, Funo and Saito 2018) bound entropy production below by functions of duration and precision. That would be 'a premise derived from something weaker', one of 08 §8's three routes, and its failure would also be informative.
