## 1. Where the work is heading

The work wants one map on which a person, a society or an AI can trace what lets it form, keep going, change and end. It wants to show when a way of choosing helps remake the conditions it depends on, when it uses up those conditions for itself or for others, and how it crosses from one way to the other. Its first use is alignment: can damage to what sustains humans and AI still change their choices together, early enough, even when those doing well under a rule gain power over the rule itself? Its ethical offer is to count who bears a loss, including someone whose loss will never come back to the chooser. It also wants to bring what traditions and sciences have seen into a shared account whose proofs anyone can check and whose interpretation can still be changed by a case or a reader. ## 2. How far it has got

**There are proofs on both sides of the question. What is missing is an account of the passage between them.**

- **What is proved about a selection that locks.** In the closed model, the loss affects neither the next favoured share nor the rule. If favouring never lowers that share, a larger share sets a rule at least as favourable, and a higher rule keeps favouring, a locked start stays locked. If the loss also increases by at least one unit each step, it passes every bound while the lock holds. Those are substantive premises. Whether a market, reward or trained judge works that way has to be established in the case. [Chapter 2, line 415](/Users/ren/theories/theories/becoming/chapters/02-MAP.md:415)
- **What is proved about running down.** In the model of units drawing on a stock, two courses starting with the same units follow the same descent for as long as their levels meet the same needs. There is a useful limit on what one can infer from a failure: in a kept counterexample, a parent needs 1, its children need 13, and the stock stays at 12. A need goes unmet without the stock having fallen. To infer drawdown from the unmet need, the stronger theorem requires, among its other premises, that offspring need no more than their parent. [The model, line 23](/Users/ren/theories/experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/becoming/Becoming/LineOnAStock.lean:23) · [The stronger theorem, line 1057](/Users/ren/theories/experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/becoming/Becoming/LineOnAStock.lean:1057) · [The counterexample, line 1542](/Users/ren/theories/experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/becoming/Becoming/LineOnAStock.lean:1542)
- **What is proved about renewal.** An October theorem gives an exact result. Under a supplied law with finitely many kinds, *some* history can stay live forever exactly when there is a nonempty population in which each kind has a supporter that lasts through the next tick, and the population brings in enough over its lifetimes to cover formation. The choice of what to form is free, the outside supply is unlimited, and supporting another costs the supporter nothing. The theorem does not identify the history a selector will take. In the twenty places of the main package examined on 20 November, the readers found no corresponding general law of things continuing by passing on what they receive from beyond themselves.
