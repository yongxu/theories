# What the survey missed

*AI-written (Claude Fable 5.2, the last reader), 18 November 2026, 16:30 to 17:00 WIB. Read: the brief, the areas, the thirteen checks whole, astra's `whole.md` and `cold-start.md`, the cold-start pass marks, and the headlines of `claude/summaries.json`. No first report was opened. Nothing was changed but this file. No build, test, gate, render or lake; `check_becoming.py` was not run. The load average was about 300 when I started, so I ran seven light commands and two small read-only scripts (given at the end). No person has checked this page.*

## In short

The checks hold up the counts. What they cannot hold up is the question both views are about. Every reader measured bytes, paths, hashes and word-for-word overlap. No reader judged whether a text is wrong, says again in other words what is said elsewhere, or says what its theorem proves. So Ren's "garbage" and the coordinating session's "judged against the Lean" were both tested only in the sense of "can it leave the tree", and the answer to that was the same in every area: not before tools, records and links change.

Three further things are missing from the picture. The experiments, which are the map's coupling to something actual, belong to no area. The Lean, which both views take as the reference, was not itself looked at for mass. And the repository was read on a day when it moved under the readers five times.

## 3. Against the two views

**Against "much is garbage that can be compressed or removed".** Where the meaning is, almost nothing is a copy: no record, map store or entry page has a twin blob, and word-for-word overlap between chapters, records and the Lean's comments is 3 to 19%. The fronts are the exception: half the one page lies in long runs shared with the overview, and the README embeds the opening by construction. The largest mass by files (receipts, run copies and saved history, about two thirds of tracked paths) is on no reading path and is about 2% of reachable git. Nearly everything large is bound: 11,491 hashed references over 1,746 paths, 13,096 links into history and evidence, a gate that fails when a named study path is gone. Things found only in unlikely places: Ren's eight messages in `worldbuilding/archive`, the original record of a failed prediction in a draft folder, one bound Lean source kept only at its run path, 78 Lean files in history with no other copy, the reads that tie the direction's sentences to theorems. October modules that no session had opened for the direction turned out to carry its later sentences. What supports the view is also counted: 4.0 million words of one paper in twelve near-identical editions that nothing live reads; 1.47 million words of AI session trace in `sources/`; a 0.6-million-word mirror; 33,501 saved Lean copies with 6,468 contents; fronts that still give October's aim.

**Against "most is trace and machine output that can leave the reading path without loss".** The volume claim holds (14.7 of 26.76 million Markdown words are history or generated; four places hold 81,571 paths and 12,842 contents). "Without loss" does not hold today in any area: every check found tools, records or links reading what was called trace. The evidence for the direction is itself in trace and scratch. Receipts are not regenerable output; they record a past check, one passed run already cannot resolve 728 of its artifacts from today's tree (git history was not searched), and the Mathlib periphery cannot be rechecked from the repository. Taking it all away shortens no reading: the account is about 370,000 hand-kept words, the Lean holds a million words of prose, and what remains is five or more separate tellings of each theorem. Some of the mass is neither: 2.28 million words of Ren's earlier projects, and the experiments. And "prose judged against the Lean" has no instrument: no tool does it, the tests hold the fronts to a sentence of `AGENTS.md`, about half the chapters' words are prose or readings that no theorem passes or fails, and the only reads of this kind took rounds of readers for about 21 sentences.

**Against both.** Neither was tested in its own sense (gaps 4 to 7).

## 4. How reliable astra was

In twelve areas the checks counted 3 errors of fact for astra and 63 for Claude (68 with memory, which astra did not read). The comparison is uneven: Claude's reports were wider, and every claim in a claims file was tried, while from astra the checker chose 9 to 30 statements. The checkers were Claude models, which would favour Claude if anything. Astra was right against Claude mostly on what depends on what or where something else exists: the core run needs periphery receipts; records bind copies inside run folders; generation reaches beyond the manifest; `worldbuilding/` holds Ren's words; the Chinese one page has its own text; half the one page is shared in long runs with the overview. Its three errors: a gate failure called a disabled check, 538 for 543, five files for four. Its limit is breadth. It missed the outward pins, the links into history, the proof outside git, the unreachable half of `.git`, the standstill of the account. Its `whole` read sampled 9 modules and 1 record, so its inferences there are thin. It suits bounded claims to count or refute, and the second look at anything about to be called unused. It should not be the only reader of what an area depends on.
