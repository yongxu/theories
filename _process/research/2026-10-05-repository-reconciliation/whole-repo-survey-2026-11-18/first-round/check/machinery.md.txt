## Where the two reports differ

- **What is generated.** Claude takes the manifest as the whole (1,416 files). Astra saw that it is not: 37 history indexes, and I find 49 marked pages in all outside it. Astra is right.
- **Publishing.** Five builders (astra), not the site plus three (Claude).
- **Removing a study folder.** Claude: the gate stops. Astra: a live check is disabled. Claude is right; the gate fails.
- **The board history file.** Only Claude treats it, and calls it never opened and a candidate to leave. It is written at every trim and when a row lands, by a session following the board's rules and by the untracked trim script. It is trace that is still growing, not dormant trace.
- **Landing cost.** The two are consistent: Claude's medians over 387 commits and astra's single landing (183 s of checks for a three-file render commit). What astra adds is that seven tests with no input rule run on every landing, a board-only commit included.
- **What each saw alone.** Claude: the trim tool outside git, the 13,096 links into history and evidence, the 411 records bound to one study log, the stale-rule failure. Astra: generation outside the manifest, snapshot blobs recoverable from git history, the recorded trim override, the core checker's no-copy change. I confirmed all of these.

## What a decision can rest on now

The machinery couples the tree by hard rules. The gate fails when a named study or history path is gone. 13,096 links from live and generated pages land in history or evidence. 411 records hash-bind one 3.8 MB study log. So no folder named in a rule, a record or `move_pinned` can leave until those change. Generated output is at least 1,414 manifest files (3.26 M words of Markdown, the mirror 0.6 M of them, near-verbatim) plus about 49 marked pages outside the manifest. Snapshots are named by records and their bytes are in git history. Gate checks for a landing take a median of about 6 minutes, and seven unruled tests run on every change. The Queue trim depends on scripts outside git. Nothing in the machinery judges prose against the Lean.
