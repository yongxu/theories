### tellings-H (added after the first forty came back)

The first forty were judged on the first declaration listed for each record. Those lists are alphabetical, so most of what was judged was a definition or a helper, where little can go wrong; the few theorems with several premises were where the faults sat. This sample asks the same question of headline theorems.

`sample-H.json` lists 20 more theorem records, drawn at random with a fixed seed from those not in the first forty. For each record, the declaration to judge is its headline theorem, chosen by this rule and not by your judgment: the first declaration cited at the record's chapter home (the anchor `r-<id>`, file and line given) that is a `theorem` in the Lean and not a definition; if the home cites none, the first theorem named in the record's `laws`. Say which rule gave it. Then do exactly as in "tellings-A and tellings-B": read the Lean statement itself (count its hypotheses and put the count at the start of `statement_gist`), judge the record, the chapter and the docstring each against it, quote the words at fault, and say whether the three agree.
