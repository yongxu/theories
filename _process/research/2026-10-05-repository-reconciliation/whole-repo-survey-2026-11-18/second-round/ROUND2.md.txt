## Where the first round left things

Thirteen areas were surveyed by a Claude reader and an astra reader, and checked by a second reader. What was found, in brief (`../THE-PICTURE.md` has it whole; do not take it as settled where your task tests it):

- The Lean core is 409 modules, 20,352 theorems, checked on 16 November, with no axiom. About a quarter of its lines are prose.
- Each theorem is told by hand in several places: its docstring, a note in the root file, a README row, a record, a section of a chapter (its "home"), an atlas card. These are not copies of one another.
- No reader, and no tool, has set those tellings against the theorem statements. Names resolve and hashes match; that is all any check does.
- About two thirds of the files are history, receipts and generated pages. Records, tools and links are bound to them.
- Two views are still untested in their own sense. Ren's: much of the repository may be poor or overtaken and could be compressed or removed. The coordinating session's: prose should be judged against the Lean and the rest follows.

The second round takes the gaps that bear on reorienting the repository. Decide nothing and change nothing. Say what goes against either view.

## The tasks

Your task is named by the coordinating session. Write or return only what your task asks.

### tellings-A and tellings-B

`sample-A.json` and `sample-B.json` in this folder each list 20 theorem records, drawn at random with a fixed seed from the 537 live theorem records. You did not choose them and may not swap any. For each record:

1. Open the record. Take the first declaration listed for it in the sample file (open a second only if the record's statement is plainly about another one, and say so). Read that declaration's statement in the Lean file: its hypotheses and its conclusion. Do not rely on its name or docstring for what it says. You need not read the proof.
2. Judge three tellings of it against that statement, each on its own:
   - **record**: the record's `statement` and the law in `laws` that names this declaration (English);
   - **chapter**: the sentences of the chapter home (the sample gives file and line of the anchor `r-<id>`) that tell this declaration;
   - **docstring**: the comment directly above the declaration in the Lean file.
3. For each telling give one verdict: `exact` (says what the statement says, premises included or plainly pointed to); `says-less` (true of the statement but drops a conclusion or is vaguer); `says-more` (claims something the statement does not give: a dropped premise, a stronger conclusion, a reading told as proved); `says-other` (about something else); `absent` (no telling of this declaration there).
4. Quote the words at fault, briefly, and name the premise or conclusion involved.
5. Say whether the three tellings agree with one another.

Be exact about `says-more`: a premise that is stated one sentence earlier, or in the record's `hypotheses`, counts as pointed to only if the telling sends the reader there.
