## A. The scope of the ethics

**A1.** Paragraph 8, its first sentence. Now:

> This law, with that one step to say whose losses count, and, to order them, what anyone who counts everything at stake as worth something already holds, that what keeps all there was and adds more is better, is offered as the basis of all ethics, for all beings, in all universes.

Proposed:

> …is offered as the basis of ethics for life, for every being whose own line runs through life, in all universes.

*Why.* T8 (07 §8): "For life, the way is 生生 … It is the way of those whose own line runs through life, of whatever substrate … No verdict binding every possible mind is claimed." *For life* says whose way it is, not whose losses count: under the condition every continuation's losses still count, so the sentence's "to say whose losses count" stands. Its naming of the two premises by role (the step for scope, C for order) is current and stays. Its gloss of C, "what anyone who counts everything at stake as worth something already holds", is a little stronger than 07 §4.4 ("calling it better is C"), but `[^ethics]` states that, and the gloss is left as Ren's.

**A2.** The same paragraph. Now:

> …and it draws a line exactly, the line I call the line between good and evil.

Proposed:

> …and it draws a line exactly, the line I call, for life, the line between good and evil.

*Why.* The line itself (widening, narrowing, mixed) is proved with no premise, and generation never overlaps degeneration, for every relation and every class. The preference for one over the other is life's, not absolute (Ren, 30 October: "there is no absolute universal good vs evil - it is just for us as life"). Paragraph 17's "We need to do good, not evil" then needs no change. (Paragraphs are counted after the heading, leaving out the key line and the dateline.)

**A3.** Paragraph 8, "I take it to be the truth, the dharma, the Dao." No change if A1 is taken, since "it" then carries "for life". Otherwise: "I take it to be the truth, the dharma, the Dao: the way for life."

## B. The least loss

Paragraph 3. Now:

> …and the least loss holds wherever each exchange that takes forgets something of what it held.

Proposed:

> …and the least loss holds wherever each exchange that takes cannot be undone.

*Why.* DF1b: Landauer's bound bounds the heat an erasure exports, and an erasure can be thermodynamically reversible, so forgetting alone gives no floor of lost work. The least loss holds where each taking exchange destroys at least a floor of available work (`dissipative_of_floor`), or, with whole units, where each is irreversible (`dissipative_of_irreversible`). `[^conditions]` already says so. The whole-unit premise stays in the note.

## C. The step, and how far the law takes it

**C1.** Paragraph 6 keeps its first sentences ("That takes one step, which the work states as a condition and does not assert: …"). Its last sentence, now:

> Whether the law takes this step for us too is our first open question.

Proposed:

> Whether the law takes this step for us too, and how far, is our first open question.

*Why.* Chapter 7 §4.6 gives a path that, under P1 to P4, carries one's concern for one's own continuation to everything downstream of one's acts, which is part of the condition's scope, without the step. What that path does not reach is the step's. So the question is no longer only whether, but how far.

**C2.** Paragraph 7. Now "Where they agree most is the one step the law has not yet taken for us: …". Proposed: "…the one step the law has not yet taken for us in full: …", to match C1.

**C3.** Paragraph 10. Now "It drops the rest: what that continuation depends on, and that the continuation of others matters too." Proposed: "It drops the rest: what that continuation depends on, and the one step, that the continuation of others matters too." Optional; it ties the cancer paragraph to the step by name.

## D. The axiom and the nets, which the notes already correct

**D1.** Paragraph 4. Now:

> None of this needs an axiom, not even those most mathematics takes for granted: each law is proved from how the theory represents a world and from the conditions it names, …

Proposed:

> None of this, save the reading of our physics, needs an axiom of logic, not even those most mathematics takes for granted: each law is proved from how the theory represents a world, where its real assumptions lie, and from the conditions it names, …

The rest of the sentence is unchanged. *Why.* An empty axiom report concerns logic only and says nothing of the modelling choices (discrete steps, whole-number amounts), where the real assumptions lie. The second-law reading (`EntropyProduction`) is proved in the periphery with Lean's standard axioms. `[^proof]` and `[^conditions]` say both. The twenty-first review flagged the body's sentence (A-UNIFICATION, caveat i).

**D2.** Paragraph 4. Now "…every place/transition net among them, and so of every reaction counted in whole molecules, …". Proposed: "…every place/transition net among them, and, read as such nets, of every reaction counted in whole molecules, …". *Why.* `[^proof]`: reading a reaction network as a net "is a modelling correspondence, not a theorem". The note has said so since version 9.

## E. The optimizer

Paragraph 9. Now "…and where the selection is optimized to the full, it draws down whatever lies beyond and shares what it rewards, …". Proposed: "…it draws down whatever lies beyond, where that gives room, and shares what it rewards, …". *Why.* The law for optima holds each condition beyond at the least level any eligible outcome leaves, exactly where lowering it leaves room. T3 is titled so ("where that gives room"), and `[^alignment]` states it.
