## Freezing a test on a trained system

LA1, LA2 and LA3 each froze predictions, verdict rules, exact values and code in one commit before any call to the model. Their verdicts stand as frozen, and each README states its design's faults ([LA1](commons-llm-feedback/README.md#post-hoc-reading), [LA2](self-rewarding-lock/README.md#failures-and-limits-first), [LA3](shaping-what-comes-back/README.md#failures-and-limits-first)). What those faults teach the next frozen test:

- **Set a verdict only on a difference the feedback resolves.** LA1 required exactly m = 0 in its locked settings. In the two that failed, a round at m = 1 reported at least as much reward as a round at m = 0 with probability 0.28 and 0.45, so, as its post hoc reading says, the rule cannot tell a model that follows its feedback from one that does not. A power check on simulated feedback, run before the freeze, shows which differences a verdict can rest on.
- **Give each verdict rule the law's own quantifier.** The lock says that at every step neither the share nor the rule is below where it started, but LA2's rule for `late` compared only step 8 with step 4. Its judge answered whole calls one way, so its picks gave exactly inverse rules in turn, (11, 1) and (1, 11), and a course that never locked passed by an exact tie. The count over every step was computed and only reported.
- **Simulate the system the test may meet, not only noisy forms of the law and its rivals.** The power checks' scripted agents drew their answers with probabilities short of 0 and 1, and the followers among them explored. LA2's judge answered by what it read, all one way in 112 of its 119 calls (which is also why a test's unit is the call, not the pick), and so made the exact tie possible. LA3's agent followed its scores where it had seen both policies and its prior elsewhere, and never tried the other policy in 9 of 30 seeds. Readers whose answers are fixed by what they read, and such hybrids, belong among the simulated agents.
- **Measure exploration apart from selection.** LA3's bridge counted in one measure the seeds that chose by their scores and those that never saw the other policy, and every failure was of the second kind. A reviewer had named the point before the freeze and it was left in; a point that changes what a verdict says is settled before freezing. Assigning one round of each policy before free choice is another way.
- **Guard each support rule against contradiction.** A frozen rule that says what a pass supports reads every measure bearing on that claim, and reports the claim contradicted when one is resolved against it. LA2's rule for *the counts decide* read neither the one-note measure nor `late`'s first reading; both were resolved against it, and the rule printed the claim as supported.
- **Add a control wherever a rival passes every contrast.** In LA2's power check a judge deaf to the notes passed every contrast, so the design added a control, `in_time` with no note of the loss. It turned in 4 of 5 seeds, so `in_time`'s turn cannot be read as the loss, read in time, turning a course that would otherwise be kept.
