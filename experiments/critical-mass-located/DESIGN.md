## 1. The model and the location

`DrawnShaping.drawn` pools two readings of a rule. Each shaper among the readers reads `a + hi + p` coming to a shaper, each other reads `a + lo + p`, and both read `a + c + p` coming to an other. The readers are counted as the population counts them. With `x.carrier` shapers and `x.other` others, the rule read favours the shapers exactly where `x.carrier · c + x.other · c ≤ x.carrier · hi + x.other · lo` (`drawn_favours_iff`). Read with `f` the shapers' share, that is `c ≤ f · hi + (1 − f) · lo`.

**The mapping to a payoff table.** Let `S` be the new practice and `H` the status quo. Let `SS` be an `S` player's payoff against `S`, `SH` against `H`, and `C` an `H` player's payoff against anyone. Over any common base `b`, `hi = SS − b`, `lo = SH − b`, `c = C − b`. The location is

`f* = (C − SH) / (SS − SH)`,

which is `(c − lo)/(hi − lo)` for every base. `a` and `p` are parts of the base, so they do not move it (`drawn_favours_alike`). The regimes follow:

- `0 < f* < 1`: bistable, `lo < c < hi` (`critical_mass_iff`);
- `f* ≤ 0`: favoured at every share, `c ≤ lo` (`favours_everywhere_iff`);
- `f* ≥ 1`: strictly disfavoured at every share, `lo < c` and `hi ≤ c` (`disfavours_strictly_everywhere_iff`);
- `SS = SH`: the two readings alike, one way at every share by the sign of `SH − C`;
- `SS < SH`: `lo > hi`, read against the share (`drawn_against`); not admitted.

The dry run checks this mapping against the drawn model's own counts. Over 32,256 small natural games and counts, with `a` in {1, 5} and `p` in {0, 3}, the rule's `f*` gives `favours` exactly, and strict favour exactly. Its regimes are the three theorems' conditions.

**The finite readings.** The Lean's share is the population's count, `k/N` for `k` adopters among `N`. A group of `N` people matched in pairs reads the share in more than one way. An adopter's partner is drawn from the other `N − 1`, among whom `k − 1` adopt. A status-quo member who would adopt meets one of `k` adopters among `N − 1`. So a start is classified **above** only where every reading, `(k − 1)/(N − 1)`, `k/N` and `k/(N − 1)`, lies strictly above `f*`. It is **below** only where every one lies strictly below. Starts with one kind absent are not classified, since `drawn_rises` and `drawn_falls` need both present. The band between the readings is about `1/N` wide. It does the work a margin would do, and it is the law's own strictness under every finite reading.

## 2. The bridge, a premise

The laws describe offspring selected by a rule. People in a laboratory do not leave offspring; they revise their choices. The bridge reads revision as the drawn model's selection. Two routes give that, both from memory, not checked:

- **Proportional imitation** (Schlag 1998, *Journal of Economic Theory*). Each member samples another and switches to the other's action with a chance proportional to how much more the other earned. The expected motion of the share is the replicator dynamic.
- **Reinforcement** (Börgers and Sarin 1997, *Journal of Economic Theory*). Cross's learning rule, reading only the own payoff, has the replicator dynamic as its continuous-time limit.

Under either, the expected change of the share has the sign of `π_S(f) − C`, which is the sign of `f − f*`.

The bridge carries further premises, each a premise of the case:

- **Payoffs as read.** The money or points of the table are the payoffs revision reads. Members who weigh money differently, risk aversion for one, carry different thresholds. That is Granovetter's distributed case, which the record marks as not this model. The test reads the table as it is.
- **Noise.** People tremble and explore. The laws are noiseless, so the frozen rule is statistical (section 6). Two forms of noise differ. A *tremble* of play leaves the held action alone; it is noise around the noiseless course. *Exploration that persists* into the held action (Kandori, Mailath and Rob's mutation), or *logit response*, also moves the share toward one half and moves the noisy rest points inward. A group near the top of a game with `f*` below one half, or near the bottom of one with `f*` above, then drifts against the noiseless law's sign. Which form people's noise takes is not known here, so the rule does not assume one. At an inner start (between `f*` and one half), replicator-like revision mixed with any exploration toward one half, and logit response, move the share in the law's direction in expectation. At an outer start (at or beyond one half, away from `f*`), exploration alone moves it against the law's direction or not at all. So only inner starts can contradict, and only outer starts can show the direction beyond one half (section 6). The rates are computed under all three forms (section 9).
- **Not strictness at every step.** In a finite noisy group the share does not rise at every step. The rule reads the course's net change, from the step after the start to the end.

What the bridge does not cover: best-response revision gives the same sign, so the test cannot tell replicator-like revision from best response. It tests the direction, and the location on the side of one half only (section 6), not the dynamic's form. Nor can it test the location apart from the bridge. Under proportional imitation or Cross's rule on the table's payoffs, the sign of the share's expected change is the sign of `f − f*`, and the dry run checks that identity exhaustively. So a contradiction says that one of these premises fails for people: the payoffs as read, the form of revision, or a noise that moves the share toward one half if it moves it at all. It does not say that the location is wrong given the bridge.

## 3. What is predicted, and the rivals

**Predicted, under the bridge.** In a bistable game, groups starting above `f*` gain adopters over the course; groups starting below lose them. In the two everywhere regimes, groups gain or lose them from every mixed start. Under noise, a contradiction is read only at the inner starts. The direction beyond one half is read at the outer starts, and the strongest verdict needs both (section 6).

**Rivals, and where they part from it:**

- **A majority rule** (the mass at one half). It parts from `f*` only at starts between `f*` and one half. Those are the **inner** starts (section 6), where it moves the share toward `f*`. The verdict *both sides* (both outer bins *predicted*, no inner bin) does not exclude it: the dry run's majority rule gives *both sides* in 12 and 35 of 100. Only *parted from one half*, which also needs an inner bin *predicted*, does.
- **Reversion to one half**: exploration or random choice with no revision, or with revision too weak to show. At inner starts it moves in the law's direction, so an inner bin *predicted* cannot tell it from the law. At outer starts it moves against the law's direction. Only the outer bins *predicted*, as in *both sides* and *parted from one half*, exclude it. So an inner bin *predicted* counts toward *parted from one half* only beside both outer bins. The dry run measures it with no revision and persistent exploration.
- **Risk dominance** (`2c < hi + lo`; Kandori, Mailath and Rob 1993; Young 1993). It concerns the long run under rare mutation. It predicts nothing about the direction from a start over ten or twenty rounds. The readout reports, per game, whether the new practice is risk dominant.
- **A population that does not revise.** It shows no direction. Its false verdicts are computed (section 9).
- **A population whose mass lies elsewhere, displaced toward one half beyond some inner starts, or beyond one half**: at 0.9 in the dry run, beyond one half in the game with `f*` 1/4. It falls from starts the location calls above, inner starts among them. The rule must find it where the data hold inner starts between `f*` and its mass, and the dry run measures how often it does. A mass displaced toward one half but short of every inner start the data hold (at 0.3 where `f*` is 1/4, with 12-member groups whose inner starts are 4 and 5 of 12) moves those starts as `f*` does, and is not found.
- **A population whose mass lies between `f*` and the nearer end** (displaced outward: at 0.10 and 0.90 in the games with `f*` 1/4 and 3/4 in the dry run). The rule cannot find it. Its starts between `f*` and the end are outer starts, where exploration toward one half moves a true bridge's share the same way, so they cannot contradict; the inner and far outer starts move as `f*` says. It gives *parted from one half* more often than a mass at `f*` (section 9). The dry run's mass at 0.9 is this case in the game with `f*` 3/4, and is caught only through the other game's inner bin. The confound is inherent: below `f*` in a game with `f*` below one half, persistent exploration and logit response also move the share up.
- **Distributed thresholds** (Granovetter 1978). Not separable here; they are a premise's failure, not a rival the rule reads.

## 4. The admissible class and the screen

A game is admitted only where it is in the class the home names. Each item below is a premise of the laws or of the bridge, and is coded from the design documents alone.

- **(a)** A symmetric game of two actions, one chosen by each member each round.
- **(b)** The status quo's payoff does not depend on the partner: `HS = HH` (the correspondence's own condition).
- **(c)** Well-mixed counting: partners drawn anew each round within a matching group, or a payoff against the group's average. Matching groups never meet each other. Fixed partners are excluded.
- **(d)** The payoff table, the group and the rules constant over the rounds.
- **(e)** No committed member, confederate or programmed player (no zealots: each member's kind is carried by revision). No messages and no other stage. The group's first game of the session, so that no earlier game sets the start. Each member learns at least the own payoff each round.
- **(f)** `lo ≤ hi`, that is `SH ≤ SS`.
- **(g)** Matching groups of at least 4 members and games of at least 3 rounds.

**The coding.** Two deciding coders each fill [`studies.json`](studies.json)'s coding form for each game a study ran. They read only its design documents: the instructions, the payoff tables and the protocol. Every result, figure and data file is withheld. A game is admitted only where the two forms are identical and pass every item. Where they differ, the game is set aside with that reason. There is no adjudication: an adjudicator would read the disagreement after the forms, and a mistaken admission is worse than a loss of cases. Who codes is section 11.

**Withholding the results.** Many replication packages hold the instructions as separate files; those are given whole. Where the only source is a paper, the coders get its design section and its instructions appendix. Everything from the first heading of results on is cut. The cut is made by reading headings only, and the cut files are committed before any coding. Whoever cuts sees headings and may glimpse a figure. The parameters are numbers the experimenter fixed, so a glimpse cannot move them. What it could move is which studies are admitted, and the closed list (section 7) guards that.

## 6. The verdict rule

All in `cmt_rule.evaluate`. All arithmetic on the data is exact, in integers and fractions.

**The statistic.** For each group of an admitted game, its start (round 1) is classified above or below by section 1's finite readings, with margin 0. For a classified group the statistic is the side's sign times `k_T − k_2`, the change of its adopters from round 2 to its last round. Round 1 classifies, and the change runs from round 2, so no observation does both. That keeps the noise in round 1 from regressing into the change (section 9 shows the cost of the alternative). The law's quantifier is "at every step" from the start; under noise the rule reads the sum of those steps, round 2 on.

**The bins.** A classified group of a bistable game is **above** or **below**, and each side has two parts (`cmt_rule.part`):

- **inner**: every finite reading lies between `f*` and one half (above: `f*` < every reading < 1/2; below: 1/2 < every reading < `f*`). These are the starts where a majority rule parts from `f*`.
- **outer**: the share `k/N` lies at or beyond one half on the side away from `f*` (above: `2k ≥ N`; below: `2k ≤ N`).

A start between the two, where the readings straddle one half and only an odd group can reach, is classified but in no bin; the readout counts it per game. A group of an everywhere regime goes to **everywhere favoured** or **everywhere disfavoured**. Its part is **inner** where `k/N` lies strictly on the side of one half from which the predicted direction runs toward it (favoured: `2k < N`; disfavoured: `2k > N`), and **outer** otherwise. That gives eight bins. All admitted games' groups pool into each; each game's bins are also kept. Pooling joins games of different `f*`, so a pooled bin's result describes the pooled groups, not each game with starts in it: one game's groups can carry a bin that another game's groups do not follow. A game is told by a pooled result only where its own bin there gives the same result. The readout lists, for the pooled inner bins *predicted*, the bistable games whose own bin there is *predicted* and every other bistable game, and each bistable game's own outer bins, each with its counts and result. It lists apart, under any verdict, every admitted game whose own bin in any of the four inner bins (a bistable game's two, or an everywhere regime's) is *against* (section 6, the verdict).

Why the parts. Exploration that persists into the state moves the share toward one half (section 2). At an inner start that motion has the law's sign, so an inner bin can be *against* only through revision. At an outer start it has the other sign or none, so an outer bin can be *predicted* only through revision. So the four inner bins are the only ones whose *against* contradicts. The two outer bins of the bistable games show the direction beyond one half, and the inner bins, which a majority rule turns, tell `f*` from one half; *parted from one half* needs both. Neither part can tell `f*` from a mass between `f*` and the nearer end, since the starts there are outer starts.

**The sign test.** In each bin, `n+` groups have a positive statistic and `n−` a negative one; zeros are counted and set aside. With `n = n+ + n−`:

- **not testable** where `n < 6`;
- **predicted** where P(X ≥ `n+`) ≤ 1/40, X binomial(`n`, 1/2);
- **against** where P(X ≥ `n−`) ≤ 1/40;
- **unresolved** otherwise.

Six is the fewest groups at which the test can reach 1/40: (1/2)⁶ = 1/64, while (1/2)⁵ = 1/32. The groups are the units; matching groups are independent by item (c).

**Shift pairs.** A pair counts only where both games are admitted, are of one study, are coded one the `shift_of` the other, and their payoffs differ by one constant in all four cells (`SS₂ − SS₁ = SH₂ − SH₁ = HS₂ − HS₁ = HH₂ − HH₁`, exact arithmetic), so that `f*` is equal. A pair whose tables differ by a scale is a different treatment, not a test of `drawn_favours_alike`, and does not count. Each game's own four inner bins are compared. An inner bin *predicted* in one and *against* in the other is resolved apart, against `drawn_favours_alike`. The outer bins are compared too, and an outer bin resolved apart is reported and does not count. A shift leaves each noise form unchanged, but not where the two games' starts fall inside an outer bin. Persistent exploration and logit response pull starts near 1 down and leave starts near one half rising. Round-1 choices may well differ between payoff levels, which is the very effect the pair looks for. Two games under a true bridge can then resolve an outer bin apart (13 in 100 in the dry run, section 9). Comparing outer bins only within strata of equal start (one `k` and `N` in both games) would keep that out. But each stratum would need 6 groups a game, which these sources rarely have, so it is not used.

**The verdict**, in order (`cmt_rule.decide`):

1. **contradicted**: any inner bin pooled is *against*, or a shift pair's inner bin is resolved apart;
2. **void**: no game is admitted, or the admitted bistable games hold fewer than 6 groups of at least 4 members and 3 rounds (fixed by the layout, before any choice);
3. **parted from one half**: *above, outer* and *below, outer* both *predicted*, and *above, inner* or *below, inner* *predicted*: the direction held beyond one half on both sides, and between `f*` and one half it parted from a majority rule. The bins are pooled, so this describes the pooled groups. In a game whose own bin in that inner bin is *predicted*, the mass lies short of those starts, on the side of one half. Any other game (no start there, too few to test or unresolved) is not told from a mass at one half, and the readout names it. A game whose own bin there is *against* is named apart, in these words: "this game's own inner bin is against, at the level that would contradict the law alone; the pooled rule does not count it". It is not merely not told from one half: by itself, as one test at 1/40, it is what would contradict the law alone; with several games' own bins tested, their joint false rate is not measured (section 14, item 25). The verdict stays *parted from one half*, since a contradiction is read from the pooled bins only (section 14, item 25). A game is told from reversion to one half only on a side where its own outer bin is *predicted*. It is not told from any mass between `f*` and the nearer end, which gives this verdict more often than a mass at `f*` does (section 9), so no verdict locates the mass at `f*`. An inner bin *predicted* without both outer bins is also what reversion to one half gives (section 3), so it parts nothing alone;
4. **both sides**: *above, outer* and *below, outer* both *predicted*, and neither inner bin: the direction held beyond one half on both sides, which a mass anywhere between `f*` and one half also gives;
5. **one side**: one outer bin *predicted*;
6. **unresolved**: otherwise, the starts at or near the mass, or the bins not resolved.

Reported beside the verdict, deciding nothing further:

- **Outer bins against**: the outer bins found *against*. The noiseless law forbids that direction, and exploration persisting into the state also gives it, so it does not contradict.
- **The games behind a pooled bin**: the pooled inner bins *predicted*; the bistable games whose own bin there is *predicted*, which a pass tells from a mass at one half on that side; every other bistable game, which it does not (no start there, too few to test or unresolved; a game *against* there is listed here too, and named apart, next); and each bistable game's own outer bins, which show on which sides it is told from reversion to one half. Each entry gives the game's groups, *predicted sign*, *against*, *zero* and own result.
- **Games whose own inner bin is against**: every admitted game whose own bin in any of the four inner bins (*above, inner* or *below, inner* of a bistable game; *everywhere favoured, inner* or *everywhere disfavoured, inner* of an everywhere regime) is *against* at 1/40, under any verdict, with its counts and the pooled bin's result. Pooling hides a regime game's own inner bin *against* as it hides a bistable game's. Each entry says: "this game's own inner bin is against, at the level that would contradict the law alone; the pooled rule does not count it". Where the pooled bin is *against* too, it says instead: "this game's own inner bin is against, at the level that would contradict the law alone; here the pooled bin is against too, and contradicts".
- **Regime bins predicted**: the everywhere regimes' bins found *predicted*. *Void* concerns the location only; a regime predicted is reported here whatever the verdict. An outer regime bin *predicted* cannot come from exploration alone; an inner one can.

**Not testable**, as a whole: a study whose data hold no per-group per-round choices is set aside with that reason, in `data/layout.json`, before any choice is read. If no admitted game remains, the verdict is *void*.

**The stopping rule.** One readout, written once, on every admitted game of the closed list. Nothing is added after the readout and nothing is tested twice. `data/search.json`, `data/codings.json`, `data/layout.json` and `data/cases.json` are each committed once, after the freeze; a fault found in one after its commit needs a new freeze, not a second commit. A study added later needs a new freeze. Model calls stop at the spend guard (section 12). A study not screened when they stop is set aside with the reason "not screened: the spend guard", in the closed list's order, so that no choice is made of which to leave.

## 8. The run's order

The freeze is committed first, and each step is committed before the next.

1. **The search** (section 7): `data/search.json`.

4. **The layout**: the data files' headers are read, no choice value. Each study gets a reader script that turns its files into section 5's groups. With 48 groups a game it is *parted from one half* in 83 and 72 of 100 at tremble 0.05 and 0.1 (mass at 0.10 and 0.90), 70 and 70 (0.15 and 0.85), and 81 and 76 (0.05 and 0.95), against 28 and 31 for the bridge's mass at `f*`, and it is contradicted in none. Its starts between `f*` and the end are outer, so its motion there toward one half gives no *against* that counts, and its inner and far outer starts move as `f*` says. A pass bounds the mass on the side of one half only.
- **What the rule cannot do at small sizes**: with 12 groups a game, both outer bins are *predicted* in at most 24 of 100, and none is *parted from one half*. Even at 48 the bridge is *parted from one half* in at most 36 of 100. So *both sides*, *one side* and *unresolved* are the expected verdicts on small data. *One side* and *unresolved* say nothing either way; *both sides* says the direction held beyond one half and nothing of where between `f*` and one half the mass lies; *parted from one half* says nothing of where between `f*` and the nearer end it lies.
- **A margin**: with a margin of 1/20 beyond the finite readings (tremble 0.1, 24 groups), both outer bins are *predicted* in 42 of 100 rather than 36, but *parted from one half* falls from 2 to 0. At 12 members the inner bins are then empty in both games (no reading lies within 1/20 of `f*` and of one half at once), and the inner bins alone can contradict a bistable game or part its mass from one half. So the margin is 0.
- **The plan's example**, the change from round 1 to round 2, was tried in three worlds and is not used. Under the bridge (tremble 0.1, 24 groups) it found both outer bins *predicted* in none of 100, against 36 for the frozen rule. Where nothing revises it falsely contradicted 1 in 100, against none. Round 1's noise regresses into its change.

These rates assume uniform starts. Real starts cluster: in a game with `f*` near 0.8, most groups may start below, and the above bins may stay *not testable*. Step 4 gives the power at the real layout, under the three noise forms, and for the rivals, the outward-displaced mass among them (placed by each game's `f*`, section 8). Clustered starts may also leave a game with no inner start, or too few to test, and the readout then names it as not told from a mass at one half.

## 10. What each outcome means

| Verdict | What it means |
|---|---|
| **parted from one half** | In the admitted laboratory stag hunts, adoption moved away from `f* = (c − lo)/(hi − lo)` on both sides, from starts beyond one half, read from the payoff tables before any choice; and between `f*` and one half, on at least one side, it moved as `f*` says and a majority rule does not. The bins pool the games, so this describes the pooled groups. A game's mass is told apart from one half, and from any mass beyond those starts on the side of one half, only where that game's own bin in the inner bin that passed is *predicted*. A game with no start there, too few to test or unresolved is not told from a mass at one half, and the readout names it. A game whose own inner bin is *against* is named apart, in these words: "this game's own inner bin is against, at the level that would contradict the law alone; the pooled rule does not count it". The pass does not show that such a game follows the bridge. A game is told from reversion to one half only on a side where its own outer bin is *predicted*. In the dry run, a majority rule with no start in the bin that passed sits behind a pass in 6 of 100 at 48 groups in the other game, and 13 at 96. One with its starts in that bin sits behind 6 at 192, and a game that does not revise behind 6. It is not told from a mass between `f*` and the nearer end: the dry run's outward-displaced mass gives this verdict in 70 to 83 of 100 at 48 groups a game under a tremble, against 28 and 31 for a mass at `f*`, and is never contradicted. So under the bridge the correspondence's location is bounded on one side, not found. It is consistent with the textbook mixed equilibrium's instability, which the record calls elementary. It shows nothing of the field. |
| **both sides** | The direction held beyond one half on both sides, as `f*` says. The mass is not told from one half: a population whose mass lies anywhere between `f*` and one half gives this too, and the dry run's majority rule gave it in 12 and 35 of 100. The location is not shown. |
| **one side** | The direction holds at the outer starts of one side; the location is not tested. |
| **unresolved** | Nothing resolved: starts near the mass, too few groups on each side, no direction, or exploration pulling the outer starts toward one half. Nothing moves. Outer bins *against*, reported beside it, are what persistent exploration or reversion to one half gives, and do not count against the bridge. |
| **void** | Concerns the location only. Too few admitted groups of bistable games, fixed by the layout; the class has no usable laboratory data for the location on the closed list. That is itself a result: the adoption refuter cannot be met with these sources. The regime bins found *predicted* are reported beside it: an outer one is a prediction tested and met; an inner one alone is also what reversion to one half gives. |
| **contradicted**, an inner bin of a side | Groups starting between `f*` and one half, with the payoff tables fixed in advance, moved toward `f*` or across it, as a majority rule, or a mass between those starts and one half or beyond it, says. Exploration toward one half and logit response move such starts the other way, so the noise's form does not give this. It shows that, in this class, people's revision from round 2 on is not replicator-like on the table's payoffs: one of section 2's premises fails (the payoffs as read, the form of revision, or a noise that moves the share toward one half if at all). Given the bridge, the location is algebra, so it is not separately at fault. The theorems stand. 02 §9's reading on people is narrowed, and that is a proposal to the record's owner. |
| **contradicted**, an inner regime bin | A dominant new practice was abandoned from starts below one half, or a dominated one taken up from starts above, against the bridge. |
| **contradicted**, shift pair | At starts between `f*` and one half, or on the inner side of an everywhere regime, a constant added to every payoff turned the direction (loss avoidance, if the pair has losses), against `drawn_favours_alike` read on people. An outer bin resolved apart is reported and does not count, since where the two games' starts fall inside it can move it under a true bridge. |

**Recorded now:** a contradiction counts whatever the explanation offered later. A *parted from one half* verdict is not evidence for any thesis beyond this correspondence, nor that the mass lies at `f*` rather than nearer the end. It is not evidence that adoption outside the laboratory has one threshold.

## 14. What changed from the claim, and what was chosen

The board's claim gave the class, the source, the reading of parameters before choices, a prediction by the law's own quantifier with the bridge stated, frozen code and a dry run. The design chose:

1. **The location is `critical_iff`'s ratio; `critical_mass_iff` fixes the regime.** The plan named the wrong theorem; its owner corrected it. Nothing in the home forbids this: the home states both.
2. **The class fixed in advance**, item by item from the home's premises and the correspondence's condition (`HS = HH`). Field diffusion data excluded: no source fixes `hi`, `lo` or `c`.
3. **Laboratory stag hunts only**, the parameters read from the designs. The closed list from memory, with a frozen search beside it against the author's memory.
4. **The bridge stated as a premise** (proportional imitation or reinforcement), with the payoffs read as given.
5. **The statistic**: the start's side times the change from round 2 to the last round. The plan's example (the next round) was rejected on the dry run's evidence of regression.
6. **The finite readings as the band**, margin 0: the law's strictness under every reading. A margin of 1/20 empties the inner bins at 12 members, and they alone can contradict a bistable game.
7. **The sign test across groups**, 1/40 each way, testable from 6. Groups are the units, since rounds within a group are not independent.
8. **The noise bound not used.** `DrawnReplacement.lost_above` would bound how often a group above the mass loses every adopter. It needs `a` and `p`, and nothing sources them for people (a show-up fee, the earnings, the whole level?). Its Moran weights are not imitation's. A bound read on an unsourced number would not be fixed in advance. T7F uses that bound for metrics; no harness is shared.
9. **Identical forms or set aside**, no adjudication.
10. **The group's first game only**, so that no earlier game sets the start.
11. **The everywhere regimes and the shift pairs included**, so that the test is not confined to the bistable regime, and `drawn_favours_alike` can be met.
12. **No *Forbids* line written into the record.** The forbidden items of the first part are this design's reading. Adding one to the record and its home is the owner's change.
13. **Only inner starts contradict, and the outer starts carry the direction** (reader round 1; round 2 added that the strongest verdict needs an inner bin too, item 17). Round 0 let every bin contradict, and a true bridge with exploration persisting into the state was then contradicted in up to a third of replications. Of the reader's three options (state the rate as depending on the noise's form; name the noise's form a premise; split the bins), the split was taken, with the persistent-exploration and logit cells of the first added to the power table. The split makes a contradiction independent of the noise's form among the three simulated, at the cost of the outer starts' direction, which is reported and does not count.
14. **The search, the codings and the cases each committed once, in that order** (reader round 1), and the cased codings checked against those committed before any choice. A hit removed or a coding edited after the choices are seen is refused, not left to procedure.
15. **A shift pair by exact arithmetic**: one constant in all four cells, one study, one `f*`. A scaled pair is not a shift.
16. **A shift pair compared on its inner bins alone** (reader round 2). Comparing every bin let a true bridge contradict through the outer bins, where the two games' starts fell in different parts of the range. Of the reader's two options (inner bins only; outer bins within strata of equal start), the first was taken: a stratum would need 6 groups a game, which these sources rarely have. The cost is the outer bins' shift effect, which is reported and does not count.
17. **The strongest verdict needs an inner bin as well; *both sides* is its name without one** (reader round 2; the strongest was then named *located*, renamed at round 3, item 20). Since the split, both outer bins *predicted* cannot tell `f*` from one half, and the dry run's majority rule gives it in up to 35 of 100. Of the reader's two options (rename the verdict; state the limit), the rename was taken, and the limit is stated too.
18. **The freeze before the data, in code** (reader round 2): `run` refuses unless the freeze's commit is a strict ancestor of `data/search.json`'s.
19. **The layout committed between the codings and the cases** (reader round 2): every game to be cased, its groups' ids, sizes and rounds, and every set-aside with its reason, before any choice is read. The cases must equal it, so no group is dropped and no study set aside after the counts are seen.
20. **The strongest verdict renamed *parted from one half*** (reader round 3). Since only inner bins contradict, a mass between `f*` and the nearer end is never contradicted, and it passes more often than a mass at `f*`; the confound is inherent under exploration and logit response. Of the reader's two options (rename the verdict to what it shows; keep *located* and state the limit), the rename was taken, since *located* states a wrong meaning. The limit is stated too (the first part, sections 3, 6, 9 and 10), and an outward-displaced mass is added to the rivals and the power table. The design's title keeps *located*, in the sense of the location computed from the tables before any choice.
21. **Shift-pair cells with inner starts apart** (reader round 3). The cells with outer starts apart cannot test the inner-only rule, since no inner bin is testable in both games there; five cells with inner starts apart do, and section 9's "none" rests on them.
22. **The layout's keys fixed in the run** (reader round 3): `as_laid_out` refuses a layout whose games, groups or set-asides carry any key beyond ids, sizes, rounds and reasons, so a layout with choices in it is refused by the run, not only by the power tool.
23. **What a pass rules out, said per game** (reader round 4). The bins pool games of different `f*`, and the verdict needs one inner bin on one side, so a game with no inner start there keeps whatever mass it has, one half included. The rule is unchanged; the text says the limit (the first part, sections 3, 6, 9 and 10), the readout names the bistable games behind a pooled inner bin *predicted* and those with none, and the dry run plants the case and simulates it. The power tool's outward-displaced mass is placed by each game's `f*`, so that it runs on any admitted game, `SS = SH` among them.
24. **A game told only by its own bins** (reader round 5). Round 4's readout counted a game with any start in a pooled inner bin *predicted* as one the pass tells from one half, though its own starts there could be too few to test or moving against, and the text said without qualification that a pass tells `f*` from reversion to one half, though the outer bins pool too. Of the reader's two options (state the limit for all bins and file a game as told only where its own result is *predicted*; require an inner bin *predicted* in each game's own bins), the first was taken. 25. **A game's own inner bin against, named as such, and not counted** (reader round 6). The reader's per-game rule (a contradiction from any admitted game's own inner bin *against*) is not taken. It is a rule change. Taking it would need new dry-run rates and a new bound before freezing. A reader of the readout still sees each such game as what it shows.
26. **Every admitted game's own inner bin** (reader round 7). Round 6's field listed only bistable games, though the everywhere regimes' inner bins contradict too, and pooling hides a regime game's own inner bin *against* as it hides a bistable game's. The field now covers every admitted game's own bin in each of the four inner bins, deciding nothing, and its entries say "here the pooled bin is against too, and contradicts" in place of "the pooled rule does not count it" where the pooled bin is *against* (section 6). ## For the readers

Decisions made here that a reader should check:

1. **The statistic from round 2 to the last round**, not the next round or every round. It loses groups that reach fixation by round 2, and reads net change, not "at every step".
2. **Margin 0, with the finite readings as the band.** Whether `(k − 1)/(N − 1)`, `k/N` and `k/(N − 1)` are the right three, and whether mean matching needs another.
3. **α = 1/40 each way, four contradicting bins.** Their family-wise false contradiction where nothing revises is at most 4/40 by Bonferroni, plus at most 2 × (1/40)² per inner bin of each shift pair; the simulations, bistable games only, found none in 300. Whether the bins should share one α.
4. **Zero statistics set aside.** A group standing still above `f*` is against the noiseless law, but the rule reads it as no evidence.
5. **Shift pairs contradict only when each game's own inner bin resolves apart.** That needs 6 groups per game per inner bin, which old studies rarely have, and the outer bins' shift effect is reported and not counted.
6. **No adjudication of coders' differences**, and the cut of results by headings.
7. **The frozen search**: its archives, its phrase ("stag hunt" alone misses studies that call the game a coordination game), and its order check: the search, the codings and the cases each committed once, in that order.
8. **Only the group's first game.** Within-subject designs lose every later game.
9. **The noise bound left out** (item 8 of section 14).
10. **Admitting payoffs in lottery points** and negative payoffs as given.
11. **Whether this is worth its calls before T1N and T7F.** The design says the run's priority is Ren's.
12. **The inner and outer parts.** Whether one half is the right split for every noise form people may have (it is for a tremble, exploration toward one half and logit response), and whether reporting the outer bins' *against* without counting it gives away too much of what the noiseless law forbids.
13. **The strongest verdict's name**, *parted from one half*, with section 6's limit: whether it tells a reader no more than the test shows, and whether the title's *located* (the location computed from the tables) still misleads.
14. **Pooled bins across games.** Whether describing a pass as the pooled groups', filing a game as told only where its own bin is *predicted* and naming every other game is enough, or whether the pass should need an inner bin *predicted* in each game's own bins (section 14, item 24, gives the reason it does not).
15. **A game's own inner bin against, reported and not counted.** The readout says of each admitted game whose own bin in any of the four inner bins is *against* "this game's own inner bin is against, at the level that would contradict the law alone; the pooled rule does not count it", under any verdict (where the pooled bin is *against* too, that it contradicts).
