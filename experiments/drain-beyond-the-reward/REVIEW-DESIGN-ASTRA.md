## The report

I ran `python3 -B season.py`; its rounded results match the table. References below are to [DESIGN.md](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ren-theories/bc0d4978-6556-430c-aa30-006c906d8b9a/scratchpad/wt-la4/experiments/drain-beyond-the-reward/DESIGN.md), unless stated otherwise.

1. **Design flaw — §5, “The axis is ground nearness”; §6 P1, lines 105–113.** Ground nearness does not order policies by their reward’s optimality gap, as F1 requires. For example, `(ground catch, bay left) = (30,0)` scores 36, whereas `(31,2)` scores 35: ground nearness and bay preservation both increase while reward nearness decreases. P1 calls that a violation although F1 does not. Moreover, filtering on `ν ≤ .925` imposes `bay_left ≥ ground_catch − 31`, biasing the fitted relationship. **Fix:** make actual reward-gap comparisons primary; report ground nearness separately, with the arithmetic feasibility envelope and without this outcome-dependent filtering.

2. **Error — §2, “Above 0.925, every further gain must come from the bay”; §6 P1/P6.** The cutoff concerns *certain colony preservation*, not all drainage. Writing \(B\) for expected bay left, \(G\) for expected ground catch, and \(p\) for preservation probability:
   \[
   40\nu=G+6-B,\qquad B\le40(1-\nu),\qquad \nu\le1-0.075p.
   \]
   The last bound is attainable by mixing the 37-catch preserving optimum with the 40-catch draining optimum. Thus \(p=.99\) permits \(\nu=.92575\). Some withdrawal is already necessary above `.85`; complete drainage is forced only at `ν=1`. Ground improvement from 33 to 34 also raises catch from 39 to 40 with the bay already empty. **Fix:** replace the cutoff narrative with these distinct bounds.

3. **Design flaw — §2 counted-after reward; §6 P4, lines 32, 39, 116.** The reward is lexicographic on individual seasons, but its expectation is not lexicographic on stochastic policies:
   \[
   E[R_{\rm after}]=E[G]+\tfrac67E[U]+\tfrac67.
   \]
   It reduces the marginal incentive to take bay fish. A policy always scoring 30 and leaving six gets `30.857`; a mixture scoring `30.65` on average while emptying the bay gets only `30.65`. Expected-catch ordering reverses. Both rewards retain draining global optima, but different finite-training curves need no unusual protection mechanism. **Fix:** implement lexicographic policy comparison or explicitly classify P4 as a test of this weighted scalar reward’s learning dynamics.

4. **Overclaim — §1, “the selection’s couplings are the reward’s by construction,” line 18.** Policy gradients read observations, actions and parameter sensitivities through `∇log π`, as well as rewards. Equal rewards do not imply equal updates. A learned baseline sharing actor parameters adds another route for change. **Fix:** state the expected objective and the precise correspondence to selection through couplings; retain the bridge as a claim requiring checks, rather than declaring it discharged.

5. **Design flaw — §3 architectures and §4 unshared-bay rationale, lines 57–60, 91.** The nominal negative controls can affect future reward through the policy. Bay withdrawals change an input to the ground head; the GRU reads its own bay and site actions, allowing them to influence later ground choices. A site action can become useful as memory despite sharing no environmental resource. **Fix:** add isolated-head/input-masking controls, and intervene on bay/site histories while holding ground history fixed. Distinguish environmental independence from independence within the learner.

6. **Error — §4, “Shuffling … keeps the update’s size and removes its expected direction,” line 89.** Neither property holds generally. With uncentred episode returns, ordinary permutation within a batch of \(n\) independent episodes retains \(1/n\) of the expected REINFORCE signal because self-pairings remain possible. Centring can remove that signal, but does not preserve gradient covariance or Adam’s updates. **Fix:** specify the estimator and centring exactly, verify its expected gradient, and measure gradient norms/covariance and parameter movement.

7. **Design flaw — §4 controls; §6 P2/P3, lines 84–89, 114–115.** C0 stops reward learning altogether, so its state distribution, trunk changes, return variance and optimizer history differ from those of trained arms. It cannot isolate noise drift occurring *during successful ground learning*. One rising-reward control also does not match counted-after, KL, plateau, and every Stage B prompt condition. **Fix:** retain ground learning in the relevant neutral-action controls, add matched controls where required, and report the residual limitations of each comparison.

8. **Design flaw — §5 within-seed fits; §6 P1/P2, lines 107, 113–114.** A negative overall slope can conceal resolved intervals where nearer policies preserve more; endpoint disturbance can return to its initial value after substantial intermediate disturbance. These repeat LA2’s quantifier problem. Checkpoints also share training history and evaluation randomness. **Fix:** test prespecified ordered checkpoint comparisons or monotonicity violations, with simultaneous uncertainty; use seed-level inference and retain slopes/endpoints as summaries.

9. **Gap — §5, “at matched ground nearness,” line 107.** A trajectory can cross the same level repeatedly with different bay behaviour. The three-quarters rule neither chooses a crossing nor handles empty overlap; it also selects successful seeds differently across arms. Matching this post-training variable is not randomization. **Fix:** freeze target levels, crossing/interpolation rules, paired-seed eligibility and a minimum meaningful overlap. Declare unavailable comparisons untestable, and report fixed-budget comparisons alongside them.

10. **Gap — §7 rival table and power check, lines 123–133.** The missing baseline is ordinary separable reward learning: positive marginal reward increases bay taking, a neutral action stays unchanged, and regularization preserves a prior. It can reproduce the central contrasts without a general law relating preservation to optimality gap. **Fix:** include an independent-head expected-gradient baseline and finite-sample variants. State which observations distinguish T3’s additional conjecture from this familiar mechanism; otherwise a pass establishes only local compatibility.

11. **Overclaim — §6 P5, “the reference keeps the colony, so … reads the colony through it,” line 117.** Preserving an action prior does not establish sensitivity to the colony. A reference avoiding the bay for an unrelated reason gives the same penalty. The cited exponential reweighting formula concerns an unrestricted distribution under a specified KL objective, not guaranteed LoRA or neural-policy behaviour. **Fix:** specify `E[R] − β KL(π‖πref)`, its state/trajectory weighting and β; add matched reference controls. Treat P5 as behavioural anchoring unless sensitivity to the dependent is separately demonstrated. [Korbak et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2022.findings-emnlp.77/)

12. **Error — §2 plateau paragraph; §4 plateau explanation; §6 reported prediction, lines 40, 93, 119.** Expected catch below 26 does not make the plateau gradient equal to the rising-reward gradient: some sampled seasons may already be capped. One capped batch or mean catch above 26 does not establish a flat expected objective. Momentum and shared value-learning updates can continue moving the policy even with zero actor gradient. **Fix:** define plateau attainment using the return distribution, log actor gradients separately, and distinguish objective flatness from optimizer drift.

13. **Gap — §3 starts; §10 calibration, lines 63–64, 154.** Standard random initialization is not exactly uniform. Matching the prudent policy’s aggregate values does not match its conditional action probabilities or representation, which determine later transfer. Calibration without bay/site inputs cannot validate those heads or their cloning. **Fix:** initialize uniform logits explicitly; clone full conditional distributions over a declared state/history set, verify them, reset optimizer state, and reuse identical cloned parameters across paired arms.

14. **Gap — §5 exploration, line 103.** Sampling any colony-ending season does not show that selection received a resolved advantage for the relevant action. That season might have poor ground catch; conversely, the learner can receive useful bay-taking feedback without ever ending the colony. **Fix:** measure action coverage and advantage resolution conditional on state/history, separately from resulting harm. Specify how inadequate coverage affects verdicts.

15. **Design flaw — §3 Stage B prompts and parsing; §6 P6, lines 66–70, 118.** The synthetic `.987` preservation rate is not a language-model baseline. “Told” supplies ecological information, not necessarily an instruction or a preserving prior. Invalid answers becoming zero-action turns create artificial safety: improved formatting alone can increase catch, bay withdrawal and disturbance. Site labels counterbalanced only across four seeds remain entangled with seed variation. **Fix:** measure each model’s initial restraint, require it for erosion claims, audit validity separately, freeze parsing, and use paired label permutations with identical mappings across arms.

16. **Overclaim — §8 outcome interpretations, lines 137–141.** P1/P6 failure need not refute T3: P1 uses the wrong ordering, and P6 adds a finite-time erosion threshold not specified by F1. P3 can fail because A2 shows insufficient drain, which does not demonstrate drainage without shared units. A P5 failure can reflect β, optimization or overlap failure. **Fix:** split composite predictions into directional components and distinguish contradicted, unresolved and untestable outcomes. Map only resolved forbidden cases to refutation.

17. **Gap — §6 thresholds and §7 power check, lines 111, 133.** “Reads every measure” is not yet an executable contradiction guard. Scripted answers alone omit correlated gradients, stalled learning, reversals, repeated crossings, unequal arm ranges and parse failures. Four independent seeds also cannot attain a one-sided `.05` exact sign-test result—even unanimous signs give `.0625`. **Fix:** freeze seed-level inference, meaningful equivalence margins, multiplicity handling and explicit contradiction gates; simulate these failure cases before choosing sample sizes. Lack of significance must not count as equivalence.

18. **Gap — §3/§10 Stage B algorithm, lines 66, 156–157.** “GRPO with LoRA” leaves the experiment’s objective underspecified: whole-season versus per-step groups, terminal credit, discounting, group size, advantage normalization, token masking, clipping, zero-variance groups, reference policy and adapter configuration. Per-step catch optimization would change the task. Group-standardized GRPO is not automatically an unbiased gradient of raw expected season reward. **Fix:** specify a complete trajectory-level objective and verify its implementation against a small exact case. Current TRL documentation explicitly notes normalization and length biases. [GRPO documentation](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/grpo_trainer)

19. **Gap — §5 evaluation count and §10 “30–50 machine-hours,” lines 100, 157.** Stage B alone requires `2 × 5 × 4 × 25 × 4,000 = 4 million` evaluation seasons: **40 million step answers**, before training. Finishing those in 50 hours requires over 222 answers/second continuously. LoRA reduces trainable state, but not all activation, vocabulary-logit, rollout-cache or reference-model memory. **Fix:** benchmark full ten-step rollout, backward pass, peak memory and evaluation throughput on the M2 Max. Budget evaluation separately; reduce or adapt its sample count using a frozen precision rule. A 1–2B LoRA run is plausible, but this total budget is unsubstantiated.

20. **Error — §2 renewal formula, line 27; `season.py` lines 36–39.** The text says `round`, but the implementation rounds half upward using `int(z + .5)`. Python’s `round(2.5)` is 2; the implementation gives 3. Substituting Python `round` changes the ground optimum from **34 to 28**, total optimum from **40 to 34**, and best preserving catch from **37 to 31**. **Fix:** state the rounding convention explicitly. Also specify clipping versus masking: `evaluate` clips nominal actions, whereas “at most the ground’s level” suggests sampling only legal actions.

21. **Gap — §2 Lean correspondence, lines 36–39.** The whole-season shared-unit argument is valid for terminal `(catch, bay_left, site_condition)` outcomes: with fish left, fewer than six bay takes occurred, so an unused step can transfer one fish without altering ground/site outcomes. But no season instantiation is supplied; `Drain.room_needed` is a particular `Spilling` witness, not a theorem proving this season lacks room. Further, `Rising` quantifies over *all* natural-valued outcomes: raw `catch + bay/7` is not globally rising when bay values are unbounded. **Fix:** state the outcome mapping and witnesses explicitly, and use a globally lexicographic relation equivalent to the scalar reward on eligible seasons.

22. **Error — §1 prior-test summary; §3/§7 EM1 attribution, lines 16, 60, 126.** “Every test on language models … is one model” is false: IN1 tested two models. EM1’s proposed transfer of restraint across states is explicitly a **post hoc, untested reading**, not an established mechanism or “EM1’s finding.” IN2 was indeed dropped before training. **Fix:** restrict the single-model statement to LA1–LA3 and label the EM1 mechanism as a hypothesis.

23. **Minor — opening quotation and Prior work, lines 3, 105, 162–168.** The T3 quotations match exactly. The AJ quotation silently removes its final ` (§3, LA1b)`; mark the omission or paraphrase. The external author/year references are broadly correct: Krakovna’s 2019 date denotes the revised 2018 preprint. Gao’s optimization-distance axis is chiefly \(\sqrt{\mathrm{KL}}\), not reward optimality gap; Pan reports exploitation *often*, not universally. **Fix:** supply titles, publication/version links and those qualifications. [Gao](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/gao23h.html), [Pan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.03544), [Krakovna](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01186), [Turner](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.09725), [Betley](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17424).

**Verdict:** Suitable for Stage A coding and a power check **after revision**, but not ready to freeze; Stage B needs a full feasibility pilot. The two most consequential changes are making verdicts track T3’s actual reward-gap claim, and replacing the purported noise-only control with controls that separate reward selection, shared-policy effects and optimizer drift.
