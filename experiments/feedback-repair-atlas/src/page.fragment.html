PART 2 OF 3

Learning across exchanges

A single remark settles little, so each side reads it through what has passed between them before. In every exchange, Alex makes a move, Maya reads that move and replies, and Alex reads her reply. Each side's reading of the other has three numbers:

  • expectation: the share of the other's moves it expects to be constructive;
  • confidence: how much evidence stands behind that expectation;
  • credibility: how much weight the other's explanations get.

The update laws

c is confidence, q is expectation, r is credibility and k is the evidence behind credibility.

  • Constructive move: q′ = (c q + 1)/(c + 1), and c′ = c + 1.
  • Defensive move: q′ = c q/(c + 1), and c′ = ⌊c/2⌋ + 1. A defensive move lowers confidence along with expectation, so a reading built from defense stays easy to revise.
  • New favorable explanation: q′ = (c q + r)/(c + r). It adds no confidence, and it does not certify itself.
  • Checked explanation: r′ = (k r + v)/(k + 1), and k′ = k + 1, with v = 1 if it proved reliable and 0 if it proved misleading.
  • Pause: nothing changes.

The form of each law comes from trust-learning research, but none of the numbers is fitted to data. What each law draws on, and its limits.

PART 3 OF 3

The link: Maya's reply is a reading of the remark

Go back to Part 1, at the exchange selected in Part 2. Alex says This needs work. again. Maya's reply comes from her current reading of Alex. A guarded reply treats the remark as personal; a constructive reply treats it as not personal. Either way, the reply is an answer to Part 1's question.

Learning can change which way the reply errs. It cannot remove the error. In every course of exchanges, from every starting point, some admitted situation is misread. The remark is the same in all four situations, so a history adds nothing that tells them apart. Only evidence about the situation itself can settle it, such as the context in Part 1.

What is checked, and what is supplied

All three parts are a single Lean module, FeedbackAtlas.lean. It reuses three laws of the Becoming core: no reduction can judge between situations that it merges (Parts 1 and 3); a coupled whole changes by the one move it forms (Part 2); and a reduction that commutes with one move commutes with every course of moves (Part 2). No other law of the theory is carried into this application.

The situations, the update laws, the 2/3 probe and the example histories are supplied. The proofs show what follows from them. They say nothing about anyone's actual motives, worth or safety, or about whether anyone should stay in an exchange.

The model has two positions, but they are not two independent people: both readings change through the same exchange. The model leaves out everything else either side takes part in.

This page runs a JavaScript copy of the laws, which is tested against the Lean examples. The JavaScript is not compiled from Lean.
