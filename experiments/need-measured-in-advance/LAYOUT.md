# T1N and T1R on the records as published: the refusal, and how far the rules could reach

6 November 2026. AI-written (a Claude Code session), on [Ren's yes](../../sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-11-06-t1n-t1r-download-and-run.md) to download the records and run both tests. *About 25 minutes to read; the first three paragraphs take two.*

**What happened.** R1 and R5 were downloaded, the layout was read with no value shown, and each frozen command was run once. Both refused at R1's header, before reading a row: the agency's public table of adult estimates has no upper bound and no acres column ([T1N's output](results/run-refused.txt), [T1R's](../stocked-above-the-need/results/run-refused.txt)). T1N's [§9](DESIGN.md#9-data-and-what-has-been-seen) says the reader refuses where a quantity the rules need is missing, both interval bounds among them, and an addendum may change the layout's names, not supply a quantity.

**What it means for T1: nothing.** No need was measured and no fate read. V1 is not decided, and neither test is a test of T1 until a design that can read these records is frozen and run.

**What else the records show, from their structure alone.** Had the columns been there, the rules could not have decided on that table with the stocking database, as the frozen reader reads them. They could read at most 9 lakes below the need with a fate resolved, whatever the table's values, where V1 needs 10, and the need's fit could have about 162 lake-years as the fall tables were read, where it needs 200 (below). The 9 is close to the line, and other readings of the labels or the stocking give 10 or 13, which the section on it says. T1N's [§1](DESIGN.md#1-what-is-tested-and-the-answer-expected) had expected *not decided* as the likeliest outcome, since managers stock and relabel a falling lake; it had not counted how few lakes the published table could reach.

## Why the run stops there

An upper bound could be supplied. R1's lower bound goes below zero in 9 rows, so the recorders' interval looks symmetric on the natural scale, and the upper bound would be the estimate's reflection of the lower. That would not make the frozen command run. `run.py` refuses any input file `downloads.json` does not record with its hash, and that file is committed once and never changed, so no derived table can pass under this freeze; the acres and a table of R2 would be missing as well, and the label's texts would need an addendum. Nor should it pass. A bound read from the other bound would decide the ratio test's error and which readings count as wholly below the need, a reading the freeze never held, chosen after the layout was seen. Whether it is right has not been checked either. It belongs in a design frozen on its own, as [UE31b](../state-named-in-advance/README.md#ue31bs-result) was after UE31.

## How far the frozen rules could reach

[`reach.py`](reach.py) reads from R1 the lake, the year and the label, and from R5 the lake, the year, the species and whether the number stocked is above zero, as the frozen reader keeps a stocking. Each value the rules need is put in by the script, chosen for the lake that favours reading it, and T1N's and T1R's rules are then run unchanged. So its ceilings on lakes hold whatever the table's values are, for this file of R5 and these readings of the label. The output is [`results/reach.json`](results/reach.json), and the other counts here are in [`results/reach-readings.json`](results/reach-readings.json). The script takes the table's NATURAL as natural and REMNANT and NONE as not, and, on the one lake-year whose repeated rows disagree, the natural row. 74 of the 269 lakes with two or more estimates change between natural and not, so the rules read the label year by year (T1N's 5% rule).

| What the rules need | At most, on the records as published |
|---|---|
| **T1N's V1:** 10 lakes read below the need with a fate resolved | **9, whatever the table's values.** Of 399 lakes, 56 have two estimates to 2012 at most ten years apart, with the label natural at both and no stocking from five years before the first to the second, as T1's premise there needs; 22 of those have an estimate from 2013 to 2020 with the label natural and no stocking in five years; 11 of those have a later estimate three to ten years on; and 9 can have a resolved fate |
| **The need's fit:** 200 lake-years from 30 lakes, to 2012, with an estimate and a fall survey, the label natural and no stocking in five years | **About 162 from 101 lakes as the fall tables were read**, counting as surveyed every lake of a county in a year where a GLIFWC fall table's row matched no lake of R1, which errs high since some of those rows name lakes outside R1, and every lake-year in WDNR's three years whose image tables the OCR did not read in rows. 96 from 69 by exact match, with those three years mostly unread. A fuller reading gives 164 from 102. Granting a survey at every lake-year from 2002, the first year of WDNR's lake tables, with the lists before that, gives 177 from 108, so the shortfall does not rest on the OCR of those tables. With no reading of the tables at all: 204 from 121 if every lake-year from 1997, the first lake-level table found, had a survey, and 311 from 155 from 1990 |
| **T1R's V1:** 10 lakes read | 25 by exact match, 35 counted as above, 40 with a survey in every year, but it imports T1N's need, which that fit would not give |

The fit falls short because R1 holds WDNR's estimates and WDNR's lake-level fall tables begin in 2002. GLIFWC's fall tables from 1997 to 2001 list mostly other lakes in those years: 4 of R1's lake-years by exact match.

**How exact these are.** The ceiling on lakes reads no fall table, since a year of the lag unsurveyed does not set a lake aside. The 96, the 162 and the 164 rest on rows read by OCR on page images, matched to R1 by county and name or by lake code, through a list of renamed and misread names ([`aliases.csv`](reach-kit/aliases.csv)), and not checked against the pages. The 177 rests on GLIFWC's list for 1997 to 2001 alone, and the 204 on the reports found. Some rows reach a lake only because the matching keeps one lake for a name that two share: four spring rows for Twin in Vilas, two fall rows of 1999 for Big in Vilas, twelve for Long in Oneida. The lists are in [`results/reach-input/`](results/reach-input/): lake, year, label, the name as parsed and where it was read, with no value. [`reach-kit/`](reach-kit/README.md) has the tools that made them.

## What the 9 rests on

Most of the cut is the estimates' timing, and the last of it is the labels and the stocking. Of the 399 lakes, 130 have one estimate and the mean is 2.6. With the rules' years and gaps alone, 35 lakes can be read. Adding the labels leaves 16, adding the stocking instead leaves 10, and both leave 9. That last step, from 35 to below the minimum, is §1's reason for expecting *not decided*.

So the 9 is a ceiling on this file as the frozen reader reads it, and it moves with the reading:

| Read otherwise | Lakes, at most | The fit on the same list as the 162 |
|---|---|---|
| R5 without its fry rows | 10 | 170 from 104 |
| R5's DNR rows without fry, the database as §3 described it | 13 | 180 from 111 |
| No R5 at all (R1 alone) | 16 | 222 from 141 |
| REMNANT as natural | 10 | 176 from 114 |
| NONE as natural | 9 | 162 from 101 |

Counting fry and private fish as stocking is what the rules ask: §3 reads stocking "of any size", §2's premise asks for giving from beyond "absent or measured", and §5 sets out the stocking a database misses as a confound, so the rows are not an error of the file. A database as §3 expected would have let more lakes in by leaving those stockings unrecorded. Three lakes (2112800, 2391200, 2742100) are each one stocking year from being read, so an error in R5 could raise the 9. REMNANT as natural adds lake 2109800, REMNANT at all four of its estimates with no stocking from 2005 on. `run.py` would not refuse an addendum mapping it so, but GLIFWC's definition above is a population with no recruitment, and §9 mapped it before any of this was seen.

A ceiling also says little of what would be read, since every value is put where it favours reading. Ten lakes at the ceiling would be far from ten lakes in a run.

## What was seen

Up to the refusals and the counts below, no estimate, bound, density, catch or number stocked was shown to the author, and every printed row had its digits masked. Scripts touched two values and kept one bit of each: whether R1's lower bound is below zero, counted, and whether R5's number stocked is above zero, as the frozen reader does. Three kinds of thing were seen: lake names, counties, lake codes, label texts, table captions and page structure; the "LAKE ACRES: n" texts the frozen reader's facts printed, up to 60 of them, which are acres and not estimates ([layout-facts](results/layout-facts.json)); and the counts in this record, each from a kept script ([`reach.py`](reach.py), [`readings.py`](reach-kit/readings.py), [`widen_read.py`](reach-kit/widen_read.py)) that prints none of those values, save a reader's reruns, named where they are quoted. The OCR's output and some of its lists hold the reports' values on disk, unread.

§9's disclosure stands: before the freeze a search summary showed one reader fall-survey values for three lakes, and the author saw only their names. Crane and Chase Lakes are one row of R1 (lake 2237500, one estimate); it is not among the lakes above.

After the refusals, looking for a level by which a successor might set its floor, the author ran a web search and read a plan, and both showed figures from those sources. The plan is WDNR's Walleye Lakes of Concern plan (April 2022, with the Lac du Flambeau Band and GLIFWC), a 950.6 KB PDF not among the files Ren's yes named, fetched by the web tool and kept outside the repository. It gave the author adult densities with years, age-0 and age-1 averages and some stockings for Clear and Katherine (Oneida) and Anvil and Laura (Vilas). The search summary gave statements on the recruitment of Diamond Lake (Bayfield and Douglas) and Worcester Lake (Price) and a fall catch for Fox Lake (Dodge). No such figure enters any count here. A later design must disclose them, and read its verdict also without those lakes and without Crane and Chase.

One reader of this record read R1's and R5's name and county columns and R5's source and age columns, to test the matching, and fetched WDNR's public lake pages with prompts barring fish numbers, seeing names, acres and associations only.

## What the design got wrong

- **§10 argued that the headers could not be read before the downloads, which waited on a yes.** The report's page names its column heads in its HTML with no download, and they name no upper bound and no acres. §9 had taken the columns from a search engine's summary and assumed the rest. This is UE31's lesson again, which §10 quoted and thought it had met. The rows load by script, so a reader who must stay blind reads the page's HTML without running it.
- **The dry run estimated every synthetic lake's adults every three to five years, and surveyed age-0 yearly at 70% of its lakes and in 40% of years at the rest, in archives of 300 and 900 lakes.** It tested the rules against the worlds, and could not show what R1's own structure allows. The ceiling above could have been counted once R1's lake, year and label columns and R5's lake, year and whether fish were stocked were in hand, with no estimate read, and the rules' minimums set against it. R1 alone would have raised no alarm: 16 lakes and a fit of 441 lake-years from 203 lakes.
- **§3 named R1's recorders as WDNR and GLIFWC.** The table holds WDNR's alone. GLIFWC's estimates, about 15 lake-years a year, are in its reports.

## What follows

**Adding GLIFWC's estimates lifts the ceilings, and T1N's worlds still reach no verdict.** GLIFWC's Wisconsin spring tables of 1997 to 2025 give 433 rows, read by OCR and not checked against the pages; 429 have a lake code once names are matched, and they add 422 lake-years to R1's 1,048. On that grid of 405 lakes, as those rows were read, V1 could read at most 31 lakes, or 30 on T1N's own map of GLIFWC's codes. The fit reaches its minimum only on the widest reading: with GLIFWC's NR, C-NR and C- and the 14 rows whose code was not read as natural, 211 lake-years from 95 lakes by exact match, and 287 from 121 on the generous list; with NR alone, T1N's own map, which `run.py` holds, 195 from 90, or 186 from 87 with those 14 not natural ([`results/reach-with-glifwc.json`](results/reach-with-glifwc.json)). R1's own wide NATURAL stands in all of these, so they are high-side counts. The 211 also rests on two renamings, Squaw as R1's Amber Lake and Bass-Patterson as Bass: without both, in a reader's rerun, it is 198. WDNR's lake pages support both, as that reader found.

T1N's own worlds can say more than a ceiling. Their simulated values were put on that widest grid, with its real labels, stocking years and listed fall surveys, 40 archives for each of the 30 of T1N's 48 worlds whose settings the script carries; the other 18 change how labels or estimates are made. V1 was decided in 1 of the 1,200 archives, in the refuted form, with 10 lakes resolved, in a world where renewal goes on below the floor. In T1's own world a need was fitted in 16 archives of 40, and there V1 read a median of 1 lake below the need, at most 3, where it needs 10. A need was fitted in 22 worlds of the 30, and in 35 to 40 archives of 40 in six worlds that have no need, so neither a need found nor none found tells T1's world from one with no need. With a fall survey granted at every estimate from 1997, a need was fitted in 30 of T1's own archives, V1 read at most 4 there, and it was decided in no archive of any world. Those worlds' numbers are T1N's and are fitted to nothing, so this speaks of T1N's rules on this grid in those worlds, not of the lakes.

**So a successor keeping T1N's rules by hash would, in those worlds, reach no verdict on V1.** Whether the lakes would is not known, and T1N's [§4](DESIGN.md#4-the-need) calls no need found a finding; but on this grid that finding would not tell T1's world from one with no need. T1B, on [the board](../../_process/BECOMING-WORKBOARD.md), would therefore be a design with different rules, whose dry run on the real grid counts which of T1's worlds and the worlds against it can be decided, frozen before any value is used. T1's plain world may stay undecided under any such rules. That differs from what Ren was told when he said yes to the reports, a successor that "keeps their rules by hash" ([the question and his words](../../sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-11-06-t1n-t1r-download-and-run.md)). T1B is named on the board and not being worked on. Later on 6 November Ren held paid runs and new test designs until the first readers report ([his words](../../sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-11-06-hold-paid-runs-and-new-test-designs.md)), and whether that reaches T1B, which he had said yes to preparing, has been put to him. The fall tables' values are used only once such a design exists, and its run waits on a further yes, which he has not given.
