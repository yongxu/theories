5 November 2026. AI-written (a Claude Code session). From the second round on, by the coordinator's rule, the same reader, continued, reads the diff of the last round's fixes and the sections it touches, and checks this record rather than trusting it. The reader is given the files, T1's home and the studies it rests on, not the session's conclusions. It works read-only. It downloads nothing, opens no page showing a value of the walleye records, and calls no model. Each round reads a commit on the branch, so the files it read are kept in the history.

**Found after round 1, by the author's dry runs, and in the rework the second round reads:**
- **Renewal pending sets a survey aside.** In T1's own world, lakes below the need came back above it on year classes already in the water: after a winterkill, and in lakes exploited hard, which cycle between strong years. Read as returns, they refuted T1 in its own world. A year class recorded at or above the floor in the three falls before is part of the lake's condition, so such a survey is not read as below the need; it is set aside and reported (§6). The dry run shows what that costs: where compensation returns a lake within a year or two (*survival compensates*), V1 is not decided; a world where it holds a lake below the need for years (*renewal below the floor*) was added, and is refuted.
- **The recovery.** In worlds with no need where renewal fails by chance for years, a survey after three failed falls is followed by a dip that recovers. A decline followed by a rise by the test, before any recorded giving, is not a fall.
- **The first readable survey in time.** A world with no need where a fifth of lakes lose all renewal after 2012 was supported in 9 of 40 archives, because the reference was the first survey below the need wherever it fell: every lake that fell for that cause was read from below, and the lakes left above were those that did not fall. Each lake's reference is now its first readable survey in time (§6), and that world is not supported.
- **The *episodic* world** had q = 1.5, so where renewal truly stopped lay well below the 50% point, and lakes just below the need recovered: it was a world with the need misplaced, not one where T1 holds. It now has q = 3, and q = 1.5 is *weak depensation*, in the need-misplaced family.
- **Worlds added** to the no-need family at the coordinator's request and beyond it: *lakes differ 1.5*, *episodic, no need*, *episodic, lakes differ*, *renewal stops after* and *no renewal in some*; and to the need-misplaced family *need falls far*, *weak depensation* and *renewal below the floor*.
- **The power check, restated.** It asked *need* to be supported in 30 of 40 archives at 0.03, set when the draft gave 36. The rework gives 29 there and 40 with no managers. The check now asks 30 with no managers and half at 0.03. The draft's 36 came with supporting a no-need world in 40 of 40 (N-03), so it is no baseline for a test that supports none. §8 gives why the eleven archives at 0.03 went undecided. The coordinator asked that worlds with a need keep their chance to be supported "as now"; this is where the rework does not, and why.
- **Survival compensates** was refuted in some form in up to 27 of 40 archives under the draft. It is now never decided, because its lakes below the need return within the lag, with renewal pending, and are set aside. Its returns could not be told from those in T1's own world, where year classes already in the water lift lakes too. With the fate read as a measured change and those lakes kept, T1 was refuted in its own world: *refuted unless* a confound in 8 of 10 archives at 0.03, in the author's runs before the rule. *renewal below the floor* is the testable T1-false world that replaces it, and *weak depensation* a second.
