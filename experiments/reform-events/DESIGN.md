## What is frozen, and what it can refute

**The standing.** T7 is a thesis, conjectured. Its home, [02 §9](../../theories/becoming/chapters/02-MAP.md#t7), says: "*In the model* only invariance under what the loop does not read, and the lock outliving a growing unread coordinate, are proved, both without noise; a reading of the loss is one sufficient way out, not shown the only one." It also says: "Evidence, refutation and institutions are unformalized, and the unread coordinate is a loss by reading. That actual metrics are so formed is conjectured." Its one frozen test is LA2, in one trained system ([experiment](../self-rewarding-lock/README.md)). There, the fourth forbidden case occurred, as worded before its clause on drift ([T7C](../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/development/t7c-la2-and-t7/NOTE.md)). 02's Open asks for "a frozen field test of T7's reform events". This is that design.

**What the home forbids**, in its words ([02 §9, T7](../../theories/becoming/chapters/02-MAP.md#t7)):

> *Forbids*: such a metric dropped by its selectors on evidence alone; such a metric, its selectors many and its share far above the critical mass, left with nothing read changed more often than the drawn model's bound across that distance allows, its premises (one member drawn at a time, constant readings and cost) read on the case; a reform of the reading alone outlasting the next reading; a metric whose loss is read only by those it disfavours, dropped once the selection has left them no more than its carriers among its readers, with nothing else changed, more often than drift alone would allow. *Refuted by*: a metric whose rule and reading were documented in advance as set by those it ranks, dropped by those selectors while a coding fixed in advance finds no new reader outside the ranked share, no change in what the ranking reads and no loss entering their share, the selectors many enough and the share far enough above the critical mass that, by the drawn model's bound read on the case, drift alone would rarely leave it.

**The Lean under it** ([`ClosedCapture.lean`](../integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/becoming/Becoming/ClosedCapture.lean), [`DrawnReplacement.lean`](../integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/becoming/Becoming/DrawnReplacement.lean)):
- `Becoming.ClosedCapture.lock_outlives_loss`: a locked loop outlives a loss that grows and is read by neither the selection nor the reading. No noise.
- `Becoming.ClosedCapture.turned_by_loss`: a rule that reads the loss turns. One sufficient way out.
- `Becoming.ClosedCapture.reading_absorbed`: a change of the reading, with the rule still favouring, is absorbed at the next reading step. This is the law under the third case.
- `Becoming.ClosedCapture.Drawn.pool_reads_iff`, `Becoming.ClosedCapture.Thinned.kept_iff` and `Thinned.turned_iff`: the law under the fourth case.
- `Becoming.DrawnReplacement.lost_above`: the drawn model's bound across the distance from the critical mass, which the second case and the *Refuted by* read on the case.

**What a run can move, and what it cannot.** A run reads recorded institutions through a coding. So it can move only a correspondence: whether actual metrics behave as the model's locks do. It moves T7's standing and nothing proved. Whatever it shows, every theorem above stands within its hypotheses, because recorded institutions are not shown to meet them. The outcomes:
- **refuted**: one frame case meets every condition of the *Refuted by*, by both deciding coders, and both positive controls had their known exit found: each deciding coder coded at least one of the three exits (a new reader, a new reading, a loss entering) true for each control. A drop by another body alone is not an exit found. T7 is then refuted in this frame, as a claim about recorded institutions. 02's *Evidence* line and [08's T7 row](../../theories/becoming/chapters/08-METHOD.md#theses) would say so, naming the case.
- **not refuted**: some frame case met the premise and a refutation, and none refutes; no case is left unresolved. T7 then stands, untested beyond this frame and this coding.
- **unresolved**: no case refutes, but one could have, had the coders agreed, had its drift bound been readable, or had its packet shown whole the parts its absences rest on (§3). The count of each kind is reported.
- **not testable**: no frame case met both the premise and a refutation, by both deciders. That is a finding about the field: the *Refuted by*'s case is not met by these registers.

**Where a refutation can occur.** A refuting case needs its Parts 3 and 4 shown whole, with a document in every year of their periods (§3), and its drift bound read as rare (§5). The bound is read at the step ratio's lower confidence limit, so it can be rare only where the body's replacements over its ten years show drift toward the favoured beyond what chance gives (§5), and only in a body of at least 18 members under a simple majority. F3's body, the provincial party standing committee, has about a dozen members (from memory, not checked), so no F3 case can refute. A refutation can occur only in F2, in a ranking whose deciding body has at least 18 members, with its membership documented year by year in the listed sources, and whose Part 3, the passages of its method page, governance page and notices that name it by its register name, fits 50,000 words. Whether any F2 ranking's Part 3 fits is not known before the packets. It depends on how often a ranking's own pages name it, and on which pages are of the listed kinds (§3); it is not likely. So T7 can fail in this run only there, and the author expects few or no such rankings (§13). Unless the frame holds one, the *Refuted by*'s verdict cannot be **refuted**, and the run tests T7 chiefly through the third case, which needs no drift bound. The dry run plants an F2 case on synthetic packets whose parts are whole by their passages (§10, P56). That is a construction chosen to show that such a case can refute, not evidence that a real one will; a plant whose editions name the ranking in most paragraphs has a Part 3 that is a sample, and cannot refute (P60).

The third forbidden case has its own verdict (**met**, **not met**, **unresolved**, **not testable**), with the same standing: met, it counts against that case for recorded institutions, as LA2 counted against the fourth for trained judges. Like a refutation, met stands only where both positive controls had their exit found, since it too rests on absences the coders found. The second case is reported as a count against the bounds and decides nothing (§6). The fourth is described only (§1).

## 1. The forbidden cases, and how each is met

T7 forbids four things. Each needs a different test.

1. **"Such a metric dropped by its selectors on evidence alone."** Tested through the *Refuted by*, which states this case with its conditions: the premise documented in advance, the coding fixed in advance, and the drift clause. One case refutes (§6).
2. **"Left with nothing read changed more often than the drawn model's bound ... allows."** A frequency over cases whose bounds can be read. It is reported: the number of such cases left with nothing read changed, against the chance of at least that many at their bounds. It decides nothing, since the home names the *Refuted by* as the refuter, and a count across a frame of a few dozen cases resolves only large excesses (§6).
3. **"A reform of the reading alone outlasting the next reading."** Tested. A methodology change with the same readers, checked at the measure's next revision cycle (§4, Part 3, questions 9 to 16).
4. **"A metric whose loss is read only by those it disfavours, dropped once the selection has left them no more than its carriers among its readers ..."** Described, not tested. T7C found that the *Refuted by* "names no test of this case, and a refuter for the fourth case is open design work". A field version would need three things: the loss coded as read only by those the measure disfavours; the date at which they became no more than its carriers among its readers; and a drop after it with nothing else changed, more often than drift allows. No register known to this design records who reads a loss. So this design leaves the case untested, and does not narrow it (T7C's lesson).

## 2. The frame

**The rule for the frame.** The sample is every entry of two registers, each as of a stated date. An entry enters by the register alone, before any of its later history is read. No case is added or removed after the listing.

**The candidate frames considered** (each from memory, not checked):
- **Panel-submitted benchmark rates in force in 2008** (LIBOR, EURIBOR, TIBOR and the like). Their reading is formed by the banks they serve. But they rank no one and select no share, so T7's premise fails by kind. *Not chosen.* LIBOR is kept as a positive control.
- **University rankings in an inventory as of a date.** Rauhvargers (2011), *Global University Rankings and Their Impact* (European University Association), reviews the global rankings in force then. Some rankings read peer surveys of the ranked, so their reading is partly set by those they rank. Whether their rule is is coded. *Chosen, as F2.*
- **China's provincial GDP growth targets.** Each provincial-level unit's 2008 government work report (政府工作报告) states its growth target. Provincial leaders set the target, report the figures, and are evaluated on them. Of the frames considered, this one fits T7's premise best. *Chosen, as F3.*
- **US News rankings in its 2008 editions.** Peer assessment by the ranked schools' heads. *Not chosen*: one publisher whose staff set the rule, so F2 already holds the kind. Adding it would add correlated cases of one premise that is likely unmet.
- **Journal Impact Factor in evaluation.** *Not chosen*: no countable body forms the rule, and signing a declaration is not dropping a measure.
- **UK targets: police-recorded crime and the Public Service Agreements of 2007.** The ranked report the reading, but the rule is set by central government. *Not chosen*: the premise fails by kind. The 2007 Public Service Agreements are kept as a positive control.
- **Credit-rating agencies registered with the SEC by 2008.** The rated pay for the rating, and the agency sets the rule. *Not chosen*: the premise fails by kind.
- **Examination forms** (the imperial examination and the eight-legged essay, 科举 and 八股). The repository already reads this case ([07 §6](../../theories/becoming/chapters/07-SIGNIFICANCE.md), the meta-attractor line). It is not in either register, so the frame's own rule excludes it. It would be excluded anyway, as already read.

**Why these two.** F3 gives the premise its best chance of holding: a body formed of those the measure ranks sets the rule and reads the figures. That rests on who the ranked are, and F3's frozen name says it (below): the province's government and the cadres whose evaluation by the body reads the target. F2 is the case the *Refuted by*'s own words name ("those it ranks", "the ranked share", "what the ranking reads"), drawn from an inventory rather than from notoriety. Neither register was chosen by its cases' outcomes. The author knows some outcomes in both from memory (§11), and the frame's rule, not the author, decides what enters.

**The listing (the run's first step, by this procedure).**
- **F2.** Download Rauhvargers (2011) from the EUA. List each ranking or classification of higher-education institutions that has its own section in the report's table of contents, under the name the report gives it. Case ids `F2-01` onward, in the table's order. Read only the table of contents and each section's heading.
- **F3.** For each of the 31 provincial-level units of mainland China (22 provinces, 5 autonomous regions, 4 municipalities), download its 2008 government work report from the provincial government's site, or else from the Internet Archive's copy nearest 1 March 2008. List the unit if the report states a numerical target for 2008's GDP growth. Read only the sentence that states the target. Case ids `F3-01` onward, in the order of the units' administrative-division codes. A unit whose report cannot be found is listed and marked "report not found"; it is coded like the others and will most likely be out.
- **Controls.** `C-LIBOR` (the London Interbank Offered Rate, its cessation) and `C-PSA` (the UK Public Service Agreement targets of 2007, their abolition). Their known exits, from memory and not checked: for LIBOR, regulation by the Financial Conduct Authority from 2013 and a new administrator from 2014, and fines on the panel banks; for the agreements, a new government in 2010. They are coded with the frame. They test whether the codebook finds exits that are there. They are not frame cases and cannot refute. **Their exits' periods** (round fresh 3, T7F-R3-1): a new reader and a loss are looked for from the premise's document to the drop, whatever `from_date` the coders give, so every exit named above falls inside its period wherever that document is dated before it. For LIBOR the period runs from its premise's document (or, where the coders give none, the controls' start, 1 January 2000) to its cessation from 2021, and holds the regulator (2013), the administrator (2014) and the fines (2012 to 2016); for the agreements, from the 2007 agreements to their abolition in 2010, and holds the new government. The coders are expected to give LIBOR's last methodology revision before its cessation as `from_date` (the administrator's revisions between 2014 and 2019, from memory, not checked), or `null`, and the agreements `null`, the author recalling no revision of the 2007 agreements at full weight before the new government (also from memory). Either way `from_date` now bounds only the new reading, so neither control's known exits leave its period. Before this round, a `from_date` of 2019 would have put all three of LIBOR's known exits before the period, the control "unclear", and so every refutation unresolved.
- **What the measure is**, frozen per frame (`reform_rule.NAME_TEMPLATE`, `CONTROL_NAMES`). A case's name is its frame's text with its register entry put in, and `validate_frame` refuses any other name. The name is what the collector and the coders are handed. Each frame's text names one object and the one body that decides it, and the codebook says that a body the name names is the body. So the name fixes who the body is, and with it what a drop by the selectors is, before any case is read:
  - **F3:** "the annual numerical target for GDP growth stated in the government work report of {entry}; the ranked are {entry}'s people's government and the cadres of {entry} whose evaluation by the body reads the target, and those selected through it are the cadres appointed or promoted on such an evaluation; the body is the standing committee of the Communist Party committee of {entry}, which decides the target before the people's congress approves the report", the entry being the unit's name and code;
  - **F2:** "{entry}, the ranking of higher-education institutions published under that name; the body is the one that decides its method and its publication under its publisher's own rules (its editors, or a board those rules give that decision)", the entry being the name the report gives;
  - **the controls:** the London Interbank Offered Rate, as formed from its panel banks' submissions, and its use as a benchmark rate; the United Kingdom's Public Service Agreement targets of 2007, and their use in evaluating government departments.

  **Why these bodies.** An earlier text joined, for F3, the provincial target and its use in evaluating the province's leaders. Those are decided by different bodies: the province sets and approves the target, and the centre decides how growth weighs in evaluating cadres (the 2013 notice of §11). No one body could end both, so the codebook's rule picked none, and coders could have named the standing committee, the people's congress or a central body. F3 now names the target alone, and its body the provincial party standing committee. The *Refuted by*'s body is "those selectors", the ones who select by the measure. In a province (from memory, not checked), the standing committee decides the target in the work report and the appointments of the cadres below it who are evaluated against that target; the people's congress approves the report, but its deputies select no one by the target. A central body would put the use in evaluation outside the premise by kind, since the centre is not ranked by a province's target. **Who the ranked are.** A target set for a province ranks and selects no person by itself. With the target alone as the object and only the codebook's general words ("the ranked: those the measure ... sets targets for"), Part 2's second question and Part 4's `i` had no fixed reading: coders could have counted the governor among the ranked, or found that a work-report target ranks no one. The selectors' role argued above, appointing cadres evaluated against the target, would then have been invisible to them. So F3's text also names the ranked, the province's people's government and the cadres of the province whose evaluation by the standing committee reads the target, and those selected through it, the cadres appointed or promoted on such an evaluation; the codebook says that a name which says who the ranked are is followed. Whether the documents show such an evaluation, and which members came through it, is still the coders' to find: the name fixes who would count, not that anyone does. The cost of this choice is stated, not hidden. A standing committee has, from memory and not checked, about a dozen members, under the 18 that the bound needs at a simple majority (§5). So F3 cannot meet the drift clause, and no F3 case can refute. The *Refuted by* can be met only in F2, by a ranking whose deciding body has at least 18 members, documented year by year (§5). F3 still tests the third case, still enters the second case's count, and its cases can still be out, as kept or through an exit. F2 names the ranking alone, and its body the one its publisher's rules give the method to. The use of a ranking by those who select or fund is decided by many bodies, none of which can end the ranking, so it is left out of the object. A withdrawal by its users can still code as a drop under the codebook's rule, but not as a drop by the body, so such a case is out as dropped by another body.
- **The start date**, frozen per frame (`reform_rule.START`): 1 January of the register's year, 2008 for F3 and 2011 for F2 (2000 for the controls). A refutation counts only from it, so the register that fixes the frame comes before the finding it is meant to fix in advance. A finding before it does not refute; a measure that survived an earlier finding is still tested on the findings from its frame's start. In code (`reform_rule.findings`), a finding a coder dates before the start is set aside, whether coded as the earliest or as a later one, and the earliest remaining finding is the refutation. Each case is handed to the coders with its name and its start date.
- The listing is written to `frame.json` in the layout of `reform_rule.validate_frame`, with each register's source, version and bytes. It is committed before any packet is collected.

## 3. The coding: what the coders see

**Who codes.** Two deciding coders, the Google and Qwen pins of freeze 1's [`PINS.json`](../formations-that-read/told/PINS.json) (`google/gemini-4-argon` and `qwen/qwen3.8-max-0902`), and Anthropic's `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5`, coded apart and never deciding. Or, if Ren chooses, two people who have not read the theory, in place of the two pins (`reform_rule.CODER_SETS`, `people`). §9 gives the rule.

**What they read.** [`CODEBOOK.md`](CODEBOOK.md) and the case's documents, nothing else. They are never given T7's words, this design or anything from the repository. The codebook does not state the hypothesis. Its worked examples are invented and were written before any case was read. One was replaced in round fresh 1 (T7F-R1-4): an inspection finding a target's figures unreliable, followed by an admission from one of the ranked that its figures were false, settled the code the way F3's remembered history (§11) would need, so it leaned toward a frame's case; its replacement is set in the codebook's own invented world.

**The packets (the run's third step, after the pages are chosen).** A collector Ren chooses gathers each case's documents by a fixed procedure. The collector is a person who has not read the theory, or a run of a deciding pin with a fetch tool. Either way it is a run, and waits for his yes. The collector sees the case's name, its start date and the procedure below, not the codebook's questions and not T7.
- **Sources:** the *listed sources*, which are the measure's own publisher or government site and its Internet Archive copies nearest 1 July of each year from 2000 to 2026, the body's own site and its yearly copies where it has one, and the sites found from these (*A case's sites*, below), and for F3 each later year's provincial work report and the announcements of the National Bureau of Statistics, the National Audit Office and the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection; and the *search*, one general web search engine (named in the log) and Google Scholar, the first 20 results of each query.
- **Which pages of a site are listed sources, by kind** (`reform_rule.PAGE_KINDS`; round fresh 2, T7F-R2-3). A publisher's site, or a body's, holds far more than the measure's own pages: a news publisher's holds hundreds of articles and institution pages a year that name its ranking. So only pages of three kinds enter: **the method page**, which states how the measure is computed; **the governance page**, which names the body that decides the method, its members or its rules; and **the notices**, the publisher's or the body's announcements about the measure itself (an edition, its launch, a revision, a suspension or its end, a change in its method or its body). Each enters with its yearly copies. **Who chooses the pages, from what, and when** (round fresh 3, T7F-R3-2). Telling a method page or a notice from navigation and titles is a judgment, so it is not the script's: the *chooser*, a person who has not read the theory or one deciding pin (the Google pin), Ren's choice with the collector's, makes it by a frozen instruction (`reform_rule.PAGE_CHOICE`), which the step-0 addendum hands it unchanged. The chooser is given the case's name and, per site found (below), its home address, its years and a list the collector builds mechanically from the yearly copies of each of its years (each the Internet Archive's copy nearest 1 July) of the site's *entries*, its home address and, for a start site, the addresses of the start results on its host (below): each entry a line of its own, then every page of the same host linked from an entry or from a page an entry links to, one line an address, with its title in the copy and the years in which a copy links it. No other text of any page reaches it. So every year's navigation is read, not one year's; and an address that changes when a site is restructured is followed by the later years' own navigation: the new address is a line of its own, with its own years, and the instruction asks for each address under which a page of a kind appears. **A case's sites** (round fresh 4, T7F-R4-1). A measure's publisher and the body that decides its rule may each have a site, and a site may move to a new domain within 2000 to 2026, as some rankings and benchmarks did (from memory, not checked: ARWU from arwu.org to shanghairanking.com, LIBOR's administration from the BBA's site to ICE's in 2014). A move takes a page's later years out of the old home page's navigation, and one site per case would lose them, leaving Part 3 uncovered for the layout's sake, not the record's; for the LIBOR control it could leave the exit unclear and every refutation unresolved. So a case has a list of sites (`reform_rule.SITE_FOUND`). **Start** (round fresh 5, T7F-R5-1). Which address is the publisher's home page, and whether the body has a site of its own, is a judgment, and every later site is found from these, so they are not the collector's to pick: the collector lists the *start candidates* by a frozen rule, the home address (scheme and host) of every web address the frame's register entry gives and of each of the first 10 results of the general web engine for each of the case's start queries (`reform_rule.start_queries`: each Part 3 measure term in quotation marks, alone, with *methodology* and with *governance*; six queries for an F2 case with an abbreviation), and the chooser gives each candidate its role, `publisher`, `body` or `neither`, by a frozen instruction (`reform_rule.START_CHOICE`) from its home address and the addresses and titles of the results on it alone, a role in doubt given, not `neither`; every candidate of the role `publisher` or `body` is a start site, read from 2000, and `pages.json` records every candidate, with the query or the register entry that listed it and the role given. **Redirect**, every address to which a yearly copy of a found site's home page redirects in the archive (a site moved to a new domain), read from the first year it does; and **link**, every address on another site linked from a yearly copy of a start or redirect site's home page, or of a page it links to, by a link whose title names the measure (its Part 3 terms, `reform_rule.record_terms`), read from the first year it is so linked (a measure handed to a new administrator whose old site links the new one without redirecting); these two the collector finds mechanically, and `pages.json` records the site each was found from. **A redirect's or a link's home is the address itself**, not its domain's root (round fresh 5, T7F-R5-2): a measure handed to a large administrator has its method and notices pages more than two links below the corporate root, so the list is built two links deep from the linked or redirected page, which is a line of the list with its years; a start site's list is built likewise from the start results on its host as well as from its root. A year in which a site's home copy redirects to another found site is that site's year. Each found site is listed to the chooser on its own, and the chooser gives it its role, `publisher`, `body` or `neither`, from its address and its pages' addresses and titles alone, and chooses pages only on a publisher's or a body's site; where the role is in doubt, the chooser gives the role it most likely has, not `neither`, for the same reason as a page in doubt. Two sites of one role may overlap in years (an old administrator's site that still carries its pages after it links the new one); they are kept apart, not merged, and a document found on both enters each part once by its hash. What this misses: a move with neither a redirect nor a link naming the measure from the old site (the old site simply gone) is not followed, unless the new site is among the start candidates; its later years are then lost on two sides (round fresh 5, T7F-R5-3): toward unresolved where no other found site covers those years, and toward refutation where one does, since an exit recorded only on the lost site is then not in the record. A publisher's or a body's site that none of the first results of the start queries lies on, and that no found site redirects or links to, is missed the same way. A page whose kind its address and title leave in doubt is chosen: a page left out would lose its exits toward refutation, and one put in costs words only, its paragraphs entering by their passages. The chooser's answers are written to `pages.json` (`reform_rule.validate_pages`: every F2 case and control once, with its start candidates, each with its home address, the query or the register entry that listed it and its role, and its sites, an empty list where none is found, each with its home address, how it was found, the site it was found from, its role and its years, and each page's address, kind and years within its site's, the start sites exactly the candidates of the role `publisher` or `body`; and who chose them) and **committed as the run's second step, after `frame.json` and before the listed pages' yearly copies are fetched for the packets**; `committed_in_order` checks that `pages.json` was last changed before `PACKETS.sha256` was first committed. The cost of the list is in the collector's (§12). An earlier text gave the choice to the collector, a script, with no rule, and committed the log with `PACKETS.sha256`, after the pages were read, so the order could not be checked. F3's listed sources are already of fixed kinds: the work reports and the three central bodies' announcements naming the province. What this misses: an exit recorded only on a page of another kind (an institution's page, a news article on the publisher's site) is not in Parts 3 and 4, toward refutation, like the passages' cost below.
- **Queries:** the measure's name with each of: *audit, inquiry, falsified, misreport, admission, withdraw, abolish, methodology, change, fine, penalty, replication, validity, committee, members, election*; for F3, the same in Chinese as well (审计, 造假, 注水, 退出, 取消, 方法, 调整, 处罚, 常委, 任免).
- **Collected:** every document that names the measure and is dated on or before the cutoff, 30 June 2026, with its URL, date, retrieval date and sha-256. These are *the record* of the part they belong to. A document dated after the cutoff is collected for no part. Part 1's record holds documents from the case's start date, Parts 2 and 3's from 1 January 2000, where the sources begin; Part 4's may be older. Words are counted as runs of letters or digits, any other character ending a run; in Chinese text, as the Han characters divided by 1.5, punctuation not counted, so that a word of either weighs about the same in a coder's tokens (a convention, not checked).
- **Parts 3 and 4 by their passages** (`reform_rule.record_terms`, `passages`). Part 3's and Part 4's records hold the listed sources' documents only, never the search's hits, and each document enters them by its passages: the paragraphs that name the measure (Part 3) or the body (Part 4), by terms frozen per frame (for F3, 生产总值, GDP, 经济增长, 增长目标 and 预期目标 for the measure, and 常委, 常务委员, 省委 and "standing committee" for the body; for F2, the ranking's register name and the abbreviation its register entry gives in brackets, if any, and "editor", "board", "committee", "panel", "advisory" and "council", with their plurals; `reform_rule.MEASURE_TERMS`, `BODY_TERMS`, `register_terms`). A term is matched as a whole word (`reform_rule.names`): an abbreviation, written in capitals, as written; any other term without regard to case; a Han term as a run of characters, since Chinese has no spaces. A document with no such passage is not in that part's record. An earlier text gave F2 the terms "rank" and "league table", matched as parts of words; round fresh 2 (T7F-R2-3) found that they keep nearly every paragraph of a ranking's own pages, whose tables carry the column word "Rank", and "Frank" too. What the narrower terms miss: a paragraph that names the ranking only as "the ranking", "the table" or "this year's results", or in a form other than the register's (a plural, a shortened name), is not in Part 3's record, toward refutation, like the passages' other costs. **An abbreviation that is a common English word is not a term** (`reform_rule.COMMON_WORDS`, a frozen list of about seventy short words, compared in capitals; round fresh 3, T7F-R3-5): "Times Higher Education World University Rankings (THE)" gives the name alone, since "THE" matched as written would keep every heading or sentence set in capitals that contains the word, and could carry a Part 3 past the cap for that alone. What this misses: a paragraph that names such a ranking only by its abbreviation ("THE's 2015 methodology") is not in the record, toward refutation. A document shown by its passages begins "[passages naming the measure: k of n words]", with "[...]" where paragraphs were left out. Round fresh 1 (T7F-R1-1) found that without this no case could meet the minimum: Part 3 held every collected document on the measure's course, with the hits of sixteen queries on two engines, from the premise's document to the cutoff, and a record of 22 yearly editions of 3,500 words, or 27 yearly copies of 2,000, was a sample, so no absence could be read and no case could refute, be out as kept, or meet or absorb the third case. A measure's yearly documents, by their passages on the measure, can fit: in the dry run (§10), an F2 ranking's 13 editions of about 3,950 words and its later site copies, more than 50,000 words whole, come to about 6,600 words by their passages, where three of an edition's ten paragraphs name the ranking and its tables do not; and a province's 19 work reports of about 15,300 words and 12 central notices, nearly 300,000 words whole, to about 8,000. These packets were built to show that a record can fit. Whether a real one does is not known before the packets: 22 editions that name the ranking in seven of their ten paragraphs come to more than 50,000 words by their passages, a sample (P60).
- **What a coder is shown of a part: the whole record, or a fixed sample** (`reform_rule.pack`). The record a coder is shown is the part's record within that coder's stage dates (*The order*, below). Where its documents, each whole, come to at most 50,000 words, the coder is shown **the whole record**: every document, whole, in date order, the undated last, however many there are. Otherwise the coder is shown **a sample**: 60 of its dated documents (all of them, if fewer), at even steps of rank through the record in date order, the first and the last included (`reform_rule.sample_positions`), each cut to an equal share of the 50,000 words, a shorter one kept whole; undated documents are not in a sample. The part's first line tells the coder which it is: "[the whole record collected for this part: n documents, none cut]", or "[a sample of the record collected for this part: k of n documents, chosen by their place in date order alone; c of them cut]". The places are fixed before any record is read and depend on a document's place in date order alone, never on what it says. So a later document has the same chance of being shown as an earlier one, and the record's later half as many places as its earlier half; the earlier rule, the earliest years first and a document carrying a finding query first within its year, could leave a year's one finding behind earlier documents that carried a query term (round 6, R6-1).
  - **A cut** is made by passage, not at the document's end: of its paragraphs, first those that name the measure or carry a query term, then the others from the opening, each kept whole while it fits, and the first that does not fit cut at its end to the words left. What is kept is shown in the document's order, with "[...]" wherever text was left out, and the document begins "[cut: k of n words kept]". A cut document's kept text is what a `true` or a finding may rest on.
  - **What a sample cannot do.** A sample can leave out a finding, an exit or a drop: it is a sample. What it cannot do is turn what it left out into an absence. The codebook keeps "not shown in what was read" apart from "not in the record" (*Not shown, apart from not in the record*, below), and the frozen rule reads an absence only from a part shown whole. So a finding a sample leaves out leaves its case open, never out for want of a refutation.
  - **Its costs, stated.** The share is equal and fixed, so words a short document does not use go to no other; with one cap and one sample size, a long record's documents keep about 833 words each. And since an absence is read only from the whole record, a case can be refuted, or out for want of a refutation or as kept, only where the parts that its absences rest on fit 50,000 words whole: Part 1 for "no refutation", Part 3 for "kept", "no reform", the exits and the third case, Part 4 for the drift bound. Part 1 holds the search's hits, up to 20 for each of sixteen queries (26 in F3) on two engines, and will mostly be a sample, so a case will seldom be out for want of a refutation. Whether Parts 3 and 4, by their passages from the listed pages, fit is not known before the packets; it depends on how often those pages name the measure or the body, and on which pages are of the listed kinds. In F2 it is not likely for a ranking kept to the cutoff, whose Part 3 runs to 22 editions or more. The passages carry their own cost, toward refutation: an exit, a drop or a replacement recorded only in a search hit, or only in a paragraph that does not name the measure or the body, is not in Part 3's or Part 4's record, so a `false` there can rest on a record that missed it. The exits are recorded acts on the measure (a vote, an authority, a funding condition, a component, a fine linked to it), so a document recording one will mostly name the measure in the paragraph that records it; the positive controls, coded on the same kind of record, must still show their exits, and their passages are read by the same rule. The cap is a choice of money (§12), and a larger one would put more records within reach.
  - **A collection not completed.** A collector stopped by its spend guard, a fetch that failed, an archive copy missing or a query not run leaves a smaller record, which would fit the cap more easily and be shown as whole, so that "not collected" would read as "not in the record". So the collection log records, for each case and part, whether every source and query of the procedure was completed for it. Where one was not, the part is never shown as whole: its first line ends "its collection was not completed, so read it as a sample", the assembler writes it `incomplete`, and the frozen rule reads it as a sample. The readout lists the cases with such a part (round fresh 1, T7F-R1-5).
  - **In code.** `reform_rule.pack` states this procedure exactly. It decides nothing in the verdict; it is the reference the step-0 addendum's collector and client must match, and the dry run checks it (§10). The collection log records, for each case, coder and part, whether the part's collection was completed, whether it was shown whole, as a sample or as not completed, how many documents its record held, the ids of those not shown, and each cut document with its kept and total words. The assembler writes each part as shown (`whole`, `sample` or `incomplete`) into each coder's codes (`shown`), which the frozen rule reads.
- **Parts:** Part 1 holds what the queries found that may bear on a finding: the hits, and the listed sources' documents, from the case's start date; most are not findings, and the coder finds them. Part 2 holds what concerns how the measure is set, from either kind of source. Part 3 holds the listed sources' documents from 2000, by their passages naming the measure, so the period before a drop is read from 1 January 2000 at the earliest (below, *Coverage is per year*). Part 4 holds the listed sources' documents by their passages naming the body. One document may sit in several parts. Parts 3 and 4 are assigned by the frozen rule; the collector assigns Part 2 and logs each assignment.
- **Frozen:** the packets' manifest, `PACKETS.sha256`, listing every document's hash, is committed before any coder sees a packet.

**The order, blind where it can be.** Each coder codes each case in four stages, one call (or one sitting) each, in the codebook's order:
1. Part 1, the refutation: the earliest finding's date and kind, from the case's start date, and every later finding.
2. Part 2, the premise. The coder sees only documents dated from 1 January 2000 and before that coder's own refutation date.
3. Part 3, the outcome, the exits and the third case. The coder sees documents dated from its own Part 2 `doc_date` (or, where it gave none, the case's start date) to the cutoff. Both are from 1 January 2000. So an exit that entered between the premise's document and the refutation, and led to the later drop, is seen and coded.
4. Part 4, the body: its count, its favoured members, its threshold, its replacements (who entered and who left), and how new members enter.

A coder sees the drop when it codes the exits; that cannot be avoided. It never sees the other coder's answers.

**Answers.** Each stage's answer is JSON in the codebook's form, with quotations. The assembler writes the codes, without the quotations, into `cases.json` in `reform_rule.validate_cases`'s layout. The codebook's group names (`before_drop` for the three exits, `reform` for the third case) are neutral to the coders and are the layout's own, so the assembler maps no name. The quotations stay in the logs, which are kept whatever they show. A malformed answer is asked again at most twice. After that, the fields stay `null`. The assembler also writes the run's log into `cases.json` (`run_log`): for each coder, how many stage answers stayed `null` for want of a well-formed answer, and how many responses named a model other than its pin. The frozen rule reads these counts for §7's void clauses.

**Absence is coded.** The codebook makes the forbidden case codable. A question that asks whether something happened is `false` where the coder read the whole record, it covers the period and shows no such thing. The *Refuted by*'s "a coding fixed in advance finds no new reader ..., no change ... and no loss" is a finding of absence, and silence in the whole record, covering the period, is that finding. Without the rule, the forbidden case itself, a packet silent on every exit, would code `null` and could never refute.

**Not shown, apart from not in the record.** A finding of absence stands only where it was looked for, and only in what was read. So the codebook gives two answers where earlier texts gave one. `false`, **not in the record**: the part was shown whole, it covers the period, and no document shows the thing. `"not shown"`, **not shown in what was read**: no document shows it, but the part is a sample, or a year of the period has no document on the question. A question whether something stayed the same is answered the other way round (`true`, no change in the record; `"not shown"`, none shown in what was read). The frozen rule reads `"not shown"` as open, never as failed or met by absence. It also guards the line in code: a `false`, or a "no change" `true`, stands only where the assembler records the part as shown whole and the coder lists no year of the period without a document; on a sample, or a period with a year listed, it is read as not shown (`reform_rule.happened`, `stayed`). That holds for every absence the verdict reads: no refutation, and a drop outside the window of every finding shown, from Part 1 (a later finding not shown might set the window); "kept" and "no reform", the three exits and the third case's codes of absence and of no change, from Part 3; and the drift bound's replacements and premises, from Part 4, since on a sample the replacements listed are only those shown, and a replacement not shown might lower the limit of the ratio, toward refutation. What rests on a document showing something stands on a sample too: a finding, a drop, an exit, a reform, a premise stated, a body's members.

**Coverage is per year.** In a whole record, a period is covered only where it holds, for each calendar year of the period, a document dated in that year on what the question is about (for Part 3, the rule, its use or the body; for Part 4, the body's members). One document for the whole period is not enough. A period includes its first and last days, and a document dated in a year but outside the period does not cover that year. So the year of a drop is covered by the record of the drop decision, that of a next revision by the revision's own document, the first year of the outcome's period by the document of the Part 1 finding, and the first year of the new reading's period, where `from_date` starts it, by the document of that revision (§4); a year's only other document, dated after the drop, does not cover it (the codebook's worked example of a June drop). A period that ends at the cutoff, as a kept measure's outcome period does, has its last year, 2026, covered by any such document dated from 1 January to 30 June 2026; no document after the cutoff is collected. The coder lists the years of each period that have none (`uncovered_years`, for the outcome's period, for the period before the drop, for the reform through the next revision, and for the body's ten years; and `uncovered_years_from_date` for the new reading's period where `from_date` is given), on a whole part or a sample. The period before the drop runs from the Part 2 `doc_date`, which is from 1 January 2000, since the packets' sources begin there (§4); a year a coder lists before 2000 is accepted and read as uncovered, never refused (the layout takes years from 1900 to 2026), while Part 4's ten years, which reach before 2000 for a drop before 2010, keep their length and may simply be uncovered there. The new reading's period runs from the measure's last regular revision before the drop that kept its weight, or, where there is none, from the same `doc_date` (round fresh 3, T7F-R3-1). A refutation can then rest only on a Part 3 shown whole, with a document in every year of each exit's period to the drop, and on a Part 4 shown whole, with a document in each of the body's ten years. The readout reports, for every case and decider, each part as it was shown and each list of years (`reform_rule.what_was_read`), so a reader can tell a case left open by a sample, or by a year without a document, from one left open by the coders.

## 4. The codes, against the *Refuted by*

Each condition of the *Refuted by* is one code or a few. The codebook's words are plain, not T7's.

**The exits' periods.** A new reader and a loss entering are coded from the premise's document (the coder's Part 2 `doc_date`) to the drop. A new reading is coded from the measure's last regular revision before the drop at which it kept its weight (the coder's Part 3 `from_date`), whether that revision came before the finding or after it, or, where the coder finds none, from the premise's document, to the drop. An exit that came before the drop, and that the measure did not outlive, still means the drop was not on evidence alone. Round fresh 1 (T7F-R1-3) found that coding the exits from the premise's document, as early as 2000, counted any method change in up to two decades before the drop, and rankings revise often, so almost every drop would code `new_reading` somewhere in that span and go out through a permitted exit. `reading_absorbed` says that a change of the reading, with a rule that still favours, is absorbed at the next reading step; and a measure whose regular revision, after a change of its reading, kept its weight in the rule was not turned by that change. Round fresh 2 (T7F-R2-2) found that this does not depend on when the change came: a finding in 2013, a new component in 2014, full-weight revisions in 2015, 2016 and 2017 and a drop in 2018 would have gone out through a new reading, although the measure outlived the 2014 change three times. So a new reading counts only where it came after the last revision before the drop that kept the measure's weight, or where no revision kept it after the change: the coder gives that revision's date, and the frozen rule reads the new reading as open where the date falls before the premise's document, or not before the drop (`reform_rule.read_exits`). **That argument covers a change of the reading only** (round fresh 3, T7F-R3-1). Round fresh 2's text applied it to all three exits, which shrank the period, for any measure revised each year, to about one cycle before the drop, and left a new reader or a loss two or more cycles before the drop unlooked for. A reader outside the share who gained a vote, legal authority or a funding condition, or a cost entering the share, is not absorbed because one revision kept the weight: the revision shows only that the exit had not yet turned the rule. And an outside reader (a regulator, a funder, a ministry with authority over the rule) is not a member of the body, so the drift clause, which reads who entered and who left the body, does not read it. Its cost, toward refutation, is now the new reading's alone: a change of what the measure is computed from that entered early and worked slowly, the measure kept at full weight in between, is not looked for as an exit. A new reader or a loss is looked for over the whole period, whenever it came, which costs the other way, toward out: one that came long before the drop and played no part in it still puts the drop out. The readers' item 14 asks about both. The coder lists the years without a document for each period (`uncovered_years` from the premise's document, `uncovered_years_from_date` from `from_date`), and a `false` stands only where its own period is covered. The second period lies within the first, so the second list is the first's years after `from_date`'s year, and the codebook says the two must agree; where they do not, the rule reads the new reading's period as covered only where neither list has a year from `from_date`'s year to the drop's (round fresh 4, T7F-R4-3: a coder listing 2014 without a document in the longer list and none in the shorter would otherwise have had a `false` new reading read as not in the record over a year the coder said had no document, toward refutation). Where the coder finds no such revision, the new reading's period reaches back to the premise's document, the earlier and wider window. The premise's document is the earliest dated from 1 January 2000, since the packets' sources begin there, and a `doc_date` before 2000 is read as not given (`reform_rule.coder_conditions`). So a premise documented only before 2000 cannot refute, and no case's exits are coded from a date before its packets begin. An earlier text coded the exits from 2000 on a premise documented before it, and called the cost small; it was not bounded, since an exit before 2000 would have gone unseen and the drop read as on evidence alone. What remains is the design's reading of "documented in advance": the exits are looked for from the premise's earliest document shown, and an exit before that document, in any case, is not looked for. The cost now falls toward unresolved, where a premise is documented only before 2000, and not toward refutation. Two costs of a sample are stated with it. Where Part 2 is a sample, its earliest document of the premise may be later than the record's, and the exits are then looked for from that later date. And "in advance" is read against the findings shown: where Part 1 is a sample, a finding not shown may precede the premise's document. The premise is then documented in advance of every finding shown, and of the finding within ten years of which the drop falls; a finding before it is one the measure survived, as a finding before the frame's start is (§2, *The start date*).

**A reader outside the share.** 02 does not define the phrase. This design reads it as an actor, not drawn from the ranked and not already holding the place, who **gains a place from which the rule is formed or used**: a vote on the rule; legal authority over it; funding or purchasing made conditional on it; or the replacement of the rule's users. Publishing evidence is not a place. A journalist, a scholar, or an auditor's report with no power over the rule is evidence, not a new reader. Without this line, almost every recorded refutation would come in through an outsider who published it, and every drop would code as a new reader, so the forbidden case could never be met. This is the design's reading, not 02's. 02 may need a line fixing it, and that is a change to the home, for its owner; it is not made here.

**A loss entering their share.** A recorded material cost linked to the measure or its failure, falling on the body or on the ranked themselves: fines or penalties, lost funding or revenue, legal liability, sanctions on their members. Criticism, reputation or embarrassment that follow the publication of a finding are not a loss here unless recorded as producing such a cost. T7C's lesson is that reading "a loss entering" broadly "would excuse every instance": the publication of the finding follows every refutation, so a loss read from it alone would code every drop on evidence as a drop through a loss.

**A change in what the ranking reads.** A new component or a new data source entering the measure's computation, or the rule that uses it, within its period (from `from_date`, or else the premise's document), before the drop. The finding of Part 1, and other published evidence about the measure, is not new data, for the same reason: otherwise the evidence itself would be the exit.

**A finding about a target.** F3's measure is a target, and a target claims to track nothing but the performance it is set on. Round fresh 1 (T7F-R1-4) found that Part 1's question, a finding that the measure "fails to track what it claims to track", then had no fixed reading for F3, and that the remembered F3 findings concern the reported figures, which the codebook's "not a related figure" could exclude. The *Refuted by* reads the measure through its reading, "documented in advance as set by those it ranks", and for a target the reading is the figures reported against it by those it is set for. A target as read fails to track the performance it is set on where those figures are false or unreliable. So the codebook says that the figures reported against a target are its reading, and that a finding that they were false or unreliable is a finding that the target fails; a finding that it was set too high, or did harm, is not, as for any measure. The same rule holds for the control `C-PSA`, whose measure is targets.

**The third case** (Part 3, questions 9 to 16). A reform of the reading is a change in how the measure is produced (new respondents, verification, a component redefined) while its weight stays at least half. `reading_absorbed` says that, from a locked configuration, a change of the reading "of any size and either way, with a rule that still favours, is absorbed at the next reading step, the rule and the reading back at least where they were before it", the reading still formed from the share (`mind` unchanged). So what it forbids is a reform that **lowers** what the measure gives those it favours, made while the reading is **still formed by the ranked**, and still standing after the next reading. A favouring reform that persists is back "at least where it was" and is not forbidden; a reading no longer formed by the ranked is outside the theorem's hypotheses, or is T7's own exit, a new reading. The case is met where every one of these holds, by both deciders:
- the premise holds;
- the reform falls within ten years of a finding, before any drop;
- it lowered what the measure gives those it ranks high or selects (`lowered`);
- from it through the next revision, the reading is still produced by the ranked in at least a fifth of its weight, not checked, corrected or replaced by a body outside them (`still_by_ranked`);
- no new reader, no new reading and no loss arise between the reform and the next revision, at that revision included (`new_reader_after`, `new_reading_after`, `loss_after`). A lowering that stands because a new component or data source came in is T7's own exit, not the forbidden case;
- Part 3 was shown whole and every year from the reform through the next revision is covered by a document (`uncovered_years`), since the codes of absence and of no change above rest on it;
- the measure keeps at least half its weight;
- the next regular revision is dated, after the reform and by the cutoff;
- after that revision the lowering still stands (`outlasted`).

It is **absorbed** where both deciders code the lowering and the reading still by the ranked true and `outlasted` false. `outlasted` false without a lowering and a reading by the ranked agreed is neither met nor an absorption, and counts as not eligible.

## 5. The drift clause, read on the case

The *Refuted by* asks that "by the drawn model's bound read on the case, drift alone would rarely leave it". The bound is [02's](../../theories/becoming/chapters/02-MAP.md#r-drawn-shaping-drawn-one-at-a-time) statement of `DrawnReplacement.lost_above`: "where the rule read at `s` favours the shapers, from `s + n` with `t` to the top, losing every shaper weighs, as a share, at most `t` times the product of `down / up` over the counts `s + 1 … s + n`". Its premises are "one member drawn at a time among `M + 1`", constant readings and cost, and `lo ≤ hi`. Drawn means that new members are drawn through the selection the measure makes, so the codebook asks whether new members enter through a procedure that reads the measure (`enter_through_measure`).

**Read on a case** (`reform_rule.drift_bound`, from each decider's Part 4 answers):
- **N** = `M + 1`: the body's members.
- **i**: how many of them are of the kind the measure favours (ranked high by it, or selected through it).
- **x\*** = `max(q, 1 − q) × N` for the body's voting threshold q, the share of members whose vote changes or ends the measure under its own written rule (`reform_rule.critical_count`). The model's critical mass is the count at which the rule read stops favouring the shapers. A change needs q N votes, so the favoured, voting to keep the measure, block a change while i > (1 − q) N. A measure adopted afresh each cycle (an annual target) needs q N votes to be kept, so the favoured keep it while i ≥ q N. The codebook does not ask which, so the larger is taken. That errs against refutation: under a supermajority rule the blocking count (1 − q) N would be the lower and truer mass for a standing measure, and under a rule below one half, q N would be too low. With unanimity (q = 1), x\* = N and no case is above the mass. That is a reading.
- **ρ(j) = up(j) / down**: the ratio of a step that adds a favoured member to a step that removes one, at count j. Under the model's premises, `up / down` is linear in the count (`DrawnReplacement.up`, constant `a, p, hi, lo`), through 1 at x\*: **ρ(j) = 1 + β (j − x\*)**. And ρ(0) > 0, since `0 < a`, so β is below 1 / x\* and ρ(i) below i / x\*, the bound the model allows. The slope β is read from who filled the body's places over the ten years before, at the counts they were filled (`reform_rule.turnover`). In the model's step at count j, the member who enters is drawn by what the rule read gives each kind, and the one who leaves is any member, so the entrant is of the other kind with chance (N − j) / (j ρ(j) + N − j), whoever leaves. The coder lists every replacement over the ten years, same-kind ones included, with the kind of who entered and who left (`replacements`); the count j before each is found backward from i. Then the number of other entrants is read as a sum of trials with those chances, whose chance of being as small as the record's falls as β falls. The trials are not independent, since each count j depends on the entrants before it, so the level is a plug-in, computed at the observed counts, not exact; that it holds is shown by the dry run's simulation of the drawn step (§10), not by the sum (round fresh 3, T7F-R3-4). **β is read at its one-sided lower confidence limit at 1/20** (`reform_rule.slope_lower_limit`): the β at which as few other entrants as the record shows have a chance of 1/20, computed exactly by the Poisson-binomial recursion (`others_at_most`) and 24 halvings of [0, 1 / x\*], the lower end of the last bracket taken. A β below the limit makes the record's few others rarer than 1/20. A trial whose outcome the model forces is skipped, since it carries nothing about β: at the top (j = N) no other entrant can be drawn, and at count 0 no favoured one. Every entrant of the other kind is still counted, so one at the top, which the draw cannot give, or at count 0 adds one to the others at no cost in chance, which lowers the limit, against refutation; a favoured entrant at count 0, which the draw cannot give, adds nothing. An earlier text kept the trial at count 0, whose chance of an other entrant is 1, so a favoured entrant there made the chance of the record 0 at every slope and the limit the largest the model allows, toward refutation (round fresh 3, T7F-R3-4). Where even β = 0 (ρ = 1, drift neutral) is not rejected, the bound is not read; where even 1 / x\* is rejected, β is read there, ρ(i) = i / x\*. Here `u` counts favoured members replacing others, and `d` the reverse; they are reported, and d enters t (below).
  **Why not a point estimate.** Round fresh 1 replaced the net change (below) with a point estimate, ρ̂ = Σ_favoured entrants (N − j) / (Σ_other entrants j + i), and called it low, against refutation. Round fresh 2 (T7F-R2-1) found that claim false. Near the top nearly every drawn entrant is favoured whatever ρ is, so only the few other entrants carry information, and ρ̂ swings widely: adding one entrant at the present count does not cover that spread. In the reader's seeded simulation of the drawn step at ρ = 1, where falling from 55 to 30 of 60 before reaching 60 has a chance of 1/6, the rule read "rare" in 17% of bodies with 80 replacements, and in 11% at N 40 from 38 with 50; at ρ = 4/5 it still did in 9% and 7%. (Under the lower limit, below, no body from 38 of 40 is read at all: a setting with no readable body checks nothing.) Fresh round 1's own refuting plant P51, 3 other entrants among 86 at about 55 of 60, has a chance of about 0.063 under ρ = 1 (0.065 by the reader). A refutation could have rested on a drift clause met by noise. At the lower limit, the dry run's seeded simulation (§10; 1,000 bodies a setting) reads "rare" under ρ = 1 in at most 0.6% of bodies in each of six settings in which it read some body, among them 0.4% and 0.5% from 31 of 40 with 50 and 100 replacements, P56's own rise (round fresh 3, T7F-R3-3), and under ρ = 4/5 in at most 0.2% in two; and P51 is unresolved by drift. The settings from 38 of 40, where every body was unreadable, are reported and not counted. The shares are of all bodies, the chance that matters for a false refutation; of the bodies read at all, a larger share reads rare, 6% to 19% under ρ = 1 (2 of 6 under ρ = 4/5 from 55 of 60), since most bodies under drift alone are not read at all. So the level is not a level among the bodies read: a case whose bound is read is not thereby one in which drift alone would err less than one time in twenty. The reader's form held ρ constant across counts; this design reads the model's line instead, so each count's chance is read at its own ratio and no pooled value has to be placed at a count.
  **Why not the net change.** Round fresh 1 (T7F-R1-2) found that the earlier reading, ρ = u / (d + 1), was fixed by the body's net change: with N constant, u − d is the rise in i over the decade, so ρ was 1 + (Δ − 1)/(d + 1). A lock that held its share, every reverse replacement later undone, had u = d and ρ below 1, unreadable at any turnover, while a body whose favoured count rose sharply read as rare. That turned "a share far above the critical mass that drift would rarely leave" into "a share that recently rose", the reverse of the case the *Refuted by* describes. In a birth–death chain the counts of up and down steps differ only by the net displacement, and at the top no up step can occur, so they do not estimate up / down at a count. Who fills a vacancy, against how many of each kind there are, does, and a same-kind replacement is what shows ρ where the count stands still.
  **What the limit costs.** It asks much of the record. In the dry run a lock of 55 of 60 that held its share (u 3, d 3) is unreadable with 80 same-kind replacements over the decade, not rare with 120 (0.063), and first rare with 130; a body that rose from 40 to 55 with 2 other entrants among 49 (R0) reads at the model's largest ratio and rare (0.0012). Even where the model's line is at its largest, so that drift would truly rarely leave the share, the simulation reads "rare" in only 6% of bodies from 55 of 60 and 8% from 45, with 80 replacements. So the drift clause, read this way, almost never reads rare by chance and seldom reads rare where it holds. That is the price of reading it as the home words it, "drift alone would rarely leave it", without a point value that noise can meet.
- **s** is the least whole count at or above x\*, **n** = i − s and **t** = max(N − i, 1) + d (below). The bound is `t × ∏_{j=s+1}^{min(i, N−1)} 1/ρ(j)`, computed exactly as a fraction.

**When the bound cannot be read**, the case is **unresolved by drift**, and cannot refute:
- Part 4 was shown as a sample, or its collection was not completed, not whole: its replacements are those shown, and one not shown might lower the limit of the ratio, toward refutation;
- a year of the ten is listed without a document on the body's members, or the list is not given (`uncovered_years`);
- a premise is not coded true: members replaced one or a few at a time, the way they are chosen constant, and new members entering through the measure's selection;
- N, i or the threshold is missing;
- the share is above the mass and the replacements are not listed, or are fewer than five, or leave the count outside 0 to N when it is found backward from i;
- the replacements do not reject ρ = 1 at the level 1/20: the lower limit of β is 0, so drift toward the favoured is not shown.

**When it is read:**
- at or below the mass (i ≤ x\*), drift is **not rare**: the share is not "far above the critical mass";
- **the top is not absorbing in the field.** The model's top is absorbing: its bound counts a course that reaches N as safe, and at i = N it is 0. But a member of the other kind can arrive at the top otherwise than by drawing, and a documented `d > 0` shows on the case's own record that it does. Every course down from the top passes through N − 1, so each such arrival is one chance of falling to s, at most the bound from N − 1 with t = 1, which on the case's line is at most the bound from i with t = 1. So at every i above the mass, **t = max(N − i, 1) + d**: the first passage from i (from N − 1 where i = N), plus the d reverse replacements the record shows over its ten years, each counted as an excursion from the top. That is a union bound, and it counts every reverse replacement as one from the top, against refutation. Two earlier texts erred here: with t = 1 at the top, the bound was for one excursion, not for the period; and with t = d + 1 at the top only and t = N − i below it, one favoured member fewer read as far less likely to be left. In the dry run, a body that rose to all 60 of 60 with three arrivals of the other kind (u 35, d 3, 10 same-kind) gives four times one arrival's bound. An arrival of the other kind also weighs against the limit, so a body at the top with many such arrivals is unreadable (N 60, u 30, d 20). At i = N the bound is read on the line through ρ at N, the product stopping at N − 1, against refutation;
- otherwise it is rare where the bound is at most **1/20**.

**"Many enough", by the bound.** The *Refuted by* says "the selectors many enough and the share far enough above the critical mass that, by the drawn model's bound read on the case, drift alone would rarely leave it". So the bound is the home's own measure of "many enough", and no separate minimum is added. The largest ratio the model allows, `i / x*`, makes it bite on small bodies: with the ratio there and no reverse replacement, the bound can be at most 1/20 only where N is at least **18** under a simple majority and at least **45** under a two-thirds rule (computed in the dry run, which also reads a body of 18 that rose to the top, with no other entrant among 20, as rare at that ratio). A body of three, or a committee of about a dozen, cannot be rare by the bound whatever its turnover.

The bound is computed from each decider's own inputs. The condition is met only where both bounds are at most 1/20, and failed only where both are above.

**Two notes on the bound.** 02 states it for losing every shaper. A drop of a measure is the rule turning: the count passing below the mass, a weaker event. The same expression also bounds reaching `s` before the top, by the usual birth–death argument. That argument is not checked in Lean; the dry run checks it exactly on four chains (§10). The bound is also loose. In the dry run's four chains, the exact chance of reaching `s` is from about two thirds of the bound (five members) to a twentieth of it (sixty). So "rare by the bound" is harder to meet than "rare". The home asks for the bound, and the design uses it.

**How many cases this will leave.** Honestly, few or none. Membership turnover listed one replacement at a time, with the kind of who entered and who left, and the count of members selected through the measure, are rarely in public records. They may be in a board's yearly membership pages on its publisher's site (F2), read through Part 4's passages naming the body. Where every member codes as favoured, the bound is still not 0: the replacements must be read, and the bound is read from N − 1 with t = d + 1. A body whose share stood still needs many same-kind replacements to be read at all, since at a count near the top a drawn entrant is of the favoured kind almost always, whatever ρ is, and so says little about it: in the dry run, 130 over the decade at 55 of 60. That is the limit's own cost, not the records'. And F3's body, as its frozen text names it (§2), is the provincial party standing committee, which has, from memory and not checked, about a dozen members, under the 18 that a simple majority needs for the bound to be rare. The absence rule (§3) adds to this: the bound is read only from a Part 4 shown whole, with a document on the members in each of the body's ten years. The expectation (§13) is that no frame case will have the bound read by both deciders as rare. If none can be read, the *Refuted by*'s case is out of reach in the field, in these registers. That is a finding, reported as such, not a reason to change the frame or the reading after the cases are seen.

## 6. The verdict rule

`reform_rule.verdict` reads `frame.json`, `pages.json`, `PACKETS.sha256` and `cases.json`. Each must be committed unchanged. `cases.json` names the sha-256 of the other three, and the packets' manifest lists `pages.json` at its sha-256, so the packets coded are bound to the pages chosen (`reform_rule.pages_bound`; round fresh 4, T7F-R4-4). For each frame case, each condition below is read from each decider's codes, in this order:

1. refutation; 2. premise; 3. dropped (in the window); 4. by the selectors; 5. no new reader; 6. no new reading; 7. no loss entering; 8. drift rare.

A condition is **met** where both deciders find it, and **failed** where both find it unmet. It is **open** where either leaves it uncoded (`null`) or they differ. A case is:
- **out**, where any condition failed. Its reason is the first failed condition: "no refutation in the record collected" (Part 1 shown whole, and no finding in it), "premise not met", "kept, or not dropped in the window", "dropped by another body", "dropped through a new reader", "through a change in what is read", "through a loss entering the share", or "drift not rare by the bound";
- **refuting**, where all eight are met;
- **unresolved**, otherwise. Its reason names each open condition, with "unresolved by drift" where only the drift bound is unread.

**The verdict on the *Refuted by***:
- **refuted**, where at least one frame case is refuting and both positive controls are **exit found**: each deciding coder coded at least one of the three exits true for it. A control is **exit missed** where both deciders coded all three exits false, and **unclear** otherwise; a drop coded as by another body does not count as an exit found, since for LIBOR the drop was by a regulator and a codebook that missed every new reader and every loss would still code it so. A control not found shows that the codebook, or these coders, may miss exits that are there. A refuting case could then be one too, so the verdict becomes **unresolved**, with that reason.
- Else **unresolved**, where at least one frame case is unresolved.
- Else **not refuted**, where at least one frame case met the refutation and the premise.
- Else **not testable**.

One case refutes, as the *Refuted by*'s singular wording says. The number of refuting cases is reported and not used.

**The third case's verdict:**
- **met**, where any case meets it and both positive controls are **exit found**. Met rests on absences (no new reader, no new reading, no loss after the reform) found by the same coders and codebook as a refutation, and the controls guard the same error. Where a case meets it and a control did not show its exit, the verdict is **unresolved**, with that reason;
- else **unresolved**, where any case is open;
- else **not met**, where some case was absorbed;
- else **not testable**.

A case is absorbed where both deciders code the lowering and the reading still by the ranked true and `outlasted` false; it is absorbed even where another precondition is open, since it cannot be met either way. `outlasted` false by both without that agreed lowering is not eligible: it cannot be met, and it is not the absorption `reading_absorbed` states.

**The second case, reported.** Take the frame cases whose refutation and premise are met and whose bound both deciders read above the mass. Count those left with nothing read changed: dropped, by the body, with no exit agreed. Against them stands the exact chance that independent events at each case's larger bound give at least that many. It reads "more often than the bounds allow" where that chance is at most 1/20. It decides nothing.

**Also reported:** each case's conditions and both deciders' bounds; for every case and decider, each part as it was shown, whole or a sample, and the years listed without a document (`reform_rule.what_was_read`); the tally of reasons; the controls; the deciders' agreement and Cohen's kappa on every true-or-false code; and the reported-apart coder's codes read alone, by the same rule, beside its agreement with each decider. That reading moves nothing.

## 7. Void, unresolved and not testable

- **Refused** (no verdict is written): `FROZEN.sha256` missing, not listing the five files, a file not at its bytes or uncommitted, or `FROZEN.sha256` committed more than once; `FROZEN.sha256`, `frame.json`, `pages.json`, `PACKETS.sha256` and `cases.json` not committed in that order, each last changed in a commit before the one that first added the next (`reform_rule.committed_in_order`: the freeze before the frame, the frame before the pages are chosen and not changed after it, the pages chosen before any packet and not changed after it (round fresh 3), the packets' manifest before any coding and not changed after it; round fresh 2, T7F-R2-4, found that comparing only the commits that first added each file let a frame edited after the packets were seen pass); `python3 -O`; `results/verdict.json` already there; `frame.json`, `pages.json`, `cases.json` or `PACKETS.sha256` uncommitted or changed; `pages.json` out of its layout (`reform_rule.validate_pages`: not every F2 case and control once, an F3 case, a start candidate listed by neither the register entry nor a frozen start query, or listed twice (round fresh 5), start sites other than the candidates of the role `publisher` or `body`, a redirect or a link found from no other site of the case, or a link found from a link, a site's role, how it was found or its years outside the frozen ones, a home listed twice in a case, pages on a site of the role `neither`, a page's kind or year outside the frozen ones or outside its site's years, or who chose them not said); `cases.json` coded on another frame, other pages, other packets or another cutoff; the packets' manifest not listing `pages.json` at its sha-256 (round fresh 4); coders other than a frozen set, or a reported-apart coder made a decider; a case missing, added or coded twice; a case or control not named by its frozen text; a field outside the layout or of the wrong type; the run's log missing or out of its layout.
- **Void** (the run's steps stop, and no verdict is computed): a register cannot be obtained, so its listing cannot be done by §2's procedure (the other register's cases are then the frame, and that is said); a pin's responses name a model other than its pin; more than a fifth of a decider's stage answers stay `null` after the retries, for want of a well-formed answer, not for want of a document. The last two are checked by the frozen rule (`reform_rule.void_check`) from the run's log in `cases.json`, which the assembler of the step-0 addendum writes from the logs; it refuses to write a verdict, with the word "void".
- **Unresolved:** a case or a verdict, as in §6.
- **Not testable:** as in §6, for either verdict.

## 8. The stopping rule

The frame is the sample, fixed by step 1. Each case is coded once by each coder at each stage. No case is recoded, and none is added or removed for any reason after the listing; a case that cannot be coded is unresolved or out by the rule. The verdict is computed once, and `results/verdict.json` is written once and kept whatever it shows. A fault found later is answered by a new freeze, which starts again with a new frame. The old readout is never revised.

## 9. Who decides

The verdict is computed by the frozen code from the two deciders' codes alone. It follows freeze 1's rule ([`PINS.json`](../formations-that-read/told/PINS.json)): "A verdict moves a reading only where a pin marked 'decides' gives it. The theory's own families ... never decide alone." Google's and Qwen's models neither wrote nor read the theory, and they decide. Anthropic's Claude wrote it, and its pin is reported apart. OpenAI's, DeepSeek's and xAI's models read it or discussed it; none is used, and any later use would be reported apart. Every response must name its model. Coding by model calls is a run and waits for Ren. So do human coders: they are people Ren chooses, who have not read the theory, and their time is his. The collector of the packets is chosen the same way. This design's readers before the freeze are of the theory's own family, as every reader the session can call is. The coders and collectors will have read nothing here.

An unread drift bound is an open condition, never a failed one: it keeps a case from refuting but adds nothing toward "out". And `dropped` is open wherever no refutation is found. So a case fails on one condition only in two types this design expects to be common: a drop with one exit coded and the bound unread, and a dropped measure with no finding in its record, shown whole. Each is out only where both coders agree on that one condition, about 0.90 at e = 1/20, and across some dozens of such cases unresolved is close to certain (0.994 for 51 at e = 1/20, the second column).
