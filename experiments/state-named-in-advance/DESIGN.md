## 1. What is tested, and the answer expected

[08 §9](../../theories/becoming/chapters/08-METHOD.md#what-would-defeat-the-reading) says what would defeat the explanatory reading ("reading a subject as continuation relative to a class explains how it goes on") in each of four senses. In the third sense, a class named for a domain, a measurement defeats it: "two members alike under the class going on differently beyond measured error, another class of no more variables leaving less of the next state unaccounted for, or the course changing when what the class does not read is changed". [§8](../../theories/becoming/chapters/08-METHOD.md#known-results) names what would change its verdicts: "A theorem a specialist would not foresee, a prediction that could fail and does not, or a premise derived from something weaker". This design is for the second kind.

**Why not a theorem.** As 08 §9 counts it, every route by which a law reaches continuation has a rival name:
- Of 3,700 laws, 331 reach `«续»`.
- Of those, 82 reach it only at the one class `«一»`, where continuation is the relation itself.
- None of the 75 laws in the records of third parts reaches it.

A definition can always be unfolded, so a theorem using the word proves nothing that a theorem without it could not. UE6 searched the root's laws, within bounds, for results that no neighbouring field states. It found that "A concurrency theorist given the identification above would foresee each one" ([UE6, round 2](../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/development/ue6-beyond-the-sciences/ROUND-2-ROOT.md)). So this design does not look for a theorem. It looks for a prediction.

**Why the test must name its class in advance.** The reading's faithful class for a domain is the coarsest class under which the domain's relation goes alike, the class that is constant for it. That class is the field's own minimal state:
- Nerode's minimal realization of a system;
- the causal states of a process (Crutchfield and Young 1989; Shalizi and Crutchfield 2001);
- a delay embedding of enough dimension (Takens 1981).

Fitted to the same domain, the reading and a mature field reach the same class by construction, so a test of the fitted class can only tie. The reading can differ from the field only where it names a class *before* the domain's state is fitted, and names it across domains. Among the eight theses, one class is named that way: T2's three numbers (§2).

**What is tested.** T2 ([02 §5](../../theories/becoming/chapters/02-MAP.md#t2)) says: "The state of a renewable condition under a draw is its level, its recent giving and one lag; with the draw they fix the next giving as far as anything measured does". It "claims these three numbers for every renewable condition under a draw". Its three forbidden items are:
- "a fourth variable removing a tenth of the residual variance in the median series";
- "the level alone forecasting as well at five years";
- "the lag's coefficients changing sign between series".

The domain is laboratory cultures of the flour beetle *Tribolium castaneum*, with the adult loss set by hand. The field's state there is the beetle's life stages (the LPA model, §3). The data are the cultures' complete biweekly censuses (the Beetle Team archive, §9).

**The answer expected, stated before the run.** T2 and the field are compared in three places. The first two are settled before the run, and the third is where they genuinely differ.

1. **The next giving: settled for the field, by the life cycle.** The pupae counted now are the animals that become the next recruits. They carry the chance events between egg and pupa, which no history of the adults can hold. In the dry runs' primary groups, the pupae remove 0.59 to 0.90 of what T2's state leaves, under every generator in which they are the recruits to be, T2's own law included (§8).
   - So T2's class as worded is defeated by a class of fewer variables, the pupae and the adults. That is 08 §9's defeater, and it is met here by the life cycle (verdict W, §6).
   - T2's numbers add nothing beyond the field's state (V2), unless the stages are not the recruits' source, which the life cycle rules out.
2. **The adults' own course: a tie by construction.** T2 names "one lag" but not where it sits. The life cycle supplies the place: the adults of two censuses back laid the eggs that are now the pupae. With the lag there, T2's three numbers are the field's own state seen from the course (a delay embedding, §3). Both then predict that no fourth number of the course is needed (H1).
   - So H1 tests T2's number, three, which fails where the life cycle is longer (§8).
   - It does not test T2 against the field.
3. **The lag's sign: the one place where the two predict differently.**
   - T2 forbids the lag's effect changing sign between series.
   - The field's model gives each culture its sign without any fit, from the culture's own counts and the field's published constants. The sign is that of the model's slope averaged over the culture's rows: positive where the adults two censuses back were mostly below the turning point, negative where they were mostly above. The turning point is about 76 adults by the 2001 estimates and about 91 by the recalled ones, and a culture is predicted only where both give the same sign (§3).
   - The comparison of the two sign rules (§6) is the separating statistic on the course.
   - In the dry runs at 150 cultures, it goes to the rival in every three-stage rival world with a set loss and to T2 in every T2 world. In the control world it does not separate (11 cultures for the rival against 19 for T2): near a stable equilibrium the fitted slope's sign is close to a coin's (§3). At the archive's likely size, in the rival's world, it decides for the rival in at most 21.7% of groups and falsely for T2 in at most 0.1%. It would decide for T2 in 29% to 85% of groups if T2's law held and the cultures were dense (§8).

The reading expected is "separated on the next step: the rival's state; three numbers suffice on the course", with the sign most likely not separated. If it comes out so, the reading has not shown a class named in advance that does better than a field's state:
- on the next step, the field's does better;
- on the course, the two tie;
- their one contrary prediction is most likely not decided at this size, and if it is decided in the field's world, it goes to the field.

That is an answer, recorded as a finding (§10).

So the test has one candidate. The domain must be one where T2's three numbers and the field's state differ before either is fitted. Three domains were weighed:

- **Fisheries.** MQ4 and MQ5 ([§277, §280](../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/BECOMING-RENEWAL-READING.md#280-a-lag-and-the-draws-response-in-the-field-mq5)) read RAM Legacy, whose levels are assessment outputs. A lag found there may be the assessment's own age structure, as MQ5 said. Data independent of the rival's model would be survey indices. These carry large observation error, so "beyond measured error" would be weak.
- **Grazed grassland.** Production follows current-year precipitation and its legacies (Sala and others 2012). T2 would fail at once to a measured driver of the environment, a failure about exogenous forcing, not about the state.
- **The flour beetle. Chosen.**
  - The censuses are complete counts, not model output.
  - The draw is set by hand.
  - The environment is held constant: a dark incubator at 32 °C, fresh medium at each census.
  - The field's model fixes the state in advance and was fitted to cultures of this archive's experiments (§3).
  - Renewal's delay is longer than one census, so T2's lag has a place fixed by the life cycle.
  - The field's model gives the lag's sign in advance.

The choice has a cost, recorded here: it puts the test where the field's state counts the source of the giving. So on the next step (W, V2) the domain favours the field before any value is read. On the course (H1) the two tie by construction. Only the sign is open between them.

## 3. The rival, as its practitioners state it

The LPA model (Dennis, Desharnais, Cushing and Costantino 1995; Costantino, Cushing, Dennis and Desharnais 1995, 1997; Dennis, Desharnais, Cushing, Henson and Costantino 2001, read in §12):
- L_{t+1} = b A_t exp(−c_el L_t − c_ea A_t)
- P_{t+1} = L_t (1 − µ_l)
- A_{t+1} = P_t exp(−c_pa A_t) + A_t (1 − µ_a)

Its terms:
- L_t counts the feeding larvae.
- P_t counts "large larvae, non-feeding larvae, pupae, and callow adults" (Dennis et al. 2001).
- A_t counts the sexually mature adults.
- The unit of time is two weeks, "approximately, the average amount of time spent in the feeding larval stage".
- The noise is demographic: Poisson for larvae and binomial for survival. It is fitted on the square-root scale by conditional least squares.

**Which experiments, and which estimates.**
- **Dennis et al. 2001** analyses the experiment of Costantino et al. (1997): 24 cultures of the RR strain, three to each of eight treatments.
  - In the treatments, the adult loss was set at 0.96, "by removing or adding adults at the time of a census". Recruitment was set "by removing or adding young adults at the time of a census to make the number of new adult recruits consistent with the treatment value of c_pa".
  - Those cultures are excluded here: their giving is the experimenter's (§5). Only the three controls can enter, in the control group.
  - The CLS estimates are b = 10.45, µ_l = 0.2000, c_el = 0.01731 and c_ea = 0.01310, with c_pa = 0.004619 and µ_a = 0.007629 in the controls (its Table 1).
- **The primary group** is presumably the adult-loss experiment (Costantino et al. 1995), in which adult mortality was set and recruitment left to the culture. Its analysis is in Dennis et al. 1997 (*Journal of Animal Ecology*). That is from memory and not read.
  - No estimates for it were read. A second set is used beside the 2001 one, recalled from memory and not checked: b ≈ 11.68, µ_l ≈ 0.5129, c_el ≈ 0.0093, c_ea ≈ 0.0110, c_pa ≈ 0.0178, µ_a ≈ 0.1108. b, µ_l and c_pa were recalled for the first draft, and c_el, c_ea and µ_a by the design's reader (§13, third round, finding 2). Both are memories of models of one family, not a reading of the text.
  - Its source is uncertain. The reader places it, from memory and with moderate confidence, in Dennis et al. 1995, fitted to earlier cultures, not in an analysis of the adult-loss experiment (§13, fourth round, finding 1).
  - So it is a second regime for the dry runs (generator lpa-recalled, §8) and the other end of a robustness band for the rival's sign (below). It is not the field's estimate for this archive's primary cultures. The layout records which analysis applies.

**How the model fits.** The 2001 paper's figures are a generalized R² of the stage counts on the square-root scale, each treatment keeping its own mean (its Table 3):
- 0.899 for the L-stage overall and 0.985 for the P-stage overall;
- 0.996, 0.995 and 0.995 for the A-stage in the three controls.

The A-stage figure is mostly the level persisting from census to census, not variance in the giving. The P-stage figure is P_{t+1} on L_t. Neither says how well the next giving into the adults is fixed. The noise is uncorrelated between stages, which its authors read as demographic, not environmental.

**Its refinement.** The LPAA model (Brozak, Peralta, Phan, Nagy and Kuang 2024, *SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics* 84(6)) splits the adults into newly emerged and mature, and lets only mature adults cannibalize. On *T. confusum* its fit was comparable to the LPA's, better in four of eight groups. The LPAA's newly emerged class holds callow and newly sclerotized adults. The archive counts the callow adults in its P-stage and the rest in its A-stage, so its counts do not give the LPAA's two adult classes. The strongest rival this archive can read is therefore the LPA. An LPAA-like generator is in the dry runs.

**The state at the delay.** In the noise-free LPA with a set loss, the pupae now are an exact function of three adult counts:
- the recruits into A_t give P_{t−1} = (A_t − (1 − µ) A_{t−1}) exp(c_pa A_{t−1});
- that gives L_{t−2} = P_{t−1} / (1 − µ_l);
- so P_t = (1 − µ_l) b A_{t−2} exp(−c_el L_{t−2} − c_ea A_{t−2}).

The design's reader checked this numerically (error 10⁻¹³) and step by step (§13). So T2's three numbers, with the lag at the delay, are a delay embedding of the LPA's three stages. The two states agree on the noise-free course. They differ by the life cycle's chance: the pupae count what has happened to a cohort since it was laid, and the adults' history cannot.

**The lag's sign, from the same algebra.**
- Hold the adults now and one census back fixed. That fixes L_{t−2}, and with it every factor but A_{t−2}.
- Then ∂P_t/∂A_{t−2} = (1 − µ_l) b exp(−c_el L_{t−2} − c_ea A_{t−2}) (1 − c_ea A_{t−2}).
- At fixed A_t the recruits are G_t = P_t exp(−c_pa A_t). So the sign of ∂G_t/∂E_{t−2} is the sign of 1 − c_ea A_{t−2}.
- It turns at A_{t−2} = 1 / c_ea: about 76 adults at the 2001 estimate c_ea = 0.01310, and about 91 at the recalled 0.0110. This is the adults' cannibalism of the eggs they lay: past that density, more adults two censuses back means fewer recruits now.

**The rival's sign for each culture.** The rule is fixed now. The sign follows from each culture's counts, with no fit.
- T2's law is quadratic in its three numbers, so its slope at the culture's means is its slope averaged over the culture's rows. The rival's matching prediction is the sign of the model's slope averaged over the same rows:
  - sign of Σ_t w_t (1 − c_ea A_{t−2}), with w_t = exp(−c_el L_{t−2} − c_ea A_{t−2} − c_pa A_t);
  - the sum runs over the culture's in-sample rows, and the factors common to every row drop out of the sign.
- The weights are largest where the density is low. So a plain comparison of the culture's mean density with 1 / c_ea is biased toward a negative sign in a culture that cycles. Under lpa at loss 0.73 the plain rule predicted the wrong sign for most cultures (§13, third round, finding 1).
- The sum is computed under both sets of constants (c_el, c_ea, c_pa): the 2001 estimates and the recalled ones, a robustness band. A culture is predicted only where the two give the same sign. Where they differ, the sign is left open within the band, and the culture is counted as not predicted.

The rule is a prediction, not an identity. The fitted slope carries demographic noise, and near the turning point the true slope is near zero, so the fitted sign there is close to a coin's. Near a stable equilibrium, as in a control or at a low loss, the three lags are nearly collinear and the fitted slope is barely identified, which adds more such coin flips. Both push the comparison toward no separation, whichever rule is right. The dry runs measure how often the rule holds (§8).

**Where the two differ, stated before any fit.**
- **On the next giving (W, V2).** The rival reads the pupae, the recruits to be; T2's numbers cannot hold the cohort's chance events. The rival predicts that the pupae remove far more than a tenth of what T2's state leaves, which T2's wording forbids. It also predicts that T2's numbers add nothing beyond its own state.
- **On the course (H1).** Both states are three-dimensional here, once T2's lag sits where the life cycle puts it. Both predict that no further number of the course is needed.
- **On the lag's sign (V5 and the sign comparison).** T2 forbids a change of sign between series. The rival predicts each culture's sign from its counts and the field's constants.

**Equal numbers.** The rival's state for the next giving is two numbers, the pupae and the adults, fewer than T2's three. So 08 §9's "another class of no more variables leaving less of the next state unaccounted for" applies to W as written. If W fails and V2 holds, the field's class meets 08 §9's defeater of T2's class as worded, in this domain.

## 4. The class and the grain

**The reading in T2's terms.**
- The level is the mature adults, A_t, as returned to the culture after the census (§9).
- The draw is the adult loss the experimenter sets, µ A_t. In those cultures the adults surviving to the next census are set to (1 − µ) A_t. A control has no draw.
- The escapement is what the draw leaves: E_t = (1 − µ) A_t, or A_t in a control.
- The giving is G_t = A_{t+1} − E_t. In a culture with the loss set, these are the recruits into the adults, to within the experimenter's rounding of (1 − µ) A_t to a count. In a control, the giving is the level's net change.

**The three numbers.**
- **The level:** E_t.
- **The recent giving:** G_{t−1}. With the draw a fixed share of the level, G_{t−1} = E_t / (1 − µ) − E_{t−1}.
- **The lag:** E_{t−2}, the escapement whose eggs are now the pupae.

The three span E_t, E_{t−1} and E_{t−2}. That is the LPA's own dimension.

**Where the lag sits.** T2 names "one lag" and does not say where. MQ5 read it as the escapement that spawned the recruits, as an age structure gives. Here those are the adults of two censuses back, so the lag is put at E_{t−2}, fixed from the life cycle before any value is read.

This is the field's knowledge given to T2, and it is why H1 ties (§1). The lag one census back is MQ5's position at an annual grain. Under a proportional draw it coincides with the recent giving and collapses three numbers to two. It is reported (`ONE`), and under the rival's model it fails its further-variable item (0.29, §8). So T2 passes H1 here only with the lag at the delay. That position is a specification T2's wording needs. It is proposed with §10's narrowing, not made here.

**The grain.** The census, two weeks. It is the archive's own unit and the rival's, and T2's refutation clause names survey series. The coarser grain, every other census (four weeks), is reported and is not a verdict. There the delay is one step, so T2's numbers are E_t and E_{t−1}, and the rival's state gains the larvae.

**The further variables.**
- For W: the pupae, entered in the rival's own form to first order as P_t and P_t E_t (P exp(−cA) ≈ P − c P A).
- For H1: the escapement three censuses back, E_{t−3} and its square, which is more of the condition's own course.
- Reported: the larvae, and the rival's exact term P_t exp(−0.004619 A_t).

## 5. Fixed choices

**The models.** Every law is linear in the columns named, with no constant: no adults and no giving.

| Name | Columns | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `T2` | E, E², E³; E_{t−1}, E_{t−1}², E·E_{t−1}; E_{t−2}, E_{t−2}², E·E_{t−2}, E_{t−1}·E_{t−2} | T2's state for the in-sample tests: quadratic in the three numbers with every cross term, cubic in the level (ten columns) |
| `H1` | `T2` with E_{t−3}, E_{t−3}² | more of the condition's own course |
| `W` | `T2` with P, P·E | the pupae, the recruits to be |
| `RB` | P, P·E, E | the rival's state for the next giving; E carries the controls' natural adult loss |
| `V2` | `RB` with E_{t−1}, E_{t−2}, E_{t−1}², E_{t−2}² | the rival's state with T2's numbers beyond it |
| `NOLAG` | E, E², E³; E_{t−1}, E_{t−1}², E·E_{t−1} | the level and the recent giving, for detecting the lag (V5) |
| `T2F` | E, E², E³, E_{t−1}, E_{t−2} | T2's lean law for forecasts: MQ5's form with the lag at the delay |
| `M1` | E, E², E³ | the level alone, with the same level law |

**Why so many columns for T2's state, and so few for its forecasts.** T2 names the state, not the form of its law, and a wrong form must not count against the state.
- The cubic level law came from a first dry run. There a stage copying the true law's curvature made a quadratic base fail T2 in 19% of groups of six cultures, against 0.05% with the cubic.
- The cross terms were added after the reader's second finding (§13).
- An iterated forecast punishes every spare column, so T2's forecasts use MQ5's lean form. The level alone gets the same level law, so the comparison reads only what the recent giving and the lag add (§13, second round, finding 3).

**The data.**
- **Scale.** Every count is divided by the culture's largest adult count over its training censuses.
- **In-sample rows.** Every census from t = 4 on, with t + 1 in the run: 36 rows of a run of 41 censuses (t = 0 to 40). The first four censuses are the start's transient, and E_{t−3} is read from t = 3. A culture needs at least 20 rows.
- **Disappearance.** A culture is read through its last census with any animal. Disappearance is in T2's scope, but a culture with fewer than 20 rows cannot be fitted. It is left out of every verdict and counted.
- **Zero columns.** A column identically zero over a culture's rows (an adult count of zero at a regular phase makes a product vanish) is dropped from every model in that culture alike, and recorded.
- **Rank.** It is tested on columns scaled to unit norm, with a relative tolerance of 10⁻¹⁰. Where a comparison's models are still singular in a culture, that culture is left out of that comparison only, and counted.
- **Training and test.** For the forecasts only: the first 60% of a culture's rows, in time order, train, and the rest test. A culture needs at least 8 test rows.
- **Horizon.** Five census steps, ten weeks: T2's "five years" read at this domain's grain, about one and a half generations here (one generation is about three census steps).
- **Floors.** Forecasts and observations are floored at 1% of the training level's peak.

**The groups.**
- **Primary:** cultures with the adult loss set by hand and recruitment left to the culture. Verdicts are on this group.
- **Control:** cultures with neither set, pooled across experiments and strains. Reported only: an experiment holds few controls, and pooling strains mixes parameters.
- **Excluded:** cultures whose recruitment was set by hand, as in the 1997 experiment's c_pa treatments. There the giving is the experimenter's, not the culture's.

**The median.**
- A verdict needs at least 6 cultures.
- The statistic is the median of per-loss medians over every set loss present, each loss's median taken over its cultures whatever their number. A control group is one level.
- This keeps a verdict from turning on which losses the archive holds most cultures at (§13, first round, finding 3; second round, finding 4).

## 6. Rules (all in `state_rules.combine`; each verdict on the primary group)

**The separating statistic for the states.** The excess reduction of q added columns is the share of the base's residual sum of squares they remove, less the chance level q / (n − p), where p is the number of the base's columns (MQ5's S3). It is computed per culture over the in-sample rows. The verdicts use its median of per-loss medians against T2's bound, 0.10. Detection, reported beside each verdict, uses the heteroscedasticity-robust Wald test (HC3), referred to F(q, n − p) at p < 0.05.

**The verdicts.**
- **W, T2's first forbidden item as worded ("as far as anything measured does").** P and P·E are added to T2's state.
  - *The wording holds* if the statistic is below 0.10; otherwise *the wording fails*.
- **V2, the rival's own refuter.** T2's numbers beyond the pupae and adults: E_{t−1}, E_{t−2} and their squares are added to `RB`.
  - *The rival holds* if the statistic is below 0.10; otherwise *the rival fails*.
- **H1, T2's first forbidden item on the condition's own course.** E_{t−3} and E_{t−3}² are added to T2's state.
  - *T2 holds* if the statistic is below 0.10; otherwise *T2 fails*.
- **V5, T2's third forbidden item, the lag's coefficient changing sign between series.** In each culture, the lag's effect is ∂G/∂E_{t−2} at the culture's means, read off T2's law fitted on every in-sample row.
  - It counts only in cultures where the lag is detected: T2's lag columns beyond `NOLAG`, p < 0.05.
  - It needs at least 6 detected cultures; otherwise it is *not testable*.
  - *T2 fails* if both signs occur and the less common sign has at least two cultures and at least 20% of those detected. Otherwise *T2 holds*.
  - Under T2's generator the effect is λ, T2's lag coefficient itself (§8).
- **The sign, the separating statistic on the course.** Two rules for the sign of the lag's effect are compared:
  - **T2's rule:** one sign for every series. Since T2 does not name the sign, it is taken as the majority sign found over every culture with a lag effect, which favours T2.
  - **The rival's rule:** each culture's sign under the field's constants, where both sets of them agree (§3). A culture where they disagree is not predicted, and is counted.

  They are compared on the cultures where the two rules differ, every culture with a lag effect counted, detected or not. In each such culture exactly one rule is right. Noise in the slopes pushes the comparison toward no separation, whichever rule is right.
  - The sign is read only where E_{t−2} is the state's lag. It is *not read* where H1 fails. It is also *not read* where H1's further lag is detected (p < 0.05) in more cultures than chance gives: the one-sided binomial tail of the detections, at a rate of 0.05 a culture, below 0.05. The median can hold while a fifth of a small group needs the further lag (§13, third round, finding 3).
  - It needs at least 6 cultures where the rules differ. Otherwise the reading is *not separated: the rival's turning point does not split the cultures*.
  - *Separated on the sign, the rival's:* the rival's rule is right in more of these cultures, two-sided sign test p < 0.05.
  - *Separated on the sign, T2's:* T2's rule is right in more of them, at the same level.
  - Otherwise, *not separated on the sign*.
- **V4, T2's second forbidden item, the level alone forecasting as well at five steps.** It follows MQ5's S4.
  - On each culture's training rows, T2's lean law (`T2F`) and the level alone (`M1`) are fitted. Each is iterated five steps from every test origin, with the set loss known.
  - *T2 holds* if T2's pooled median |log error| is below the level alone's, and T2 is better in more cultures than not, two-sided sign test p < 0.05.
  - *T2 fails* if the level alone is better on both, at the same level. That is the strong form of the item T2 forbids. T2 forbids the level alone forecasting *as well*, and "as well" cannot be shown at archive size, so the rule reads only "better". This is lenient to T2.
  - Otherwise *not decided*. T2 not shown better is not the level alone shown as good.
  - It needs at least 6 cultures with forecasts; otherwise it is *not testable*.

**How the verdicts combine** (fixed now). The reading has two parts.

*The next step*, from W and V2:

| W | V2 | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| the wording fails | the rival holds | *separated on the next step: the rival's state* |
| the wording holds | the rival fails | *separated on the next step: T2's state* |
| the wording holds | the rival holds | *tie on the next step: both states suffice* |
| the wording fails | the rival fails | *both states incomplete on the next step* |

*The course*, from H1: *three numbers suffice on the course*, or *the course needs more than three numbers*.

The sign stands beside the two parts, and V5 and V4 beside it as T2's own items.
- T2 narrowed to the condition's own course holds in this domain only if H1 holds, neither V5 nor V4 fails, and the sign does not separate for the rival.
- T2 as worded holds only if W holds too.

**Reported, not verdicts:**
- **V3, the out-of-sample comparison.** The rival's mean squared error on the next giving is divided by T2's lean law's, over each culture's test rows. The median ratio and the sign test are reported.
- Every rule on the control group and at the four-week grain. At the coarser grain the lag's sign and the measured error are not computed: there the giving spans two steps.
- The larvae beyond T2's state (`TL`), and the rival's exact term (`TX`).
- The one-step reading: `ONEH` is its further-variable item and `ONEW` its pupae item.
- The lag's excess beyond the level and the recent giving.
- The residual over the measured error (§7).
- Every statistic by set loss, the detections, the sign's counts, the cultures left out, and the cultures with a zero column dropped.

## 7. The measured error

The counts are complete censuses. Every animal is counted at every census, so the counting error is taken as zero. It would be the same for both states, which read the same counts. What no state can remove is demographic: each pupa becomes a mature adult or does not.

In a primary culture that error is fixed in advance, before either state is fitted.
- Per census, it is the binomial variance of recruitment given the pupae, G_t (1 − G_t / P_t), with G_t clipped to [0, P_t].
- Its mean over a culture's rows is the culture's floor.

The rules report each state's in-sample residual variance as a multiple of the floor:
- A state near 1 fixes the next giving as far as the cohort's own chance allows.
- A state well above 1 leaves something unaccounted for beyond measured error.

The floor is reported, not a verdict, because it is the rival's own noise model: it reads the pupae. A verdict on it would favour the rival by construction.

Censuses where the giving exceeds the pupae by more than half an animal are counted and reported, with a tolerance for floating-point rounding. In real cultures they would mean that the reading and the protocol disagree. In the dry runs they occur only where the pupae are not the recruits' source (t2-copy, §8). The floor and this check are taken at the census grain only.

- There are 30 cultures at each loss.

**The rival's world:**
- **lpa:** the demographic LPA model at the 2001 CLS estimates.

- **t2-copy:** the giving is Poisson about m, and the pupae are a noisy copy of the mean (Poisson, mean 1.5m + 5), not its source. No real culture can be like this: its recruits exceed its pupae.
- In T2's world the cultures are dense, so the rival's rule predicts a negative sign in 143 to 150 of the 150 cultures, while T2's law gives the positive λ.

**Recording rules** (fixed now and coded in `returned_adults` and `reading`):

- **Adults counted before the manipulation.** Where the archive gives the adults that way, with the dead counted, the returned adults are reconstructed:

  - recruits are the counted number, less the survivors;
  - the returned number is the recruits plus the set share of the returned number before.

- Deriso 1980; Schnute 1985; Royama 1981; Turchin and Taylor 1992; Courchamp, Berec and Gascoigne 2008; Myers and others 1995; Scheffer and others 2001, 2009; Dakos and others 2012; Zhuang and Hadfield-Menell 2020; Gao, Schulman and Hilton 2023; Espeland and Sauder 2007; Sala and others 2012;
- the minimal-state literature: Nerode; Crutchfield and Young 1989; Shalizi and Crutchfield 2001; Takens 1981.

**Limits, fixed before the run:**
- **Both states enter as linear forms** in the columns named, so a misspecified form counts against its state. The dry runs bound that for T2's law (cubic in the level, every cross term). The rival's form is its own term to first order; its exact term is reported.
- **The floor uses the pupae.** It is the measured error of any state that reads them, and it is the rival's own noise model.
- **The domain was chosen where the field's state counts the source of the giving.** So the next step favours the field before any value is read, and the course ties by construction. Only the sign is open between them.
- **Power at archive size.**
  - The sign rarely decides in the rival's world.
  - V5 is mostly not testable.
  - V4 is mostly not decided.
  - The layout records the primary group's shape before any value is read, and the power at that shape is read from the dry runs.
- **The sign rule** is the rival's skeleton, its slope averaged over each culture's rows, under two sets of constants: one read, and one recalled whose source is uncertain. Where the sets disagree the culture is not predicted. If the layout names the analysis whose estimates apply and its values differ from both sets, the rule stays as frozen; that is recorded beside the result.
- **T2's sign is taken as the majority found**, since T2 names no sign. This favours T2 in the comparison. It follows the archive's mix of losses: where most cultures are sparse the majority is positive, and the cultures where the rules differ are the dense ones.
- **A loss with one culture weighs as much as a loss with many** in the median of per-loss medians. That is chosen, so that no verdict turns on which losses hold most cultures; a single culture's noise then counts as a whole loss's.
- **The test cannot separate T2's lag from an age structure within the adults.** The LPAA-like adults and the stock replacement are of that kind, and V2 does not detect them at the dry runs' rates.
- **The parameters.** The dry runs' estimates come from two analyses, one of them recalled. The archive's experiments may use other strains and losses.
- **One domain.** A failure here refutes T2's universal clause, not T2 in the fisheries.
- **What T2 names.** The verdicts test T2's state at the census, the domain's own survey. T2's wording fixes no grain, and the coarser grain is reported, not a verdict.
- **V2's power against T2's law** depends on the synthetic pupae's noise in t2-copy. A real culture cannot be like t2-copy, since there the recruits exceed the pupae.

## 13. Review

One independent reader, an Opus agent, read this design three times: the first draft, the rewrite that took its first findings, and the revision that took its second. A fourth, narrow check read only the revision that took its third. A cross-model reader was not available: Codex was rate-limited until 4 November. Each time it read the design, the rules and the dry runs, and ran its own probes.

On the rival it concluded the first two times that the rival is fairly stated ("The rival is not a straw"). The second time it added that V2 "leans toward the rival, not T2". The third time it found the opposite lean in the new sign rule, which could hand T2 the sign in the rival's own world (finding 1 below). It confirmed:
- the chance level;
- HC3, the F tail and the sign test;
- the normal equations against QR;
- the §3 derivation, step by step;
- that the dry runs reproduce byte for byte.

Its probes stay in the session's scratch space and are not part of this design.
