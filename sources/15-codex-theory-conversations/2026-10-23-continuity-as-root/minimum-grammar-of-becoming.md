# 文理生生：The Minimum Grammar of Becoming

What must be understood to comprehend anything that arises, persists, transforms, and passes away?

My provisional answer is:

The minimum grammar is not a theory of things, but a grammar of how distinctions, relations, and continuities arise and transform together.

I think the classical Chinese notion of 文 is a particularly promising foundation for constructing it.

A concise formulation:

# 异而相文，相感而化，化中有续，续而复生。

Differences form patterns through their relations; their interactions bring transformation; continuity is found within transformation; and what continues conditions further becoming.

This is my proposed formulation, not a historical quotation.

The important insight is that coming into existence, persisting, changing, and passing away need not be four independent phenomena. They may be four descriptions of a single continuous process, made from different perspectives and over different intervals.

Consider a flame:

- Its material continually changes.
- Its form may remain recognizable.
- Its existence depends on ongoing relations among fuel, oxygen, heat, and surroundings.
- It changes those surroundings, thereby changing its own conditions.
- When those relations can no longer sustain combustion, the flame disappears, although the processes and materials involved continue transforming.

The flame is neither an unchanging thing nor a mere collection of unrelated events. It is a distinguishable, temporarily continuing pattern of activity.

This is the kind of understanding our grammar should be able to generate—whether its subject is a flame, a cell, a civilization, an institution, or a pattern of thought.

## 1. The six conceptual necessities

I would begin with six conceptual functions, each expressible through classical Chinese characters.

These are not six fundamental substances or separate layers of reality. They are six operations that a language of becoming must be able to perform.

| Concept | English                    | What it makes intelligible                                                          |
| ------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 辨 · 异   | Distinction                | How anything becomes discernible; how one pattern differs from another              |
| 相 · 交   | Relation                   | How differences condition one another, forming a larger pattern                     |
| 时 · 序   | Temporal order             | How occurrences have precedence, duration, and timing                               |
| 化 · 变   | Transformation             | How relations and patterns become otherwise                                         |
| 限 · 可   | Constraint and possibility | Why some transformations are possible, likely, or impossible under given conditions |
| 续 · 继   | Continuity                 | How something remains identifiable through change, or ceases to continue            |

The sixth is particularly important. Without continuity, we could describe one configuration replacing another, but we could not explain what it means for the same system to persist while changing.

The fifth makes the difference between describing change and explaining it. It introduces conditions, alternatives, and constraints, without assuming that the rules are fixed or external to what happens.

And 文 is not a seventh substance.

文 is the discernible patterning that arises through these functions operating together.

These statements do not establish a universal systems theory. Their significance is that they offer ingredients for a remarkably compact conceptual language.

Here is how I would reconstruct their relationship.

## 文

可辨之纹理 · Discernible patterning

Conceptual diagram: each function is interpretable through the others. The central 文 is their discernible configuration, not an independent object.

From this, other classical concepts acquire a useful compositional meaning:

- 位 — the position or role constituted by relations.
- 势 — the tendency of a configuration toward certain transformations.
- 机 — a condition or juncture at which a small difference can change subsequent developments.
- 度 — proportion, threshold, extent, or the measure relevant to an outcome.
- 理 — the intelligible regularities in how a particular 文 forms and changes.
- 通 — the capacity of a pattern to continue or adapt through changing conditions.
- 复 — recurrence, restoration, or renewed formation.

Crucially, a system's 势 can reshape its own 位, 限, and future possibilities. A regularity can be temporarily self-maintaining without being eternal.

Thus we need not divide reality into a world of fixed structures and a separate world of dynamic forces.

The discernible configuration itself changes what can happen next.

## 3. Deriving 生、存、变、亡

Once the grammar is available, our original four phenomena can be defined more precisely.

| Phenomenon      | Derivation                                                                                                    |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 生 — emergence   | A configuration becomes distinguishable as a new continuing pattern under some conditions                     |
| 存 — persistence | Sufficient continuity remains despite ongoing transformations                                                 |
| 变 — change      | Some discernible aspects of the pattern, its relations, or its conditions become otherwise                    |
| 亡 — dissolution | The continuity that constituted the pattern can no longer be sustained or traced under the relevant criterion |

An essential distinction follows:

The disappearance of a pattern does not imply the disappearance of everything through which it existed.

A civilization can cease to exist while its language, people, technologies, and customs continue into other formations.

Similarly, a pattern can persist without retaining the same components.

A river is continually changing its water. A community changes its members. An organism exchanges matter with its surroundings.

Their identities cannot be established by demanding that their components remain unchanged.

This idea even has a striking precedent in Xunzi's Zhengming: it explicitly distinguishes changes of appearance from changes of identity, allowing transformation without treating the underlying referent as necessarily different.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://zh.wikisource.org\&sz=32)

維基文庫，自由的圖書館



But this leaves an important question: What determines sufficient continuity?

There may be no single universal answer.

Material continuity, causal ancestry, functional organization, institutional recognition, and information preservation can establish different kinds of identity.

The grammar therefore needs to represent the criterion of continuity, without assuming that every domain shares one criterion.

## 4. The generative grammar

The six concepts alone are a vocabulary. To become a grammar, they need composition rules.

I propose the following as a first reconstruction:

### 文理六则 · Provisional generative rules

异可辨，故有文。 文相涉，故有感应。 感应有时，因缘而化。 化有所限，故有可否、缓急、趋避。 化而能续，则其文存；续不可继，则其文亡。 既成之文，复为他文之缘；相续相化，生生不已。

Again, this is a modern construction written in a classical style.

Notice something important: the final sentence returns to the beginning.

A pattern that forms through relations subsequently affects the conditions under which other patterns form.

And the effects may return to transform the original pattern.

That is the conceptual mechanism behind self-organization, feedback, cumulative selection, coevolution, and the emergence of higher-order regularities.

But none of those specific mechanisms needs to be imposed on every phenomenon. They are possible constructions from the more general grammar.

This matters for your Meta-Attractor theory.

A social pattern can reshape the conditions determining which patterns subsequently arise and persist. Selection itself can become subject to selection.

We can express that recursively without treating an institution, a person, a rule, or an economic incentive as an isolated, self-sufficient actor.

## 5. Can we make it formal?

Yes, at least as a research program.

Let \\(W_t\\) denote a discernible configuration at some chosen resolution and context.

A first representation of becoming is:

\\[ W\_{t+\Delta}\in \Phi_t(W\_{\leq t},C\_{\leq t}) \\]

Here:

- \\(W\\) represents a configuration of discernible relations, not an immutable substance.
- \\(C\\) represents relevant conditions, including what the model treats as surroundings.
- \\(\Phi\\) represents admissible transformations, potentially deterministic, stochastic, or only partially known.
- The subscript \\(t\\) allows the transformation conditions themselves to evolve.

The division between \\(W\\) and \\(C\\) is an analytical distinction. Both belong to the same changing reality, and their interactions must be modeled when relevant.

Then we need a context-sensitive continuity relation:

\\[ W_t\sim\_{\kappa}W\_{t+\Delta} \\]

where \\(\kappa\\) specifies what warrants tracing the latter configuration as a continuation of the former.

This can express all four phenomena, including uncertain or ambiguous cases.

However, these equations are only a formal scaffold. Any arbitrary sequence can be fitted by a sufficiently unconstrained transformation rule.

A genuine explanatory theory must additionally constrain what can happen and generate testable consequences.

Without that, the grammar is descriptive, not explanatory.

## 6. Is this truly minimal?

Not yet proven.

This is where I would be careful about making the strongest philosophical claim.

There are at least three different meanings of minimum:

1. Logical minimum: What conceptual distinctions are indispensable even to state the problem?
2. Explanatory minimum: What is required to explain why particular transformations occur and others do not?
3. Generative minimum: What smallest compositional system can reconstruct the widest range of useful concepts and explanations?

These minima need not coincide.

For example, temporal order may be encoded within a formal transformation relation rather than supplied as a separate primitive. Constraint and possibility may be represented by the transformation relation itself. Likewise, continuity might be definable through a more fundamental account of relational correspondence.

So I would not declare the six characters irreducible.

I would treat them as a candidate minimal semantic basis, then investigate which can be derived from others without sacrificing explanatory power.

There is also one indispensable methodological operation:

观 · 验 — observing, testing, and revising our distinctions against what actually happens.

This is not necessarily another constituent of becoming. It is what makes our supposed understanding accountable to reality.

## 7. The decisive test

A genuinely universal grammar must survive cases in which our familiar assumptions fail.

I would test it against at least the following:

| Case                            | What it challenges                                                     |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A radioactive decay event       | Must every transformation involve feedback or reciprocal interaction?  |
| A flame or vortex               | Can persistence be explained without fixed components?                 |
| A living organism               | Can self-maintenance and adaptive regulation be derived?               |
| A corporation or government     | Can identity persist despite replacing people, rules, and purposes?    |
| An evolving belief system       | Can patterns transform the conditions governing their own propagation? |
| A system with no clear boundary | Can the grammar handle overlapping and observer-dependent identities?  |

The grammar succeeds only if it can express these cases coherently, explain relevant distinctions, and reveal where additional domain-specific principles are needed.

It must also distinguish three claims that are easy to conflate:

- Something is necessary for describing every system.
- Something is necessary for explaining every system.
- Something is a law obeyed by every system.

The third is much stronger than the first two.

## 8. Where I think the deepest insight lies

I suspect our most important discovery will be about 文、理、势、道, and how they can be understood without treating them as separately existing entities.

My proposed interpretation:

文 is the discernible configuration of differences and relations.

理 is what can be understood of its regularities and transformations.

势 is how the present configuration conditions its possible continuations.

道 may name the course or intelligible way of becoming, but whether it also carries normative significance requires a further argument.

These are different ways of apprehending an unfolding reality, not four independent components.

And perhaps this is why the classical Chinese language is so interesting for our project: a compact expression can carry relations, transformations, situational conditions, and implications together, allowing the reader to reconstruct an increasingly elaborate model.

Still, we must avoid mistaking the expressive possibilities of Chinese for evidence that every classical text was already describing the same unified theory.

My current best formulation of the research goal is:

> To discover the smallest compositional language from which the emergence, persistence, transformation, and dissolution of any discernible pattern can be reconstructed, while preserving the conditions under which its explanations can be tested.

In Chinese:

> 究万象之所以成文、相因、流变、相续与消长；求其最简之辞，明其可通之理，验之于万事。

I think this is a stronger and more precise foundation for 《文理生生》 than beginning with a fixed list of metaphysical categories.

One last distinction is worth preserving: this grammar might eventually explain how living and social systems sustain themselves, but it does not yet establish what is good for them. Deriving 善、恶、中、和、仁、义 from this foundation—or proving why some evaluative principles are universal—would be the next and much harder step.
