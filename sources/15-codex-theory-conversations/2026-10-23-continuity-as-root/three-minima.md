# 文理生生：Three Minima of a Universal Grammar

We need all three, and I think they should become three separate but connected research programs.

A crucial correction to my earlier proposal: the six conceptual functions are a plausible starting vocabulary, but they are not yet demonstrated to be minimal. In fact, the logical minimum may require fewer concepts than the explanatory minimum, while the generative minimum may use an entirely different set of primitives.

THREE DISTINCT RESEARCH OBJECTIVES

I. Logical minimum — 立言

Expressibility

What must be expressible for 生、存、变、亡 to have coherent meanings?

II. Explanatory minimum — 穷理

Understanding

What must be known to explain why a particular pattern arises, persists, changes, or disappears?

III. Generative minimum — 成文

Construction

What smallest set of compositional operations can reconstruct the broadest useful range of patterns and explanations?

These correspond to three different standards of success:

- Can we say it?
- Can we explain why?
- Can we construct the explanation from simpler principles?

Their relationship is important, but they should not be conflated.

## I. Logical minimum — 立言之本

What must be conceivable for becoming to be intelligible at all?

### Candidate: 辨、时、续

I believe the logical minimum may require only three independent semantic roles.

| Primitive          | What it enables                                          | What happens without it                                                     |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 辨 — distinction    | Recognizing a pattern, presence, absence, and difference | Nothing determinate can be identified or compared                           |
| 时 — temporal order | Distinguishing before, after, duration, and succession   | Emergence, persistence, and disappearance lose their temporal meanings      |
| 续 — continuity     | Reidentifying a pattern through successive occurrences   | We cannot distinguish one changing system from successive unrelated systems |

Here, 辨 does not presuppose independently existing objects. It means that some difference can be made or recognized.

时 need not be an external universal clock. It may be merely an ordering of occurrences.

续 does not mean that something remains materially unchanged. It means there is a defensible criterion for tracing a continuation.

### Formal reconstruction

Suppose we have discernible occurrences \\(e\\), equipped with:

\\[ \mathcal L_0=(D,\prec,\rightsquigarrow\_\kappa) \\]

where:

- \\(D\\): distinctions and discernible attributes;
- \\(\prec\\): an ordering of occurrences;
- \\(\rightsquigarrow\_\kappa\\): a continuity relation under criterion \\(\kappa\\).

Then we can already define:

|   | Definition                                                |
| - | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| 生 | A distinguishable continuing pattern begins               |
| 存 | A pattern can be traced across successive occurrences     |
| 变 | Its discernible attributes differ along that continuation |
| 亡 | The relevant continuation terminates                      |

Notice that 变 does not necessarily require an additional logical primitive. It can be derived by comparing distinguishable occurrences connected by continuity.

Likewise, a boundary can be supplied by the criterion of distinction rather than postulated as an independently existing wall around the system.

### An important result

At this logical level, causality, feedback, adaptation, equilibrium, selection, and optimization are not necessary assumptions.

We can coherently describe the arrival and disappearance of a pattern without yet knowing why either happened.

This distinction matters enormously. Otherwise we may mistakenly elevate features of particular natural systems into universal requirements of existence.

The deletion test is straightforward:

1. Remove 辨: there is no determinate pattern to discuss.
2. Remove 时: there is no before or after.
3. Remove 续: there is no basis for identifying persistence through change.

This suggests a compact logical foundation:

> 有别而可识，有时而可序，有续而可言存变。

But it is not yet a theorem of irreducibility. Alternative formal languages could encode these functions differently, and the criterion \\(\kappa\\) requires further examination.

We have identified three necessary roles, not proved that precisely three primitive symbols are necessary in every formalization.

## II. Explanatory minimum — 穷理之本

What must we know to understand why a particular transformation occurs rather than another?

### Candidate: 缘、限、验

Logical description tells us what happened. Explanation must account for why that happened under those conditions.

Consider a room that remains at 22°C for several hours.

Two very different mechanisms could produce the same observation:

- The room is in thermal equilibrium with its surroundings.
- A thermostat continually compensates for heat loss.

Both satisfy the logical grammar: a distinguishable configuration persists through time.

But they behave differently when a window opens.

The difference between the two explanations lies in their conditional dynamics.

I propose three additional conceptual functions:

| Concept | Function                              | Central question                                                                  |
| ------- | ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 缘       | Conditional dependence                | What circumstances and interactions make this development possible?               |
| 限       | Constraints and possible alternatives | Why does this transformation occur within these possibilities rather than others? |
| 验       | Discriminating evidence               | What observations or interventions could show that our explanation is mistaken?   |

Here, 缘 should not be understood as a simple linear chain of causes. Conditions and conditioned patterns may mutually constitute and transform one another.

Likewise, 限 need not refer to fixed laws imposed from outside a system. Constraints may arise within processes and subsequently change.

### The explanatory requirement

A model must represent a set of possible developments, together with what determines their actualization or relative likelihood.

For a configuration \\(W\\), history \\(H\\), and conditions \\(C\\):

\\[ P(W\_{t+\Delta}\mid H_t,C_t,M) \\]

where \\(M\\) denotes a proposed mechanism.

This probability notation is illustrative. Some models are deterministic; others specify possibilities without assigning precise probabilities.

But merely reproducing the observed history is insufficient.

We must ask what the mechanism predicts under changed conditions:

\\[ P(Y\mid \operatorname{do}(X=x),M) \\]

This connects to the distinction in modern causal inference between association, intervention, and counterfactual reasoning. Judea Pearl's structural causal framework formalizes these different questions.&#x20;

For our theory, interventions are a powerful way to discriminate explanations, although some systems cannot be experimentally manipulated and must be investigated through natural variation, comparison, or other evidence.

### What does this add?

The explanatory grammar enables us to derive more sophisticated concepts:

| Derived concept | Meaning                                                                 |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 因               | A relevant causal or conditional dependence                             |
| 势               | The tendencies of a configuration toward possible developments          |
| 机               | A consequential juncture or sensitivity to a difference                 |
| 度               | A quantity, extent, ratio, or threshold relevant to a transformation    |
| 制               | Constraints that maintain or restrict particular developments           |
| 通               | Transformation that enables continued operation                         |
| 穷               | Exhaustion or obstruction of a currently available mode of continuation |

These are not universally separate mechanisms. They are reusable descriptions of relationships between conditions and developments.

For example, the classical formulation 穷则变，变则通，通则久 expresses a particular connection between reaching limits, transforming, and continuing.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://zh.wikisource.org\&sz=32)

维基文库，自由的图书馆



But it must not be promoted into an unconditional law. Some systems reach a limit and simply cease; transformation does not guarantee successful continuation.

### A crucial test: observational equivalence

Suppose two theories explain every event observed so far.

One says a social institution persists because its participants voluntarily reproduce its practices.

Another says it persists because penalties prevent participants from leaving.

If the theories make identical predictions under every available condition, we cannot yet empirically distinguish them.

If they disagree about what happens when penalties disappear, we have found a potentially discriminating test.

This is the explanatory minimum in action.

We should also recognize an epistemic limit: causal structures are not always identifiable from available evidence. Even interventions can fail to identify mechanisms without additional structural assumptions, as contemporary causal-discovery research demonstrates.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://proceedings.mlr.press\&sz=32)

proceedings.mlr.press



Thus:

> 察其缘，明其限，辨其异变之所由，而以实证之。

My proposed explanatory minimum is therefore:

\\[ \boxed{\text{缘}+\text{限}+\text{验}} \\]

Built upon the logical ability to distinguish, order, and trace patterns.

## III. Generative minimum — 成文之本

What smallest set of compositional operations can construct the widest range of useful models?

This is the most ambitious of the three.

The objective is no longer to list necessary concepts. It is to find a small number of operations capable of generating the larger conceptual vocabulary.

Here I would begin with three candidate operation families:

# 辨

Distinguish

Make differences discernible

# 相

Relate

Compose relational patterns

# 化

Transform

Produce conditional change

These are candidates for a generative basis, not yet demonstrated primitives.

The grammar must also permit two kinds of composition:

1. 合成 — combining operations into larger constructions.
2. 递归 — applying constructions repeatedly, including to configurations that encode their own transformation rules.

That second capability is especially important for your Meta-Attractor work.

### What might be generated?

| Derived notion | Candidate construction                                                    |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 文              | Distinguishable relational configuration                                  |
| 位              | Relations locating a distinction within a configuration                   |
| 界              | A context-dependent distinction between included and excluded occurrences |
| 时              | Ordering induced or represented across transformations                    |
| 势              | Conditional tendencies across possible transformations                    |
| 机              | Sensitivity of subsequent transformations to particular differences       |
| 续              | Traceable correspondence through successive transformations               |
| 生              | Formation of a newly distinguishable continuing pattern                   |
| 存              | Sustained traceability through change                                     |
| 亡              | Failure or termination of that traceability                               |
| 理              | A compact, supported account of regularities and conditions               |
| 复              | Recurrence or iteration of a configuration or operation                   |

The critical challenge is whether all these constructions can be made precise and non-circular.

For example, saying that 化 means “any possible transformation” hides the entire problem inside one word.

That would be syntactically minimal but explanatorily empty.

We need specified, typed transformation rules whose expressive capabilities can be studied.

### A mathematical formulation

Let a candidate grammar be:

\\[ G=(\Sigma,\mathcal O,\mathcal R,\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket) \\]

where:

- \\(\Sigma\\) supplies basic expressions;
- \\(\mathcal O\\) supplies construction operations;
- \\(\mathcal R\\) supplies valid composition and recursion rules;
- \\(\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket\\) maps expressions into interpretations or models.

Then the research question becomes:

\\[ G^\*=\arg\min_G \operatorname{Complexity}(G) \\]

subject to adequate coverage, faithful interpretation, and explanatory performance over a specified class of systems.

But even this is incomplete.

### Why the shortest grammar might be the worst grammar

A universal programming language can encode an enormous variety of systems.

Yet being able to encode a phenomenon is not the same as understanding it.

One could create a language with a single primitive meaning “execute this arbitrarily complicated model.”

Its vocabulary would be tiny, but its actual explanatory content would be hidden elsewhere.

This is why the number of primitive symbols cannot be our sole objective.

We need to measure the complexity of the grammar and the descriptions required to use it.

The Minimum Description Length approach, originating in Jorma Rissanen's work, provides a relevant foundation for evaluating representations through their total description costs.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://research.ibm.com\&sz=32)

IBM Research



For our project, I would evaluate grammar candidates along several dimensions:

- Compression: How economically can a theory express many systems?
- Reconstruction: Can the grammar recover the distinctions and mechanisms that matter?
- Transfer: Can the same concepts explain unfamiliar systems without adding ad hoc primitives?
- Causal fidelity: Can constructed models distinguish different mechanisms and their consequences?
- Interpretability: Can we understand the composition, rather than merely execute it?
- Robustness: Does the grammar preserve useful meaning across changes of representation and observational scale?

These objectives may conflict. Rather than immediately collapsing them into one score, we should investigate their Pareto frontier.

This gives the generative minimum a stronger objective than merely counting Chinese characters.

## IV. How the three minima fit together

The three results should be connected through explicit derivations.

逻辑核心 · Logical basis

解释机制 · Explanatory basis

## 缘 · 限 · 验

Why does one possible development occur rather than another?

Seek constructive equivalence

生成文法 · Generative basis

## 辨 · 相 · 化

With composition and recursion, can the concepts and explanatory mechanisms above be constructed?

This is a diagram of our research dependencies, not a claim that reality has three separate levels.

The generative grammar need not literally contain the other primitives. Its task is to reconstruct their functions without losing information essential to understanding.

That gives us three distinct proof obligations.

| Program     | What we must demonstrate                                                            | What would falsify the claim                                                         |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Logical     | Necessary semantic roles and precise derivations of 生、存、变、亡                         | A supposedly indispensable role can be removed without losing expressive power       |
| Explanatory | Adequate representations of dependence, alternatives, and evidential discrimination | The framework cannot distinguish causally different systems relevant to the question |
| Generative  | Constructive derivations with small total description cost across domains           | A concept cannot be generated, or a simpler grammar consistently performs better     |

## V. One example, examined three ways

Take an institution that has existed for fifty years, changed its leadership and rules repeatedly, and eventually dissolved.

Logical analysis — 立言

We ask which occurrences belong to the institution's history and what criterion justifies continuity.

We distinguish its beginning, periods of recognizable continuation, transformations, and eventual dissolution.

This gives us a coherent account of its existence without explaining its causes.

Explanatory analysis — 穷理

We investigate the interaction of resources, shared beliefs, obligations, incentives, practices, and surrounding institutions.

Some of these reinforce the institution's continuation. Others undermine it.

Rules affect behavior; behavior reproduces or alters rules. Resource flows change power relationships, and those relationships change how resources are allocated.

We investigate which differences matter, under which conditions, and how the resulting explanations could be tested.

Generative analysis — 成文

We attempt to reconstruct the entire account through a small grammar:

- Distinctions emerge among practices and roles.
- Relations among these distinctions create recurring configurations.
- Local interactions alter the configurations and their conditions.
- Iteration and feedback produce persistence, transformation, and eventually dissolution.

The crucial question is whether these phenomena can be reconstructed from the grammar's formal operations, without silently inserting a ready-made concept such as “institution,” “power,” or “self-preservation.”

If so, the grammar has demonstrated genuine generative power.

If not, we learn which additional capabilities are necessary.

## VI. The research program I would adopt

I think the strongest method is progressive constraint and adversarial reduction, consistent with the approach you've been developing in your philosophical benchmark.

1\. Formalize the logical minimum

Define a small language capable of distinguishing appearance, persistence, transformation, and disappearance. Test its proposed primitives by deletion and reconstruction.

2\. Construct the explanatory extension

Add conditional dynamics, possible alternatives, and methods for discriminating explanations. Use pairs of systems with identical histories but different underlying mechanisms.

3\. Search for a generative basis

Compare candidate operation sets. Require explicit derivations of concepts such as 文、位、势、机、理、续 rather than relying on intuitive analogy.

4\. Adversarially test the entire framework

Use unfamiliar, stochastic, nonliving, self-modifying, nested, and boundary-ambiguous systems. Record each failure and the least additional conceptual commitment required to repair it.

5\. Reconstruct the 文言 system

Map the validated formal operations to historical Chinese concepts, keeping historical textual meaning distinct from our modern theoretical reconstruction.

That last distinction is important. Classical Chinese should inform the conceptual architecture from the beginning, but a successful modern derivation should not be retrospectively attributed to ancient authors without textual evidence.

## 辨其异，察其缘，穷其变，明其续，验其理，以尽生生之文。

These are three hypotheses to test, not three established mathematical minima.

The deepest objective is to make them converge: to find a small constructive language that preserves logical expressibility, supports genuine explanation, and reconstructs the widest possible range of phenomena without unnecessary assumptions.

If that succeeds, 文 could serve as the central organizing concept of a rigorous theory of becoming.

And we would have something substantially more powerful than a glossary of ancient concepts: a compositional framework capable of generating and testing models across natural, biological, cognitive, and social systems.

The next decisive task is a formal reduction: attempting to derive every function in the logical and explanatory grammars from the generative one, identifying precisely where each derivation succeeds or fails.
