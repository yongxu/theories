# The Theory of Becoming in one page

[中文](ONE-PAGE.zh.md) · [The overview](THE-THEORY-OF-BECOMING.md) · [The eight chapters](chapters/README.md) · [The theory graph](results/THEORY-GRAPH.md)

**What it is.** An account of how anything comes to be, lasts, changes and passes away within one relational unfolding, and of how life, evolution, knowing and evaluation take shape inside it, without first assuming that becoming is good. Its laws are proved in Lean; its readings, conjectures and premises are labelled as such.

**The root.** One kind of symbol is enough to begin: a relation, read as condition (`x` is among what `y` comes from). Sameness is always sameness in some respect, a class. Something goes on where an occurrence is a condition of the next and the two are alike under the class: that is *continuation*, and the theory reads every subject as continuation relative to a class. Beginning, abiding, altering and ending are positions in such a line, each relative to a class, and none stands alone. Where the class is carried by the relation (alike occurrences go on to alike ones), continuation never ends; the largest such sameness within a class is its *core*. A pattern can go on while its parts begin and end, as fire passes from one piece of wood to the next: read at two grains, a pattern's course can generate (go on through renewals of its parts), keep (go on with none) or degenerate (end on every branch). Generation never overlaps degeneration, and on a single line generation and keeping exclude each other; that every course falls in one of the three is not shown. These are theorems for every relation and every class ([the root](chapters/01-ROOT.md), [the map](chapters/02-MAP.md)). That this grammar explains how things go on is the theory's claim, argued from the laws below; that every becoming can be represented in it is open.

**What the laws say.** Most laws are proved on richer models of the root, a web of conditions with amounts and discrete steps, each law with its own hypotheses:

- *Change and return.* Arising and ceasing are one change read three ways, and for a decided property of a finite family, coming to be recurs exactly when ceasing does. Within finitely many configurations a law loses nothing exactly where every configuration comes back; what keeps its whole history never returns, so it needs a carrier no finite list holds.
- *What ends a pattern.* Continued arising needs renewal. Takers each within what is given can together exceed it, and where the renewal depends on what it renews, the excess ends the renewal and whatever depends on it. Under a standing draw, a condition whose giving is read off its level holds exactly from a least level up, within the levels the law states: the regime's point of no return; around loops of couplings acting at every step, the most difference that can arrive grows exponentially; a rule can capture what it governs, and in a stated model the lock survives the loss that accrues under it ([the map](chapters/02-MAP.md), [collectives](chapters/05-COLLECTIVES.md)).
- *Knowing.* A selection reaches exactly what couples into the selector; what lies beyond cannot inform it. Testing tells occurrences apart only within the relations available; what finite records can refute and verify has an exact form (closed and open classes of histories, up to double negation); a model of structure and change is one lumping of states ([knowing](chapters/03-KNOWING.md)).
- *Life and evolution.* Arising replenishes; population genetics' classical laws (Price's equation, Fisher's theorem in its exact case, multilevel selection, Hamilton's rule, drift and fixation) hold in counting form beside the flow that bounds how many descendants there can be ([life](chapters/04-LIFE.md)).
- *Optimization.* Where there is room, an optimum lowers what lies beyond its couplings as far as any eligible outcome does: one law, of which drawers in turn, drawers at once and races for position are cases ([collectives](chapters/05-COLLECTIVES.md)).

**The ethics.** No statement that reads only the represented dynamics entails that anything's continuation has significance: how things go, as represented, does not settle what matters. With no premise, widening what can form is an order. The ethics rests on two premises with two roles, and the theory asserts neither: *the continuation condition* (for anything, its continuation has significance) fixes whose losses count, and *C* (widening what can form is better) is the only premise that ranks. Under the condition, the significance of what lives on is a conditional theorem; reasons and choices need further premises, named where they are used, and every ranking needs C. Alignment is first a question with no premise, what a selection can reach, and only then of direction, under the premises named ([significance](chapters/07-SIGNIFICANCE.md)).

**The classical texts.** The grammar's terms are reconstructed from classical Chinese texts (the Mohist canon, Xunzi on names, the Changes, Chinese Buddhist treatises); about 4,100 sentences were classified, and the divergences are kept, not smoothed ([the classical texts](chapters/06-CLASSICAL-TEXTS.md), in Chinese).

**The evidence.** 419 recorded results, each with a record naming its premises, evidence and what it does not show; a Lean package whose core imports only Lean's prelude and whose every source declaration has an empty axiom report (a constructive discipline, not an absence of premises: the real assumptions lie in the modelling, stated where a law depends on them); a few frozen empirical tests of the theses, some passed and some failed ([method](chapters/08-METHOD.md)).

**What is not yet done.**

- Most laws are proved on the models, not yet restated on the grammar itself.
- The root and the ethics are not yet one account: the continuation condition is stated on its own formalism, not on continuation.
- Whether either premise of the ethics can be warranted is open.
- The test systems the theory should meet first (radioactive decay, an organism, an institution, a belief system) have no models yet.
- No human has yet reviewed the work; review by scholars of the classical texts, of Buddhist thought and of logic is the next step.
