<a id="1-冲突在同一关系中形成--conflict-forms-within-a-shared-history"></a>
## 1. Conflict forms within a shared history

Basic conflicts need not start from individuals who are independent of one another and contend over interests. Shared life forms care, expression, maintenance and participation, and at certain moments it also makes the same support unable to meet all claims in time. Which lives are damaged, who can respond, who controls access, and why the claims cannot for the time being be reconciled are all differentiations of this history. Losses fall at different positions; they are still real and cannot be borne by one another.

We therefore first explain how the conflict forms. Where a support can be rearranged, cooperatively shared or transformed, the present way of distributing it should not be taken as a natural limit. Where exclusion, earlier wrongdoing or unreasonable goals created the shortage, the responsibility to repair should be kept. In urgent situations, rescue that is already reliable must not be missed for the sake of completing the whole historical inquiry first. [General practical judgment](PRACTICAL-JUDGMENT.md#5-真实冲突有限认识与及时决定--conflict-and-uncertainty) has already completed this procedural account, and this chapter uses it directly.

What follows examines only conflicts that genuinely cannot all be met within the relevant time limit. Restricting the cases this way does not shrink the whole into an eternal zero-sum competition; it lets one unresolved comparative problem show: **when every claim is genuine and the feasible plans have been identified, why might one distribution of loss still be better grounded than another?**

<a id="2-既有原则在哪里留下未定--what-the-existing-principles-leave-open"></a>
## 2. What the existing principles leave open

Basic-condition priority handles one explicit case: a continuation that keeps all the basic claims of the other plan and adds protection takes priority over one that only adds waivable richness. If two plans keep different lives, each also loses the claims the other could have kept; the original inclusion comparison alone cannot decide. One may not delete the unrescued from the list and thereby forge a "no-loss improvement".

The degrees of basic loss are also not necessarily equal. "Basic" is a layer of protection with content, not a way of compressing death, serious but recoverable injury, loss of minimal participation and every other impairment into one point. Even when the classification holds, one must still explain the duration, depth, recoverability and actual continuation of the loss. The two-layer comparison of basic-condition priority does not automatically supply a complete ordering within a layer.

This round concentrates on one restricted part of this: **different positions have equivalent basic rescue claims, while the actual plans preserve different numbers of lives.** The conclusions of this round cannot be carried directly to conflicts with unequal gravity, uncertain efficacy or different historical undertakings.

<a id="3-新增承诺-bc1同等基本救助--the-additional-commitment-bc1"></a>
## 3. The additional commitment BC1

**BC1: Within a determinate rescue scope, the losses and available protection faced by the affected lives are equivalent in the ethically relevant respects; all plans being compared already have independent permission, the rescue is reliable, and there is no differing imposed harm, effective prior undertaking or other burden sufficient to distinguish them; when not all can be accommodated and no particular chance-allocation entitlement has been established, the plan that preserves more of these lives should be preferred.**

This is a declared, scoped comparative commitment. It is not written into the definitions of "life", "basic" or "equivalent", and it is not a conclusion already proved by living significance, full comparison or basic-condition priority. Whether each person should additionally be guaranteed a chance of rescue is a normative dispute that this chapter faces directly; it cannot be deleted from the question on the ground that "there is no other difference".

When it applies, the following must be done together:

- **Equivalence is stated before the comparison.** State the lives, injuries and recovery content that may actually be lost; do not substitute social standing, output, intelligence or institutional preference. Judging "equivalent" is a grounded ethical judgment, not a demand that entire lives be identical.
- **What is compared is actual rescue.** Feasibility, time, supporting conditions, burden on the responders and later dependence must all enter. Different probabilities of success or depths of rescue may not be passed off as the same unit.
- **Each life enters only by its actual differences.** An advantage in number may not be manufactured by copying records, splitting one need, adding labels or redrawing the boundaries of an organization. The recognition of new life or unfamiliar intelligence still has its own requirements of ground.
- **Permissions and undertakings are tested independently first.** More rescuable lives do not automatically make it a qualified means to take support that another has legitimately obtained, to create new injury, or to abandon an effective undertaking. Whether the relevant rights may be limited by urgent reasons still needs independent argument.
- **Every unmet claim is kept.** Receiving comparative priority does not make the loss of those not chosen disappear, nor does it show that their lives are lower. Feasible new support and well-grounded later responsibilities must still be answered.

<a id="4-为什么支持这项展开--why-endorse-this-extension"></a>
## 4. Why endorse this extension

Living significance affirms actual living and its further generation. For losses that are equally grave and equally avoidable, every effective preservation has positive ground; whether someone belongs to the larger or the smaller group does not change this. The whole comparison must answer to this jointly realized distribution, and cannot, merely by identifying "which is the gravest kind of loss", stop forever asking over how many lives the same loss falls.

We accordingly hold that within the limited scope of BC1, fair response requires not only acknowledging each loss but also letting feasible rescue be realized for as many equivalent claims as possible. This judgment admits a **limited aggregative consideration**. It sets up no common unit of happiness across different lives, and it does not stipulate how many ordinary pleasures offset one grave deprivation; but it does allow several equivalent rescue reasons together to support a stronger choice. To avoid the dispute, one may not call this "no aggregation at all".

Its normative cost must also be stated plainly: when the choice is only between preserving one life or preserving several equivalent lives, this candidate allows the smaller side to receive no chance of rescue in this allocation. The ground is that the equivalent protection achievable this time is fuller; it is not that the smaller side matters less, nor a declaration that chances have no value. Within this scope, we give actual basic protection priority over a separately posited equal-chance requirement.

This step has an ethical ground but is not an irrefutable deduction. What supports it is that, once a shared capacity to respond has formed, how its activity preserves real lives should itself carry comparative weight; this needs no assumption of a "whole person" who feels all the benefits. An opponent can instead regard each equivalent claim's receiving a chance as the stronger constraint. The two dispute the content of legitimate shared response; the opponent may not be diagnosed as not understanding relationality or not caring about others.

<a id="5-同等机会的对手与本轮修正--the-equal-chance-rival-and-a-correction"></a>
## 5. The equal-chance rival and a correction

A strong opponent can fully acknowledge every life, actual dependence and rescue effect, and still hold that each equivalent claim should be given a fair chance. It can also support, once a plan has been chosen, doing one's utmost to rescue everyone that plan can cover. **So one cannot say, merely because it does not change the lottery probabilities as numbers grow, that it simply ignores the added lives.** [Otsuka's primary-source critique](https://sites.rutgers.edu/michael-otsuka/wp-content/uploads/sites/837/2024/05/Otsuka-Saving-Lives-Individuals-PAPA-2006.pdf) makes this round withdraw that overly quick defence.

A stronger compromise plan can also give the plan protecting more lives a higher chance without giving any side zero chance. It acknowledges that number carries weight while keeping the consideration of chance. So "every added reason must make a difference" shows at most that purely ignoring it is unsuitable; it cannot by itself decide whether one should give priority to rescue or how to draw lots.

This chapter still supports BC1's scoped priority: if equivalent protection that has been fully established can be reliably realized, and there is no prior chance entitlement or other distinguishing burden, one cannot, merely because the allocation has not yet taken place, reduce the reason for realizing more protection to a suggestion that must be diluted again by randomization. This sentence states the strength of the added commitment explicitly; it has not proved that any opponent who values chances is self-contradictory. If further counter-review shows that this priority cannot legitimately explain giving the smaller side zero chance, BC1 should be narrowed or withdrawn, keeping the original open points and procedural scope of the conditional alignment route.

<a id="6-一段共同支持历史中的条件性判断--a-conditional-judgment-within-shared-support"></a>
## 6. A conditional judgment within shared support

Consider an explicitly constructed example: a jointly maintained shelter network faces two imminent dangers at once, and the existing team can complete only one rescue route within the time limit; one route can save one person, the other can save two. The three face equivalent losses, the rescue effect is reliable, the burdens on the rescuers are comparable, and there is no special prior undertaking, differential permission or additional imposed harm; cooperation, staggering, outside help and any other means that can arrive in time have been checked within the stated scope. These complex conditions are not here taken to hold naturally in reality.

Under these conditions this chapter supports the route that saves two. The one not saved retains equal standing, his loss still belongs to the whole continuation, and if new support is available in time there is still ground to rescue him. This is not the no-loss improvement of full comparison, nor the inclusion case that basic-condition priority has already settled; the decisive reason comes from BC1's added normative commitment.

| Changed condition | Why the verdict needs reassessment |
|---|---|
| The two face optional disruption while the one faces basic destruction | Equivalence fails; basic-condition priority remains relevant |
| Rescuing more requires imposing a new basic injury elsewhere | Means and additional injury alter the case |
| Efficacy differs | BC1 supplies no risk conversion or expected-value rule |
| The smaller side holds a valid prior claim to this allocation | The claim and any limits need independent appraisal |
| Only one of two otherwise equivalent claims can be met | BC1 supplies no numerical priority; the inherited procedure remains available |
| Reorganization can meet all claims in time | Genuine joint feasibility changes the problem |

The constructed rescue history yields a determinate judgment only through the new commitment and its stated correspondence. It is not a field assessment or a new formal result. Optional losses, additional imposed injury, uncertain efficacy, prior allocation rights and newly feasible joint rescue each change the grounds. Symmetric unresolved cases retain the existing procedural account.

<a id="7-冲突判断的三种结果--three-outcomes-of-conflict-judgment"></a>
## 7. Three outcomes of conflict judgment

This continuation lets the theory give one added substantive comparison, without requiring a ranking of all basic conflicts.

First, **there is already a sufficient ground of priority**: equivalent rescue within BC1's scope, or the basic-protection inclusion of basic-condition priority. Procedure must then answer to these reasons; fairness does not mean first flattening them all and then drawing lots.

Second, **there is no sufficient substantive ranking, but there is a legitimate decision procedure**: for example symmetric equivalent requests, or cases that remain unresolved after real discussion yet require timely action. The procedure may legitimately decide who carries out which plan, without having to claim that the chosen result is already better on all values. The procedure's own authority, participability and actual effects still need grounds.

Third, **a current proposal lacks even a sufficient ground of permission**: exceeding authority in its means, concealing basic injury, or pushing the whole burden onto the excluded position cannot gain exemption by "it can't all be met anyway". If no fully qualified plan exists, one enters the existing scoped treatment of urgent conflict and openly keeps the claims that cannot be met; incomparability is not arbitrary permission.

The existing legitimate-selection commitment already provides a basis for these distinctions. The new contribution of this chapter is to add to them one substantive priority that has been tested against opponents, and to state explicitly its relation to chance, fairness and the original protection principles. A decision's being permitted still does not make real loss disappear; the existing [unmet grounds after fair allocation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/history/root-foundation/PATIENT-GROUND-AFTER-FAIR-ALLOCATION.md) continue to apply.

The result distinguishes justified substantive priority, legitimate procedural selection without a complete ranking, and proposals lacking adequate permission. These categories reuse legitimate selection. The new contribution is BC1's explicit comparative content and its contested relation to fair chances. Neither a selected plan nor a fair procedure erases the actual unfulfilled living ground.

<a id="8-生生的整体判断因此意味着什么--what-this-adds-to-whole-generative-appraisal"></a>
## 8. What this adds to whole generative appraisal

**Whole appraisal can seriously compare the extent of shared preservation without turning lives into material that can be freely exchanged.** "One may not sacrifice a basic life to increase waivable richness" and "equivalent basic protections cannot jointly constitute a stronger reason" are not the same commitment; this candidate keeps the former and rejects the latter within the scope above. Diversity, future output and level of intelligence also cannot directly stand in for the ground of equivalent rescue.

This adds an explicit requirement to generative alignment: a forming intelligence should be able to say which comparative ground it is applying, which lives remain unmet, and when it should return to procedure, rather than covering every conflict with the single sentence "the whole is better". A shared normative direction can have substantive power of choice and keep well-grounded indeterminacy.

This round completed the philosophical candidate of equivalent basic rescue and its counter-review. It did not complete a general ranking of all basic conflicts, and it did not modify the verified comparator `PracticalJudgment.Better`. If the added BC1 enters an executable system or a unified formal rule, its equivalence classification, compatibility with other priorities, cross-situation consistency and actual correspondence must be separately checked; the existing Lean receipt does not certify this new commitment.

The **unequal-gravity comparison** that this round leaves is now continued in §§9–14 below: first complete a scoped one-to-one comparative reason, then keep as unresolved the connection in which gravity and number vary together. The scope, counterexamples and argumentative costs of the original BC1 are unchanged.

Whole appraisal can compare the extent of equivalent protection while rejecting unrestricted interchangeability. BC1 adds a defended, contestable comparison to generative alignment; the existing checked comparator remains unchanged. The unequal-gravity question left by that round is continued in §§9–14 below. ## 9. What makes a basic loss more serious

**Continuation of 2026-10-08.** This round's [argument, primary-source assessment and author counter-review](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/history/root-foundation/BASIC-LOSS-SEVERITY-INTEGRATION.md) explain BC2's one-to-one scope: when the actual destruction that can be avoided differs, a comparative reason leaning toward one side can arise, but that reason does not by itself decide the whole allocation. Gravity here is explained from the damage to lives and their reachable continuations; it is not obtained by subtracting some items from a preset capability list of a "normal person".

Shared life forms the concrete ways of expression, care, action and participation. What is compared is how the breakdown of support changes these lives and what the restoration of support actually brings back. The same functional change can carry different significance of injury when substitute channels exist and when there is complete isolation. Conversely, that no one can for the time being repair it does not make the loss lighter. **Lack of treatment is not lack of reason for care; a loss that cannot presently be avoided still lies within the whole appraisal.**

At least three things should be stated separately: what plight one is already in; what loss this plan can avoid or lessen; and when action must be taken for this protection still to be realized. Together they belong to one history, but none can stand in for another. The worst-off is not necessarily the one who can be helped most this time; helping the most also does not automatically cancel the existing entitlements of the others. A window for effective action that closed earlier explains timing, and cannot by itself prove that a loss is graver. If timely sequential implementation can accommodate both, the earlier "only one can be chosen" needs rewriting.

The depth, duration, recovery and support-dependence of actual injury carry weight because they explain how a life is blocked, not because these words naturally carry fixed weights. The existing [loss-comparison study](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/history/root-foundation/GENERATIVE-LOSS-COMPARISON-INTEGRATION.md) completed the transport of reductions on the same actual life; the new question of this round is: when may differences of this kind of injury be used to compare protections that fall on different lives.

**Gravity concerns the impairment of actual living and its reachable continuations.** Baseline deprivation, avoidable loss and the window for effective action must be distinguished within that history. Lack of a cure does not cancel grounds for care. Existing loss-transport results already cover matched reductions for the same living; they do not by themselves establish interpersonal priority. This continuation addresses that additional connection.

<a id="10-bc2-的一对一理由范围--bc2-a-one-to-one-comparative-reason"></a>
## 10. BC2: a one-to-one comparative reason

**BC2: Between two basic claims that have each been given ground, if only one can be reliably and sufficiently protected; the plans each already have independent permission; burdens, prior undertakings, existing disadvantage, chance entitlements and the like supply no other ground of distinction; and an examination of the full reachable continuations shows that one avoidable basic loss is not lighter in any of the respects already explained and is clearly graver in at least one key respect, then, as regards actual basic protection, there is a reason leaning toward preventing this graver loss. It must still be judged together with chances, rights and other reasons, and does not by itself demand a determinate allocation.**

The "relevant respects" must first have their significance for life explained, and then both sides tested; an unfavourable dimension may not be deleted after the conclusion is known. This round supports one narrow and concrete way of applying it: both sides involve comparable basic conditions of life, with comparable depth and effective support during the deprivation; one side will also undergo a clearly longer period of severe impairment, loss of effective recovery or further basic injury, while the other side has no omitted reverse difference in gravity. Where the losses are mutually lighter and graver across dimensions, this way gives no ranking. "Clearly" means that the difference is sufficiently grounded and is not measurement noise or a difference of naming; this round sets no numerical threshold.

This commitment crosses two lives that cannot bear each other's losses, and therefore **is neither the old theorem of reduction on the same bearer, nor a conclusion derived from living significance alone**. Living significance gives the direction of concern for actual life; BC2 further commits that, in this kind of comparable one-to-one protection, the degree of avoidable destruction constitutes a comparative reason for response. Whether equality of chance should limit this priority is still a normative dispute, and one may not pretend that it has been eliminated by "other conditions being comparable".

Its affirmative ground is: if the two sides have the same standing, shared support's concern for basic life cannot stop at acknowledging that both sides have claims. When a deeper destruction can be reliably avoided, wholly flattening the difference in degree on the ground that the category is the same would make the equally acknowledged concern lose part of its effect on actual consequences. This candidate therefore acknowledges a reason leaning toward deeper actual protection. In drafting, it was at one point further argued that this determinately gives priority in allocation; after absorbing the [parallel counter-review](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/history/root-foundation/UNEQUAL-BASIC-LOSSES-REVIEW.md) and reading the primary sources it pointed to, this round withdraws that step. Explaining that the difference carries weight has not yet explained that it suffices to cancel the other side's entire chance. Merely giving a name to a "clear" difference also cannot replace this argument.

<a id="11-比较损失不给生命排等级--compare-losses-without-ranking-lives"></a>
## 11. Compare losses without ranking lives

Consider a constructed shared communication-support network. Two users each rely on a channel formed for them to achieve minimal expression, calls for help and participation in care. After a support failure, the repair that can arrive in time can cover only one place. If both are repaired, both can reliably keep these basic activities; if not repaired, the severity of the initial isolation is comparable. Place A then has reliable substitute support, while place B has no effective substitute for a long period sufficient to affect its basic life, and the continued loss of contact in the interim would cause deeper damage to participation. It is also stipulated here that A has no omitted irreplaceable time-bound loss such as a unique farewell or key decision, that the two sides have no differing undertakings or other basic consequences, and that feasible joint, deferred and transferred arrangements have been checked within the scope of this example.

Under these constructed conditions, as regards actual basic protection, there is a reason leaning toward preserving B's channel. Whether it suffices to decide the final allocation still requires accounting for the requirements of chance. This leaning comes from being able to prevent a graver destruction of basic life; it is not that B is cleverer, more productive or closer to some normal life, nor that A's short-term severe isolation has become dispensable. The real losses on both sides remain in the judgment. This example is not a recommendation for actual medical or facility allocation.

"Recovery" is also not limited to copying the former function. A newly formed way of expression, shared care or organization of life, if it actually provides sufficient support, can change the judgment of long-term loss of continuation. This directly reuses the existing results on formed basic conditions and substitute channels. But technically listing the name of a substitute is not yet its being usable for this life.

By the same token, the ground for preventing a person's death cannot first deduct the disability he already has and then discount it by "less remaining capacity". Another kind of conflict, in which the same improvement falls between the long-excluded and the better-off, also cannot erase past disadvantage merely because the improvement is the same. **This round has not established a complete "improvement only" theory; it merely refuses to let overall condition or the rank of a person replace the actual protection content of this occasion.**

The constructed communication history supplies a reason favoring protection against sustained basic exclusion without timely effective replacement. It does not alone settle how that reason interacts with fair chances. The shorter exclusion remains serious. Restoration may occur through newly formed adequate support rather than a return to an old functional pattern. Neither disability nor lower output discounts the worth of a life. Historical disadvantage remains potentially relevant; BC2 does not establish a benefit-only theory.

<a id="12-哪些看似自然的推广不成立--extensions-this-argument-does-not-warrant"></a>
## 12. Extensions this argument does not warrant

| Proposed extension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Longer is always worse | Depth, support and irreplaceable timing can change the comparison |
| Irreversible loss always takes priority | Irreversibility alone cannot rank unlike stakes |
| The worst-off always receives all support | Need matters without uniquely determining effective means or canceling care |
| One extra capacity makes a life more rescuable | A capacity count measures neither standing nor the whole protection at stake |
| Any extra gravity defeats any number of lesser basic losses | Neither commitment supplies the mixed gravity-and-number verdict |
| Every basic conflict requires a lottery | Basic status preserves claims without erasing grounded differences |

These limits do not exclude all counterexamples and then declare victory. They fix when this round makes no comparison, and allow the affirmative conclusion to be corrected in turn: if the supposedly lighter side has an omitted irreplaceable loss, that application must be withdrawn; if some real difference of degree in fact does not carry the claimed comparative weight in rescue, BC2 itself must be revised; and a stronger reason of chance can also limit the final allocation without denying this difference in loss.

These are limits on the argument, not proofs that every rival fails. Missing reverse losses defeat a proposed application. A fair-chance constraint can limit the allocation without denying a gravity reason; an objection to that reason itself would require revising BC2. No numerical weighting, universal irreversibility priority or within-basic lexical hierarchy is established.

## 13. What the primary comparison changes

[Voorhoeve's primary text](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/voorhoev/How%20Should%20We%20Aggregate%20Competing%20Claims%20--%20Ethics%20accepted%20version%20Dec%202013docx.pdf) considers both the size of the improvement and the disadvantaged starting point, and argues for a route that first selects the relevant claims and then aggregates. This means that this round cannot reduce the opponent to blind summation, nor disguise "which kinds of claims may be aggregated" as a measurement problem.

This chapter keeps a narrower conclusion: a profile of loss can support some judgments of gravity, but cannot by itself decide the threshold of aggregation. That a person may legitimately give priority to caring for himself also does not mean that public shared response already has a rule of aggregation across persons; this connection needs additional ethical argument. The primary-text route offers an opponent worth continued testing, and is not taken as this theory's ready-made answer.

The primary comparison motivates a separate burden: establishing gravity does not yet determine aggregation eligibility. We do not adopt a cardinal welfare sum or infer a public allocation rule directly from permissible personal concern. The remaining disagreement concerns substantive commitments, not the cultural origin of a vocabulary.

<a id="14-本轮推进与下一项联合问题--result-and-the-next-joint-question"></a>
## 14. Result and the next joint question

The structure is now clearer: basic-condition priority keeps the priority of basic protection; BC1 compares the lives that can be jointly preserved when gravity is equal; and BC2, in the one-to-one case of this section, gives a comparative ground, which other reasons may limit, for avoiding the deeper destruction. They share a concern for actual basic protection, but **do not yet constitute a complete choice rule under arbitrary numbers, degrees, risks and histories**. Unmet living grounds, finite undertakings and necessary procedure continue to use the existing results directly.

The next central question is: **when gravity and number vary together, how can a defensible joint choice be formed, and how can the account of reasons stay consistent when the actually feasible plans change?** One may not first give answers by pairwise intuition and then assume by default that they already form a unified rule; nor may the inconsistency be removed merely by renaming plans. It must be explained whether what changed is the actual loss, the feasible support, the comparative reason, or only the description, and all affected lives must be kept at the same time.

This round completed BC2's account of gravity, its concrete judgment of reasons and the author's counter-review, and absorbed substantive criticism from the parallel research; there is no new mathematical theorem or empirical validation. More general matching and formal diagnosis are advanced by the [parallel study](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/history/root-foundation/UNEQUAL-BASIC-LOSSES-PROTOCOL.md); this section does not certify in advance that it is complete, nor set up a competing BC2. The old `PracticalJudgment.Better` and the existing Lean receipts keep their original scope; if the unified rule proposes new mathematical properties, the premises must be stated explicitly and actually pass a Lean check. The substantive grounds of BC1/BC2 are likewise not proved automatically by successful encoding.

The completed contribution is a defended account of gravity within BC2, a determinate pro tanto reason in the constructed case, and substantive counterreview. The concurrent matching study retains ownership of its broader candidate and formal checks. Joint variation in gravity and numbers, responsiveness to changing feasible alternatives, uncertainty and executable integration remain open. No new theorem, empirical validation or independent acceptance is claimed. Existing formal results keep their original hypotheses and reach.

## Joint number and gravity comparison

2026-10-08. The [joint loss-comparison study](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/history/root-foundation/UNEQUAL-BASIC-LOSSES-INTEGRATION.md) continues BC1 and the current BC2: when the range affected and the gravity vary together, how do the two existing reasons jointly support a direction? This round proposes **LC, the joint-loss comparison reason**, and gives 14 new Lean theorems / 23 declaration audits; the original 30 practical theorems / 46 audits are inherited only, and two checked application examples are not counted as new theorems. The [independent review](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/history/root-foundation/UNEQUAL-BASIC-LOSSES-REVIEW.md) and the [scope of the primary sources](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/history/root-foundation/UNEQUAL-BASIC-LOSSES-SOURCES.md) explain the philosophical ground and the limits of the checks respectively.

**LC's declared commitment.** If the actual losses in the full relevant history are not duplicated, each basic loss of A can be compared, on grounds, with a distinct loss of B that is at least as grave, and B has in addition an extra loss or some matching genuinely lessens, then these reductions can jointly constitute a reason supporting A. It is not a deduction from living significance alone, nor does it compensate the sufferer with improvement elsewhere; shared support, concentration of loss, permissions, prior requirements and chances cannot be erased by the matching. Whether the reason suffices to decide the allocation requires separate argument.

**A positive application.** A constructed shared-care network has two feasible repairs; A is left with two lighter basic losses, and B with one lighter and two graver basic losses, so the damaged positions cross. Once the layering, the different realizations and the relevant linkages are explained, A reduces both the number and the degree of the remaining losses, and there is the reason LC describes. The two lighter ones are still real basic losses. Nothing here prices lives by complexity, output, level of intelligence or number of identities.

The two-tier diagnostic uses cumulative counts: all basic losses, and higher-tier basic losses. Coordinatewise improvement is equivalent to agreement across a specified family of positive additive diagnostics, with a strict counterpart. This is an exact result within that family, not a welfare representation of all life or all ethical positions. The positive joint example is `(2,0)` versus `(1,2)`; the separately checked `(2,0)` versus `(1,1)` retains its narrower scope.

**A grounded boundary.** One graver and two lighter losses make the directions of the two cumulative quantities cross. Two positive-weight diagnostics both respect same-layer number and one-to-one gravity, yet rank in opposite directions here. Accepting these two sensitivities separately does not amount to having obtained a joint exchange rate; the indeterminacy of this interface does not prove that the world itself is incomparable. The existing timely-decision procedure continues to apply.
