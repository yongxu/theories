## 2. Exact criterion for contraction

Write \(x\leadsto y\) for the existing `Reach`: a finite history takes configuration \(x\) to \(y\). Given a composition \(g\otimes x\), two conditions are stated:

1. There is a left unit \(I\) with \(I\otimes x=x\).
2. **Context compatibility of primitive steps**: if \(x\xrightarrow e y\), then \(g\otimes x\xrightarrow e g\otimes y\).

Let reachability mean an existential finite trace in the original process. Supply a context operation with a left unit and preservation of primitive steps under each declared left context. No associativity, commutativity or unlimited copying is hidden in this theorem's premises. Primitive compatibility implies finite-path compatibility by the already checked path construction.

\[
\frac{g\otimes(a\otimes a)\vdash c}{g\otimes a\vdash c}.
\]

\[
a\leadsto a\otimes a.
\]

The checked theorem is `admissible_contraction_iff_duplicate`. A duplicate-producing trace lifts through the context, then composes with the proposed continuation. Conversely, instantiate the rule at the empty context and the target containing both copies; soundness yields the duplication trace. The semantic counterpart is `contraction_iff_duplicate`.

The converse relies on a goal language containing the duplicated configuration itself. Reachability permits preparation and forgets the event word. The result does not promise bounded latency, probability, inevitable execution, or preservation of effects absent from the state. Binary duplication gives every finite positive multiplicity, not an infinite run or the full exponential modality.

## 15. Substitution as continuation of dependence

**Checked advance.** In the existing finite term language, simultaneous substitution is constructed so that entering an inner hypothesis keeps the new position and shifts the old references. For a given substitution σ and assignments η, ρ to the before and after contexts, all consumers evaluate the same if and only if every substituted term supplies the ground that the original position requires:

\[
\bigl(\forall t,\;\operatorname{eval}(t[\sigma],\rho)
 =\operatorname{eval}(t,\eta)\bigr)
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
\bigl(\forall p,\;\operatorname{eval}(\sigma(p),\rho)=\eta(p)\bigr).
\]

The sufficient direction follows by induction on the syntax; the necessary direction uses position-wise reference terms. A single consumer may ignore a position, so this necessary condition must not be misapplied to an arbitrary single term. Further checked were single-hypothesis substitution, the semantic composition of two substitutions, and the preservation of existing hypothesis guarantees when the grounds produced meet the input requirements. The guarantees themselves and the satisfaction of the inputs remain open premises.

**The real contrast.** A correct substitution and a term wrongly attached to an inner hypothesis can both preserve types; when the outer ground is true and the inner one false, the two give different Bool values. When both grounds are true, they give the same answer. Hence neither sameness of type nor sameness of the answer on one occasion suffices to identify the original dependence. Equal evaluation here also does not mean equal complete meaning, actual experience or source history.

This joins producer output to the consumer's introduced position throughout the finite expression, including under binders. The existing application-after-abstraction equation only supplied an environment; this construction inserts open syntax and proves its evaluation law. The known substitution technique is credited. The exact criterion establishes denotational fidelity under the supplied language; it does not independently ground that language, identify every semantic content, preserve physical cost, or discharge the truth of its hypothetical assessment premise.
