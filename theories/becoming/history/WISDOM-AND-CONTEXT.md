## 1. From overlap to explanation

The [mathematical core](MATHEMATICAL-CORE.md) composes finite histories from declared relations of change, and tests when a statement holds within them. [Huigen](../history/HUIGEN.md) has already explained how valid inference transmits grounded premises; this round reuses those results. That a historical sentence resembles a present formulation is only the starting point of an explanation. A stronger explanation should state: keep which relations, and the conclusion holds; change which relations, and the response should change; omit which relations, and the judgment loses its ground.

What is proposed here is a hypothesis open to counterexample: some wisdom traditions repeatedly captured similar structures of situation. The tradition's recognition, expression and transmission of these relations also belong to the formative process of life. The hypothesis does not presuppose that the traditions already hold the same values, nor does it first name every transmitted saying correct wisdom. How people form these discriminations, and how social selection retains accurate or mistaken expressions, still carry an empirical explanatory responsibility.

The substantive hypothesis concerns recurring relations, their recognition and their cultural retention. Textual resemblance, causal formation of a belief, validity of an inference and ethical warrant are different questions within that process. A common notation cannot discharge them. This study advances the relation from process understanding to situated judgment; it supplies no premise-free normative foundation.

<a id="2-成功怎样改变下一步--how-success-changes-the-next-step"></a>
## 2. How success changes the next step

Take an explicit technical example: the quantity is a natural number, each step adds one unit or removes one unit (removing at zero leaves zero); the declared operating requirement is that the quantity stay between 1 and 2. At 1, adding gives 2 and removing gives 0; at 2, removing gives 1 and adding gives 3. So the same requirement permits opposite responses at the two boundaries. The numbers are the local law and operating range of the example, not a total of value, nor a scale of a person's temperament.

More crucially, 1 can actually reach 2 through one successful addition. Copying the operation that has just succeeded then leads to 3. **No outside disturbance is needed here: success itself changes the action that the next success requires.** This explains why "it worked last time" cannot by itself be a reason to "keep doing the same". By contrast, identifying the present difference and switching direction can satisfy the operating requirement after any given finite number of steps; infinite physical operation, the formation of sensors and actual control capacity are not within this proof.

The supplied law derives a reversal without changing the target after seeing the result. `success_changes_next_response` checks the actual join: add at 1 succeeds, repeating add fails, and add followed by remove succeeds. `contextual_finite_continuation` checks every finite horizon for the supplied state-sensitive itinerary. The example is a mechanism witness, not evidence that human judgment is scalar regulation or that persistence is always good.

<a id="3-结果正确还需要怎样的继续条件--correct-endpoints-and-continuation"></a>
## 3. Correct endpoints and continuation

The core's existing `May` says that some completed outcome satisfies the requirement, and `Must` says that all completed outcomes satisfy it. The new companion `Assured` additionally requires, prefix by prefix: the next event has at least one permitted successor; every permitted successor satisfies the same requirement on the remaining process; and when the event word ends, the goal is satisfied. In the model an atomic event is already represented as a completed step.

This gives the following composition formula, checked in Lean:

\[
\operatorname{Assured}(uv,Q,s)
\iff
\operatorname{Assured}(u,\operatorname{Assured}(v,Q,-),s).
\]

It also implies the existing existence of an executable history and the universal end-point condition. The converse does not hold: take an explicit process whose first step can reach 1 or 2, and only 1 can reach the goal 3 through a second step. All completed two-step histories reach 3, and there is indeed one such history; but the branch that goes to 2 is interrupted. So looking only at completed outcomes would miss the failure of the continuation condition.

This is a standard finite predicate-transformer pattern, credited to the primary Dijkstra comparison. It does not prove that an external scheduler attempts an enabled step or that a physical event completes within a deadline. `Assured` checks terminal content and intermediate enabling, not every morally relevant intermediate loss. Such losses must be represented in the state/history or checked by an additional invariant. Existing repository work already distinguishes readiness from scheduling and completion; this adds a compact interface over the pedagogical core rather than rediscovering that distinction.

## 5. Truth and wisdom

The connection this round can support is that the same structure of conditional reasoning can hold together the questions of preserving truth, actual continuation and acting according to situation. Mathematics makes premises, conditions and inferences public; wisdom must also identify which relations actually hold, what life is cared about, what legitimate undertakings there are, and how to respond at this moment. The ways of knowing, the judgment of relations and the capacity to respond are themselves formed and changed in action; the quantity reading and response rules of this round are still given, and cannot be passed off as a complete generation of these capacities.

Accordingly, what the strong version of the historical overlap should test is not how similar the words are, but **whether, while retaining the original reasons, it can explain the preservation and reversal of what action requires, and give fallible judgments on cases not yet used in constructing the explanation.** When the aims, relations and scope of application have each been independently explained, apparently opposite maxims may be the unfolding of one principle under different conditions; if their ethical grounds really conflict, the conflict is kept.

A usable truth core makes inferential conditions inspectable. A universal constitution of all truths, a causal explanation of historical recurrence, and a complete ethical wisdom require further arguments. Ordinary conditional reasoning, feedback control and practical particularism can explain the present cases too. Their agreement is a positive comparison of structure; it does not establish exclusive metaphysical necessity of our formulation.

The affirmative candidate is: common generative relations give different judgments content that cannot be replaced at will, and explain how they are acquired, connected and revised.
