# The Selfishness Spiral and Narrative-Value Frames (no fixed Chinese name in the sources; the Chinese draft names the parts 赢家通吃机制 "winner-takes-all mechanism", 权力最大化者的自我强化选择 "self-reinforcing selection of power-maximisers", 主导的吸引子 "dominant attractor")

**The theory in one paragraph.** Every actor — a person, a firm, a state, and soon an AI — acts out of a *narrative-value frame*: a constructed ordering of what is worth pursuing, which values outrank which, and which strategies are licensed in their pursuit. The ordering is not chosen. It is written into the actor by the environment that made it, which means it is contingent, and which means it can be rewritten. Call a frame *selfish* when its ledger counts only self-centred measures, so that any cost falling outside the ledger — a broken law, a degraded norm, a drawn-down stock of trust, a poisoned river — is expendable whenever the actor's gain exceeds the actor's own share of the cost. In a system that rewards winning with more capacity to win, that frame compounds, and four loops close in series. First, winnings convert into leverage, so advantage accrues to advantage and the more balanced frames are out-competed — not refuted, out-competed. Second, the winners arrive at the position where the rules are written and rewrite them, so that competitors must adopt the same pattern to survive, and everyone else must assume everyone else already has. Third, accumulated power buys discourse, so the winners' logic becomes the common sense with which the next generation is constructed, and at the limit becomes "human nature" — the point at which the loop is no longer arguable and the people it harms defend it. Fourth, every society has a regulator against exactly this — the *consensus code* of norms, laws and shared understanding — but a regulator only regulates if it updates as fast as the world changes; in a fast-accelerating society it cannot, and the gap between the world's rate of change and the regulator's update rate is precisely the space in which unchecked selfishness compounds. Winner takes all, and winner justifies all. None of this requires a villain and none of it is a moral claim about individuals: what is being described is an attractor that strengthens relative to rival attractors, that runs on any token whatever (capital, power, status, attention), and that eventually consumes the *generator function* — environment, trust, commons — it feeds on. The endgame is not single: a few surviving players may freeze in mutual deterrence, or freeze by a consensus they believe will hold, or change their narrative so as to see themselves as one whole and thereby recover the ability to cooperate, or keep going and collapse, or converge on one winner. The design responses follow the loops. Cap the compounding, so that the reward for winning is anything other than more advantage in the game. Restore the regulator's update rate, by augmenting collective cognition or by delegating coordination to an intelligence that grows at least as fast as the problem — a move Ren derives as theoretically forced and immediately flags as unproven. Rewrite the narrative, because the frame is the highest leverage point and cannot be reached by facts or by shame. Build an intelligence whose job is not to govern but to teach people to see the loops, on the ground that trust is not a precondition for coordination but an *output* of shared seeing. And hedge against irreversibility by increasing the sample size — splitting into many independent smaller entities, as cells do, or city-states, so that when some collapse the others keep evolving.

**Status.** Substantially complete as an argument and unusually well documented as a genealogy — three years of Ren's own formulations (2024-03 to 2026-06), a full conceptual chapter on narrative-value frames, a seven-premise deductive rendering, and an eleven-step precursor cycle in his own words — but not publishable as it stands: there is no single authored text in Ren's voice that carries all four loops, the definition, the endgame taxonomy and the sample-size hedge together; the empirical base is AI-sourced and untested; and the strongest formulations of individual pieces are scattered across a raw note, a synthesis prompt, a Grok transcript and a set of system prompts. Near-publishable at chapter length with one essay of writing.

## The argument

### The question, and why the obvious answer is wrong

The theory begins as a question, not an assertion, and the question is asked in a form that would later violate Ren's own style rule against reducing plurality to one root: *"is the root cause for multi-polar trap, moloch and collapse of many civilizations just selfishness?"* (`01-writings-2025/compound_selfishness.md`). The word "just" is doing the work. Ren is not proposing that people are bad. He is asking whether one dynamic underlies several apparently distinct failures — the multipolar trap, Moloch, the metacrisis, the historical pattern of civilizational collapse — and whether that dynamic has anything to do with what we ordinarily call selfishness.

The obvious answer, that humans are selfish by nature and societies therefore decay, is the one thing the theory is built to refute, and it refutes it from biology first. The dominant frame is partly biologically constructed, yes — but *"we are also biologically constructed to be satisfied once reach certain level, biologically constructed to be cooperative and feels good when we do"* (`01-writings-2025/compound_selfishness.md`). Biology delivers satiation and reciprocity as well as acquisition; it does not select between them. Something else does. And Ren had already spent 2024 establishing that "people are selfish" is a claim with a datable genealogy rather than a fact about the species: he asks whether the assumption is *"a western or capitalist ideology"* (2024-07-26, `12-claude-ai-chats/2024-07-26_The_Historical_Roots_of_Selfishness_Narratives_278c1729.md`), reports that *"the whole western society is so deeply disciplined by this principle, or we could say, brainwashed by it, it becomes the default way of seeing things"*, and concludes with a commitment: *"So selfishness is not just an idea, it's a dominant paradigm of the society. Can we create a persuasive narrative to transcend the paradigm? My position is this has to be done"* (2024-07-26, `12-claude-ai-chats/2024-07-26_The_Ideology_of_Human_Selfishness_39df7eaa.md`). The theory is therefore not an account of human nature. It is an account of how a particular value ordering came to be selected, and of what selects it.

### The unit: the narrative-value frame

The carrier of the dynamic has to be something that can be selected, transmitted, and out-competed. A motive cannot do that work; a frame can. In the origin conversation the unit is a *self-image*: *"A human construct an image of self - and that image is more important and worth pursuing than any other goals. And that construction, created by its environment, constructed images of the subject and defined what it should do, what has value, and what value is more important than other values"* (2025-02-03, `12-claude-ai-chats/2025-02-03_The_Self-Reinforcing_Cycle_of_Selfishness_a64bd768.md`). Four days later Ren rewrites the same text and replaces "image of self" with a link to a new document, `NVF.md`. That edit is the moment the theory acquires a unit of analysis.

A narrative-value frame is *"a structured framework through which an entity (individual, organization, or institution) interprets reality, constructs meaning, and justifies actions based on an embedded hierarchy of values. It defines what is valuable, which values and actions take precedence over others, and what strategies are acceptable or necessary to pursue those values"* (`01-writings-2025/NVF.md`). Three properties matter for the argument. It is *ranked* — it is not a set of values but an ordering, and orderings conflict where sets do not. It carries *strategies*, not only ends, which is how a frame can license breaking a rule without ceasing to feel virtuous. And it must be *embodied by a collective to function*: a frame with no carriers does nothing, which is why the fight over frames is a fight over populations.

Frames operate at three levels that feed each other. At the individual level a frame filters perception (what is salient, how it is classified, whose word counts as authoritative), supplies justification (what counts as a good reason), and functions as a decision compass. At the collective level, individuals with similar frames converge through shared language and networks, institutionalise when they gain power, and then reach mass adoption, at which point those born into the frame experience it as common sense rather than as one option among many. At that third stage the dominant frame manufactures the next generation's individual frames — through media and algorithmic curation, ritual and tradition, law and economic structure (shareholder-primacy corporate law making co-operatives structurally less viable is the worked example), punishment and deterrence, emotional conditioning, and control of what is taught and archived (`01-writings-2025/NVF.md`).

Because every act of interpretation is an act of transformation, frames mutate as they are transmitted, sometimes by drift and sometimes by design, and are therefore under selection. The selection traits `NVF.md` lists are traits of *spread*, not of truth: frames that propagate fast, target audiences, create strong identities, coordinate large groups, legitimise power and convert it into discourse dominance, stabilise hierarchies, and — the one that matters most here — are *hostile and expansionist*, which "often outcompete others. However, their dominance does not necessarily mean they are beneficial." Ren had reached the same conclusion about narratives generally a year earlier: *"it is the ones that are mostly fit to spread in the current environment that will most likely to spread… Good narratives, also happens often, but they are usually not evolutionarily fit as the ones that don't match reality"* (2024-06-13, `12-claude-ai-chats/2024-06-13_The_Subjective_Nature_of_Self_62c06d50.md`). This is the single most load-bearing claim in the whole apparatus, because it is what disconnects the winning frame from the true one and makes it possible for a civilization to run, in Ren's phrase, *"on more and more mythologies like reasoning"*.

Frames also fail, and `NVF.md` lists the ways: outcompetition, alienation, dependency on expansion, over-optimisation and resource depletion, loss of contact with reality, self-destructive feedback, technological disruption — and one that the AI draft credits explicitly to Ren, *insufficient intelligence to navigate complexity*, "which explains why some powerful NVFs collapse despite their early success" (`01-writings-2025/convo_on_selfishness.md`). That failure mode is where the fourth loop of the spiral will attach.

### What selfishness is, once it has to carry an argument

Ren notices the hole himself: *"I know I been using this as core of the argument, but we didn't define it"* (`01-writings-2025/prompt.md`). The definition he then gives has five facets, and it is worth keeping all five, because the theory changes character depending on which are in play.

The first is **externality neglect**: *"the behavior pattern of neglect negative externalities, and willing to throw other values, consensus, morals under the bus as long as the final outcome is favored by self centered measurement"* — where breaking a law, alienating a narrative, decreasing the total trust of a society or wrecking an environment are all "justifiable" so long as "the cost is less than the gain for the actor". Note what this is not: it is not greed, and it is not malice. It is a *measurement* problem — a ledger whose columns exclude most of what is being spent.

The second is **hypocrisy**, and it is the facet the later drafts lose: an actor "doesn't actually believe in these purely calculated ideologies, but behave as if he does, and use some other beliefs to justify it, for example, doing all above but still believing he is good". This matters because it dissolves the last refuge of the moral reading. You do not need cynics. Sincere people running a self-serving justification produce the identical outcome, and are harder to argue with.

The third de-subjectifies the whole thing, and is the hinge on which the 2025 theory turns into the later meta-attractor program: it is *"an attractor that creating a self-enforcing dynamic that makes it stronger and more reinforcing comparing to other attractors and overtime dominating, but eventually causing the underneath generator function(or the environment to collapse), like the tragedy of the commons, but more broad, **it is not subject that make decisions like humans**"*. The bolded clause is the theory's real content. What is being described is a dynamic that has no seat of decision, that selects agents rather than being chosen by them, and whose currency is substitutable — Ren had already said so in 2024: *"any competitive advantage accumulation game, in nature, will fall for this cycle, in the dynamics, the power can be replaced with capital, status, or any tokens"* (2024-11-05, `12-claude-ai-chats/2024-11-05_The_Ouroboros_Cycle_Self-Consuming_Power_Dynamics_946a5c5e.md`).

The fourth is **zero-sum myopia**: winning over others becomes the salient metric, so short-term optimisation becomes rational for survival, and everyone's local rationality sums to a global coordination failure. The fifth is **disconnection**: "looking at things through us-vs-others lens, missed the fact that we are actually interconnected". These two explain why the pattern is stupid as well as extractive — it is not merely unfair, it destroys value it would itself have wanted.

Grok's compression of the definition is the useful one: selfishness here is "outcome-driven, not intent-driven… cold, rational optimization with blinders on", and therefore "not just a human flaw — it's a systemic engine" (`01-writings-2025/prompt.md`, file_7). The word "selfishness" survives, but it is now the name of a system property, and the strain shows. This is a real cost of the theory and is treated below.

### Loop 1 — compounding

*"here is the key issue - selfish gets rewarded and it compounds, it out grows other behavior patterns(NVFs)"* (`01-writings-2025/compound_selfishness.md`). The mechanism is that the reward for winning is convertible into more capacity to win: wealth buys political influence buys rule-writing buys wealth. The loop is positive, so it does not equilibrate; it runs until an external limit or a crash. Cooperative frames do not compound the same way — a co-operative that returns its surplus to members converts less of its winnings into future advantage than a firm that reinvests all of it — so over iterations the balanced frames are removed from the population without ever being argued against. Ren's cleanest statement of this is the general rule he draws from game design: *"a winner of the game should be rewarded with anything other than more advantages of the game, otherwise the game will eventually converge and winner will take all"* (2024-07-28, `12-claude-ai-chats/2024-07-28_Avoiding_the_Winner-Take-All_Trap_in_Game_Design_0e95ddf1.md`). Read forwards it is a diagnosis; read backwards it is the design constraint that the whole intervention program later tries to satisfy.

Two years later he restates the same loop without any of the vocabulary: *"if you use what you have to compete for more and what you win is yours, and you use that to keep compete, we will end up racing faster and faster and trade everything for winning"* (2026-06-03, `14-claude-code-prompts/Users-ren-repos-speculative-future.md`). The three ingredients are visible there in isolation: *what you win is yours* (privatised winnings), *use it to compete for more* (convertibility), and *keep compete* (no terminating condition). Remove any one and the loop opens.

### Loop 2 — rule capture, and adopt-or-die

Compounding alone would produce inequality. What produces a *spiral* is that the winners arrive at the position where the rules are written. Ren's compression of this is in an HTML comment — a note to himself in the margin of his own draft, never expanded into text: *"NVFs define rules of the game, and the rules of the game determine the rewards of the players"* (`01-writings-2025/compound_selfishness.md`). The loop closes because the arrow runs both ways: the frames of those who hold power write the rules, the rules determine who is rewarded, and who is rewarded determines whose frames write the rules next.

The consequence is that other agents do not need to be corrupted; they need only be rational. *"they are rewarded with more power, other agents observe them and understand in order to compete with them they also have to follow their rules, like not being so cooperative, throw moral values under the bus when needed."* The system no longer merely rewards the pattern, it *requires* it; cooperation becomes, in Grok's phrase, a sucker's bet. The Chinese good-bot draft names the resulting appearance the *"看起来自私"效应* — the "appears selfish" effect (`01-writings-2025/goodbot_cn.md`): people are not selfish, they are behaving as though everyone else is.

And here the belief is not incidental to the mechanism, it is part of it. Ren's 2024-11 amendment to his own eleven-step cycle inserts exactly this: *"Agents also realize the competitors are also competitive and selfish in nature, therefore they would not trade self-loss to system gain to lose their relative advantage to other agents"* (2024-11-05, `12-claude-ai-chats/2024-11-05_The_Ouroboros_Cycle_of_Systemic_Collapse_8a4e5d29.md`). System-positive trades are refused not because anyone dislikes them but because each party models the other as a defector. In 2026 he makes the reflexivity explicit and it becomes the theory's main opening: *"if we don't believe that others will also compete and play this winner takes all game, even if we don't ourselves, and not participating means losing, and we can not lose but keeps on going, this kind of thinking itself creates the trap and reality"* (2026-06-03, `14-claude-code-prompts/Users-ren-repos-speculative-future.md`).

### Loop 3 — narrative capture, and the terminal justification

*"as they accumulate more, they create and slowly dominate the narrative, so its environment becomes more leaning to construct other subjects with this narrative"* (`01-writings-2025/compound_selfishness.md`). The third loop runs through the frame-transmission machinery: accumulated power buys discourse power, discourse power shapes the environment, and the environment constructs the next generation's frames. Ren had already isolated this in 2024, and had insisted on it as a *second* loop that his interlocutor had missed: *"as more and more power concentrates, the discourse power also shifts to the lens of the wealth… the discourse power they had through their wealth accumulation will also make the way they see the world onto the populous"*, and *"as we praise the successful ones, their discourse power grow, and the norm of what is acceptable and what is right, the fundamental idea of what we are, and what value we should defend, also changes"* (2024-05-13, `12-claude-ai-chats/2024-05-13_Wealth_Concentration_and_Systemic_Imbalance_8acde127.md`). Note the escalation inside that sentence: first what is acceptable, then what is right, then *what we fundamentally are*.

The terminus is the naturalisation: *"this makes the next iteration to think it is more justified, and to an extreme, take it as 'human nature', as granted, as everyone has to follow this because we can not change it"*. This is the deepest lock in the theory, and its depth is a function of its form: once the pattern is registered as a fact about the species rather than as a property of a game, it stops being the kind of thing that arguments apply to. `NVF.md` enumerates what the naturalised frame contains — endless growth, zero-sum competition, work as moral duty, inevitable hierarchy, rational self-interest as the default, transactional relations, fixed scarcity, the nuclear family as biological imperative, land as private property — and observes that "the very idea of an immutable 'human nature' is itself part of an NVF". The evidence offered is that even reproduction has been reshaped by frames (monasticism, celibacy), from which "no single NVF is inevitable."

The final and worst symptom is that the harmed defend the arrangement. Grok's version is the sharpest: cognitive lock-in, *"we're not just outgunned; we're outthought"* (`01-writings-2025/grok.md`). Ren's own is bleaker and later: *"我不这么认为，一般人应该都能看清里面利害关系，只是大家原教旨式的遵循着他们的邪教而已"* — "I don't think so; ordinary people can generally see the stakes clearly, it's just that everyone follows their cult fundamentalistically" (2026-04-11). That is a materially different claim from false consciousness, and the theory has not decided between them: is the lock cognitive (people cannot see) or is it participatory (people see and comply anyway)?

Taken together, loops 1 to 3 are Ren's one-line law, the sentence he most wanted confirmed: *"If a system reward winner more edge to win, eventually the system go into winner takes all, but also winner justifies all"*.

### Loop 4 — the regulator and its lag

The first three loops are old. Societies have run them before and survived, and the theory says why: *"In the past many societies has its own value system to prevent selfishness to get compounded beyond systems regulatory control, so they last longer, or even keep a equilibrium"* (`01-writings-2025/compound_selfishness.md`). Honour codes, religious sufficiency norms, shame attached to hoarding, redistribution rituals, jubilee — these are balancing loops, and they are the reason the first three loops do not automatically end a civilization. Ren draws the normative conclusion immediately and structurally: *"This is the root cause we need equality."* Equality is not being defended here as a moral end. It is being defended as the regulator that keeps compounding inside the system's control range.

The fourth loop is the claim that this regulator has a *speed*. *"in an growing(changing) society, where the speed of update of the consensus code(cultural norms or governance mechanisms like laws) has to be able to keep up with the pace of change and complexity of the society), in order to keep selfishness in check. (so basically our understanding of the world has to keep up with reality)"* (`01-writings-2025/cognitive_lag.md`). The consensus code is the society's updatable regulator; if the world changes faster than the code updates, the difference is a window, and in the window the first three loops run unchecked. *"In an accelerating society… more and more unchecked selfishness will start to compound and eventually lead to runaway selfishness and collapse."*

Grok adds the reflexive twist that makes the lag self-widening rather than merely large: selfishness drives acceleration, because higher stakes and faster tools are advantages, and acceleration shields selfishness by widening the lag it exploits (`01-writings-2025/grok.md`, P4). The lag is thus not an external misfortune but an output of the first three loops.

Ren then draws the conclusion that the whole cluster hangs on, and hedges it in the same breath: *"That basically means human, has no chance of both be in charge of this kind of society and keep them stable(without runaway effect), the only seemingly possible solution is to simultaneously develop intelligence and delegate coordination to it, surrendering our power to AI(or exponentially growing intelligence) becomes the only possible resolution(Doesn't mean it can work, only in theory)"*. Read carefully, this is a conditional with a disjunction hidden in it: *either* raise the regulator's update rate to match the world's rate of change, *or* lower the world's rate of change, *or* accept the runaway. Delegation to a fast-growing intelligence is one way to satisfy the first branch, and Ren says only that it is the only one he can see, not that it works.

Two things are worth separating here that the sources run together. The lag argument does not require exponential growth. It requires only that the world's rate of change exceed the regulator's update rate — a much weaker and much more defensible premise, and one that does not smuggle in acceleration grammar the later corpus explicitly rejects. And the argument has an older, cleaner statement in Ren's own 2024 voice, which frames it as risk rather than doom: *"for a system that is evolving in a fast pace, there will be a lot of uncertainties, or we call risk, for this risk, if not properly managed, will cause the system to destabilize, just like cells die"* (2024-03-18).

### The intensifiers, and why the system eats its own foundations

Three further mechanisms make the composite worse than the sum of the loops, and all three are Ren's own additions to his eleven-step 2024 cycle rather than AI elaborations.

**Competition intensification.** As more accumulates at the top, more agents compete over less, which forces more extreme optimisation, which feeds the primary loop. The Matthew effect is not only distributive; it raises the temperature of the remainder.

**Paradigm convergence.** Ren inserts this as an explicit third loop his interlocutor had missed: *"there is a third loop, paradigm convergence towards power capture optimization across all agents(more and more agents will also becoming single value optimizers through iteration)"* (2024-11-05). This is the loop that converts a distributional fact into a civilizational one. It is not that a few agents win; it is that all agents become the same kind of agent.

**Foundation erosion.** *"In pursuit of ever-higher power capture efficiency, dominant agents will sacrifice even the foundational values and rules that keep the system stable - corrupting and eroding the very mechanisms that enabled their rise"* (2024-11-03/05). This is why the terminal state is collapse rather than stable tyranny: the optimisation does not know to stop at the load-bearing walls, because the ledger that would price them is the ledger it does not keep.

Hence the image Ren chose after rejecting "competitive convergence" as too dull and asking for "something more creative, like moloch": the **Ouroboros**, the snake eating its own tail, chosen because it "captures both the self-reinforcing loop and self-destructive nature" at once (2024-11-05). Hence also the terminal statement in his own words: *"The intensity of value capture continues to increase until the system loses fundamental stability and collapses under its own optimization pressure"*, and, generalised, *"these types of systems, including capitalism, is inherently unstable… it creates moloch which in long term kills itself"* (2024-10-30, `12-claude-ai-chats/2024-10-30_Cybernetic_Feedback_Loops_of_Capitalist_Wealth_Concentration_0e5e11e1.md`). In 2026 the same terminal image returns with a different name: *"becoming something like cosmic cancer"*.

The reason collapse rather than plateau is the default is the generator function. The pattern's gains are drawn from a base — ecological, social, epistemic — that regenerates on its own terms and is not on the ledger. Exhaust the base and the extraction stops, whether or not anyone intended it. This is the tragedy of the commons "but more broad", and the broadening is the point: trust and shared understanding are commons in exactly the relevant sense, and they are drawn down by the same mechanism as a fishery.

### The endgames

The theory's most-often-flattened part is its ending, because Ren's own version is plural and every later essay replaces it with a different list. When most players have been out-competed and few remain, his taxonomy has five branches (`01-writings-2025/prompt.md`, file_5). They may develop *"mutually destructive weapon, or similar ability"* and freeze, because taking a move is suicidal and each believes the others rational — a MAD equilibrium. They may *"dead-lock if the progress(societal change) complete stagnate, and freeze the power structure"* — a consensus among the remaining players, conditional on each believing the consensus will not be violated. They may *"change their narrative to gain the ability to cooperate (see themselves together as a whole instead of individuals)"*. They may keep going and collapse. Or winner takes all.

Three things follow from taking this list seriously. First, "winner takes all" is one absorbing state among several, not the theorem; the essays that make it the conclusion overstate the theory. Second, the third branch is the only one that is not a trap, and it is a *narrative* change, not a policy change — which is what licenses the entire intervention program. Third, the two freeze states are stable but dead: they preserve the structure by stopping change, which is exactly what a possibility-space argument counts as a loss.

The hedge that follows is the piece of the theory that appears exactly once and nowhere else: *"But if progress or change(could be environmental) happens faster than the growth of intelligence and coordination, collapse seems inevitable except grow sample sizes(split into smaller entities that are independent of each other, for example, like cells do, or something like city states), so when some collapses others can replace them and keep evolving(evolution case)."* This is a different kind of move from all the others. It does not try to prevent collapse; it tries to make collapse survivable by decorrelating it. It is also the earliest form of the plurality-and-exit architecture Ren builds out through 2024-11 ("if you don't like the society you are in, you can always opt-out and join other societies or start your own") and after.

### The responses, loop by loop

Against loop 3, rewrite the narrative. The justification is Meadows' leverage hierarchy — paradigm is the highest-leverage place to intervene in a system — and the operational claim that follows is Ren's: *"If we don't solve dominant attractors, focusing on minor adjustments won't change the outcome"* (2025-03-14). The manifesto material supplies the how, and is mostly a list of what does not work: moral-superiority contests and technocracy both fail, because "you don't bring a spreadsheet to a story fight"; the myth gap gets filled by dark anti-myths if it is not filled by better ones; the antagonists in the replacement story must be impersonal forces — greed, fear, humiliation — and not scapegoated groups, or the new myth reproduces the old one's structure (`01-writings-2025/new_narrative_manifesto.md`).

Against loops 1 and 2, restructure the incentives so accumulation has counter-forces: progressive taxation, quadratic funding, public-goods rewards, reputation-weighted rather than wealth-weighted decision-making, anti-accumulation feedback loops, tokenised economies that reward cooperative behaviour, steward ownership, commons law. The design constraint underneath all of them is the game-design axiom: the reward for winning must be something other than more advantage in the game.

Against loop 4, raise the regulator's update rate. Three exits are named — augment human cognition; slow the acceleration; delegate coordination to AI — and each is given its own defeater in the sources. Augmentation is slow. Slowing down "risks being outcompeted by others that don't", which is the multipolar trap re-entering one level up, at the level of interventions. And delegation is the double-edged one: "If AI aligns with the selfish winners (and it's trained on their data), it could turbocharge the spiral."

Against the trust deficit that blocks every one of the above, the **good bot**. Its premise is the theory's most useful single inversion: *"Trust is not a prerequisite—it is an outcome. It arises only when we have a shared understanding of reality and can reasonably expect others to act accordingly"* (2025-03-14, `12-claude-ai-chats/2025-03-14_Analyzing_Systemic_Awareness_Prompts_78cfd475.md`). If trust is an output of shared seeing rather than an input to coordination, then the bottleneck is not goodwill but *alignment of perception*, and an intelligence that can teach many people to see the same loops at the same time is a coordination technology rather than a moral one. Hence: *"the real first step is not just teaching people what is good, but teaching them how to see"*, and the constraint on method, *"You do not tell them to resist—you make them see why they no longer wish to comply… True teaching does not impose—it awakens"* (`01-writings-2025/good_bot_jusus_3.md`). Ren's own statement of the goal is plainer: *"My goal is to make people see the current world as is, and gain understanding, so that we can collectively create a path to better future."*

And underneath all of them, the affirmative side that the theory needs in order not to be merely a diagnosis: interconnection as a fact rather than a sentiment (systems theory, trophic cascades, Ubuntu, Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam, Buddhist interdependence), superordinate goals as the demonstrated mechanism for redirecting tribalism, and regenerative interdependence as the replacement pattern — systems whose outputs are other systems' inputs, so that the generator function is restored rather than merely spared.

### Counterexamples the sources raise, and how the theory answers them

*Large-scale cooperation demonstrably happens.* Smallpox eradication, the Montreal Protocol, the ISS, the EU. The answer in the corpus is that these are cases where a shared threat or superordinate goal temporarily re-aligned frames — which is evidence that the narrative lever is real, not evidence that the spiral is absent. The weakness of this answer is that it is unfalsifiable as stated: any cooperation can be re-described as temporary alignment.

*Some societies held equilibrium for centuries.* Monastic orders, tribal reciprocity norms, isolated communal cultures. The answer is that they satisfied two conditions at once — they contained compounding internally *and* they were not exposed to catastrophic external competition — and that they collapsed or were absorbed when colonisation and global markets removed the second condition (`01-writings-2025/prompt.md`, file_8). This is consistent with the selection trait that hostile expansionist frames outcompete others, and it is also a serious limit on the narrative intervention: a cooperative frame must additionally solve the external-competition problem, and nothing in the corpus shows how.

*Dominant frames collapse on their own, so dominance is not permanent.* `NVF.md` says exactly this — "dominance does not guarantee persistence" — and the theory absorbs it: the collapse of a dominant selfish frame is the spiral's last stage, not a refutation of it. The cost is that the theory then predicts the same outcome (collapse) whether it is right or wrong about the intervening stages.

*Facts and moral argument should be enough.* The manifesto's answer is that they demonstrably are not, and Ren's own version is prior and blunter: judgments are affect-first and rationalised after, *"so collectively we make political and economical decision based on this?"*, and *"doesn't this mean as the world gets more and more complex, we are fucked because I are essentially incapable of making decisions based on phenomena, only connotations created by our narratives?"* (2025-02-02, `12-claude-ai-chats/2025-02-02_Emotion_Shapes_Conceptual_Judgments_6720f703.md`). He extends it to the top of the hierarchy, which is what makes it a systemic rather than a populist claim: *"this is not only the behavior pattern of majorities, but also the decision makes and business leaders right? they also driven by mythologies like free-market, effective accelerationism, maximizing profit?"*

*Slowing down avoids the lag.* Answered above: it re-enters the trap one level up. Ren's later position accepts the cost anyway — *"I don't mind we slow down or complete stop, if possible"* — which is a value stance, not a solution to the trap.

*AI is the regulator.* The corpus splits and does not adjudicate. `cognitive_lag.md` and `caboc.md` want AI as governor (with caboc noting that a "Platonic guardian" carries its own risks of tyranny and error); the good-bot lineage silently substitutes AI as teacher, which is a response to the same lag that keeps humans in charge. The substitution is never stated as a choice, and it should be, because the two designs have opposite failure modes.

### What 2026 changes

The last layer of material, from June 2026, does not add loops. It re-bases the argument, and in three ways that matter.

The objection to competition becomes **dynamical rather than distributional**: *"It is not just about a few wins and most loses, it is about the fact that this kind of dynamics is not inherently stable, and is destructive in nature."* This removes the theory's dependence on inequality as evidence and puts the weight where the mechanism actually is.

The inevitability claim is identified as **self-fulfilling rather than descriptive**: *"as long as we recognizing political realism and accelerationism are not reality, but self fulfilling 'gods'"*, and the belief in Moloch is diagnosed as itself a cult dynamic — "collectively behave as if we are hopeless but the only move is to keep going". The 2025 theory had described the naturalised frame as the deepest lock; the 2026 formulation notices that *the theory's own conclusion*, believed, is one of those locks. That is the sharpest self-critique in the corpus and it comes from Ren, including as a correction addressed to Claude: *"最近的对话，你的逻辑倾向于这是一个totalizing attractor，只有希望，改变可能性不大"* — "in recent conversations your logic tends toward this being a totalizing attractor, with only hope and little chance of change" (2026-04-11).

And the trap is restated **without the word selfishness at all**: three beliefs drive the race — that we must race, that not racing means irrelevance, and that being rational means optimising self-interest — and *"Even if each actor understands the danger, each actor still feels forced to race, because unilateral restraint means losing."* Beside it stands the constructive condition, which is where this theory hands off to the rest of the corpus: *"as long as we are generative, as long as we keep the possibility space open, as long as we recognize the things that will lead to an end, like totalizing attractors, and as long as we recognize we are not selfish selves competing with eachother"*.

## Genealogy

Dates are the file dates recorded in `_process/history/2026-09-consolidation/timeline-raw.tsv` and the conversation timestamps in `sources/12-claude-ai-chats/`.

**2021-05-21 — the frame at individual scale, in recorded speech.** `sources/00-early-2021-2022/麦.pages`, decoded 2026-09-04 and readable at `sources/00-early-2021-2022/recovered-text/2021-05-21_麦_dali-field-notes.txt`. **Not Ren's writing.** These are Dali field notes by a companion working on his machine — 「在任的电脑上记录田野笔记，不习惯的苹果系统」 (recording field notes on Ren's computer; not used to the Apple system) — and the two items below sit under the heading 「和任在古城聊天」 (chatting with Ren in the old town). They therefore record a conversation with Ren, taken down by someone else; which turns are his and which the note-taker's cannot be separated from the file, so they are evidence that the material was in circulation around him in 2021, not evidence of authorship. With that caveat they are the earliest statement in the corpus of what an NVF does, and they state it at the scale the 2025 files drop.

First, a narrative that changes the thing it describes: 「中医：有一套 Bio-politic 的叙述，可以改变脑海中对于自身病痛的建构，不再激起负面的想法，而是建构起一套中药会起作用…让人产生积极正面的感觉」 — Chinese medicine carries a bio-political narrative that changes how a person constructs their own illness in their mind, so that instead of igniting negative thoughts it builds up an account in which the medicine will work — with the mechanism spelled out: 「一套新的话语及神经机制的建构会导向新的心理状态进而改善身体状况」, a new discourse and the neural construction that goes with it lead to a new psychological state and thence to an improved bodily one. That is the NVF mechanism — a frame constructs what is inside its scope, and the construction has effects that are not merely reported feelings — running on one body rather than on a society — nearly four years before `compound_selfishness.md` names the unit, and two and a half years before the 2023-12 Wamotopia poems that `TIMELINE.md` had been dating the construction claim to.

Second, 「现代人的分裂」, the split in modern people, which supplies what the individual level of `NVF.md` later leaves out: 「社会有一套"你该成为怎样的人"的论述，但每个人有其个性化的真实感受，常常与单一的作为劳动力/高效参与到生产过程中的社会期待的"我"相异」 — society holds a discourse about who you should become, each person has their own felt reality, and the two are routinely at odds with the single "I" expected of them as labour-power efficiently participating in production. The consequence is named as a system property, 「这样的分裂就是不自洽的状态」 (this split is a state of not being self-consistent), with exactly two exits: 「要么去完成社会期望的我压制住感觉，彻底变成某种人；要么去正视自己的感受，重新审视社会期待，去选择为自己负责的个性化道路」 — either complete the socially expected self by suppressing the feeling and become that kind of person entirely, or face your own feelings, re-examine the social expectation, and choose an individual path you take responsibility for. Note what is attached and what is not: the socially imposed self is already specified as *labour-power in a production process*, which is the same structural framing as the 2021-10 city critique and is a content the 2025 NVF files state more abstractly; but there is no selection dynamic here, nothing about frames out-competing frames, and no account of why the social discourse is the one it is. The frame is present; the spiral is not.

## What was lost along the way

- **The entire 2024 Ouroboros layer.** The eleven-step cycle, its three named loops, the substitutable-currency claim, the foundation-erosion step, and the Ouroboros name itself do not appear in any 2025 file. The 2025 drafts restate a subset of it as though for the first time.
- **"An image of self."** The origin wording — "A human construct an image of self - and that image is more important and worth pursuing than any other goals" — is replaced by "narrative-value frame" on 2025-02-07, and the self-image and identity angle is never developed in `NVF.md`. The frame apparatus gained a population-level unit at the cost of the psychological one.
- **"People are constructed with a more balanced mind."** The sense of a *constructed mind*, not merely a held frame, survives in `compound_selfishness.md` and disappears from the AI essays.
- **The NVF→rules→rewards formula.** Ren's HTML comment is realised only partially in `NVF.md`'s Law/Policy section and is never stated as the explicit loop it is.
- **"This is the root cause we need equality."** The justification of equality *as the regulator of compounding* is dropped in `selfish_sprial.md`, `caboc.md` and `grok.md`, which treat inequality as a symptom and taxation as a remedy but never restate equality's structural role.
- **The endgame taxonomy.** The five branches in `prompt.md` file_5 — MAD freeze, consensus-frozen power structure, narrative change to "see themselves together as a whole", collapse, winner-takes-all — appear nowhere else. `selfish_sprial.md`'s five futures are a different and less interesting list.
- **The sample-size hedge.** "Grow sample sizes… like cells do, or something like city states" appears exactly once. It is the only proposal in the cluster that plans for collapse rather than against it.
- **The five-facet definition of selfishness.** It exists only in `prompt.md` and in Grok's reply. `NVF.md`, `selfish_sprial.md`, `caboc.md` and every good bot use "selfishness" undefined. **The hypocrisy facet is never carried forward at all** — and it is the facet that makes the theory proof against the objection that it requires cynics.
- **"It is not subject that make decisions like humans."** The explicit de-subjectification is lost in every essay that goes on talking about "selfish actors" and "elites". Ren's 2024-08-11 version — "the optimization of the capital itself" — is lost in the same way.
- **The AI-delegation hedge as a hedge.** "Doesn't mean it can work, only in theory" is preserved by Grok ("razor's edge") and by `caboc.md`'s "Platonic guardian… risks", but the good-bot lineage silently replaces AI-as-governor with AI-as-teacher without ever stating that a substitution has been made.
- **`good_bot.md` §4's propositions.** "Trust is not a prerequisite—it is an outcome" and "Once enough people see the same reality, the consensus becomes real… a self-sustaining force" are dropped from `good_bot_3.md`, `goodbot_4.md` and `good_bot_jusus_3.md`, which replaces them with non-imposition. Only `goodbot_cn.md` keeps a trace, in 信任是关键 and 塑造共同的理解.
- **"Sacrificing the real world for a game of optimizing abstract tokens."** Present in `good_bot.md` and `goodbot_2.md`; dropped from `good_bot_3.md` and `goodbot_4.md`; survives only in Chinese in `goodbot_cn.md`.
- **The libertarian / e-acc example of consensus failure.** Named in `good_bot.md` and `goodbot_2.md`, dropped after `good_bot_3.md`; `goodbot_cn.md` keeps a generic "市场自由、科技进步或持续的资本创新".
- **Credit for the intelligence-limitation collapse mode.** `convo_on_selfishness.md` attributes it to Ren; `NVF.md` drops the attribution.
- **"Building rockets without science"** and the emotion-first observation exist only in `prompt.md` file_8. The manifesto's emotional-literacy program builds on the second without citing it.
- **`copy_paste.md`'s framings.** "Money is… an emergent AI running in human wetware" and humans as "obsolete training data" are not carried into any essay.
- **Meadows' explicit leverage argument.** `runaway_selfishness.md`'s "perception shift comes first" survives only implicitly, as the good bot's claim that narrative is the greatest leverage point.
- **The concrete economic mechanisms.** Reputation accumulation replacing wealth accumulation, quadratic funding, reputation-weighted decision-making, tokenised cooperative economies, DAOs and experimental communities are named in the ChatGPT roadmap and reduced in `selfish_sprial.md` and `caboc.md` to "decentralized intelligence" and "blockchain governance".
- **The 2024 framing of lag as risk rather than doom.** "if not properly managed, will cause the system to destabilize, just like cells die" is a weaker and better claim than the 2025 exponential-acceleration version, and it is not carried forward.

## Open questions and critiques

### Raised inside the corpus

- **The central term was undefined for most of the theory's life**, and Ren says so himself. When he defines it, the definition moves the theory from a motive to a systemic attractor, which strains the word "selfishness" past its ordinary use. Grok keeps the word while conceding the point — "This isn't just a human flaw—it's a systemic engine."
- **AI is simultaneously the exit and the completion.** "If AI aligns with the selfish winners (and it's trained on their data), it could turbocharge the spiral"; "if implemented incorrectly, it could become the final stage of runaway selfishness." Ren's own hedge is "Doesn't mean it can work, only in theory."
- **Slowing acceleration re-enters the multipolar trap** between societies: "Any society that slows down risks being outcompeted by others that don't."
- **Incumbent resistance is not modelled**, only noted: "selfish actors will resist any reforms that limit their advantage"; "Actors benefiting from the current spiral have strong motivation to resist or co-opt any movement toward deeper awareness."
- **The good bot's register is the register the manifesto rejects.** `new_narrative_manifesto.md` argues that lecturing and moral-superiority contests reinforce the frame they attack; the good bot is didactic by design. `good_bot_jusus_3.md` half-registers the objection by replacing instruction with awakening, but the two files never confront each other.
- **The novelty claim is an AI's flattery**, and the same reply lists Gramsci, Foucault, Marx, Piketty, Axelrod and Moloch as partial prior art. Ren's question — "Did someone else also sees this or did I discover it?" — is never independently answered.
- **A cooperative frame must also survive external competition.** `NVF.md` says expansionist frames outcompete others and that enduring communal societies collapsed on contact with global markets. `hyperconnected.md`'s cooperation precedents do not address this.
- **The race between correction speed and loop speed is asserted, not estimated.** Ren states the condition — "if none of these are happening fast enough comparing to the speed of the feedback loop, it collapses" — and no source attempts either rate.
- **Ren's own late correction**: the theory's conclusion, believed, is itself a trap. "最近的对话，你的逻辑倾向于这是一个totalizing attractor，只有希望，改变可能性不大" and "political realism and accelerationism are not reality, but self fulfilling 'gods'".

### Found by reading the corpus

- **Two incompatible AI roles coexist without adjudication.** AI as coordinator or governor (`cognitive_lag.md`, `caboc.md`) versus AI as teacher accelerating human alignment (the good bots). The second is a response to the same lag that keeps humans in charge, and it is never stated as a choice against the first. The 2026 position — AI as planetary technology governed as common-benefit infrastructure — is a third option and is also never reconciled with the first two.
- **"Winner takes all" is one absorbing state among five in Ren's own taxonomy**, and the essays flatten this into a conclusion. The MAD freeze and the frozen-consensus deadlock are structurally different endings with different interventions, and both are stable. A theory that predicts five outcomes and names one is weaker than it looks.
- **The single-root question conflicts with Ren's own later rule.** "is the root cause… *just* selfishness?" violates "never flatten a plurality into one root", and the NVF apparatus already offers the plural, selection-based answer the question forecloses. The 2026 restatement — three beliefs, four instantiations, "not only bad people, it is the structure" — is the corrected version, and it should be adopted retroactively.
- **Acceleration grammar is smuggled in** by the AI drafts and by Ren's own "exponentially accelerating society". The lag argument does not need exponential change, only change faster than the regulator updates — which is both weaker and truer, and which Ren's own 2024-03-18 statement already had before the 2025 drafts hardened it into doom.
- **The empirical base is AI-sourced and untested.** Rome's top 1% at 16%, Maya despotism, 12% executive psychopathy, "collapse within a decade": web citations of uneven quality, none of them Ren's, none checked. ChatGPT itself proposes testing wealth-accumulation curves against narrative shifts; nobody does.
- **The definition is not falsifiable as stated.** "Outcome-driven, not intent-driven" plus the hypocrisy facet make selfishness hard to distinguish, in retrospect, from any successful strategy. Which externalities count, and measured on whose ledger, are the two questions that would make it testable, and neither is answered.
- **Equality is asserted, not derived.** The mechanism requires that compounding be *capped*, not that outcomes be *equal*. The game-design axiom — reward the winner with something other than more advantage — is the correct derivation and is stronger than the equality claim; the corpus has both and does not notice that the second subsumes the first.
- **The NVF selection list mixes incompatible traits.** Spread traits (speed, identity, hostility) and truth traits ("provide knowledge, truth and good strategies") are listed side by side without asking when they conflict — which is exactly the case that produces a civilization running on "mythologies like reasoning". Ren's 2024 formulation, that good narratives are *usually less* evolutionarily fit, is sharper and contradicts the list.
- **The lock's nature is undecided.** Loop 3 says people cannot see alternatives (cognitive lock-in, "outthought"); Ren's 2026 line says people see the stakes and comply anyway. These call for different interventions — the first for teaching, the second for changing the payoff of defection — and the good bot only addresses the first.
- **The 2024 Ouroboros layer was lost and not recovered.** The 2025 drafts are, in several respects, a less complete restatement of a machine Ren had already built. Any future version should start from the eleven steps, not from `compound_selfishness.md`.
- **The good bots are prompts, not experiments.** Whether teaching people to see the loops changes behaviour is empirical, and no file addresses it. The Jesus persona additionally raises an authority and impersonation problem that the manifesto's own critique of top-down narrative would flag.
- **`regen.md` and `hyperconnected.md` are generic research reports.** Their connection to the spiral is thematic; neither engages the lag or the lock-in arguments, and neither is written in Ren's voice.
- **Transmission, partly answered (added 2026-10-02).** The critique below asks how the theory spreads. In the conversation with Alex, Ren gives a strategy: prediction that makes an unwanted destination common knowledge, affirmative argument, demonstration, and grounding in the rising power's tradition (*Said aloud, 2026-10-02*). That is a strategy, not a propagation model. It still has no estimate of who adopts the frame, how fast, or against what counter-frames. The good-bot trial remains the one test the corpus could run.
- **The theory has no account of its own transmission.** It says frames spread by fitness, not truth, and it is itself a frame with poor spread characteristics — complex, unflattering, and demanding. The five-condition specification for the needed narrative (2025-03-01) is aware of this; nothing else is.
- **The lock, by population (added 2026-10-04).** If the sorting in *Said in Chinese, 2026-10-04* holds, the two diagnoses above are not rivals; each is true of different people. A test would survey belief and preference separately, including second-order beliefs about others, in three groups: the general public, people in positions of power, and people working on AI safety and governance. The sorting predicts a gap between preference and belief in the first group, a preference for the race in the second, and in the third a preference against the race combined with remedies that presuppose it, a stronger party as the cure.
- **A ledger or a costume (added 2026-10-04).** What makes a legitimating vocabulary constrain conduct rather than cover it (claim 168)? The proposed test is whether the governed can invoke its terms against the governors without penalty, and whether doing so changes conduct. The test applies to every polity.
- **Loop 3, approved when the direction is approved (added 2026-10-04).** [39:22] grants direction-setting narrative the most legitimacy. The theory never made the badness of a direction part of the mechanism, so it needs a criterion that separates direction-setting from capture without asking whether one agrees with the direction. The candidates are already in the corpus: whether the frame can be contested in public (公开可纠), whether it measures what it selects rather than asking to be judged on intent, and whether it rewards the winner with more capacity to win. Applied symmetrically, the test Ren ran on the San Francisco abundance narrative on 2026-10-02 is the test to run on any narrative he endorses, with "don't read them as a believer" as its method. Until the criterion is stated, [39:22] reads as an exception to the theory for one direction.
