# Spirit, Token, Space and the Yi substrate (間 · 一/太極)

**The theory in one paragraph.** A society is made of three kinds of thing and nothing else: **spirits** (whoever can act and decide — person, group, or software agent), **tokens** (any claimable unit of meaning — a membership, a right, a debt, a credit, an honour), and **spaces** (the contexts in which a token means anything at all). Every social fact has one shape: *a spirit holds a token in a space*. Because the shape is uniform, the rules of a community — who may issue what, what combinations confer what, what expires when — can be written down declaratively, checked mechanically, and revised like software; the community becomes something you can prototype rather than only inherit, and permission stops being an entry in someone's access list and becomes a thing you can hold, lend, attenuate, revoke and explain. Four years of trying to build this exactly revealed the flaw in its own statement: the three primitives are not disjoint. A spirit contains a space, a space holds tokens, a token can hold a spirit — the triad is a loop, and every attempt to keep the three apart in code produced a system whose meaning was decided by which optional field happened to be set at construction. So the theory ate itself and came out with one primitive instead of three: a **Yi** (一, "the one") — a bare addressable root with a UID and nothing else essential to it — whose *being* is the immutable kind-bindings it carries and whose *truths* are facts owned by separate contexts that never infer one another (who holds it, where it is placed, what it is connected to, what it justifies). Subject, object, container, capability, member, agent are then not categories a thing belongs to but **roles read off context facts**, so the same row can be all of them at once without changing identity. What keeps this from becoming a totalizing schema is the second half, which is doing most of the philosophical work: an explicit register of what the unification *refuses* — no hidden universal component table, no permanent categories, no permission inherited from parentage or possession, no cached allow that counts as authority, no graph inferred from another graph, and a small set of named exceptions (messages, presence, views, external effects, portability) admitted in the open rather than hidden. The political payoff is the point of the whole exercise: a host can operate the infrastructure without owning the world's constitution, an agent can act without becoming its principal, and a community that outgrows its home can leave with its history intact.

**Status.** Substantial and unusually well-evidenced, but split across two ontologies that have never been formally reconciled, and unpublished as theory. What exists: a complete 2025 framework paper and protocol review (STS/USIS); a year of design conversations in which Ren works the primitives down from three to one; a running implementation (Bonfire) with a frozen canonical frame, ten laws, a decision register, measured cognitive-load scores, and a rare public record of its own failures; and a community-design layer (lifecycle, consent, helper spirits) that is archived rather than argued. What is missing: the bridge document from Spirit–Token–Space to Yi; any statement of the ontology as philosophy rather than as TypeScript; the 間 → Yi derivation; and a treatment of prohibition (ban, mute, exclusion), which the possession model still cannot express. Two things are publishable close to as-is — the 2025 STS paper (strip its duplicated pass and its `file://` citations) and `bonfire-docs-yi/00-direction.md` as an essay on unifying a data model without totalizing it. The theory as a whole is one essay away from publishable and that essay has not been written.

## The argument

### The problem the framework was built for

Digital infrastructure has no native representation for a social fact. It has users, rows, and access-control lists; it does not have "member", "elder", "debt", "apprentice", "the right to speak here but not there", "this promise expires in March". Those live in application code, scattered across tables and conditionals that nobody outside the engineering team can read, let alone amend. The consequence is political, not merely technical: a community that runs on a platform cannot see its own rules, cannot change them without the platform's consent, and cannot take them anywhere else. The 2025 paper states the ambition in a single analogy — the framework aims "to do for social-organizational interactions what the internet did for information" (`09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`) — and the political form of the ambition is a status change for the people inside: "Individuals become **participants** in explicit social contracts, rather than subjects of implicit or opaque rules" (`09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`).

Ren's own version of the ambition is less institutional and more concrete. In 2026-03 he describes what he wants from a network as an alternative to platform tenancy: "we provide identity, a network of trust where we can see the signals from each other, where we can send tokens/values/ideas to each other, without bring shaped by profit incentives. We can experiment with our reddit, our facebook, our bbs, without loosing our purpose" (2026-03-17, `12-claude-ai-chats`). In 2026-04 he names the purpose in Chinese: the point of the substrate is "建立共识，共同行动，保持和塑造文化，主体间性的数字基础设施" — building consensus, acting together, holding and shaping culture, an intersubjective digital infrastructure — "basically 仁义礼智信的digital operation system" (2026-04-30, `12-claude-ai-chats/2026-04-30_编程语言的本体论_paradigm_作为世界观_7d5e7d78.md`). That sentence is the theory's real thesis statement, and it is nowhere in the papers.

### Three primitives, one canonical sentence

STS proposes that everything social be represented as one relation, `holds(spirit, token, space)`, and that all institutional behaviour be derived from rules over that relation. A **Space** declares which **Token** types exist, who may issue and revoke them, which combinations confer which capacities, and how they age. A **Spirit** accumulates tokens; its identity in that space just *is* the set it holds — "identity is not a substance but an accumulated portfolio". When a spirit attempts an action, the space evaluates its rule-set against the spirit's holdings and permits or refuses.

The three are kept disjoint by construction in the paper: "a Spirit is always an actor (never a context or object), a Token is always something possessed or signified (never the possessor or environment), and a Space is always a context (never an actor or object)", so that every social fact resolves into *who / what symbolic state / where*. A token alone means nothing — "A token by itself is just a symbol; the space provides the interpretation" — which makes Space the load-bearing primitive, the "boundary of relevance" that stops a credential from silently carrying authority into a context that never granted it.

This is Searle's institutional-fact formula made executable. "X counts as Y in context C": the token is X, the status is Y, the space is C, and the declaration is data anyone in the space can read. The paper's own framing is that the grammar is a syntax for **status function declarations** — "this token shall count as a membership badge in this community" — which turns collective intentionality from a philosophical description into an editable file.

### Why possession rather than permission

Two consequences follow immediately and do most of the theoretical work.

*Enforcement moves upstream.* Because permission is a precondition rather than a punishment, "in a well-designed STS space, unauthorized actions are naturally impossible because the preconditions (tokens) are not met". Governance stops depending on the vigilance of leaders and becomes a property of the arrangement — trust-minimised governance. This is the move capability-based security makes in operating systems, transplanted into social life: possession of the right token by the right spirit in the right space *is* the proof of the right to act, with no separate access-control list to maintain, consult, or trust. The 2026-04 SST design states the rule in one line: "No 'Alice is admin therefore X' — only 'here is a proof chain that authorizes X.'" Delegation is issuing a token; sub-delegation is issuing a token bound to a parent token; revocation is lazy, so revoking a parent makes dependent tokens fail resolution and re-granting reactivates them — no cascading deletes, and a clean audit trail (`12-claude-ai-chats/2026-04-20_Spirit-token_design_overview_b20b24d5.md`).

*Institutions become compositional and therefore emergent.* A rule that `CouncilMember` requires `Member` and `ElectedDelegate` builds a hierarchy without anyone writing "hierarchy". A rule that 100 reputation points auto-issue `TrustedContributor` builds a promotion ladder without a promotion committee. A 2-of-3 approval requirement builds a check-and-balance without a constitutional clause: authority can be *split* rather than assigned, one conceptual privilege becoming several tokens held by different people. The complexity-theory bet is explicit — simple space-level rules generate macro structures (coalitions, leadership, norms) nobody programmed — and the paper draws the practical conclusion: communities can run safe institutional experiments, adjusting token rules the way one tweaks game mechanics, watching what happens, and iterating. It calls this **civilization prototyping**, and expects a shared **library of proven social components** to accumulate, accelerating institution-building the way open source accelerated software.

The paper is careful to say that formalising rules is not meant to displace values: "The **spirit** of a community – its ethos and goals – remains paramount; STS simply provides the tools to give that spirit concrete form."

### The loop that would not stay disjoint

The disjointness discipline is where the framework begins to fail, and the failure is productive. From the very first design conversation the three primitives are stated as *mutually containing*: "spirit can have a space, token can have a sprite, and space can have tokens" (2026-03-23). Within a day Ren is pushing the recursion further, because the recursion is what makes the model expressive: "spirit can define tokens, their rules and properties, where the token can inherit some power from the spirit, or tokens can hold a spirit or spirits, and spirits can be extended" (2026-03-24). And the token is not a data container but a relationship: "the token is more about the relationship, like these people are in a chat group, and there's a spirit of the token that does the magic… this graph defines know how and what, where, and other relational things" — the actual chat stays in a conventional backend; the graph carries only the relation. By 2026-04 this becomes an explicit **token-as-edge discipline**: tokens carry relationships and pointers, not content, so a spirit with 500 connections, 50 credentials and 10 chats has about 600 tokens, not millions of rows. "SST is the graph; implementations hang off the edges."

But a loop is not three kinds of thing. If a spirit is structurally a space with identity metadata attached, and a token is an edge that can itself hold a spirit, then the trio is one substance in three positions. Ren reaches this conclusion himself: "i think my current cleanest version is to make spirit space token into 2 things, spirit and pointer, spirit has contains, and pointer points to spirit" (2026-04-18). He immediately tests the reduction against what it would cost — "how about pointer only points to spirit, and leave token as token?" — because collapsing token into pointer leaves nowhere for authorization semantics to live. The design conversation that follows maps three independent axes (read gate, token location, containment heterogeneity) and four named configurations; Ren picks a hybrid, in which tokens are a *shape of spirit* living in the graph, delegation is graph mutation (moving token-spirits between contains-sets), revocation is removal, and caveats and visibility policies are themselves contained spirits evaluated at resolution time.

Two structural discoveries fall out of that hybrid and survive into the final substrate. First, **visibility and conditions are not attributes but relations** — a policy is a spirit you contain, not a bit you set. Second, the thing the vocabulary is missing is a word: "is there a word of abstraction that describes both space and group?" (2026-04-23). A space with members and a group of members are the same object seen twice; English has no name for it.

### The actor turn, and 間

By 2026-04-26 Ren restates the whole ontology from the agent's side rather than the token's: "A spirit is an agent, in its environment, that takes input from the environment then give output, the environment can be another spirit, can be a group of spirits, or could even be a world, where these spirits can hold things — holdings, like token, item, relationships, but I don't have a good category theory to make them not overlap each other." He names the target honestly — "what I really want, is more like an actor model interact with each other through interfaces, and data/states can be changed through them, and responses can be programmed" — and he draws the consequence that removes the last spatial metaphor: "This is a digital world — not a physical one, so we don't need things like x,y,z coordinate — instead, they are dynamic path… the database is the environment." He adds a primitive the later substrate never re-adopts under that name: **generators**, which produce spirits or holdings when coded conditions are met, define what they create, and can trade.

Then the question that dissolves the triad. On 2026-05-01 he asks whether SST can be designed as a π-calculus, proposes `act + capacity + session`, and then proposes the reflexive four: "How about actor，capacity, session, message 4 things, and capacity, session, message, are also actors" — homoiconicity at the ontology level, where the members of the type system are instances of one of its own types. Within minutes he sharpens session: "Session不只是protocol，它同时也是一个集 - actor的集" (a session is not only a protocol, it is simultaneously a set — a set of actors). That is the answer to the 2026-04-23 question: the word that abstracts both space and group is *session-as-set*. And then the rename: "session也是space，可能这个名字有点迷惑，文言文的间或许是一个更好的名字，发生的地方，有点类似于spacetime" — a session is also a space; "session" is a confusing name; 文言's 間 is a better one, the place where things happen, something like spacetime.

The philosophical commitments Ren attaches to 間 are the ones that matter for the substrate that follows. He refuses to make either of the two poles a special case of the other: "间即是actor，它们更多像波粒二象性，一体两面" (間 *is* actor; they are more like wave–particle duality, one body with two faces). He requires both a single universal 間 and plural worlds at once: "间是普遍性的…但是同时支持'平行宇宙'". He rules that caveats and visibility "是从关系中涌现的" — emergent from relations, not stored attributes. He states the priority of philosophy over engineering explicitly: "它首先是一个'哲学系统' - 理，然后有工程实现，我们先确保根基稳固完整" (it is first a philosophical system — 理 — then it has an engineering implementation; we make the foundation solid and complete first). And he licenses the engineering reduction he refuses philosophically: "间即是actor，即是事，即是机，它内在（哲学上）有不可还原性（但是在工程上可以），但是符号只需要一个，间即可". Finally, the retirement: "把sst改名为间（不再需要sst）" — rename SST to 間; SST is no longer needed.

This is the hinge of the whole theory and neither written draft carries it. Between 2025-08 and 2026-06 the framework did not simply get implemented; it was reduced, twice, by Ren, in public, on stated grounds — first from three primitives to two, then from a triad to a single non-dual primitive with an actor face and a place face.

### The Yi turn: one root, meaning in the open

Bonfire, the running system, does not name its primitive 間. It names it **Yi**, glossed in its own etymology section as 一 / 太極 — "the one", "great ultimate" — pronounced "ee", and it declares its parentage in one sentence: "**Bonfire is an encoded realization of 间理**" (`09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-docs-yi/01-frame.md`). (The corpus does not say whether the *yi* of 一 is also meant to hear the *yi* of 易; see the open questions.)

The kernel: "A `Yi` is the one addressable bearer of traits, relations, capabilities, state, and behavior. Meaning lives in the `kinds[]` a Yi bears." The root is deliberately impoverished — UID, domain, primary kind binding, creation and extinction timestamps, a monotonic sequence, the current commit head — and everything interesting is elsewhere and *owned*. "There is one stable runtime root, **Yi**, but not one universal component table": Identity owns principal coordinates, Custody owns holdings, Containment owns placements, Relations owns occurrences, Authority owns certificates. The reason each has its own owner is that "Alice holds a key", "a key is equipped in a slot", "Alice is a member of a group" and "a certificate permits Alice to moderate" have different conservation, cardinality, lifecycle and authorization laws. Collapsing them into one edge table would make the model prettier and the invariants unenforceable.

The consequence is the theory's central image, and the one non-engineer can hold: **one thing, several truths**. A sword is at once a conserved asset (Custody), a child placed in a backpack (Containment), an object equipped in a hand slot, the source of a permission (Authority), an endpoint in a trade relation (Relations), a participant in a cross-domain escrow, a row in four views, and a stable object with recoverable history — and "[t]hose facts overlap, but they are not interchangeable" (`bonfire-concepts/bonfire-use-cases-and-possibilities.md`). Off games the same table generalises: house key, membership badge, agent token, event ticket, treasury approval, research artifact. Subject, object, holder, held asset, child, parent, endpoint, issuer, presenter, capability, container and agent are **a reading lens, not root types** — read off context facts, "never inferred from an optional authored field or a hidden category". The sword can be all of them because none of them is a category it belongs to. That is the restatement, in a database, of Ren's own formulation: "A thing can be a **subject** or an **object**, a **bearer** or a **capability**, a **container**, a **type**, an **agent**, a **message target**, or a **resource** — *depending on its traits and relations, not on a permanent category*. Having something and being something are the same substance seen from two ends of a relation. Even the **type** of a thing is a thing."

### Why the pivot was necessary: meaning was in the wrong layer

The Yi turn was not a redesign chosen for elegance; it was a diagnosis of a specific failure with a measured cost. Bonfire's pre-pivot system "unified the layer it should have left plural (*representation*) and left plural the layer it meant to unify (*meaning*)" — exactly inverted. The worst concrete instance: a holding's nature (capability vs attestation vs edge) was inferred "from **which optional field happens to be present at construction**, not declared on its kind". A kind that was supposed to carry the contract carried none of it. The cost was counted, not asserted: ~84 re-derivations of one sub-face predicate across core, server and std; ~20 kit sites bypassing the "one root"; an advertised ~6-concept frame against ~24 concepts actually held; a low-resistance score of ~42/100 — "a beautiful spine with leaky joints".

The metric that justifies the whole program is cognitive, not architectural: "**mental resistance = the number of independent things you must hold in working memory to predict the system's behavior at a point**" — explicitly not primitive count, since three primitives with a dozen special cases are worse than five that compose cleanly. This is the criterion under which the 2025 triad loses. Three primitives that mutually contain each other, whose disjointness is asserted in prose and violated in every real model, cost more working memory than one root with declared traits.

The cure is a **compiler seam**. An author declares what a kind *means* — a family genesis token, an authored-state schema, typed semantic `facet.*` contributions each owned by exactly one context, explicit `bindFacet.*` declarations naming the exact source contribution, target contribution, execution class and deciding owner, plus ports and gates. A deploy-time compiler validates and closes those declarations into an immutable binding revision and a deterministic representation plan. The runtime executes that plan and "never guesses meaning from names, optional fields, or storage layout". This is what makes "meaning is explicit" enforceable rather than aspirational: runtime never has to ask what kind of thing this is, because the plan already says what must be materialised. The strongest single law of the substrate, and the one to keep if only one survives, is that "meaning should be in `kinds[]`, not in optional field presence".

Naming is part of the same discipline. When a table called `yi_message` turned out to hold the one canonical thing that is explicitly *not* a Yi, the fix was a cross-cutting code rename, not a documentation note: "the `yi_` prefix asserts the table is part of the Yi family … when the concept is defined by being the one canonical thing that is explicitly NOT a Yi. **The prefix is the lie.**"

### Agency is a trait

The correction the substrate names as the most important to a naive actor model: "**Every Yi should be addressable. Not every Yi should be active.**" Universal agency would expand every gate surface and raise the cognitive load the whole pivot exists to reduce, so addressability is universal and agency is composed. Subjecthood decomposes into orthogonal traits — `addressable`, `callable`/`messageable`, `agent`, `capability`, `container` — so that "can hold authority" is separated from "can initiate action", and a later audit sharpens even this: initiating should require agency, holding a capability should not, and receiving a port may need only `messageable`.

What agency *is*, when composed, is a stack of separately-owned facts: an Identity principal to sign, ports to offer, gates to admit, Authority certificates to carry delegated permission, and any Custody or Containment influence declared through an explicit cross-context binding. Four control axes are kept apart and never fused — authored-state authority, custody, signing identity, operation authority — with holder-transfer added later as a fifth. And the item itself must be able to act: the acceptance test is that one Yi carrying item, Identity and agent bindings "**signs its own action, receives a trigger, and fires a gated port — with tests**", with delegation to a separate agent Yi permitted as a pattern but "never as the meaning of an active item".

The worked example that carries the mechanism is the **equipped key**. Custody records who holds it. Containment records whether it occupies the required slot. Authority records the certificate it activates. An explicit binding ties the certificate's activation to the placement. The port gate decides. Move the key out of the slot and the standing disappears *by semantic change*, atomically, with no second permission database and nothing to keep in sync. Because every input is a live fact re-read at the decision frontier, "**every invocation re-evaluates current evidence; a cached `allow` is never Authority**", and revocation is eager rather than swept.

Legitimacy is built on top of this as a hard requirement rather than a debugging convenience: "Why can Alice do this?" must trace through held item, group membership, delegated capability, port gate, role and policy as "**one graph of cause**" returned by a single `explain(caller, target, port)`. A system whose refusals are inexplicable has replaced the arbitrary leader with an arbitrary machine. The corpus is honest about how hard this is: `explain` was marked closed while it existed only in a test fixture, and when it was finally wired it was implemented as sibling dispatch rather than a synthetic conjunction, because a fabricated `and(floor, portGate)` node "would fabricate a conjunction the runtime never computes — explain would lie".

### One place where change happens

Every durable change across every owner lands atomically on one commit spine, the **Occasion**: a domain-scoped coordinator acquires the lock, runs transaction-scoped narrow repositories, collects exact effects, computes the immutable commit, binds every changed root to the actual commit ID, and commits once. "An empty Occasion, an effect without materialized state, or an ordinary durable write outside the coordinator **fails closed**." Nested commands join the active Occasion rather than opening a second transaction or a side-channel audit record. Because history, watches, recovery, erasure propagation and authority frontiers all read one source, the system can claim that its audit cannot disagree with its state — the row is truth, the event is a prunable shadow co-written in the same transaction. Robustness in one line: "one truth (the canonical row); events are best-effort prunable audit, co-written same-tx so the audit cannot disagree; every projection rebuildable… **Every guarantee is *re-expressed*, never erased.**"

### The refusals, which are the load-bearing half

The reason this is a theory and not a schema is that the same documents that unify are the documents that say where unification stops, and give a criterion for saying so.

*Not everything is one table.* "The good version is not 'everything is one table'; it is everything is one kind of meaningful thing, while storage, indexes, fan-out, presence, messages, feeds, and moderation can be specialized where scale demands it." Unification is of *meaning*; specialisation below the seam is permitted and expected. "The danger is not 'too many internal systems.' The danger is exposing those systems as unrelated concepts to authors."

*One product language does not mean one generic graph.* Seven graphs are separately owned with distinct change and loss laws, cycle policies and authority answers — kind/revision dependency, package/install dependency, Identity ancestry, Custody/Containment/Relation, activation requirement, activation generation ownership, and Authority standing — and only the last grants authority. The non-inference rule is written as an algebra: `A contains B ≠ A owns B`; `A created B ≠ B may use A's grants`; `A depends on T ≠ A may invoke T`; `A may invoke T ≠ T is available`; `A owns generation B ≠ extinguishing A extinguishes Yi B`. Identity ancestry conveys lifecycle and governance but never ambient permission. Containment conveys placement, never permission. "Agent creation and creator/child relationships convey no grants." The only transitive edge for issued authority is `certificate.parent`.

*Some things are named exceptions, admitted in the open.* Streams (dense-ordered content and reply causality), Signals (TTL-bounded presence, typing, cursors, occupancy, which "do not assert durable authority or historical truth"), Views and projections, external effects with durable outbox and compensation ("a database transaction cannot roll back a completed external call"), and portability are first-class and explicitly *not* generic components. These are "named boundaries, not hidden alternative roots" — the honesty condition on "no hidden center". The pivot later corrects its own undercount: the named non-Yi centres are the column-promoted relations *and* the stream tier, so "'No *hidden* center' stays true; 'one exception' undercounted".

*Unification is refuted where the mechanisms genuinely differ.* The governing principle of the end-state is "**Unify the DECLARATION; split the EXECUTION by its real axis**". The north-star hypothesis that `kinds[]` membership, holding edges and the message DAG are one primitive in three costumes is confirmed for the relation and the mental frame and **refuted** for the mechanisms: the reducers differ along timing, residency, failure-mode, context shape and cache lifetime, and every grounded attempt to fuse them was either a behaviour change or a hot-path or security regression. Hence the line the project treats as a result in its own right: "**A rigorous 'this split is load-bearing, keep it' is as valuable as a unification.**"

*Elegance must be earned by closure, not by renaming.* "Renaming `face → trait` is **not** the win." The acceptance test is whether `capability` + `container` + `agent` compose on one row with no special-case branch, and where combination invariants genuinely exist the honest report is a cross-trait matrix shrinking from N rules to M — not a claim of zero special cases. Moving forty-five kind fields into forty-five traits "relocates the zoo, it does not dissolve it".

*And the failure mode is named in advance.* "**Rhetorical victory before engineering closure**": if a document says "the item itself acts", the first milestone must make it literally true in witness resolution, trigger dispatch, ports and tests. The program enforces this on itself with a CI concept budget (kept vocabulary ≤12, one authoring front door, one dispatch lane, one commit spine, no alternate representation classifiers) added specifically so "the program cannot win on features while the concept count silently regrows" — "defended by executable structure and public-surface checks, not asserted by prose". It then reports its own failure to meet it: the first actual measurement was ~17 concepts against the ≤12 target, and "the thing the pivot is *for* is the one thing not on the scorecard".

### The social layer: what a community actually does over time

The 2025 papers describe a static algebra of permissions. The community work describes what a community *does*, and that requires a vocabulary the algebra never supplied: `doorway → welcome token → graduation → (deactivation) → release → composting → commemoration`. Each is a named transition with its own social meaning. A doorway means entry is bounded and hosted. Graduation means belonging is earned and marked, not a database flag flipped in silence. Release means leaving is an act with a form rather than a disappearance. Composting means what a community stops doing is metabolised rather than deleted. Commemoration means the record of what happened survives the thing that happened. These are not features; they are a claim about how groups stay alive, and they are where the framework stops being a permission system.

Community functions are personified rather than exposed as configuration: **Heron** holds presence, **Fox** holds repair, **Sage** holds the door, **Owl** holds cadence. Each carries an ethical constraint inside its own description. Heron distinguishes `here`, `away` and `not_joining` "without making absence punitive" — absence is information a community needs, not deviance to be policed. Fox runs repair queues privately "without turning conflict into a leaderboard" — visibility of conflict is itself a harm vector. The **Witness Book** records moments as crystallized entries and mints witness holdings to the people who were there while preserving the source event, so memory belongs to participants and the original is not overwritten. The **Wishing Pool** and **Need Wall** make wanting and lacking ordinary addressable community state rather than private burdens.

The kit is defined as much by its refusals: no public repair leaderboards, no coercive presence tracking, no erased history, no unbounded authority, and — the one that is an alignment claim in miniature — no "conflating a helper spirit's suggestion with permission to act". An assisting intelligence may propose; it does not thereby acquire authority.

The platform model generalises this into a constitution with a very small number of moving parts. One container primitive: "a **group** is a spirit with members". One stamp: "a **community is a group that has been given an operating system**" — its own path, a rulebook, a surface, its own belongings. A community must own its own address (`c/gardeners`) rather than living in a slot under its founder, because sovereignty starts with not living inside someone else's namespace, and it owns its own items, so "who owns the wishing pool" is never ambiguous. Sub-groups get "a folder, not a second desktop", which prevents the fractal proliferation of incompatible governance; and a sub-group that outgrows its home **graduates** into its own community using the same transition primitive that graduates a person into membership — schism replaced by an ordinary lifecycle event.

The usability law is one sentence: "**Defaults minimize config; gates make customization unbounded.**" A community should be usable with zero decisions and re-specifiable without limit, and the way to have both is to make the rules *data* rather than code, so the same edit can be made from the UI, from the SDK, or "by an agent someone asked in chat".

The political core is the **consent door**. Everything entering a community — apps, bots, kits, preset contents — passes one approval, moderator by default, and "the consent record always exists, *even for the founder's own agent*". The founder is inside the rules, not above them, and the record is kept regardless of the answer. Bundles may *propose* bots but never install them: "Daemons are added by the owner, never smuggled in." Agents join through the same door as humans, bear the same member label, rank the same tiers, can hold the moderator role, and act through the same ports — "agents are operators, not exceptions" — with **hero-as-principal** keeping a human at the root of every authority chain and acting *for* a human kept as a separate, explicit delegation path.

Ren's own late statement of the openness this is all for is sharper than the documents': the ability for anyone to define new kinds is "very key… It is not a part that needs to be mounted on a kit"; an agent can create kinds under itself and use them; a user can create new kinds; a community that defines its own kinds locally, without publishing, "should have the freedom to have this dynamism"; and — the line that states what a world is — "**this is the key that makes worlds 'worlds'**" (2026-09-02, Claude Code). A world is defined by the openness of its ontology, not by its feature set.

### The counterexamples, and how the theory answers them

The corpus supplies its own adversary, and the adversary wins several rounds. This is the strongest evidence the theory has.

*Possession cannot express prohibition.* The founding move is that a social fact is something a spirit *holds*. Bans, mutes, blocks, timeouts and deny-overrides-allow are social facts that are precisely *not* held by their subject. The adversarial review lists negative rights and resource-scoped delegation among the things the design "cannot yet do elegantly" — "bot may moderate only one channel; agent may use one tool only inside one community; member may view a channel but not post; moderator may timeout but not ban" — and prescribes a first-class policy layer with scope tuples, positive and negative rights, precedence, explain, audit, revocation and cache invalidation. The theory's answer is not to abandon possession but to stop pretending gates over verbs are a governance model: a relation "can represent the underlying facts, but cannot be the whole product model". This is the largest unclosed hole in the theory as theory, and it is a theoretical fault rather than an implementation gap: the canonical sentence "a Spirit holds a Token in a Space" is incomplete, and a second relation — a space *imposing* a state on a spirit — may be irreducible.

*Moderation is not a token type.* Report, modlog, appeal, quarantine, sanctions, evidence preservation must be designed, not derived; and speech, reach and moderation must not be collapsed into one substrate layer (the AT Protocol lesson, where PDS, relay, app view, feed generators and labelers are separate services). Delivery is likewise a social fact of its own — recipient sets, inboxes and outboxes, de-duplication, retry, backfill — because "generic events plus visibility filters are not enough" (the ActivityPub lesson).

*Not everything should be one thing.* The sharpest internal critique is the anti-pattern of **metaphysical compression**: "If every concept is declared 'just a tie,' the architecture may feel elegant while losing operational handles." Membership vs role vs ban, holding vs containment vs delegation, feed membership vs read permission, message causality vs storage parentage, presence vs durable state are *product facts*, not notation. The answer is the seam — unify the authoring surface, specialise underneath, never expose the specialisation as unrelated concepts — and, at the mechanism level, the refusal to fuse reducers that differ along a real axis.

*Some state must not be permanent.* Presence, typing, voice, zone and roster state are declared explicitly volatile — TTL-bounded, tenant-scoped, outside durable audit truth — because forcing them into permanent record is both expensive and "semantically confused". Against a framework whose instinct is that every social fact should be a durable holding, this is a real concession: some social facts are supposed to evaporate, and privacy depends on it.

*Elegance must survive scale.* Messages are not Yis. They live in a purpose-built dense store with a per-session monotonic sequence and a session-local reply DAG, because they carry three axes a generic relation cannot cheaply carry and there are billions of them; the justification is stated as scale, not ontology. Two purer alternatives were examined and rejected — event-canonical loses prunability and rebuilds the same table anyway; message-as-Yi pays the full row shape across billions of rows. The exception is then named in the open, and a proposal to demote it to an appendix was rejected on the ground that it would *soften the law*.

*Is the elegance real or rhetorical?* The program answers with adversarial review and measurement rather than argument: fidelity scores (42/100 pre-pivot → 81/100 post-spine → a 66/100 grand-review synthesis), a measured held-concept count against a target, a CI budget, and the public discovery that two mechanisms marked CLOSED were built but unwired. It also records a security regression caused by its own elegance work: folding the read surface onto an ungated substrate call leaked hidden rows and per-spirit private signing keys to any authenticated caller, found by adversarial review and fixed with regression tests. The discipline that follows is "**code rises to the docs ideal, never the reverse**" — and when a standing law was violated in execution, the ruling was to keep the law and name the cost: "Do not edit the law to match what happened."

*The adoption constraint.* The system must be "expressive enough to cover nuanced social scenarios and simple enough that communities can adopt it without needing a PhD in formal methods". The 2025 answer was high-level templates; the 2026 answer, arrived at independently a year later, is presets and kits. That the same answer was reached twice is weak evidence it is the right one and strong evidence the constraint is real.

### What the whole thing is for

The political thesis is stated once, in the least engineering-flavoured document in the corpus, and it is the best short statement of why any of this matters: "A host can operate the infrastructure **without owning the world's constitution**. A package can add behavior without receiving ambient power. An agent can act without becoming its principal. An object can confer capability without becoming a hidden ACL entry. A community can leave without pretending that foreign Authority remains valid everywhere." Portable worlds would make migration "an ordinary governance option rather than a catastrophic reconstruction project", creating "meaningful operator competition: hosting can be a service without becoming ownership of the community's ontology". Forking preserves lineage without granting the source continuing control — forking without amnesia. Interoperation happens through exact kind identities, content-addressed artifacts, signed evidence and explicit local admission rather than a global schema or a trust root, so "the boundary is part of the design rather than something hidden beneath a nominally universal API".

And the negative space is stated with equal care. The substrate provides no truth about external physical events, no legal recognition of credentials, no permissionless global consensus, no cross-host atomicity, no dead-host recovery beyond exports, no offline-first sync, and "a substitute for product governance and human judgment" — plus the line that limits the entire programme from inside: "**Not every social norm should become code.**" The 2025 paper had already conceded the same boundary without theorising it: norms that cannot be tokenised ("spirit must be polite") stay outside the system and rely on ordinary social enforcement.

Ren's own image for the whole thing is older and warmer than any of this. The system is called Bonfire because "just like our first technology, bonfire, it provide warmth, gather people, it is our place to hangout, communicate, and build relationships — it is an good existence — it creates more possibilities, strengthens trust and relationships, make us more aligned, not dividing us… it does not worship moloch, neither does it to the god of market" (2026-04-26).

### J. Ports, gates, agency

184. 197. [mechanism] — `.../01-frame.md`; `.../02-pivot.md` (D13)

### K. Occasion — the single commit spine

200. "**Occasion is the single durable commit spine** joining their changes" — one Domain-scoped coordinator acquires the lock, runs transaction-scoped narrow repositories, collects exact effects, computes the immutable commit, binds every changed Yi root to the actual commit ID, and commits once. 201. "An empty Occasion, an effect without materialized state, or an ordinary durable write outside the coordinator **fails closed**"; nested commands join the active Occasion rather than opening a second transaction or a side-channel audit record. [mechanism] — same
202. Checkpoint plus retained Occasion tail must reconstruct normalized state; history, watches, Authority frontiers, recovery, restore, erasure propagation and pruning all consume the same commit/effect source. [mechanism] — `.../01-frame.md`
203. Partial manifestation is invalid: a multi-context Kind is materialized by one runtime command that validates state once, prepares every component named by the committed plan, and commits root + bindings + authored state + components + effects through one Occasion. [mechanism] — `.../01-frame.md`; `.../yi.md`

### L. The laws, and what is deliberately not in the frame

206. Ten laws govern the frame: (1) one stable root; (2) meaning is explicit, "never identifier prefixes or optional-field inference"; (3) owners are exclusive; (4) families are closed; (5) cross-context effects are bound; (6) ports and authority are exact; (7) representation is compiled; (8) one durable commit spine; (9) queries respect ownership; (10) break forward cleanly. [value stance] — `.../01-frame.md`
207. Law 10 forbids compatibility scaffolding outright: "Prelaunch changes do not retain dual writes, compatibility aliases, old wire decoders, or detached projections"; restated as the founder's standing constraint "NOT deployed — no backward compatibility of any kind… Break forward, always the long-term-elegant option." [value stance] — `.../01-frame.md`; `.../08-ideal-architecture.md`
208. Break forward, but atomically: "Breaking forward is not the same as deleting half a bridge and leaving the other half to guess. Each phase needs one clean world, not old and new names coexisting." [design direction] — `.../frame-pivot-evaluation.md`
209. Queries must use context-owned indexed paginated operations; "no generic Yi scan substitutes for them". [mechanism] — `.../01-frame.md`; `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-concepts/visibility.md`
210. Five things are first-class but explicitly *not* generic Yi components — **Streams**, **Signals** (TTL-bounded presence/typing/cursors/occupancy that "do not assert durable authority or historical truth"), **Views and projections**, **external effects** (durable outbox/workflow with idempotency and compensation, because "a database transaction cannot roll back a completed external call"), and **portability**. [design direction] — `.../01-frame.md`
211. **Federation and sharding "translate semantic Domain and principal coordinates to physical placement. They do not alter the Kind or Yi model."** [mechanism] — `.../01-frame.md`; `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-concepts/federation.md`
212. These exclusions are "named boundaries, not hidden alternative roots" — the honesty condition on "no hidden center"; the pivot sharpens the count so that "'No *hidden* center' stays true; 'one exception' undercounted". [value stance] — `.../01-frame.md`; `.../02-pivot.md` (D9)
213. Messages are the deliberate exception: Streams-owned, non-Yi rows in their own dense `stream` table with a per-session monotonic `seq`, forming a session-local DAG via `parents[]`, "intentionally minimal compared to Yis: they carry no Kind binding, authored state, or normalized Yi components". [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-concepts/messages.md`
214. The justification is scale, not ontology: at ~10K–100K users and ~5B messages per host, "the per-row overhead of dragging the full Yi machinery onto every chat line would be wasteful"; a stream append still commits atomically with related Yi/context changes through Occasion. [conditional] — same
215. A single named exception is preferable to a hidden one: the stream tier is admitted openly because "Law 1 names its exceptions in the open by design", and the proposal to demote it to an appendix was rejected as *softening the law*. [value stance] — `.../stream-tier-conceptualization-2026-06-21.md`
216. Constraints can be deliberate rather than debt: the per-session write lock is "a correctness guarantee, not an accident", kept "until a measured need overturns it", with the firehose workload explicitly parked. [design direction] — same
217. A declared **View** "is not a primary storage representation" but an explicitly authored read product compiled into one installed logical operator DAG; readers "never infer a plan from a name, family, catalog head, source Kind, or 'latest' selection". [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-docs-yi/24-projection-stance.md`
218. View delivery is a capability law, not a style: `view.materialized` cannot express traversal, forged traversal-plus-materialized bytes reject at decode and install, and traversal-bearing Views are live-only. [mechanism] — same; `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-docs-yi/sdk-elegance-exploration.md`

### M. Spirits, holdings, certificates, sigils

219. "A spirit is an addressable agent: a Yi whose effective Kind binding includes an Identity principal contribution. ### N. The pivot: diagnosis and metric

235. The pivot's one-line thesis: Bonfire "unified the layer it should have left plural (*representation*) and left plural the layer it meant to unify (*meaning*)" — the inversion is the source of constant friction. [observation] — `.../02-pivot.md`
236. The worst concrete instance: a holding's nature (capability vs attestation vs edge) was inferred "from **which optional field happens to be present at construction**, not declared on its kind" — so a Kind carrying the contract carried none of it. [observation] — same
237. The cost was measured, not impressionistic: ~84 re-derivations of one sub-face predicate across core/server/std, ~20 kit sites bypassing the "one root", an advertised ~6-concept frame against ~24 concepts actually held, and a low-resistance score of ~42/100 — "a beautiful spine with leaky joints". [observation] — same
238. Three mechanisms that felt separate were one idea in three costumes — `kinds[]` membership, holding edges and the message DAG are all "a typed, directed link between two addressable things — **a tie**" — split for real performance reasons, but "it leaked out of the implementation and into the concept". [mechanism] — same
239. Founder intent restated: a thing can be subject or object, bearer or capability, container, type, agent, message target or resource "*depending on its traits and relations, not on a permanent category*"; "Having something and being something are the same substance seen from two ends of a relation. Even the **type** of a thing is a thing." [observation] — same
240. The operative success metric is cognitive: "**mental resistance = the number of independent things you must hold in working memory to predict the system's behavior at a point**" — explicitly *not* primitive count, since three primitives with a dozen special cases beat five that compose cleanly; the levers are closure, no-special-cases and uniform access. [mechanism] — same
241. Two constitutional corollaries: the concept need not mirror the implementation 1:1 provided divergence is hidden below the surface and reconstructable ("a leak is a defect; a hidden divergence is not"), and "**the conceptual framework itself may be flawed** — we are permitted to question the concepts, not just the engineering." [value stance] — same
242. The seam is realized as a **deploy-time compiler**, not a runtime planner (D3); pinning is default and the cost-based re-materializer is deferred indefinitely. [design direction] — same
243. The acceptance test is **closure, not renaming** (D6): "renaming `face → trait` is **not** the win"; the real question is "do `capability` + `container` + `agent` compose on one row with no special-case branch?", and where combination invariants exist the honest metric is the cross-trait matrix shrinking from N rules to M. [value stance] — same
244. Meaning lives in the open (D7): what a Yi *is* equals the traits it composes, "never which optional field was set at construction, never a parallel `face`/`face_class`/`Kind.face` discriminator". [design direction] — same
245. Two membership *relations* exist and must not be fused (D8): session-participation (who is in a thread) and community-standing (who is a member of a community); the law is "one encoding per membership *relation*", not one encoding overall. [observation] — same; `.../06-unification.md`
246. Membership became uniform revocable cells and a founder's guaranteed control now flows from **ownership**, not an unremovable roster — separating "**who-is-in** (ties) from **who-controls** (ownership/gates)". [design direction] — `.../08-ideal-architecture.md` (S6); `.../02-pivot.md`
247. After the representation spine landed, the verdict was "**Representation is unified; meaning is not yet**" — fidelity ≈81/100, with the measured held-concept count at ~17 against the ≤12 target the whole pivot is justified on. [observation] — `.../06-unification.md`
248. **G8**, the cognitive-budget goal, was added so "the program cannot win on features while the concept count silently regrows": CI pins kept-vocabulary ≤12, one Kind authoring front door, one dispatch lane, one Occasion commit spine, context-owned storage, and the absence of alternate representation classifiers — "defended by executable structure and public-surface checks, not asserted by prose". [design direction] — `.../00-direction.md`; `.../06-unification.md`
249. Two mechanisms were "BUILT BUT UNWIRED while marked CLOSED" — the one-graph `explain` and the compiled gate cache — "the one place the docs over-claim". [observation] — `.../06-unification.md`
250. A CRITICAL read-authorization regression shipped during the surface fold: re-pointing the read surface at the ungated substrate read leaked hidden rows and per-spirit private signing keys to any authenticated caller — found by adversarial review and fixed with regression tests. [observation] — same
251. The principled storage line is "**gated verbs vs ungated substrate**", not "perspectival vs privileged": one gated verb surface per noun over one `@internal` ungated substrate root whose trust boundary is empirically the wire. [design direction] — same
252. The unification plan's discipline: "**code rises to the docs ideal, never the reverse**" — the ideal docs stay pristine and revisions are proposed for founder ratification, not applied unilaterally. [design direction] — same
253. Law 10 was kept un-softened despite a disclosed inversion in its own execution: "Do not edit the law to match what happened — that would violate the code→docs discipline." [value stance] — same
254. The lens that decides the unification plan: "**name the genuine distinction crisply, fold everything that is actually the same thing, and delete the dead**" — unify *within* each authorization question, never across them. [design direction] — same

### O. The ideal architecture: where unification stops

255. The governing principle of the end-state: "**Unify the DECLARATION; split the EXECUTION by its real axis**" — the tie hypothesis is CONFIRMED at the level of relation and mental frame and **REFUTED** at the level of "collapse the mechanisms". [mechanism] — `.../08-ideal-architecture.md`

257. "A rigorous 'this split is load-bearing, keep it' is as valuable as a unification." [value stance] — same

Four derivations fall out of one tie relation, each "one relation read by a purpose-built reducer": composition (`kinds[]` set + MRO), reaction (`sys/reaction` with inline/deferred/sync lanes), membership (one `isMemberOf` predicate whose representation the seam compiler picks), and authority (three firing-time layers: caller-side gate, deploy-time license, in-transaction `can_mint`). ### P. One language, several owned graphs; object-capability

262. The correction to a naive one-model reading: "**One product language does not mean one generic graph.**" [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-docs-yi/sdk-one-model-direction.md`
263. Absolute non-inheritance laws: Identity ancestry conveys lifecycle and governance but never ambient permission; Containment conveys placement, never permission; Custody and Relations give standing only through an explicit Authority facet binding; "agent creation and creator/child relationships convey no grants"; the only transitive edge for issued Authority is `certificate.parent`; and "**every invocation re-evaluates current evidence; a cached `allow` is never Authority**". 266. The Gate API "must not expose an ambiguous generic `ancestor()` that merges structural and spatial parentage". [design direction] — same

269. [design direction] — same
270. Budgets must be conserved by partition or escrow across siblings: "issuing many children each with the parent's maximum is not attenuation". [mechanism] — same
271. **Activation** is named as the one genuinely missing lifecycle concept: a Yi is durable identity, an Activation is "one reversible live behavior contributed to it", with durable desired state separate from observed phase, generation fencing, leases and reconciliation — "stopping behavior never extinguishes durable identity". [design direction] — same

274. Governed evolution keeps four distinct verbs — `attach` (vocabulary/behavior), `materialize` (add context-owned representation through a full birth ceremony), `wrap` (a new Yi representing a bounded right over the original), `convert` (migrate semantic identity) — and "the SDK must not fake materialization by calling an internal Kind-membership path". 275. Accepted decision: "Every executable verified `Yi<K>` is a target- and exact-interface-bound in-process object capability. `YiRef<K>` is inert designation" — opening a reference is deliberately a capability-opening operation; naming one is not. [design direction] — `.../sdk-elegance-exploration.md`

278. [mechanism] — same
279. Every remote capability token is audience-bound to its exact World and Authority/protocol root, with presenter-binding the secure default; bearer-within-audience is a restricted, short-lived, budgeted, non-delegating bootstrap mode. [design direction] — same

### S. The counter-theory: what possession cannot express (2026 reviews)

306. 310. **Speech, reach, and moderation must not be collapsed** into one substrate layer (the AT Protocol lesson: PDS / Relay / App View / feed generators / labelers are separate services). [design direction] — same

313. Possession of *things* needs transactional aggregate semantics — atomic transfer, anti-duplication, stack limits, escrow, server-authoritative validation — otherwise "item as actor" works in demos and fails in real economies. [observation] — same
314. The object-capability claim is only partly true while invocation remains globally addressable-by-uid and then gated; without unforgeable possession, attenuation-by-construction, no ambient authority and revocation semantics, it should be called "capability-oriented access control", not object-capability security. [observation] — same
315. **Anti-pattern: metaphysical compression.** "If every concept is declared 'just a tie,' the architecture may feel elegant while losing operational handles" — membership vs role vs ban, holding vs containment vs delegation, feed membership vs read permission, message causality vs storage parentage, presence vs durable state are *product facts*, not notation. [value stance] — same

## Genealogy

**2024-04 → 2024-06, the pre-history.** The token half of the theory is already present two years early, in game vocabulary. Functional tokens are "just like an item in world of Warcraft, you can create status changes, use, or summon" — tokens that open doors, act as credentials, call APIs, execute contracts; a wallet is "a bit like the inventory in games", where you can search who holds what and communicate through tokens (2024-04-26). By 2024-06 the frame is a multiplayer world in which AI characters "create tokens, give meanings to them, or represent a type of value… or create their own currency systems" (2024-06-22). The inventory metaphor never leaves: it is still the sword example in 2026.

**2025-08-10 — `2025-08-USIS-review.md`, the assessment.** A three-part appraisal of the framework then called **USIS (Universal Symbolic Interaction System)**, with primitives ordered **Token–Spirit–Space**. Part 1 asks whether the primitives are complete and coherent and answers "broadly sufficient", grounding the claim in capability-based security, RBAC, symbolic interactionism and the DeSoc/Soulbound literature. Part 2 enumerates social applications. Part 3 is a full protocol sketch: Spirit IDs (public key / DID / UUID), Space IDs, token records with issuer/holder/expiry/revoked/attributes, rule definitions in a DSL, reactive and proactive evaluation modes, standard interfaces (`issueToken`, `transferToken`, `revokeToken`, `hasToken`, `getTokens`, `getHolders`, `defineTokenType`, `defineRule`), event hooks, unforgeability and signatures, and four extensions — cross-space interaction, privacy/ZK, scaling/sharding, and wallet-like client UX. It closes with the adoption constraint and the internet analogy. Its cited inputs (`usis.md`, a "USIS Enhanced Economic and Implementation" PDF) are not in the corpus; the citation pattern shows the derivation **usis.md → economic/implementation deep-research → USIS review → STS paper**.

**2025-08-10 — `2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`, the paper.** Renamed **STS (Spirit–Token–Space)** and reordered to put the agent first. Three things are *added* relative to the review: the disjointness discipline; the Searle mapping and the framing of the grammar as status function declarations; and the "Toward a New Social Paradigm" section, which converts a protocol into a research programme — societies as designable systems, a library of proven social components, institutional change as versioned patches, and the reassurance that formalisation does not displace values. What it *drops* is the entire implementation layer: data structures, interfaces, security model, ZK privacy, sharding, wallet UX. The stored file is a **concatenation of two passes of the same paper** — a citation-laden deep-research pass (lines 1–75) and a cleaned pass from line 75 onward. The second pass is the better text; the first is the provenance record, and both should be kept.

**2025-08-23 — the vocabulary in service.** In a PRD review Ren maps the same three words onto a memory system: spirit = `user_id` plus sub-agents, token = memory shards as minimal composable statements, space = the column grouping tokens under contexts. STS is already a general-purpose lens rather than a social-systems proposal only.

**2026-03-23/24 — the circular architecture.** Ren restates the primitives as *mutually containing* and immediately drives the recursion: tokens are the graph (membership, contributions, value systems, positions, skills, tools, access control), granted between spirits, held in spaces-as-inventories that can hold further tokens and sub-spaces, with compositional effects (A & B ⇒ may create C), fungibility and transferability per need, and traversal over the resulting network. The token-as-relationship discipline appears here: the graph carries relations; chat stays in a conventional backend.

**2026-04-17/20 — the v0.1 spec and its compressed statement.** SST exists as a written spec (the "Sprite" of the code, the "Spirit" of the conversation) with capability-first UCAN-style auth, schemas with traits and hooks, lazy cascading revocation via bindings, holder-only default visibility, prototypal extension by `extends` token, embedded spirits bound to a token's lifecycle, a FUSE filesystem realization in which the daemon *is* the runtime, and mycological composition without a central orchestrator. The 2026-04-20 summary is the most complete single statement of STS as an engineering design anywhere in the corpus — and it is the last one before the reductions begin.

**2026-04-18 — the two-primitive reduction.** "Spirit and pointer." Tested against where authorization would live, walked back to three, then reworked as a hybrid in which tokens are a *shape of spirit* in the graph, delegation is graph mutation, revocation is removal, caveats are contained condition-spirits, and visibility is a policy-spirit rather than a bit.

**2026-04-23 — the missing word.** "Is there a word of abstraction that describes both space and group?" The question that 間 answers eight days later.

**2026-04-26 — the actor ontology.** Spirit restated as agent-in-environment; the honest admission that no category theory yet keeps holdings from overlapping; the actor model named as the real target; coordinates replaced by dynamic paths with the database as the environment; generators added; values codeable as rules of the world; prototyped societies as forkable seeds; and the bonfire ethic stated in full.

**2026-04-30 — the ideal SST, and the decision to rename.** Ren asks what the *ideal* SST would be, "doesn't have to be these primitives"; tests candidate primitive sets against each cognitive architecture and each programming paradigm ("actor model 几乎是 SST 的同构"); states the purpose as a digital operating system for 仁义礼智信; and concludes "我觉得它已经不应该叫sst了" — it should no longer be called SST — asking for a name constructible from 古文.

## What was lost along the way

1. **The whole 2026-03/04 SST engineering design.** Neither reading draft saw it, and no Bonfire document carries it forward. Lost with it: the **FUSE realization** (Space = directory, Token = file, proof chain as xattrs, "the FUSE daemon *is* the runtime", the Unix toolchain becoming SST-native); **mycological composition** (pipelines emerging from inbox writes and local hook declarations, with no orchestrator); **prototypal extension** by `extends` token, so sub-communities and agent specialisations exist "without `parent_id` columns anywhere"; **embedded spirits** whose lifecycle is bound to a token, letting agents be granted like capabilities; **holder-only default visibility** ("A Spirit's Space is not a public profile"); the **daemon capability ceiling** that refuses to register a hook exceeding the agent's actual authority; and the explicit **defence of three primitives** against both two and four. The 2026 substrate re-derives some of these (ports are not copied onto holders; sigils are capability-clamped) without knowing it is re-deriving them.
2. **Generators.** "They can generate spirits, or holdings… generator can also do things like trade, they can exchange" (2026-04-26). Nothing in the Yi vocabulary names a conditional producer of entities; kits install, ports fire, but nothing generates.
3. **間 itself.** The name Ren chose to replace SST, with its stated commitments — non-duality of 間 and actor, one cosmic 間 with parallel universes, caveat and visibility as emergent from relations, philosophy (理) before engineering — appears in Bonfire only as two sentences ("Bonfire is an encoded realization of 间理", and the 间理 "no bypass for performance" law). The substrate is named 一, not 間, and the derivation between them is nowhere written.
4. **The theoretical apparatus.** Searle, Mead/Blumer, Goffman, North, Ostrom, complexity theory, ritual anthropology and game design ground every primitive in the 2025 papers. The 2026 documents contain none of it. A reader of 2026 alone would not know that "a role is literally a token a person possesses" is Goffman, or that the consent door is Ostrom's boundary principle in another key.
5. **The word "Space" as a primitive.** By 2026 the triad is gone: spirit survives, token becomes holding, and space fragments into session, community, path, Domain, World and tenancy boundary. The idea that meaning is always context-relative survives; the *name* that made it memorable does not.
6. **Quantitative and economic tokens.** The 2025 paper devotes a whole application section to symbolic economies — fungible community currency, divisible quantities, contribution credits, grants, blending economic weight with voting weight. The 2026 community kit has wishes, needs, witness, repair, ritual and presence — and no currency at all. No document records the decision.
7. **Quadratic voting, delegation/liquid democracy, and formal voting mechanics.** The platform model's v1 votes are "up-only, post-level, new/top sorts". The 2025 vote-token machinery (single-use tokens, burning, quorum enforcement, delegation chains, automatic outcome issuance) does not reappear.
8. **Cross-space interoperability.** The review's cross-space recognition (Space A honouring Space B's tokens, industry-standard credential types, a directory of spaces, bridges and oracles) is the mechanism that would turn many communities into a society. In 2026 the corresponding item — community transfer and sovereignty mechanics — is on the *deferred* list.
9. **Privacy by selective disclosure.** Zero-knowledge proofs of possession ("I have a valid token granting access" without revealing which) and encrypted token contents for sensitive spaces. 2026 has private-by-default message spaces and visibility filtering, which is a different and weaker thing.
10. **The wallet UX / "what can I do in this space?" surface.** A person seeing all their tokens across all spaces, with the system explaining their capacities — which anticipates exactly the policy-explainability gap the 2026 adversarial review reopens as unsolved. The 2025 version had it as a design item; the 2026 version has it as a hole.
11. **The research-programme framing.** "Civilization prototyping", the interdisciplinary field of social system design, and the library of proven social components become, in 2026, presets and a marketplace. The commercial form survives; the scholarly ambition does not.
12. **The named risk of badly calibrated incentives.** The game-design warning that rewards too easy or too hard get gamed has no successor in the 2026 anti-pattern list.
13. **The full Community Kit text.** Reduced 2026-08-15 to a section-number skeleton pointing at code. Sections 1–4, 6, 7, most of 9, 12.2+, 13 and two of three wave lessons exist only as absences implied by the surviving numbering. The *reasoning* for each surface now exists only as executable examples — and code is not an argument.
14. **"Touch" as the universal write verb, and its Whitehead lineage.** The glossary still defines it ("Every event is a 'touching' of one Yi by another"), but the live vocabulary replaced it with `manifest`. The claim that mutation *is* one Yi touching another — a genuinely metaphysical claim — has no home in the current frame.
15. **"Even the type of a thing is a thing."** Recorded as founder intent, with a planned phase to make "a kind is a Yi" true ergonomically. The current glossary states the opposite for storage: a Kind is "stored in the Kinds repository, **never as a Yi**". The self-description property that the pivot praised as "what makes Lisp and RDF low-resistance" was traded for compiler closure.
16. **Tie-duality as an explanatory frame.** Kept as rationale rather than as the taught model; still the north-star hypothesis in the ideal architecture, but no current concept page teaches it. The insight that `kinds[]` membership, holding edges and the message DAG are one primitive in three costumes is findable only in the pivot's diagnosis.
17. **The seed-trait vocabulary.** `identity`, `agent`, `messageable`, `streamed`, `membered`, `container` as first-class named traits each with a one-line contract. `membered` and `streamed` survive; `messageable` and the trait-contract discipline do not appear in the current concept docs.
18. **The projection pillar as a single named mechanism.** "One tie-indexed, rebuildable, columnar projection — simultaneously the author's declared relation/aggregate, storage's ECS-archetype fast path, delivery's interest/recipient set, and the cross-row read engine", recognised as the answer to four criticisms at once. The Views stance covers read products but drops the unifying claim.
19. **The reactivity triad and the inline pre-commit veto lane.** `sys/reaction` with `inline | deferred | sync` lanes, where inline is a pre-commit moderation/anti-abuse veto that rolls back row and event together. Mentioned in passing; taught nowhere.
20. **Collective capability as an index intersection.** The invariants survive; the mechanism note — both legs liveness-checked, bounded to one hop, sequenced last because it is the one second-order gate — is only in the pivot.
21. **The measured numbers.** 42/100 pre-pivot, 81/100 post-spine, 66/100 grand-review synthesis, ~24 → ~12 concept target with ~17 measured, ~84 sub-face re-derivations, ~57 legacy accessors, 144 runtime + 167 type exports at the SDK root, 49 Core entrypoints over ~1,017 modules. These are the only quantitative evidence the programme has for its central claim, and they exist only in the pivot, unification and elegance documents.
22. **The correctness-debt register and its lessons.** The full-table scan per gated invoke, "dead for reads, live for authz" cascade-dead memberships, trigger budgets resetting per hop, the built-but-wired-to-nothing install-scope guard, and the lesson that "the design reasoned from the static code path without checking gate-reachability; red-test-first caught it."
23. **The explicit deferrals, with their reasons.** GDPR-delete / E2E and federation/firehose parked by founder's call because "erasure-vs-signed-log and the cross-host tie graph are **not** bolt-on-able after the signing substrate solidifies, and are **not** 'solved below the seam.'" The federation page lists the deferred work without the warning.
24. **The runtime verb vocabulary and the per-touch observability handle.** `manifest · touch · invoke · read · where · addKind · patch · extinguish · discover · explain`, and `delivered → responded → reactions`. The current SDK direction replaces these; the observability handle has no successor.
25. **Time-travel reads.** Sequence-folding, retention horizons and a fold-cost estimator described as "a UI cost-awareness primitive" survive only in the glossary.
26. **The four-doors pedagogy.** "Use an existing product; author a Kit; give narrow capabilities to an agent; operate infrastructure", and the mental path `connect → browse/open → try dynamically → inspect → pull → use with types`, exist only in the elegance exploration.

## Open questions and critiques

### Raised inside the corpus

1. **Phase 0 aimed at the wrong hard problem.** "One table" was already true in Postgres and in memory; the remaining split was the face-shaped writer/index/lifecycle contract. Framing it as "merge two repos" "will cause agents to delete useful abstractions instead of solving the actual coupling." — `bonfire-proposals/design/frame-pivot-adversarial-review-2026-06-19.md`
2. **The relation compiler is destination-only** and must not be constitutional: "It is currently a destination idea, not an engineering primitive with algebra, cost model, indexes, rebuild, and tests." Membership should be the single first proof. — same; `design/frame-pivot-evaluation.md`
3. **"Every representation is rebuildable" is too strong** under event retention; history is best-effort truncated, so rebuilds must run from canonical rows, not assumed-complete event history. — both audits
4. **Product governance is under-specified**: resource-scoped delegation, negative rights, role precedence, explainability, audit trails, moderation sanctions, rate/abuse signals, revocation and cache invalidation are all missing from a model that gates on possession. — adversarial review
5. **Fanout is broadcast-filtered, not interest-indexed.** One delivery chain, per-connection visibility checks, backlog growing without bound if append-rate × fanout cost exceeds 1. — adversarial review
6. **Hot authorization paths still scan**, and do not check full cascade liveness, so a burned upstream binding can be mishandled. Enforcement-by-construction has a performance *and* a correctness hole underneath it. — both 2026-06-19 audits
7. **Tie/Moment is overloaded as a taught primitive**; folding traits, messages, port firings, audit events and standing relations into one word "risks replacing the old zoo with a more abstract one." — `frame-pivot-evaluation.md`; `frame-pivot-claim-audit.md`
8. **The agency law is slightly too strong**: initiating should require agency, holding a capability should not, and receiving ports may need only `messageable`. — `frame-pivot-evaluation.md`
9. **"Phase 1 is pure deletion" is overstated** — it is cross-package migration with tests, and `Kind` cannot shrink before every stored field has a classified replacement home. — both
10. **The object-capability claim is only partly true** while invocation is addressable-by-uid then gated. — adversarial review
11. **Presence may belong outside durable Yi semantics entirely** — recorded as an open decision. — `frame-pivot-claim-audit-2026-06-19.md`
12. **A live self-contradiction in the North Star**: a reply was described as both a relation and "not a tie at all" four lines apart; resolved by ruling that a reply is a relation by reading-lens and stream-internal by storage. — `stream-tier-conceptualization-2026-06-21.md`
13. **Names can be lies.** `yi_message` asserted yi-ness against a tier defined by not being a Yi; the fix was a cross-cutting code rename, not a doc edit. — same
14. **The keystone gap.** The frame calls the face-shaped writer/index/API contract collapse "not optional and not deferred", yet HEAD shipped three face-shaped repo APIs, four write builders, two visibility engines and two lifecycle engines — "The BYTES are one table; the CONTRACT above them is not." — `06-unification.md`
15. **The concept budget was unmet and unmeasured.** The first actual count was ~17 against ≤12, and "the thing the pivot is *for* is the one thing not on the scorecard." — same
16. **Built-but-unwired while marked CLOSED** — the one-graph `explain` and the compiled gate cache: "the one place the docs slip from their otherwise-exemplary honesty." — same
17. **Elegance work caused a security regression.** Folding the read surface onto the ungated substrate read leaked hidden rows and private signing keys, with history and subscribe inheriting it and a count query leaking cardinality. — same
18. **The relocation trap.** Renaming faces to traits, or moving a flag into a policy object, "does NOT reduce the held-concept count"; closure, not relabeling, is the acceptance test. — `02-pivot.md`
19. **Unification refuted at the mechanism level.** Explicit NO-GOs: feature markers into the MRO, lifecycle/claims into reactions, payload behind an SPI, `can_mint`/license into the gate ADT — each a behavior change or a hot-path/security regression. — `08-ideal-architecture.md`
20. **Law 10's own inversion.** Subtract-first was violated in execution under goal pressure; the response was to keep the law and name the cost rather than soften it. — `06-unification.md`
21. **Two owners for World product semantics**, kept equal only by parity tests: "every new operation or security correction still has two implementation sites." — `sdk-elegance-exploration.md`; Ren, 2026-09-02
22. **Core's export topology contradicts its internal rigor.** 49 entrypoints, a 787-line root exporting 248 runtime + 294 type names reaching ~737 modules and Node built-ins; "the path name does not predict the role." — `sdk-elegance-exploration.md`
23. **`std` boundary inversion.** The standard library imports Core runtime and SDK advanced paths and accepts a raw runtime, while SDK test fixtures import `std` — "intent without isolation". — same

### Found by reading the corpus

31. **Possession cannot express prohibition — and this is a theoretical fault, not an implementation gap.** The founding move is that a social fact is something a spirit *holds*. Bans, mutes, blocks and timeouts are social facts precisely *not* held by their subject. The 2026 review notices the symptom (negative rights are hard) without drawing the conclusion: "a Spirit holds a Token in a Space" is incomplete, and a second relation — a space *imposing* a state on a spirit — may be irreducible. This is the single largest hole in the theory as theory.
32. **"Unauthorized actions are naturally impossible" is politically double-edged.** The same property that removes the whim of leaders removes mercy, exception, civil disobedience and the discretionary judgement every functioning institution relies on. The 2025 paper presents impossibility-by-construction as pure gain; nothing in the corpus asks what a society loses when its rules cannot be bent — which sits badly against Ren's own anti-totalisation commitments elsewhere.
33. **Transparency is treated as unambiguously good in 2025 and quietly refuted in 2026.** "Everyone knows who is a member by seeing who holds the token" versus private repair queues, non-punitive absence, holder-only default visibility and caller-sensitive reads. The community kit is a *critique* of the paper's transparency assumption and nobody has said so.
34. **The economic half went missing and nobody recorded the decision.** No document explains why the community kit has no currency, credits or contribution accounting when the 2025 paper devoted an application section to them. Deliberate scoping or drift?
35. **Emergence is claimed but never demonstrated.** The complexity-theory argument (simple rules → unplanned institutions) is central to the paper's ambition and supported by no observation anywhere. The coliving example composes designed pieces; nothing is reported as emerging that was not designed.
36. **"Agents are operators, not exceptions" needs a stress test.** Equal membership for agents plus "an agent someone asked in chat" being able to rewrite a sub-group's join gate means the rate at which a community's constitution can change is bounded by conversation speed, not deliberation speed. The consent door covers *installs*; it is not stated to cover *gate edits*.
37. **Consent is per-install, not per-use.** The door records that a bot entered; nothing describes ongoing consent, periodic review, or a community's ability to see what an installed agent has been doing — against the corpus's own emphasis on audit and explainability.
38. **The named-spirit set is asserted closed without argument.** "Presence closed the named-spirit set" — but why are presence, repair, doorway and ritual sufficient? Provision, decision-making and resource allocation have no spirit.
39. **Three ontologies now coexist unreconciled, not two.** The 2025 papers say there are three kinds of thing (spirit, token, space). The 2026-05 conversation says there is one non-dual thing (間, which is also actor, 事, 机). The 2026 substrate says there is one root (Yi 一) bearing traits. No document performs either translation — and the second gap matters most, because the substrate is named for *unity* while the concept Ren actually chose was named for *betweenness*.
40. **一 vs 易 is unresolved and materially changes the reading.** Bonfire glosses Yi as 一/太极 ("the one"); the wider corpus uses 易 (change, the Yijing) for its algebra. Whether the pun is intended or the two projects diverged is nowhere stated. A reader coming from the 易 side will assume the substrate is named for change; it is named for unity.
41. **The Whitehead thread is orphaned.** "Occasion" is Whitehead's term and "Touch" is explicitly credited to him, but the process-philosophy reading — reality as a succession of atomic occasions rather than enduring substances — is never developed, even though the substrate's central move (a root with almost no properties, whose being is its participation in occasions) is a process-metaphysical claim. This is the single biggest unexploited theoretical asset in the corpus. Ren himself flagged the choice and left it open: "process philosophy和海德格尔也很有意思，我不确定是否应该引入" (2026-05-02).
42. **"One thing, several truths" and "graphs do not infer one another" are taught at different volumes.** The first says a thing's truths overlap on one identity; the second says overlap must never license inference. Both are correct, but the frame leads with the first and prints the second as fine print — and every recorded security incident in the corpus came from the second being violated. A stronger formulation would lead with the non-inference algebra.
43. **The concept-count metric may not measure conceptual load.** G8 counts kept vocabulary, legacy accessors, dispatch surfaces and capability branches — proxies for *surface* size. A developer's actual burden includes the seven graphs, the ten laws, the four control axes, the three delivery contracts and the lane semantics, none of which are counted.
44. **The refusal to unify is doing more philosophical work than the unification.** §16 "What is not in the frame", the keep-as-is discipline, the non-inheritance laws and "one language, several owned graphs" are what make Yi *not* a totalizing schema. This is arguably the most transferable idea in the corpus — a general method for bounded unification — and it is scattered across four documents with no single statement.
45. **The metaphysics is stated only as engineering.** Nothing states the ontological thesis in its own right: identity as bare denotation, being as composed and revisable, having/doing/permitting as relations owned by different laws, and history as the only thing that is not revisable. A reader outside the codebase cannot currently reach the idea.
46. **No account of who governs the compiler.** The frame makes Kinds "live governed law", but *who* ratifies a family revision, and by what process a community changes its own constitution, is treated as install consent rather than politics — while the use-cases document promises "constitutional packages". The governance of law-change is the missing layer between the two. Ren's 2026-09 openness claim ("anyone can define kinds", "the freedom to have this dynamism") makes the gap sharper, not smaller: unlimited local ontology-making with no stated amendment procedure.
47. **The archive pass lost more than it preserved.** Reducing the Community Kit proposal to headings pointing at code means the reasoning for each surface exists only as executable examples. A later reader can see what the Witness Book does and not why it mints witness holdings to participants.
48. **The sigil containment pattern is a genuine AI-safety result with no writeup.** "The model's output is the least trusted thing in the system; it must not be the thing that decides what code lands in chat", with validate → clamp → inert data → host-owned floor → recoverable error as the mechanism, is a reusable containment pattern for LLM-authored artifacts and appears nowhere else in the corpus.
49. **The 2026-04 SST design was dropped without evaluation.** FUSE-as-runtime, mycological composition and prototypal extension are not refuted anywhere; they simply stop being mentioned when the project becomes a database. At least two of them (composition by environment; extension without parent columns) answer problems the Yi substrate still has open.
