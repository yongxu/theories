The theorems and tests behind the map

The layer under the map: the account's theorems and tests, filed on one web of {{kinds}} kinds of role and the ties between them. A theorem is proved in Lean on a stated model: it says something general about one tie or one role. A case is one test, in its own words, with what in it was taken as which role. The two are kept apart, and the map shows where they have met.

Where reasoning has met a case

A theorem says: if these hold, then that. A frozen prediction is written down before its test is run and names the theorems or the thesis it rests on; its test's record then says which of its conditions were met and what was seen. Of {{preds_frozen}} frozen predictions, {{preds_rest}} rest on a theorem or a thesis and could have come out either way: {{rest_follows}} follow from a theorem under its bridge, {{rest_beyond}} say more than any theorem, {{rest_forecast}} are forecasts. Each is placed here by its own record. Counted apart: {{preds_checks}} checks that name a theorem or a thesis, {{preds_other}} frozen entries that name neither, and {{preds_unfrozen}} entries their records state without a freeze.

What holds of anything that goes on

{{legend}} dynamics name no role: they hold of every course or every process. They are the web's legend: what staying, leaving and returning mean.

Every theorem is about a stated model and checked in Lean; its sentence here is in the kinds' words and its model's own terms, and its conditions are premises the atlas's ledger lists for it. Read a theorem with its conditions. A case cannot make a theorem wrong: it falls under the theorem or outside it. What a case can show wrong is the pairing of its things with the roles (a bridge), a measure, a scoring rule or a thesis. Roles of two models are drawn as one place where each model names that kind of role; where no proof joins the two models, that sameness is a reading.
